No charity to man, for they rather wish all damned with themselves, than any to be freed from their owne prison. As in the blessed there is perfect charity, so in the damned perfect envy. Now nothing is more repugnant to charity than malice and hatred. But it is objected, that inordinate affections are not taken away from the damned: therefore they would not have them condemned in hell, whom they <span class="ad-conj" title="Editorial conjecture: illegible in the 1633 source (lost in the gutter of the EEBO microfilm); supplied from the stated evidence (编者推补；非原书原文)">[inordinately]</span> affected upon earth. *Ans.* The love that is grounded upon vertue, is constant and durable: such charity we shall beare with us to heaven, and bee made perfect in it. But the affection grounded upon lust and sinfull passion, a disease that runs in the bloud, doth quickly vanish: like fire in wet straw, that onely makes a mother, and goes out in stench. Therefore the adulterer, though he so dotes on his Mistris, that hee is content to venture his soule for her embraces: yet having lost that soule, hee doth as heartily wish her in the same bed of torment: that as they have beene delighted together, so they might be afflicted together. *Object.* But as by the multitude of participants the joyes of heaven are enlarged, so are the sorrowes of hell encreased: how then will they desire more company, when thereby they inhaunce their owne penalty? *Ans.* Yet such is their unchangeable malice, that it contents them not to suffer their owne singular torments; but had rather endure more grievous misery, to have a more numerous society. And for the rich mans prayer for his brethren, *Luk.* 16. It proceeded not from a charitable soule, but from feare and horror of more torments to be multiplied on himselfe. *Non fratres <span class="ad-conj" title="Editorial conjecture: illegible in the 1633 source (lost in the gutter of the EEBO microfilm); supplied from the stated evidence (编者推补；非原书原文)">[respexit]</span>, fed seipsum respexit:* he desired not their salvation, but his owne lesse damnation. He knew that being the elder brother, his vicious example might draw on <span class="ad-pb" id="p512" data-p="512" data-facs="265"></span> their greater disobedience: and as their sins encreased, so he felt his owne tortures enlarged. Therefore no grace in hell but everlasting sin, no devotion, but extreame damnation.

The wicked in hell still remaine sinners. So Christ saith; *All that came before me are theeves and robbers: Are;* in propriety of speech he should have said; *<span class="ad-conj" title="Editorial conjecture: illegible in the 1633 source (lost in the gutter of the EEBO microfilm); supplied from the stated evidence (编者推补；非原书原文)">[Fuerunt]</span>, they were* theeves: no, *Sunt, they are* still, they remaine so. *Luk.* 13.27. Depart from mee all *ye workers* of iniquity: *Operarij, & ab actu praeterito, & habitu presenti, Chrys. Workers,* in reference both to the act past, and present habit. For he doth not say, yea that have wrought, but *workers: quia iniqui nec post mortem desinunt esse iniqui.* They that dye sinners, remaine sinners even dead: although they cannot sin, yet they retaine the desire of sinning: and he that is a lyer in purpose, ceaseth not to be a lyer in practice. *Mors separat animam à carne, non peccatum <span class="ad-gap" title="TCP: illegible in source">〈◊〉</span> anima.* Death separates the soule from the flesh, it separates not sin from the soule.

Seeing the effect of those horrors is *weeping,* which shall never be comforted; let us prevent them, by *weeping* where we may be comforted. The time of living, is the time of repenting. If a man dies without repentance, repentance is dead to him forever. If we compare, *Mat.* 5.4. with *Luke* 6.25. we shall finde, that the decree of God hath disposed, *Flentes ad risum, ridentes ad fletum;* weepers to laughing, and laughers to weeping.

*Gnashing of teeth;]* This is the effect of an inexpressible sorrow. A just and fit punishment, that they who once gnashed their teeth at others in contempt, should gnash their teeth at themselves in torment. The Psalmist complaines; *They gnashed their teeth at me;* and the Iewes <span class="ad-margin" markdown="1">Acts 7.54.</span> *gnashed on* Stephen *with their teeth.* Therefore <span class="ad-margin" markdown="1">Revel. 16.16.</span> *they shall gnaw their tongues for paine:* their tongues gnawed their neighbours, now they shall gnaw their own tongues. They shewed their teeth in derision, they shall gnash their teeth in damnation. No part of the damned shall be free from anguish, the memory afflicted with pleasures past, the apprehension with terrors present, the understanding with torments to come and continue, the eye with darknesse, the eare with hideous scriechings, the smell with killing stenches, the tast with gall of bitternesse, the very teeth with such an anguish, that the extreamest tooth-ach here is but a pleasure to it. Such is the extremity, universality, and eternity of those paines: if they bee so universall in all parts, O that they were not so extreame! if so extreame; O that not so universall! if both so universall and extreame! O = not so everlasting; each torment easelesse, endlesse, remedilesse! There be *Ignitae lacrymae,* and *frigidi anhelitus.* Therefore called *Avernus; absque vera temperatura* where the freezing cold shal not mitigate the scorching heate, nor the scorching heat qualifie the freezing cold. *Avernus* is a lake in *Italy,* that *Caesar* purged; evaporating such a mortall steame, that it killed the birds which flew over it. Therefore called *Avernùs quasi avibus adversus.* <span class="ad-margin" markdown="1">August:</span> *Profundus sine fundo;* full of incomparable heare, intollerable stench, innumerable griefes. *Hugo, Vermis cum tenebris, flagellum, frigus, & ignis; Daemonis aspectus, scelerum confusio, luctus.* From all these must needs arise the gnashing of teeth. Two things would seeme to mitigate the terror of hell, Patience and Hope: this *gnashing of teeth* excludes them both.

For patience; many grievons extremities have the Saints of God digested on earth by patience: that universall antidote against future evils, and qualification of present severities. It hath blunted the edge of tyranny, and made the sufferers smile in the midst of those pangs, the very sight whereof hath astonished the beholders. Whatsoever the damned suffer, let them have but patience: nay, there shall bee no patience in hell: this gnashing of teeth, is the effect of a most impatient furie. Men commonly say, in necessitated sufferings; what remedy but patience? Patience therefore is a confessed enemy, but all remedie is denyed to the reprobates there; even that poorest succour which the anguished heart can imagine, Patience. O the universall privation in that dismall place! where *Omne aderit quod cruciet, omne deerit quod relevet.* Everything is present that may vexe them, everything absent that <span class="ad-pb" id="p513" data-p="513" data-facs="265"></span> may comfort them. Where they must suffer everlastingly, and cannot suffer patiently.

For Hope, there is none. The proper object of hope is *Arduum bonum,* saith the Schoole: a difficult good. A good of difficulty not of impossibility: where no possibility can be no hope. There *nulla spes boni, nulla desperatio mali, Hugo.* No hope of good, no despaire of evill. Men say in extreme passions; If it were not for hope, the heart would burst: there is no hope, yet the heart must hold: the misery is, that it cannot burst; but lyes (like a tormented Malefactour) upon the wheele, ever dying, yet without all hope to dye. *Dissolutionem cupit, & <span class="ad-conj" title="Editorial conjecture: illegible in the 1633 source (lost in the gutter of the EEBO microfilm); supplied from the stated evidence (编者推补；非原书原文)">[dissolutionem]</span> non capit damnatus.* There is no hope in hell, no hope with us on earth, for them that are in hell. We cannot hope for the devills; they are condemned to hell, and past hope: nor can we hope for the dead, because there is no purgatory. Indeed concerning the dead, there may be hope of their happy condition, but none of their permutation. This is a double torment; neither deliverance, nor hope of deliverance. Sad and heavy despaire absolves their infelicity: comfort they neither feele, nor have hope to feele.

Seeing only hope is confined to this life, let us make much of it, that it may enrich us. <span class="ad-margin" markdown="1">Rom. 5.5.</span> *Hope makes not ashamed,* because it is never disappointed: for if it could illuded, it would be ashamed. *Spes vitae immortalis est vita vitae mortalis, Aug.* The hope of life immortall, is the life of our life mortall. The Poets faine, that all the gods and goddesses, that is, vertues and graces, did once dwell upon the earth. = finding all things so corrupt, and men so bad company, they all went up to heaven with Iustice; *Terras Astraea reliquit.* All but onely *Hope,* and shee stayed behind still. But now if we hope well, we must doe well. *Hee tempts God, does not hope in God, that hoping doth nothing for himselfe,* <span class="ad-margin" markdown="1">Luk. 13.8.</span> *Aug.* Though there be hope of the *barren figtree,* yet still the dresser labours in the manuring of it. It is in vaine for a man to hope his children shall doe well, when he teacheth them ill. The meanes must be used, where hope is nourished. Hope is onely for the present: the Saints in heaven have no hope, for they are in full possession of joy: the damned in hell have no hope, for they are in full possession of torment. Onely the living have hope, and the living GOD is their hope: which himselfe blesse and answere in IESUS CHRIST.

The last question is, *ubi sit,* what is the place of hell, my text saies. It is *Downe <span class="ad-conj" title="Editorial conjecture: illegible in the 1633 source (lost in the gutter of the EEBO microfilm); supplied from the stated evidence (编者推补；非原书原文)">[ward.* So]</span> doth the Scripture frequently. Let them be cast into the *Deepe pits,* <span class="ad-margin" markdown="1">Psal 110.10.</span> that they rise not up againe. Bring them *Downe into the pit* of destruction. <span class="ad-margin" markdown="1">Prov. 9.18.</span> They are in the *depth* of hell. The way of life is above to the wise, that he may depart from hell *beneath.* So the termes declare it, and the word describes it. *Sheol,* <span class="ad-margin" markdown="1">Prov. 15.24.</span> which is taken for a pit, grave, or hell,; all *Downewards. Mercer. in Gen.* 37. sayes that *Sheol* signifies all places under the earth. It must be below, because it is every where opposed to heaven which is highest of all. *Abyssus,* which is a great deepe, a vast gulfe under the earth, a bottomlesse pit: the devills intreated Christ not to send them to that <span class="ad-conj" title="Editorial conjecture: illegible in the 1633 source (lost in the gutter of the EEBO microfilm); supplied from the stated evidence (编者推补；非原书原文)">[place]</span>. *Luk.* 8.31. *Into the Abysse.* The Apostles that preached to the Iewes, used the word *Gehenna, Iam.* 3.6. It is set on fire of *Gehenna* Hell. They that preached the Gentiles, used *Hades:* which they tooke to be a place under the earth ordeined for punishment. The word here used is *Tartarus: Hesiod* affirmes it to be so fare under the earth, as heaven is above it. So the Rabbines held; *Sheol* is absolute below, the very center, *Esa.* 14.9. Hell from *Beneath* is moved, *Deut.* 32.22. I <span class="ad-conj" title="Editorial conjecture: illegible in the 1633 source (lost in the gutter of the EEBO microfilm); supplied from the stated evidence (编者推补；非原书原文)">[will]</span> burne *ad Infernum inferiorem* to the lowest hell. *Nic. de Lyr.* affirmes is *Circa centrum terrae. Tertull. in Apologet.* Hell is a subterrane treasure of hidden fire. The poets so tooke it; *Facilis descensus averni; Sed revocare gradum* &c. *Quicquid perit <span class="ad-conj" title="Editorial conjecture: illegible in the 1633 source (lost in the gutter of the EEBO microfilm); supplied from the stated evidence (编者推补；非原书原文)">[deorsum]</span> fertur.* All things perishing, sinke downewards.

But against this it is objected, that Dives in hell saw *Abraham* and *Lazarus* which he could not doe; if hell was so deepe and remote a bottom wherein he lay <span class="ad-conj" title="Editorial conjecture: illegible in the 1633 source (lost in the gutter of the EEBO microfilm); supplied from the stated evidence (编者推补；非原书原文)">[over]</span> whelmed. And albeit hell is below and *downeward* in respect of heaven, yet <span class="ad-pb" id="p514" data-p="514" data-facs="266"></span> happily it is not so in regard of earth, *Rev.* 12.12. Woe to the inhabitants of the earth, for the Divell is come *Downe amongst you:* yet hee was then cast no lower than the superface of the earth. There be diverse arguments on both sides. First = they that live, know not the state of the dead: so the dead know not the state of the living on earth, much lesse of the Saints in heaven, *Greg.* So *August.* As the rich man had a care of his brethren living, yet he knew not what they did: so have men a care of their dead friends, yet know not how they speed. Against this is opposed, that if they in hell had not the sight of heaven, their owne sufferings would lesse afflict them: for their most grievous torment shall arise from the vision of what joyes they have lost. <span class="ad-margin" markdown="1">Wisd. 5.</span> *Wisd.* 5.2. *When they shall see it, they shall bee troubled with terrible feare,* and be amased at the Saints salvation. So *Bern.* The faithfull shall have a sight of hell, and the unfaithfull a sight of heaven. *Vt illi magis laetentur, vide<span class="ad-gap" title="TCP: illegible in source">••</span> quid evaserint: & hi gravius crucientur, videntes quid amiserint.* That the one may be rejoyced, by seeing what horrors they have escaped: and the other may be afflicted, by seeing what comforts they have forfeited. <span class="ad-margin" markdown="1">Psal. 112.10.</span> *The wicked shall see it, and be grieved:* he shall gnash with his teeth, and melt away. Barre the sight of their eis and you ease the griefe of their hearts. *Luk.* 13.28. That weeping and gnashing of teeth proceeds from sight; *when you shall see Abraham* &c. *in the kingdome of God.* It is the exile from the presence of the Lambe, from the societie of Saints and Angels from the felicitie and joyes they see; that most bitterly scourgeth them. The not knowing of earthly affaires never troubles them: but heaven they must in part see and know, else they cannot be tormented with the losse. But on the other side it is said, that the sight of heaven is never afforded to Saints in the flesh but as an inestimable favour. It was *Pauls* greatest grace, and that which had like to have endanger'd him unto pride, to be wrapt up into the third heaven, and behold the life which the blessed live with God. But what extraordinary grace was this, if it be also granted to the reprobates? *Answ.* Saint *Paul* saw it *experiendo,* by tasting it: and hoped againe to see it *Participando,* by possessing it: such a sight is not permitted to the children of perdition. They onely see it to the griefe of their hearts, that they cannot enjoy it.

The Schoole gives this conclusive summe; that the damned shall behold the glory of heaven before the day of Iudgment, but not after. Neither shall they know it as it is in it selfe; but onely by a kind of luscous and glimmering sight perceive it to be an invaluable glory. And this shall vex them, both that they can no better see it, and shall never tast it. Afterwards they shall be deprived of that vision, and shut up in everlasting night, neither shall the withdrawing of this vision diminish their tortures: because the remembrance of that once seene, shall for ever sticke by them. Hence they shall continually grieve, finding themselves unworthy, *Gloriam t<span class="ad-gap" title="TCP: illegible in source">••</span> videre, quam Sancti permittuntur habere:* even to see those pleasures, which the godly are vouchsafed to inherite and inhabite for ever. But how could that *rich man,* or can the damned spirits be said to see the glory of heaven, when as they want those luminary organs of the body, the disposition of sight; besides the thicke interposed darknesse? *Answ.* This is no reason, for even spirits see, and have the eyes of intelligence and apprehension, able to distinguish betweene light and darkenesse. They apprehend this glory either universally or particularly. An universall apprehension they have, whereby they perceive the Saints to be in great glory; in particular what this glory is they know not. At a great feast, the beggar at the doore sees in part the joy and cheare of the guests; but not so well as the guests themselves that are banketing. And as this must needs grieve the beggar, to see it and not to tast it: so shall the damned vex, *Tam propter invidiam alienae faelicitatis, <span class="ad-conj" title="Editorial conjecture: illegible in the 1633 source (lost in the gutter of the EEBO microfilm); supplied from the stated evidence (编者推补；非原书原文)">[tum]</span> propter carentiam illius qutetis;* for envie both at others plenty, and their owne want.

Thus if we grant that the damned shall see the glory of heaven, then it will <span class="ad-conj" title="Editorial conjecture: illegible in the 1633 source (lost in the gutter of the EEBO microfilm); supplied from the stated evidence (编者推补；非原书原文)">[pro-]</span> bably follow that hell is in the aire; onely separated with an unpassable gulfe. If they doe not see it, then is it likely to be in the bowels of the earth. Howsoever, it is *below, Downewards,* in the inferior parts of Gods workemanship. But precisely to say where; whether in the aire, water, on the face of the earth, or, in the center of the worlds center; *Tegitur, non legitur: periculosè disquiritur, tutò ignoratur:* we may safely be ignorant of it, wee cannot but dangerously dispute it. Only, as just spirits dissolved from their bodyes, presently ascend to the Empyreall heaven: so the soules of the lost tarry below, confined to the inferior Elements, there to be punished. If any aske further about the locall place of hell, I answere with *Socrates,* I never was there my selfe, nor spoke with any that came from thence. When one demanded what the gods did and loved, *Euclides* answered; whatsoever they doe or love, I am sure they hate all curious examiners. Many doubt *Vbt* <span class="ad-conj" title="Editorial conjecture: illegible in the 1633 source (lost in the gutter of the EEBO microfilm); supplied from the stated evidence (编者推补；非原书原文)">[sit]</span>, where it is: none can describe *Quid sit,* what it is: but all agree *Quod sit,* that it is.

Seeing hell is a descent, and a bottome downewards, let us keepe our selves so farre as we can from it while we live, that it may never devoure us when we die. Sin doth naturally sinke downeward, and separate from God who is above. A sinner ever descendeth till he come to the lowest that may be: his affections are downewards, and sure his hope and inheritance is not above. But as we bury dead flesh under the ground, so it is not unlikely of dead soules. And as the heaviest bodies draw to the center of the earth, so doe the saddest and heaviest spirits which the mercie of God hath forsaken, *Luk.* 13.11. We read of a woman *bowed downe* with a = of infirmitie 18 yeares, and could in no wise lift up her selfe. A wofull estate <span class="ad-conj" title="Editorial conjecture: illegible in the 1633 source (lost in the gutter of the EEBO microfilm); supplied from the stated evidence (编者推补；非原书原文)">[described]</span> by the Evangelist: *Egritudinis manifestas, behold: aegrotantis fragilitas, a woman: miseriae acerbitas,* it was a *spirit of infirmitie: morbi diuturnitas, eighteene yeares: corporis curvitas, bowed together: elevandi impossibilitas, could not lift up her selfe.* Such the estate of wicked sinners; that if their bodies were like their soules, *incederunt <span class="ad-conj" title="Editorial conjecture: illegible in the 1633 source (lost in the gutter of the EEBO microfilm); supplied from the stated evidence (编者推补；非原书原文)">[Quadrupedes]</span>,* they would grovell like beasts. And indeed *Bestialior quàm ipsa bestia est homo, ratione vigens, & non ratione vivens.* They cast themselves downe, and none but IESUS CHRIST can helpe them up, *Luk.* 10.30. A certaine man *went downe* from Ierusalem to Iericho, and fell among theeves. From Ierusalem *downe* Iericho: hell is downe a hill. Iericho signifies the Moone, *Ieron.* He that walkes after the Moone of this inconstant world, must needs fall among theeves. Sin brings man easily *downe* to Iericho: *Sed revocare gradum, superásque evadere ad auras, Hic labor, hoc opus est.* The rule of Philosophy is, that *levia surgunt,* light things ascend upwards: yet is nothing lighter than vaine thoughts, and they sinke *downewards:* sinne is hells high way. <span class="ad-margin" markdown="1">Col. 3.1.</span> If ye be risen with CHRIST, *seeke those things <span class="ad-conj" title="Editorial conjecture: illegible in the 1633 source (lost in the gutter of the EEBO microfilm); supplied from the stated evidence (编者推补；非原书原文)">[that]</span> are above.*

First, understand the things, then undertake the search. Though we cannot throughly see them, yet let us throughly seeke them. This is φρονεῖν, to be wise: but in audacious curiositie to measure every foote in hell, and dispose every cabinet and amber in heaven; this is ὑπερφρονεῖν, to be wise beyond sobrietie. *Fidendo, non dendo apprehendimus.* 2 *Cor.* 5.7. *We walke by faith, not by sight.* <span class="ad-margin" markdown="1">Lam. 3.41.</span> Let us lift up our hearts with our hands unto God in the heavens. The Lord in all our holy services requires the heart: in his Temple, at his Table, *My Sonne give me thy heart, Psal* <span class="ad-gap" title="TCP: illegible in source">•</span>1. *Vnto thee ô Lord, doe I lift up my heart, Revel.* 12. There was a woman clothed with the Sun, and the *Moone under her feete, and upon her head a Crowne of twelve Stares.* The head of the Church is wrapp'd in the starres, and the world is under her feete. She forgets the land wherein she was borne, and the home-stall wherein she was bred, and seekes Ierusalem above, where Christ sitteth on the right hand of God. <span class="ad-margin" markdown="1">Psal. 62.10.</span> If riches encrease. *Nolite cor apponere, set not your heart on them:* they are heavy things, and will sinke you downewards. *Animus velut pondere, amore fertur, quocunque fertur, Aug.* If our love be to things downeward, our soules cannot rise to God upward. We never minister the blessed Sacrament, but we tell you of *<span class="ad-conj" title="Editorial conjecture: illegible in the 1633 source (lost in the gutter of the EEBO microfilm); supplied from the stated evidence (编者推补；非原书原文)">[Sursum]</span> corda, lift up your hearts:* you then answere us, *we lift them up:* but it is to be <span class="ad-pb" id="p516" data-p="516" data-facs="267"></span> feared, that many hearts are so heavy that they cannot be lift up. The Philosopher being asked which was the heaviest part of the earth, answered; that which beares an ignorant person. How little a piece of flesh so ever a wicked heart be, a talent of lead is light unto it.

The merry wanton that dissolutely lives, being asked how he scapes sicknesse, lives so long and so joviall; answers I have a light heart. But when this man <span class="ad-conj" title="Editorial conjecture: illegible in the 1633 source (lost in the gutter of the EEBO microfilm); supplied from the stated evidence (编者推补；非原书原文)">[comes]</span> to feele the waight of his sinnes, let him tell me then whether he be light hearted *Naba* could be drunke in his health; but when he is sicke, his heart lies and dies in him like a stone: nothing in the world can lift it up. The heart cannot raise it selfe, it is the Lord that <span class="ad-margin" markdown="1">Ioh. 6.44.</span> *drawes* it up. *Iohn* 12.32. *When I am lifted up from the earth, I will draw all men unto me.* The Apothecary hath no drugs so cordiall, the sycophant no jest so joviall, the Vintner no Wine so sprightly, the Musician no stroke so lusty; that it can lift up a sinfull heart. Downe, downe it sinkes without the animation of Gods holy Spirit.

But if CHRIST be our delight, our hearts are with him. *Dominus <span class="ad-conj" title="Editorial conjecture: illegible in the 1633 source (lost in the gutter of the EEBO microfilm); supplied from the stated evidence (编者推补；非原书原文)">[ascendit in]</span> coelum, ascendat cum illo cor nostrum, Aug.* His body doth not descend downe to us , we must ascend up to it. Indeed if in so large a quantitie it be presently reall in the Sacrament, as it was on the Crosse, in full dimensions; what need any man lift up his heart, to that he holds in his hand? No, he is above, contained in the heavens, till the time of restitution: and if he be our joy, thither we also aspire: *Vbi amor, <span class="ad-conj" title="Editorial conjecture: illegible in the 1633 source (lost in the gutter of the EEBO microfilm); supplied from the stated evidence (编者推补；非原书原文)">[ibi o-]</span> culus: ubi dolor, ibi digitus: ubi cor, ibi thesaurus.* The finger points to the griefe, the eye followes the pleasure, and the heart followes the treasure. God hath given us both a face to looke, and a faith to climbe upwards. *Si sit sursumos, quare deorsum cor?* <span class="ad-margin" markdown="1">Aug.</span> *Ascendamus interim corde, ut sequamur corpore.* Let us send up our hearts before, that our soules may follow after. How preposterous and mis-match'd is an erected countenance, and a groueling spirit.

Things nearest heaven take least care for earth: the foules of the aire neither plow, nor sow, nor carry into the barne. But men most love what they must leave and thinke seldome or never, of the place where they should be for ever. Some = too precise for publike prayers, without a Sermon: as if God were only to serve them, and they not bound to serve God. Are there not many that will bestow more upon a licence to eate flesh in Lent, than upon their soules all the yeare? and are their thoughts upward? the poorest piece of garment they weare; their has, their cuffes, their shooes, their shooties; cost them more than their foules: and are not their thoughts downewards? They will rather lose their inheritance in heaven, than let Christ have his inheritance on earth: and what, are their desires upwards <span class="ad-gap" title="TCP: illegible in source">〈◊〉</span> *Downe,* downewards they sinke, like the trash that God blesseth not: their minds buried in their coffers, as dead bodies are naild up in their coffins. And when they have dejected themselves so low as they can, then must this bottomlesse bottome receive them, and overwhelme them with everlasting pressures. A materiall <span class="ad-conj" title="Editorial conjecture: illegible in the 1633 source (lost in the gutter of the EEBO microfilm); supplied from the stated evidence (编者推补；非原书原文)">[mill-]</span> stone hung about their neckes, cannot sooner or surer carry them into the depth of the sea. For us, let our hearts be upward, that our soules may never sinke downe ward. Saint *Bernard* mentions foure degrees of ascending. *Primus ad cor, secundus in corde, tertius de corde, quartus supra cor.* The first ascent is of knowledge, the second of faith, the third of love, the last of glory. Let us know God, this is the first step to blessednesse: knowing, let us beleeve on him, that's the next: <span class="ad-conj" title="Editorial conjecture: illegible in the 1633 source (lost in the gutter of the EEBO microfilm); supplied from the stated evidence (编者推补；非原书原文)">[beleeving]</span>, let us loue him, that's the third: and loving, we shall live with him, that's the height and perfection of eternall joy.

*And delivered them into chaines of darkenesse, to be reserved unto Iudgement.* Here are two things; the measure of their present confusion, and the time of their future damnation. As a malefactor is first cast into a dungeon, at the Assiles brought forth to Iudgement and then led to execution. Now they are overwhelmed with the desertion of favour, then shall be confounded with imposition of plenary torture. To be chained in a blacke and confused vault, seemes an unsufferable plague to the <span class="ad-conj" title="Editorial conjecture: illegible in the 1633 source (lost in the gutter of the EEBO microfilm); supplied from the stated evidence (编者推补；非原书原文)">[delin-]</span> <span class="ad-pb" id="p517" data-p="517" data-facs="267"></span> quent: yet had he rather abide there still, than come forth to the light when he is sure to be punished with death. Therefore they <span class="ad-margin" markdown="1">Revel. 6.16.</span> cry to the mountaines and rockes, to fall on them, and cover them: the reprobates rather desire the loades of rockes, and pressure of mountaines, for concealement, than be summoned unto judgement. Their punishment is just; they <span class="ad-margin" markdown="1">Psal. 2.3.</span> *broke Gods bonds* before, now they shall have *chaines* = hold them. *Ruperunt vincula gratiae, damnantur in vincula poena.* Lucifers *Ero <span class="ad-conj" title="Editorial conjecture: illegible in the 1633 source (lost in the gutter of the EEBO microfilm); supplied from the stated evidence (编者推补；非原书原文)">[similis]</span> Altissimo,* hath made him *Inferiorem infimo.* The highest seate in heaven could not content him, the lowest bed in hell must containe him. Not pleased with the glorious light above, he is cast into the hideous darkenesse below.

*Tradidit in catenas caliginis.* Where we must suppose God sitting as a just Iudge on his throne, and having summoned the revolting Angels before him, doth here sentence them to present sufferings. Not but they shall also passe under another triall, at that day of universall retribution. When Christ shall sit on his Tribunall judging quicke and dead. But as a Iusticer finding a transgressor, makes his *Mittimus,* and sends him to the Gaole, there to lie in chaines till the Sessions. So we have here three answerable circumstances. The Mittimus, *He delivered them.* The Gaole, *Into chaines of darkenesse.* The Sessions. *To be reserved unto Iudgement.*

*He delivered them:]* but into whose hands? Indeed he delivers guilty mortals into the hands of guilty Angels. *Matth.* 18.34. *He delivered him to the tormentors:* that he might be their slave in suffering, whose subject he had beene in sinning, *Luk.* 2.20. This night *they shall fetch away thy soule:* they to whom I have given commission to doe it; divells. They shall require it, that did defile it. This was part of Saint *Pauls* excommunication. *Tradere Satanae, to deliver unto Satan:* <span class="ad-margin" markdown="1">1 Cor. 5.5. 1 Tim. 1.20.</span> who is the hangman. So he writes of *Himeneus* and *Alexander. Whom I have delivered unto Satan* that they may learne not to blaspheme. To Satan, that executioner of condemned soules. But *Cui Satanam?* to whom doth the Lord *deliver* Satan himselfe? Some answer, that themselves are the Instruments to torture themselves. *Invicem torquent <span class="ad-conj" title="Editorial conjecture: illegible in the 1633 source (lost in the gutter of the EEBO microfilm); supplied from the stated evidence (编者推补；非原书原文)">[et]</span> torquentur.* After a sort, every transgressor is his owne tormenter: and wickednesse is a vexation to it selfe. *Ambitio angit ambientes:* ambition rackes the aspiring, envie eates the marrow of his bones that envieth: the covetousnesse which would be most rich, keepes the affected with it most poore: ebrietie begets the head-ach, lust afflicts the body that nourisheth it: and we say of the Prodigall, he is no mans foe but his owne; therefore we grant that he is his owne foe. It is a foolish powder, = thinkes to blow up the house, and to scape it selfe from burning. If it were but so , that *Se sibi tradidit,* he delivered him over to himselfe: such is the power of Gods iustice, that without the least trouble to himselfe, he can make an offender his owne afflicter. How many impious wretches; after obstinate presumptions against God, have wrought desperate executions upon themselves.

How should this teach us to hate sin: we thinke our selves certainely our owne friends: No, by sinne we become our owne enemies. That which makes us at enmitie with God, will make us at feud with our selves. Though the Lords hand should not touch us, nor were any malicious divell to racke us, nor any other creature to scourge us; *Propriis lacerabimur armis,* we should thus punish our selves. If God speake the word, the hand shall rebell and strike the head, the nailes teare the skinne, the teeth gnaw the flesh, the feete precipitate the shoulders, the stomach famish the members. These that are made to take one anothers part, and to assist the whole in a peaceable communion, shall become mutinous like the Midianites, and sheath their swords in their fellowes bowels. It is a plague woful enough, when God shall deliver a man over to himselfe. <span class="ad-margin" markdown="1">1 Sam. 24.14.</span> *Let me not fall into the hand of man* was *Davids* desire: No, as I am man, not into mine own hand. There is not lesse mercie in all *Nero's* enemies, then in desperate *Nero's* owne heart to himselfe. But every man will doe good to himselfe? Yes, *Quamdiu suus;* so long as he is his owne man: but when he becomes Gods Instrument, *Timeat se,* let him feare himselfe. When the prophet had told *Hazael* the tyrannous massacres he should doe to Israel; he replies.

<span class="ad-pb" id="p518" data-p="518" data-facs="268"></span><span class="ad-margin" markdown="1">2 King. 8.13.</span> *Am I a dog that I should doe this?* No, he was not yet a dogge: but afterwards God forsooke him, then he became a dogge, and did it. *Libera me a malo homine;* that, is as *Augustine* glosseth it; *a meipso. Deliver me from the evill man; O Lord:* and because I am an evill man, and there is no worse, deliver me from my selfe. Such a deliverie should have beene to us all, but for another deliverance that came betweene: a *Liberavit,* not a *Tradidit. Luk.* 1.74. *He hath delivered us out of the hands of all our enemies. Tradidit daemones, Liberavit homines.* He delivered his owne Son to death, that he might deliver us from death.

*Into chaines of darkenesse.]* Into *darkenesse,* there's their Miserie. Into *chaines,* there's their Slaverie. *Darkenesse* signifies the wrath of God, and is opposed to that favour of his, which is called *Lux vultus sui; the light of his countenance, Psal.* 4.6. There is true light where the *Father of lights shineth:* and his absence causeth darkenesse. <span class="ad-margin" markdown="1">Rev. 21 23.</span> *That Citie hath no need of the Sunne or Moone to shine in it: for the glory of God doth lighten it, and the Lambe is the light of it.* Such a glorious light, that the very Sun is obscuritie to it. Created lights, which now so comfort us, and = some worship for Deities, shall then resigne their honours. *Mat.* 24.29. *The Sun shall be darkened, and the Moone shall not give her light.* Shall not then the Sun shine at that day? Yes, it is not darkened *Diminutione luminis, sed comparatione majoris luminis* not by losse of the owne light, but by the comparison of a greater light: as a torch is of small benefite, when the Sunne appeareth. Otherwise, <span class="ad-margin" markdown="1">Esa. 30.26.</span> the *Light of the Moone shall be as the light of the Sun, and the light of the Sun shall be seven-fold, as the light of seven dayes.* But then these lights shall be overshined; as the Moone that rejoyceth travellers in the night, gives place when the Sun riseth; and men doe not mind a Lord when the King appeareth. Therefore it is called *Lumen inaccessibile,* <span class="ad-margin" markdown="1">1 Tim. 6.16.</span> *a light which no man* (in his mortalitie and sin) *can approch unto.* In heaven there is all light and no darkenesse, in hell all darkenesse and no light. As the joy of the Saints and Angels in heaven, so the wretchednesse of the lost in hell, is so great that is cannot enlarged.

This is an unspeakable terror, to be coped up in everlasting night. If *<span class="ad-conj" title="Editorial conjecture: illegible in the 1633 source (lost in the gutter of the EEBO microfilm); supplied from the stated evidence (编者推补；非原书原文)">[David]</span>* calls the grave a tetricall place because of this darkenesse, where the Organ of seeing is not yet excercised. *Iob* 10.23. *A land of darkenesse and the shadow of death, where the light is as darkenesse:* How intolerable is the darkenesse of hell! But how agreeth this with other Scriptures, that allow the divells *vagari per orbem, & versari per <span class="ad-gap" title="TCP: illegible in source">〈◊〉</span> ,* to wander about the world, and to be conversant in the aire? 1 *King.* 22.22. *<span class="ad-conj" title="Editorial conjecture: illegible in the 1633 source (lost in the gutter of the EEBO microfilm); supplied from the stated evidence (编者推补；非原书原文)">[Iob* 1.7]</span>. *Luk.* 8.31. *Eph.* 2.2. *Rom.* 16.20. *Shortly,* therefore not yet troden downe. How then are they shut up under *darkenesse? Answ.* It was the divells censure to be <span class="ad-gap" title="TCP: illegible in source">〈◊〉</span> into hell; yet so that before the day of Iudgement, the wisedome of God hath disposed a permissive egresse into the world; and that for some of them: that as a great number of them are in hell, there tormenting the damned soules, so the rest wander in the world to tempt sinners. This is manifest, *Revel.* 9.3. Where the bottomlesse pit being opened, there *came out of the smoke innumerable locusts upon the* earth and the purpose of their comming is expressed, that they might hurt those that had *not the seale of God in their foreheads.* So *Revel.* 20.2. Satan is bound for a thousand yeares, at the expiration whereof it followes that he be loosed. Vntill the <span class="ad-conj" title="Editorial conjecture: illegible in the 1633 source (lost in the gutter of the EEBO microfilm); supplied from the stated evidence (编者推补；非原书原文)">[Iudgement]</span> day God doth lengthen his *chaines.*

Let us love the light, that darkenesse may never swallow us. All sinnes are therefore called the *workes of darkenesse;* not only because *the evill doer hates the light* but also because Satan the *Prince of darkenesse* is the founder, and shall be the <span class="ad-conj" title="Editorial conjecture: illegible in the 1633 source (lost in the gutter of the EEBO microfilm); supplied from the stated evidence (编者推补；非原书原文)">[con-]</span> founder of them. <span class="ad-margin" markdown="1">1 Thes. 5.7.</span> *They that sleepe, sleepe in the night: and they that be <span class="ad-conj" title="Editorial conjecture: illegible in the 1633 source (lost in the gutter of the EEBO microfilm); supplied from the stated evidence (编者推补；非原书原文)">[drunke, are]</span> drunke in the night.* This was wont to be the custome, sin durst not shew her ugly face by day. But now men are growne so impudent, that they make *opera <span class="ad-gap" title="TCP: illegible in source">〈◊〉</span> rum,* the workes of darkenesse, become *opera lucis,* the workes of light; committing them in the sun-shine. So <span class="ad-margin" markdown="1">2 Sam. 16.22.</span> *Absalom* had a Tent spred upon the top of the house and went in unto his fathers concubines, in the sight of all Israel, *Num.* 25.6. Zimri <span class="ad-pb" id="p519" data-p="519" data-facs="268"></span> brought a whore to his tent in the sight of all Israel, even when they were weeping before the Tabernacle. Vice was once like the Owle, only a night-bird: now proud of her borrowed feathers, she dares out-face vertue at noone day. These be the strange Epiphanies of the time: as one observed on *Matth.* 2.2. *We have seene his starre, and are come to worship him:* there were two blessed Epiphanies; a manifestation of CHRISTS starre to them, and a manifestation of their pietie to him. In stead of these, Pride struts in pompe, homicide stands on termes of Iustification, drunkennesse reeles up and downe the streets. <span class="ad-margin" markdown="1">Gal. 5.19.</span> *The workes of the flesh are manifest.* These be monstrous Epiphanies; yet still the workes of darkenesse, and precipitate into the place of darkenesse, to the enlargement of Satans kingdome. The Pope scatters his Emissaries abroad, to augment Idolaters, and augment his Supremacie: the Turke amplifies his territories; and other Princes expatiate their dominions: all these kingdomes are extended, but the kingdome of darkenesse surmounteth them all. For, though never was more light in mens braines, never more universall darknesse in their hearts.

The streame of wickednesse is so violent, that many (who had some inceptions of goodnesse) are even content to run with it, rather than swim against it, or especially reprove it. Vsurie and Sacriledge scorne to be reprehended, and he is taxed of indiscretion that meddles with them: whereupon some let all alone, resolving to sit downe and hold their peace. A Fryer that had beene for his boldnesse decourted, afterward admitted to preach to the King of Spaine, told this Fable. The Lion was faulted by the Lionesse, that his breath stanke. Being mad angry with this imputation he traverseth the forrest, to be more certainely informed. The first subject beast he met withall was an Asse, and breathing upon him, he demanded the rellish of his breath: the Asse plainely told him that it was very unsavory. Thou art too bitter, quoth the Lion, and tore him in pieces. Next he met with the hound, and = the same question to him: who answered, It is very sweet. Thou art a flatterer, quoth the Lion; and tore him in pieces. Last he lighted on the Foxe, and examining him concerning his breath: the subtile villaine replied; Indeed I cannot tell whether it be sweet or sowre, for I have caught such a cold that I cannot smell. If we should commend the times for devout and holy, you might justly condemne us for fawning flatterers. If we should say, they are starke naught, full of impietie and darknesse: then we are held too cynicall and censorious. What then? shall we answere We have caught a cold, and cannot smell or tell? No, we are bound to love our own soules, better than you can love your owne sinnes. In a word, let us receive the light of grace, that the light of glory may receive us.

*Into chaines.]* These cannot be understood litterally, for materiall chaines: but metaphorically, and so they are two; the Powerfulnesse of divine Iustice, and the guiltinesse of their owne conscience. The divels are bound, like mad men or bandogs, in the *chaines* of eternall damnation. Wheresoever they are permitted to wander, their owne guilty consciences are those *chaines* which bind them over unto Iudgement. Such are the horrors of that place, that the damned are bound to insufferable torments: they must endure what they cannot endure, without being able to remove a foote. These *chaines* shall so hamper them, that not one part of body, or facultie of soule, shall have the power of activitie to gratifie their owner withall. The mind is bound to cotemplate nothing but endles infelicity, the memory bound to recount nothing but fearefull sinnes, the fantasie bound to present nothing but horrid visions, the eyes bound to see nothing but offensive objects, the eares bound to heare nothing but howlings and roarings, the nostrills to smell nothing but the stench of brimstone, the hands to catch hold of nothing but flames, and the feete to = no further than these *chaines* will give them leave. *Delivered them into chaines of darkenesse:* the collections, and inferences here observable are diverse.

1 Conclusion, that there is certainly a God, for how else should Satan be bound? Hee is that *Strong man,* and therefore there must bee a *stronger than hee to bind him.* <span class="ad-pb" id="p520" data-p="520" data-facs="269"></span> If there be a destroying power, without question there is a preserving power, superior to it, and correcting it. For if the divells were not curbed, they would confound us all in a moment. It is not more naturall for fire to burne; nor for heavines to finde downeward, than for Satan to destroy. He is a King over all the children of pride, <span class="ad-margin" markdown="1">Iob. 41.33.</span> *Vpon earth none can match him:* but there is one in heaven that *chaines* him. If there be a roaring Lion that would devoure us, certainely there is a blessed power that preserve us.

Let this teach us to get so close as we can to God, that Satan may not reach us. The chickens be safe under the wings of their mother, and we under the providence of our Father. So long as we hold the tenor of obedience, we are the Lords subjects: and if we serve him, he will preserve us. But when a man is fallen to the state of an out-Law or rebell, the Law dispenseth with them that kill him, because the Prince hath excluded him from the benefite of his protection. All the feare of Satan ariseth from the want of the due feare of God. The more a man feares God, the lesse he feare every thing else. *Feare God, Honour the King:* he that feares God, doth but honour the King, he need not feare him. It would affright a weake Christian, to consider the presence and number, malice and power, of wicked spirits. But when with the *Prophets servant,* he sees those good Angels on his side, as present, as diligent, more able to helpe than the other to hurt, he takes heart againe. He knowes that God (most good) bounds the temptation of the one, and directs the protection of the other. Though there be many legions of divells, and every one stronger than many legions of men, and more malicious than strong; yet Christs little flocke lives and prospers. <span class="ad-margin" markdown="1">Mal. 3.6.</span> *I am the Lord, I change not: therefore yee sonnes of Iacob are not consumed.* The divell would doe it, and doth attempt it; but Gods unchangeable mercie prevents it. That we here meet, pray, worship, is against the divells will; onely our gracious GOD maintaines it. That every moment we perish not in the jawes of that Lion, let our hearts acknowledge, and our tongues praise the Lord our maker.

2 Conclusion; that Satan can doe nothing but by Gods permission: he is bound in a teddar, and cannot goe one inch beyond his *chaine.* Christ tells *Peter,* that *Satan had desired to winnow them:* <span class="ad-margin" markdown="1">Luk. 11.22.</span> *Desired,* he must begge an ill turne before he can doe it. Whatsoever he doth, is *limitata potestate,* and by dispensation from God He could not seduce a Prophet, nor take one poore sheepe from *Iob,* nor enter a hog without licence. It is an Ethnical error of our times, in strange accidents to give the honour of God to Sorcerers and conjurers. If a tempest arise beyond common experience, presently as if the God of heaven were fallen fast asleepe, and minde nothing; the judgment is given, there is some conjuring: there must needs be a pestilent convention and stipulation betwixt men and devills. As if God were not able to raise as great a storme, as the Devill. Looke upon the witches of Egypt: *<span class="ad-gap" title="TCP: illegible in source">〈◊〉</span> rum potestas defecit in muscis, Aug.* Their cunning failed in the most contemptible creatures; and they are forced to cry, *This is the finger of God.* Though the circuite of Satan be very large, even to compassing of the whole earth; yet he hath his dayes assigned to stand before the Lord for the renewing of his commission, and there is a *chaine* tyed to his power that he cannot move beyond his allowance. Yet hath hee a little liberty to tempt; for the probation of some, for the reprobation of others, in all for the glory of God. He is the basest of all creatures, a slave, a scullion: now how is that person shamed, that is given up to a base slave to be corrected!

So little he feares to tempt us, that he ventured upon Christ himselfe, *Mat.* 4. As we read *Rev.* 12.7. *There was a great battell in heaven,* so here was a monomachie or single combate on earth. It was a dainty sight to behold little *=* grappling with great *Goliah,* and great *Goliah* groueling under little *David.* A Lambe matched with the Wolfe, and the wolfe overmatched by the Lambe. First, the Devill tempts him to diffidence. Art thou hungry? *Turne these stones into bread:* not into quailes, feasants, dainties; but into *Bread,* without which man could not <span class="ad-pb" id="p521" data-p="521" data-facs="269"></span> live. Then to presumption, *ver.* 6. *If thou be the Sunne of God, cast thy selfe downe.* That he might get credite to his ministerie, he would have him shew the people some strange device. Lastly, to apostacie, *ver.* 8. which was the sinne that turned himselfe out of heaven: wherein first he propounds *Promissum, All these will I give thee:* and indents *Pactum, If falling downe thou wilt worship me.* He is like an old bitten curre, that being fleshed to the game, will not be stay'd off: hells bandog, fed with the livers of Gods cast-awaies. He tries all courses, like *Balaam,* or some superstitious gamester on the losing hand; shifts places; still in hope to winne. *Hee tooke him up into a mountaine: Cyprian* sayes, he went on foote with him: for Christ would not use him *pro vehiculo, quem novit praecipitatorem.* This opinion is not against the Text, nor the Text against it. For παραλαμβάνει doth not imply portage: no more than, *Matt.* 17.1. *Christ tooke them:* it were grosse to thinke that he carried them on his backe. But that he carried him is the most received opinion, because it is said that he set him on the pinacle. This was no disparagement to Christ: no more than to suffer apprehension, ligation, crucifixion of his enemies.

He reserves the old malice to all Christs members: why should any serve him? there is no goodnesse in him. He is the greatest sinner of all: for *Quicquid efficit tale, ipsum magis est tale.* Wicked Pharisies may make their Proselytes *two-fold more the children of hell than themselves:* but the malice of the Devill cannot be matched. He never gives man any thing, but as *Michol* was given to *David,* to ensnare him. <span class="ad-margin" markdown="1">1 Sam. 18.</span> S*t* *Peter* calles him *Contra litigator,* an *adversary at Law:* <span class="ad-margin" markdown="1">1 Pet. 5.</span> he wrangles with God against us. *Augustine* brings him in thus pleading. They were thine by creation, they are mine by prevarication: they were thine *per gratiam,* they are mine *per culpam:* They were thine by redemption, they are mine by defection. They left *tua <span class="ad-conj" title="Editorial conjecture: illegible in the 1633 source (lost in the gutter of the EEBO microfilm); supplied from the stated evidence (编者推补；非原书原文)">[Sacramenta]</span>,* thy Sacraments: and accepted *mea Blandimenta,* my allurements. He pleads = things against us, but we have one argument to confute him; our Faith, 1. *Pet.* 5.9. *Whom resist stedfast in the faith.* And all our defects are supplied by an Advocate in heaven, Iesus Christ the righteous. I know that God casteth his sometimes into the sieve for tryall, but the Lord *Iesus* strengthens them.

Satan is called a Lyon, and that fitly; for he hath all the properties of a Lyon: as bold as a Lyon, as strong as a Lyon, as furious as a Lyon, as terrible as the roaring of a Lyon. Yea worse: the Lyon wants subtiltie and suspition, herein the Devill is beyond the Lyon. The Lyon will spare the prostrate, the Devill spares none. The Lyon is full and forbeares, the Devill is full and devoures. *He seekes all:* let not he simple say, he will take no notice of me: nor the subtle, he cannot overreach me: nor the noble say, he will not presume to meddle with me: nor the rich, he dares not contest with me: for he seekes to devoure all. He is our common adversary, therefore let us cease all quarrels amongst our selves, and fight with him.

Seeing the Devill is bound with *chaines,* and cannot range further than his bonds allow him, let us not come within his reach. The bandog is tyed up that he may not hurt the passenger: but how if the passenger will come within his compasse? <span class="ad-margin" markdown="1">Eph. 4.24.</span> *Give no place to the Devill:* for the Devill hath no place unlesse we give it him. <span class="ad-margin" markdown="1">Iam. 4.7.</span> *Resist the Devill, and hee will flee from you.* He cannot come in, except we open him the doore: now who would open the doore to let in his enemie? yet many doe: by wearing, they open the doore to let him in at their mouth: by lustfull lookes, they open the doore to let him in at the eye. Pride admits him into his wardrobes, covetousnesse into our purses, adultery into our beds, Schisme into our Studies, drunkennesse into our stomachs, Idolatrie into our devotions, hypocrisie into our hearts. As if his *chaine* were not long enough, wicked men put themselves in his way. Thinke when thou art about to commit a voluntary sinne; now I am running within the Devils *chaine.* I durst not so venture within the chaine of a Lyon, beare or other savage beast, which can but teare my flesh. Hath God tyed him up from me, and shall I runne unto him? shall I trust his mercie, that is nothing else but <span class="ad-conj" title="Editorial conjecture: illegible in the 1633 source (lost in the gutter of the EEBO microfilm); supplied from the stated evidence (编者推补；非原书原文)">[malicious]</span> crueltie. O but the hand of God holds his *Chaine:* but say the hand of God <span class="ad-pb" id="p523" data-p="523" data-facs="270"></span> let goe his *chaine,* for thy presumption? what remaines then but ruine? As we hate the Devill, let us hate those workes that lengthen his *chaine.* Doe we pray to be delivered from the gates of hell, and yet frequent the gates of hell? we reade of a beast that being too unwildly to hunt for his prey, stands still and enticeth the rest unto him with his glorious spots and colours, and so devoureth them. But Satan is quicke and nimble enough to pursue men, they need not wilfully runne into their owne ruine. <span class="ad-margin" markdown="1">Psal. 55.6.</span> *O that I had wings like a dove, then would I flee away and be at rest.* Let us flie from him so fast as we = and so farre as he may never overtake us. Which is done by turning to God with faithfull repentance, and devout obedience: so shall his *chaines* be shortned: our soules delivered, our Creator glorified, and our selves everlastingly saved, through the merits of Iesus Christ. *Amen.*

3\. Observe, that Satan is punished everlastingly, without all hope of recovery: bound with *chaines,* and as S*t* *Iude* calls them, *Everlasting chaines.* There was no deliverance ever ordained for the Devils: for <span class="ad-margin" markdown="1">Hebr. 2.16.</span> *Christ tooke not the nature of Angels:* he tooke not their narure, therefore was not their Saviour. Now there are diverse reasons, why Christ should seeke lost *Adam,* rather than the lost Angels.

1\. The Angell sinned without instigation; *Non ab alio tentatus, nec ab alio servatus.* As there was none to tempt him, so there is none to save him, *Ioh.* 8.44. *When he speaketh a lie, he speaketh of his owne. A se peccatum rapuit, nullo alio suggerente:* he tooke sinne of himselfe, no other suggested it to him. He fell alone, nothing cast him downe: he must rise alone, there is nothing to helpe him up. The winde blowes out the Torch, we light it againe: bat if the winde blow out it selfe and cease moving, who shall raise it? If Satan hurt man, Christ heales him: but if Satan hurt himselfe, let him heale himselfe.

2\. The Devill was the party seducing, (man onely seduced) and still endevours what he can to destroy all: therefore none stands up to preserve him. Because his *hand is against all, therefore all hands are against him.* Being throwne out from the presence of God, in spight he wounded his Image, that he might doe him all the mischiefe he could: therefore he perisheth without redemption.

3\. The Angels were more excellent and glorious natures by creation, and nearer to God than men: more subtle, more powerfull: their dwelling in the highest heaven. Whereas one halfe of man was but refined dust, and his mansion the earth more remote from the glorious presence of God. *Angelus ergo quantò <span class="ad-conj" title="Editorial conjecture: illegible in the 1633 source (lost in the gutter of the EEBO microfilm); supplied from the stated evidence (编者推补；非原书原文)">[sublimior in]</span> gloria, tantò major in ruina, Aug.* The higher the Angell was in glory, the deeper in misery. *Homo autem quantò fragilior per naturam, tantò facilior ad veniam.* But man, the more fraile he was by constitution, the more easie he is to redemption. Therefore God tooke pity on man, who was but dust: and pitied not the divells, because they had once beene Angels.

4\. The whole humane nature fell with *Adam.* 1 *Cor.* 15.22. *In Adam all die.* All mankind was lost, and unlesse the humane nature had beene repaired, man had been wholly frustrated of his end. But all the Angels did not fall with *Lucifer,* but only some: and so none were partakers of his punishment, but such as had beene partakers of his sin. Innumerable multitudes of Angels stood in heaven, as well as a groat company sunke to hell. *Dan.* 7.10. *Thousand thousands stand before him, and ten thousand times ten thousand minister unto him.* Some of that nature stood by conservation without redemption: for redemption presupposeth losse. But if our nature had not beene redeemed, not one man could have beene saved.

5\. Man was distinguished into sexes; male and female: because they were to generate their like: as *Adam begate a sonne in his owne likenesse.* But Angels have no sexes; as CHRIST confuted the Sadduces. *Matth.* 22.30. *In heaven they neither marry, nor are given in marriage, but are as the Angels of God.* They cannot beget a generation of spirits. Every divell sinned in himselfe, and is punished in himselfe only. But *Adam* having sinned, and being to multiply his kind, must needs convey his sin to his seed. Therefore was the Lord Iesus made of his seed, that the guiltynesse which *Adam* to all his seed had propagated, by one of his seed = be expiated.

6\. Satan immediately upon his fall was cast into hell. *Ioh.* 8.44. *He abode not in the truth.* But so was not *Adam;* for howsoever he was cast out of Paradise, yet not out of the world, but had space and grace given him to repent. And albeit that menace, *In the day thou eatest thereof thou shalt surely die, Gen.* 2.17. Yet God spared him 900 yeares: indeed presently he became mortall, and fell into a consumption; as the originall speach is, *dying thou shalt dye.* And for the second death, the *Seed of the woman* excused him; he died not that death at all. Indeed *<span class="ad-conj" title="Editorial conjecture: illegible in the 1633 source (lost in the gutter of the EEBO microfilm); supplied from the stated evidence (编者推补；非原书原文)">[Augustine* mentions]</span> the *Tatian* heresie, which held that *Adam* was damned. But *Wis.* 10.1. *She preserved the first formed father of the world that was created alone, and brought him out of his fall.* Which is agreeable to the Scripture, *Luk* 3.38. Which saith that *Adam was the sonne of God:* therefore he was not the child of death and hell. God relieved him with a promised *Messiah,* a newes that never came to the apostate Angels.

7\. If the whole humane nature had perished, to what purpose had beene this world? The world was made for man, not for Angels: either heaven or hell was ordained for them, this middle walke for man. Now why should either the Sunne shine, or the earth fructifie for man, if he were not redeemed? Spirits have no use of these things, man hath the benefite: and man should not have the benefite of any creature, but for Gods favour in CHRIST. For he did forfeit his patent, and none but a Saviour could renew it. But for the Elects sake, the raine should not fall, nor the earth stand. Therefore if man had perished, all this world had beene in vaine created. Man is the summe and abridgement of all creatures, and containes in him more generalitie than the Angels. Stones have being, but not life: plants = being and life; but not sense: beasts have being, life, sense; but not understanding: Angels have being, life, sense, and understanding. Now man participates with all these; a being with stones, a life with plants, a sense with beasts, an understanding with Angels. He is the compendious Index of Gods great booke in *Folio. Mark.* 16.15. *Preach the Gospell to every creature:* no creature hath part in the Gospell; but only man is called *every creature,* as having in him the chiefe perfections of every creature. Some hold, that man beares the Image and superscription of God more fully than the Angels: and hath something more; an organicall body united to his spirit, which the Angels have not. He is the common end why is world was made: therefore, *Esa.* 9.6. *To us a child is borne, and to us a sonne is given.* To *Vs,* not to Angels. *Luk.* 2.14. *To us is borne a Saviour;* To *Vs,* not to the lost Angels. There is enmitie put betweene the *Seed of the woman, and the seed of the Serpent, Gen.* 3.15. Therefore the seed which saves man, shall be at enmity with the divell.

8\. Lastly, the principall reason of all is the free mercie, and gracious decree of God. Who made both men and Angels good in creation, and finding both men and Angels, lost in transgression, vouchsafed to men not to Angels a Redemption. What did we deserve at his hands, that he should pitie us dust and ashes, passing by those celestiall spirits? *Psalm.* 8.4. *Quis homo, what is man that thou art mindfull of him ?* For the wonder had beene lesse to say, *Quis Angelus,* what is the Angell that thou art mindfull of him? That we should find him a Saviour, whom they find a just revenger: that we should be loosed from the chaines of our sinnes, and they delivered into chaines of plagues: that the same CHRIST should with his owne bloud free us, that shall with his word sentence them: that the same Almighty hand should lift us up to heaven, that casteth them downe to hell! Oh the riches of that mercie, which even to tast will keep a man from ever being poore! Of all mixt creatures men are the best, for they have reason: of all men, Christians are the best, for they have religion: of all Christians, holy beleevers are the best, for they have <span class="ad-conj" title="Editorial conjecture: illegible in the 1633 source (lost in the gutter of the EEBO microfilm); supplied from the stated evidence (编者推补；非原书原文)">[salvation]</span>. In the sorest troubles, men have some hope, Christians have good hope, beleevers <span class="ad-pb" id="p524-2" data-p="524" data-facs="271"></span> have sure hope. Let us blesse God for making us men, but most of all, for making us Christian men: for in that he gives us his Son, he gives us himselfe. He gave the water to fishes, the earth to beasts, the aire to fowles, the heaven to Angels; <span class="ad-margin" markdown="1">*Heb.* 6.13.</span> but he gave himselfe to man, *Having no greater to sweare by, he sware by himselfe:* so, having no greater to give, he gave himselfe. *Whom have I in heaven but thee?* saith that royall Prophet. The Romists in 88. cried out, whether maliciously or blasphemously; God shewes himselfe a Lutheran, and the God of Lutherans: but indeed he shewes himselfe a Christian, and the God of Christians. By how much we finde more mercy than all creatures, let us bee more thankefull than all creatures. It is an harmonious sweetnes, to have Gods bounty, and our gratitude meeting in that middle way, the hand of Iesus Christ. Without whom neither could we receive his goodnesse, nor would he accept our goodnesse.

4 Observe that the punishments of hell are eternall: these *chaines* can never be broken: were they of cords, of wreathed trees, of iron, they might bee burst asunder, but the chaines of vengeance never. *Binde him hand and foote, and cast = into utter darknesse.* <span class="ad-margin" markdown="1">*Mat.* 22.13.</span> *i* Now if a man were bound hand and foote, and throwen into a Well five thousand fadome deepe, what hope could he have of comming forth? But how doth this stand with Gods justice, to punish temporall offences with eternall scourges? It was the rule of his owne law, that *Poena non debet excedere culpam, Deut.* 25.3. *Ans.* There is a double quantity considered in punishment: the one according to the intention of paine; the other according to the duration of time. In respect of the former, the quantity of punishment, must be answerable to the quantity of sinne, *Rev.* 18.7. How much sinne, so much sorrow: the more pestilent iniquity, the more torturing fire. For the other, we must not thinke that the continuance of punishment is limited with the continuance of the fact. Among men, adultery is but a short pleasure, yet often persued with a long pennance. But the duration of torment respects the disposition of the delinquent. *Poenae singulorum <span class="ad-conj" title="Editorial conjecture: illegible in the 1633 source (lost in the gutter of the EEBO microfilm); supplied from the stated evidence (编者推补；非原书原文)">[inaequales]</span> intensione, poenae omnium aequales duratione, Aquin.* The paines of all are equall in continuance, unequall in grievance.

But a good Iudge will make his penalties, medicines and corrections, rather than destructions. *Ans.* So doth the Lord in all corrigible offenders: but those he cannot mend by chastising, his Iustice must satisfie it selfe by confounding, But God delights not in the death of a sinner. *EZek.* 18, *What profit is there in my blood when I goe downe to the pit? Psal.* The Lord hath no use, of their eternall damnation. *Ans.* Yes, as mercy hath had her place and day; so must justice have hers. Whom mercy saves, shee saves for ever: though their workes were short, and *u* *nothing unto God,* yea, the very effects of his owne grace. Therfore, whom justice condemnes, she condemnes for ever: not respecting so much the persons that have sinned, as the person against whom they have sinned, *Greg.* Almighty God as hee is good, is not delighted with their torments: but as he is just, he is not satisfied without their torments. *Factus est malo dignus aeterno, qui hoc in se peremit bonum, quod esse posset aeternum, Aug.* He is justly plagued with an evill, that is eternall, who hath corrupted in himselfe a good that might have beene eternall.

But if Gods justice must be satisfied upon those sinners, for whom Christ satisfied not; why is not this rather in reducing them to nothing? Seeing the unthankfull deserve to be deprived of all benefits; now one especiall benefit is *Being;* therefore *ipsum esse amittant,* let them not be. *Ans.* It is true, the creature that disobeyes the Creator, deserves to lose his *Being:* but because it was given him to this purpose that he should serve him, therefore it shall never be taken away. For God will have his homage and service out of that *Being;* whether of grace and salvation to the praise of his mercy, or of punishment and confusion to the praise of his Iustice.

But one would think, that the mercy of God should terminate their sorrowes. *Wisd.* 11.23. *Thou hast mercy upon all,* and *Thou lovest all the things that are. <span class="ad-conj" title="Editorial conjecture: illegible in the 1633 source (lost in the gutter of the EEBO microfilm); supplied from the stated evidence (编者推补；非原书原文)">[Rom]</span> .* 11.32. *God hath concluded all under unbeleefe, that he might have mercy upon all. Sed <span class="ad-gap" title="TCP: illegible in source">•••</span>usit* <span class="ad-pb" id="p525" data-p="525" data-facs="271"></span> *& daemones:* hee hath also concluded the divels under sin. Neither will his = suffer that, which he made for blessednesse, to perish for ever in torment. These be the plausible conceits that over-mercifull *Origen,* hath brought for the recovery of lost spirits And whereas Christs doome is; *Depart yee cursed into everlasting fire, prepared for the divels:* he would have these words rather *Minaciter quàm <span class="ad-conj" title="Editorial conjecture: illegible in the 1633 source (lost in the gutter of the EEBO microfilm); supplied from the stated evidence (编者推补；非原书原文)">[veraciter]</span> dicta,* spoken by way of threatning, than by way of truth. But the <span class="ad-conj" title="Editorial conjecture: illegible in the 1633 source (lost in the gutter of the EEBO microfilm); supplied from the stated evidence (编者推补；非原书原文)">[Scripture]</span> delivers it *planè ac plenè. Rev.* 20.10. *The divell shall be tormented in the lake of fire day and night, for ever.* Besides, his opinion doth both *Extendere & extenuare <span class="ad-conj" title="Editorial conjecture: illegible in the 1633 source (lost in the gutter of the EEBO microfilm); supplied from the stated evidence (编者推补；非原书原文)">[misericordiam]</span>:* as it straineth, so it restraineth mercy. It extends it to the future deliverance of the damned, so it extenuates it in regard of the blessed. For if the lost be ever to be taken out of hell; then will it follow that the Saints also are one day to be shut out of heaven. And so what the bad should gaine, the good should lose: yea, the very mercy of *God* cannot get more glory by the one, than it shall lose by the other.

But though the divels be everlastingly *chained,* is there no mercy for reprobate men? shall they never get loose? *Gen.* 6.3. *My spirit shall not alwayes strive with man:* therefore his indignation shall cease. Doth he not often threaten, and not doe as to *Ninive? Ans.* God doth sometimes menace and not strike, because our repentance steps betweene: but when everlasting burning hath wasted all the moisture repenting, will hee doe so then? Here indeed we may speed as well as *Ninive: <span class="ad-conj" title="Editorial conjecture: illegible in the 1633 source (lost in the gutter of the EEBO microfilm); supplied from the stated evidence (编者推补；非原书原文)">[Eversa]</span> est quae mala erat, aedificata est bona quae non erat, Aug.* Wee shall stand if our sines fall: but we shall fall, if our sins stand. But at that day the date of <span class="ad-conj" title="Editorial conjecture: illegible in the 1633 source (lost in the gutter of the EEBO microfilm); supplied from the stated evidence (编者推补；非原书原文)">[repentance]</span> will be out. But such is the charity of the Saints in this life, that they pray for their enemies: now this charity shall be more perfect in heaven, therefore they shall interceed for them in hell: and God hath promised that their prayers shall bee heard. *Ans.* Here they pray for them that they may be converted: for if they know at such were (in Gods decree) reprobates, they would pray for them no more than they doe for divels. Their present suite is *x* *that they may be recovered out of the snare of the divell.* Now they may be recovered, not hereafter: there may be present conversion, no future permutation. For that objected out of the *Psal.* 77. *Will the Lord cast off for ever; will he be favorable no more?* There i. meant onely the temporary affliction of the Church. Still as the joyes of heaven, so the paines of hell, are eternall. Death is to men, as the fall was to Angels: as lost Angels after their apostacy, so lost men after their death, can never be recovered. Hell is made *y* *Deepe;* so deepe, that there is no hope of crawling out. *Ex inferno nulla redemptio.* Therefore it is called *Infernus, ab inferendo,* of casting in: for the wicked are so cast in, that they = never get forth. From earthly Gaoles and dungeons there may be some tricke escape: but hell is so deepe, that nor earth nor heaven can helpe out one poore soule. That rich man, *Luk.* 16. sollicited for his brethren: why did hee not beg his owne deliverance, who was able to have taught them by his owne experience? O saw *Ingentem hiatum,* a vast interposed *Gulfe:* he must let that alone for ever.

*One deepe calleth another:* the depth of hell calls for our answerable humiliation. He that will not be humbled for his sinnes here, must be tumbled into that depth hereafter. *Psal.* 130. *De profundis clamavi Domine; out of the depths have I tryed unto thee O Lord.* God will heare the voice that comes *de profundis.* The deeper we have beene in the Law, the higher we are in the Gospell: the deeper in hell, the higher in heaven. The deeper a bucket dives into the well, the more water it brings the lower a man is humbled with sorrow for sinne, the higher he shall be exalted with the grace of salvation. Never came prayer, sigh or groane *de profundis <span class="ad-conj" title="Editorial conjecture: illegible in the 1633 source (lost in the gutter of the EEBO microfilm); supplied from the stated evidence (编者推补；非原书原文)">[paenitentiae]</span>* from the depth of repentance; but it was heard *in Excelsis misericordiae,* in the height of mercies. Of *Davids* propheticall imprecation against his enemies, (*Psal.* 15. *Let them goe downe quick into hell*) we may make a good apprecation for our <span class="ad-conj" title="Editorial conjecture: illegible in the 1633 source (lost in the gutter of the EEBO microfilm); supplied from the stated evidence (编者推补；非原书原文)">[selves]</span>. Let us goe downe quicke into hell by meditation, that we be never sent quicke thither by condemnation. Let us descend every day while we live, that we never come there when we are dead.

<span class="ad-pb" id="p526" data-p="526" data-facs="272"></span>5 Observe, that God punisheth sinne wheresoever he finds it, though it be in the very Angels. For all the men and Angels in the world are not so deare to him, as his owne honour: and what dishonors him but sinne? For this cause 1 He made a Law against it, *Gal.* 3.19. *The Law was added because of transgression. Non <span class="ad-conj" title="Editorial conjecture: illegible in the 1633 source (lost in the gutter of the EEBO microfilm); supplied from the stated evidence (编者推补；非原书原文)">[scripsisset]</span> mandatum, nisi abhorruiset peccatum.* He could not have written the Law <span class="ad-conj" title="Editorial conjecture: illegible in the 1633 source (lost in the gutter of the EEBO microfilm); supplied from the stated evidence (编者推补；非原书原文)">[with]</span> his owne finger, if he had not so abhorred sinne, 2 Gracious are the promises he hath made to obedience: grievous the plagues he hath to threaten disobedience, 3. His owne hands have smitten it, the whole world is a bleeding witnesse thereof: and man may say, *Quorum pars magna fui:* the whole creature groaneth in expectance of his pacification. He hath drowned the world in a flood of waters, and he shall burne it in a flood of fire, because of sinne. The sentence shall stand unchangeable, so long as heaven and earth endureth; <span class="ad-margin" markdown="1">Rom. 2.9.</span> *Tribulation and anguish upon every soule of man that doeth evill:* Be he *Iew* or *Gentile,* learned or simple, Poore or Peere; yea man or Angell. 4 So doth he hate sinne, that he spared not his owne Sunne, when he appeared in the similitude of sinfull flesh. If the Iustice of God could ever have swallowed sinne, or dismiss'd it with impunitie, he would have forborne it in his owne bowels. Yea, such a Sonne as never knew the least thought of disobedience the Sonne of his love, the Sonne of his joy, the Sonne of his light, the Sonne of = delight: a Sonne fully as good and as great as his Father. Yea because he stood in the place, and bore the person of sinfull man, he plagued him as the most deadly enemy that ever he had. *Vt Peccatum occideret, Filium crucifixit.* That he might slay sinne, he slew his Sonne.

How should this make us all hate sinne! He doth hate, not love God; that loves what God hates. Let us be content to meete our afflictions, as *Peter* and *Andrew* met their Crosses, as their dearest friends; embracing them in our armes, and saluting them with the kisses of peace. Or as the Martyrs welcomed their deathes, running to the stakes as if they had runne for a garland. But for sinnes, were they as deare to us as the sight of our eyes, the children of our bodies, the spouses of our bosomes because they are traitors to our Father and Maker; let us deale with them as *Abraham* did with *Hagar* and *Ishmael,* put them out of our house for ever.

6 Observe, that great offenders meete with great punishments: and according to the condition of their place, is the nature and proportion of their fault. The more glorious the Angels excellencie, the more damnable their apostacie. *If the light become darknesse, how great is that darknesse!* The more notable the Person, the more notorious the corruption. The freshest summers day doth soonest taint the <span class="ad-conj" title="Editorial conjecture: illegible in the 1633 source (lost in the gutter of the EEBO microfilm); supplied from the stated evidence (编者推补；非原书原文)">[loath-]</span> some carcasse: and fester'd lillies smell farre worse than weeds. If vertue turne into vice, the shame is trebble. For many Iewes to deny Christ, was not so much as for one *Peter:* the adulteries of many Israelites lesse infamous than one *Davids.* If all the Cities of the world had done filthily, it were short of this wonder. *The virgin daughter of Sion is become an Harlot.* If *Iudas* become a traitor, how great is treason! If *Achitophel* prove a villaine, how mischievous is his villanie! If *Absalom* rebell, how unnaturall is his rebellion! The least mote that flies in the Sunne, or betweene our eyes and the light, seemes a greater substance than it is. Deepe are the blowes made by a mighty axe. Sinne in a Magistrate is not onely sinne, but sub<span class="ad-gap" title="TCP: illegible in source">•••</span>tion. *Q<span class="ad-gap" title="TCP: illegible in source">••</span> in vulgaribus nugae, in magnatibus blasphemiae.*

There is no dispensation for sinne, no protection from Iudgement. Not the rich mans opulencie, not *Belshazzars* monarchie, not *Adams* perfection, not the Angels glory; could *abscondere culpam, vel abscindere poenam,* countenance sinne, <span class="ad-conj" title="Editorial conjecture: illegible in the 1633 source (lost in the gutter of the EEBO microfilm); supplied from the stated evidence (编者推补；非原书原文)">[nor ward]</span> punishment. No place, no robes, no riches, no excellencie, can give it <span class="ad-conj" title="Editorial conjecture: illegible in the 1633 source (lost in the gutter of the EEBO microfilm); supplied from the stated evidence (编者推补；非原书原文)">[priviledge]</span>. Cloath an ape in tissue, and the beauty of the robe addes but more scorne to the beast. The richer colours, or bolder countenance is set on wickednesse, the more ugly it appeares. Therefore as they that govern well in high places, shall shine with a higher degree of glory in heaven: because they being instrusted with the treasures of God, enrich his Church. So they that are in good offices evill men, for the mischiefe of both their actions and examples, shall be cast deeper into hell. *Potentes <span class="ad-conj" title="Editorial conjecture: illegible in the 1633 source (lost in the gutter of the EEBO microfilm); supplied from the stated evidence (编者推补；非原书原文)">[Potenter]</span> punientur. Wisd.* 6.5. *A sharpe judgement shall be to them that are in high places.* Mercy may soone pardon the meanest, *but mighty men shall bee mightily tormented. <span class="ad-conj" title="Editorial conjecture: illegible in the 1633 source (lost in the gutter of the EEBO microfilm); supplied from the stated evidence (编者推补；非原书原文)">[Esa]</span>.* 30.33. *Tophet is ordained of old, even for the King it is prepared.* Kings are not exempted from judgements; *Pessimus in Imperio, maximus in Inferno.* What made = damned churle move for his brethren, but that every step they followed of his leading, he felt encreasing the pile of his torments. 1 *Sam.* 12.25. *If yee doe wickedly ye shall perish and your King.* For the Lord freeth none according to place, but according to grace; not for outward condition, but for his owne free favour.

Nor yet let the poore and ignoble clap their wings, as if they were the onely men that *God* loves. <span class="ad-margin" markdown="1">1 Cor. 1.26.</span> *Not many rich, not many wise, not many noble, are called. Non <span class="ad-conj" title="Editorial conjecture: illegible in the 1633 source (lost in the gutter of the EEBO microfilm); supplied from the stated evidence (编者推补；非原书原文)">[multi sed]</span> non nulli:* not many, but some; and not many *after the flesh;* but many wise, rich, noble, after the spirit. The gate of heaven is narrow, and but few enter of any <span class="ad-conj" title="Editorial conjecture: illegible in the 1633 source (lost in the gutter of the EEBO microfilm); supplied from the stated evidence (编者推补；非原书原文)">[condition]</span>: yet certainly the noble sooner than the rabble: more wise men are admitted than fooles: for morality is the first step to christianity. And at the last dreadfull day, it is the <span class="ad-margin" markdown="1">Rev. 6.15.</span> *Bond-man* so well as the *Great man,* that cals upon the rockes to cover him . *But doe any of the Rulers beleeve on him? Ioh.* 7.48. Yes, Christ had his Church even in *Caesars* family. *Acts* 17.11. They were the *Noble men,* and *Honourable women* at *Berea,* which received the word: it was the people that persecuted it. *Acts.* <span class="ad-gap" title="TCP: illegible in source">〈◊〉</span> . 14. There was one *Lydia a seller of purple* converted: God saved a *purple seller,* why not then a *Purple wearer?* The poore that is *Blasphemus in Deum, & tu multuo<span class="ad-gap" title="TCP: illegible in source">••</span> in divitem,* murmuring against God, and sedicious against the rich; is in more danger of judgment, than another that hath not more opulencie than charity. Wealth doth not damne the rich, but when the getting or keeping of it doth damnifie the poore. Rich *Abraham* is in heaven, not because he was rich, but because hee was good. Poore *Lazarus* is there, *Non propter meritum paupertatis, sed pietatis, Aug.* Not for his poverty, but for his piety, howsoever, let them that must be patternes, be good patternes: the life that cannot be but exemplary, should not be but holy.

Thus in generall, the particulars here considerable are two, (for I purpose no common place of the day of Iudgement.) First the *necessity* of it, in that they are *reserved* to it. The *necessity.* As the Creation was that *Principium,* which did produce things to their being: so Iudgment is that conclusion, which shall perduce things to their ending. There is a double operation of God, one that wrought the production of things, the institution of nature, and distinction of places; from this God rested the seventh day. Another of providentiall goverment, whereby he conserves and disposeth things; from this he resteth not. <span class="ad-margin" markdown="1">Ioh. 5.17.</span> *Hitherto my Father worketh, and I worke.* According to both these there is a double Iudgment: one at the departure out of this life, which answers to Gods disposition: that they which kept not the appointed rule of their maker, might undergoe the Iustice of their avenger. The other at the last day, when God to all things determines an end, as immediately of himselfe he gave them a Beginning. But it is objected, *Nah.* 1.9. *Iudgment shall not rise up a second time:* there is one Iudgment at the end of life, if there be another at the end of the world, then there is Iudgement a second time. So there is a double Iudgment <span class="ad-conj" title="Editorial conjecture: illegible in the 1633 source (lost in the gutter of the EEBO microfilm); supplied from the stated evidence (编者推补；非原书原文)">[proportioned]</span>; one at his death respecting the singularity of his person: the other at last respecting his partnership of the world: and thus as he is a member of the universe, his Iudgement must be in the universall.

But Iudgment is the determination of doubtfull things, and every one before that day shall be put past doubting of his future estate. Yet there must be a generall Iudgment, that the equity of every ones sentence may be approved, and the Iustice <span class="ad-pb" id="p530" data-p="530" data-facs="274"></span> of God glorified. *Object.* But it is against the proper forme of Iudgment, to let execution goe before sentence: now every soule as she departs, receives her reward, and is presently possessed of joy, or punished with sorrow, If therefore there be a future Iudgment, here is execution befor sentence. *Answ.* The first is but the effect of the latter: by that they presently feele, they know what they shall eternally feele. Besides, but one part of man onely passeth that censure, the soule alone is blessed or cursed: therefore a generall Iudgment must passe upon the re-united body: which as it hath served the soule in holinesse or sinne; so must accompany the soule in blisse or paine. *Object.* The body is but an Instrument of the soule: so the Philosophers, *Physicum corpus organicum statuunt;* because the soule doth use it as an organ. Therefore it is for the soule alone to suffer: *corpus ab anima separatum non dolet:* the body feeles no paine when the soule is departed from it. *Answ.* Let it be but an Instrument, yet was it a living Instrument: as therefore the soule being the Mistresse in sinning, shall be no lesse in suffering: yet the body must have his due share in being punished, as it had the full part in being delighted.

This king hath treasure enough for all; not one of the faithfull shall want mercie, not one reprobate shall scape without penaltie. Great was the lamentation of the drowning Egyptians, and no lesse the rejoycing of Israel safe on the shore: but O the unspeakable joy with the sheepe of CHRISTS right hand, and the unventable sorrow = the goates on his left? When both the songs of good men and Angels, and the cries of bad men and devills, shall eccho to the glory of one most holy God.

A great resignation, when <span class="ad-margin" markdown="1">1 Cor. 15.24.</span> CHRIST shall deliver up the kingdome to God the Father; and cease to raigne, not as God, for so he is equall with the Father, but as mediator. For then all his redeemed ones are embraced with the everlasting armes of blessednesse: and for the rest he shall never make intercession, for they had never part in his redemption. Vp goe the Saints and Angels in their eternall Quire, owne sinke the reprobates and devills to their eternall fire, where the one shall live singing, and the other live burning, so long as there is a God in heaven.

Thus *Power* had her day in creation, *Providence* hath her day in Preservation, *Mercie* had her day in redemption, and *Iustice* must have her day in retribution. That great *Sonne* of righteousnesse appeareth in foure signes of his Zodiacke. In his *conception* he came through *Virgo,* he was borne of a Virgin. In his *Birth,* through *Gemini;* two natures being united in one Person. In his *Resurrection* he was found in *Leo,* triumphing like a victorious Lion over all his enemies. When he comes to *udgement,* he shall appeare in *Libra* the *Balance;* justly waighing out to every man a portion of reward, according to the proportion of his worke. This is his second comming; the first was of Grace, this is of Iustice. The first was to propiciate, not to judge. *Ioh.* 3.17. *God sent not his Sonne to condemne the world but to save it.* The second shall be to judge, not to propiciate. *Ioh.* 5.22. *The Father judgeth no man, but hath committed all judgement unto the Son.* In the first he came a Physitian to heale, in the next an Avenger to punish that would not be healed. Then a Lambe to suffer, now a lion to triumph and conquer. His first comming was soft, *as the dew upon the mowen grasse:* his second shall be terrible, in lightning and fire.

Seeing there must be a Iudgement, and we must all be judged, let us prepare our soules for a good answere. CHRIST bad his Disciples, when they were brought before mens Iudgement seates, to study no answere: but let every one study an answere before he comes to this Iudgement seat. Yet alas, what answere can be made? If God contend with us, we cannot answere him one of a thousand. <span class="ad-margin" markdown="1">Iob 9.3.</span> *Quae verba <span class="ad-conj" title="Editorial conjecture: illegible in the 1633 source (lost in the gutter of the EEBO microfilm); supplied from the stated evidence (编者推补；非原书原文)">[valent]</span> contra verbum?* CHRISTS word must stand. *Iob* 31.14. *What shall I doe when God riseth up? and when he visiteth, what shall I answere him?* If great men = themselves more than God, *Quid facient,* what shall they doe? If covetous men love money more than Christ, what shall they doe? If men have robbed the Lord of his Patrimonie, what shall they doe? Here is a *Quid facient* for all. Men have now their colours, reasons, pretences, and qualifications; but then what shall they answere? The wicked shall plead to Christ; we are the worke of thy hands; but he will reply, you have lost my Image and superscription. But Lord, remember thy Passion; Yes, but this is no time of compassion. The sentence is terrible. *Matth.* 25.41. *Depart yee cursed into everlasting fire,* <span class="ad-margin" markdown="1">Mat. 25.41.</span> *prepared for the devill and his Angels.* When they shall cry; Lord, though we may not ascend with thee unto glory, yet let us abide still on the earth, *statu quo prius;* nay goe, *Depart.* If we must <span class="ad-pb" id="p534" data-p="534" data-facs="276"></span> goe, let it not yet be farre, not out of thy sight and gracious presence; nay depart *from me.* If we must goe, and goe from thee, yet let us have a blessing with us: nay, depart *yee cursed.* If we must goe, and from thee, and with a curse, yet somewhat qualifie thy anger, and let our curse be but easie: nay, depart yee cursed into *Fire.* If we must depart from thee, cursed, and into fire; yet let not that fire burne long suffer it to be soone extinguished; nay, but goe into *Everlasting fire.* If there be no remedie, but we must goe *from thee* the God of glory, and with a *Curse,* the character of infelicitie: and *into fire,* torment in extremitie: and that *everlasting,* without hope of recoverie: yet let us have some pleasant and loving company; nay, but the *very devill and his Angels.* A heavy doome, which if we desire to evade, let us before the day of tryall make sure the Iudge: if we can get him our friend, we shall speed well in the Iudgement.
