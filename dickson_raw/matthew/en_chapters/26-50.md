*Ver. 50.* And Iesus said unto him, Friend, wherefore art thou come? Then they came and laid hands on Iesus, and took him.

</div>

Christ speaketh so much upon the Traitour, as might make him compare his pretence of friendship to his Master, with his intention of bringing Souldiers on him, and then he suffereth himselfe to be taken and bound. *Doct.* 1. Hypocrites may deceive others and themselves, but cannot deceive God; for here Christ putteth his finger to this Traitour's intention, saying, *Wherefore came you hither?* 2. To compare our profession with our practises, and our pretences with our intentions, is a mean to give us a right sight of our selves, therefore is it that Christ calleth *Iudas, Friend,* in respect of his profession; and asketh him *why he came,* to put him to look to his intention. 3. Christ being surety for the Redeemed, who cannot defray their own debt, he behoved to answer unto justice for them, and therefore must he be *laid hands on, and taken.*
