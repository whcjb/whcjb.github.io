Thy may and thy doings have procured these things unto thee, this is thy michednesse, because it is bitter, because it reachoth unto thine heart. So we may say of the fire that consumeth these finners, This is your adultery, this is your uncleanenesse. We may say of famine, and the suord destroying other sinners, these are your drunkennesse, &c. Some uickednesse or other produceth all these.

Lastly, (saith Job) it is a fire that consumeth. Hence note.

Sin consameth all.

Eire is an unmercifull element, But sin is ten thousand times more unmercifull. Mysticall fire is more unmercifull then naturall fire. Naturall fire when it hath done its utmost can but consume corporall things, but this mysticall fire consumeth spirituall things. It consumeth and will consume the whole substance of the soule (I mean not as to the being, but comfortable being of it )unlesse the blood of Christ quench it. Sin will never leave till it hath destroyed all in one of these two sences, either by taking away our being in this world, or by giving us a most miserable being in the world to come.

Thus Tob hath purged himselfe by protestation from a third fin, the sin of Adultery; He proceeds to a fourth, And that is injustice towards or harsh dealing with his servants.
