IN this verse and those which follow to the 19mh, inclusively, T Job sets forth, First, the trouble that was upon his spirit; Secondly, the paines which were upon his body; Thirdly, he gives the summe of both.

The trouble upon his spirit is expressed under the notion of terrours, and the powring out of his soule upon bim, (V. 15, 16.)

The paines of his body are described (v. 17, 18.) My bones are pierced in me in the night season, and my sinews take no rest. By the great force of my disease is my garment changed.

The summe or issue of both is set downe (v. 19.) He hath cast me into the mire, and Iam become liße dust and ashes. As if he had sayd, what with the terrours upon my soule, and the paines upon my body, I am brought to nothing, I’am but dust in my constitution, I am become but mine and dirt by my affliction.

Thirdly, The soule is our principal one, if we consider the immortality of it. The earthly tabernacle of mans body shakes and is ready to fall into the earth every day. Dust thou art and to dust thou shalt returne, was the sentence of God upon the body of man as soone as he had sinned. Death entred at the same dore by which sin did, And therefore it is appointed for men once to die ( Heb. 9. 27.) There’s no avoyding the mortality of the body. But the soule is an immortall piece, and it is immortall not only by divine ordination, as the body of Adam was before the fall (who then had a possibility not to die, though his body considered in its materialls was in a possibility of dying) But the soule is immortall, according to its naturall constitutive properties, or the perfection of its nature, when God made the soule he stampt immortality upon the very being and constitution of it. Thus Angells and the soules of men are immortall, because free from elementary matter and all contrariety of qualities, which are the seed or root of corruption. The soule is not only spirituall, but a spirit, as the Angels are, and so is of an everlasting make, and cannot be dissolved.

But some may say, you put too much honour upon the soule, in ascribing immortality to it: the Apostle saith (1 Tim. 6.16.) God only hath immortality, &c. Why then doe you say, the soule is immortal!? is not this to put a crowne of dignitie upon the soule, which is the Lords peculiar?

I answer; God only is immortall, simply, absolutely, primitively and independently; yet The soule hath also a dependent communicated immortality; or the soule is immortall by its constitution received and derived from God; He hath not put any ingredients of death into it. And were it not thus, the soule were not above the body, but ( as to this) the bodyes equall. Besides, were nor the soule immortall, to what purpose vere al! <span class="cy-pb" id="p144" data-p="144" data-leaf="150" title="Page 144 of the printed part begins here (原书第 144 页起)"></span>those promises of eternal life, and threatnings of eternal death. Iam the God of Abraham, and the God of Isaac, and the God of jacob; From which Christ maketh this inference in the close of the verse, God is not the God sf the dead, but of the living. But were not Abraham, Isaac and Jacob dead? yes, their bodyes were dead, but their soules lived, and thence he argues that their bodyes also should be raised againe to life, and remarried to their living soules. The very questions, doubts and debates, which have been made about the soules immortality, are a proofe that it is immortal; For as none can distinguish between rational and irrational, who have not a rational soule, so none can distinguish between mortal and immortal, who have not an immortal soule. "Tis also some proofe ofit, that there is an affectation of a kind of immortality discernable in the worst of men; what pillars? what monuments have they erected to perpetuate or immortalize their memories, yea the Scripture saith (Psal. 49.11.) Their inward thought is, that their houses shall continue for ever, and their dwelling places to all generations, they call their lands after their own names. Nothing lesse will serve their turnes, then for ever, and to al Generations. All these latter considerations, are evidences from nature, besides the evidences of Scripture, That the soule is immortal. And

We call the soule immortall, not only as that which shall never have a totall and final end, or be <span class="cy-ocr" title="OCR 存疑：两份扫描都判不了">extindt</span> for ever, But mee <span class="cy-pb" id="p145" data-p="145" data-leaf="151" title="Page 145 of the printed part begins here (原书第 145 页起)"></span>call it immortall, as that which shall never have any pause of its existence, as the body shall.

The word of God and divine reason grounded thereupon, every where confutes this dreame of the soules sleepe; or as some have more broadly called it of the soules mortality, though a little veyled under the title of Mans Mortality, by man understanding the whole man, consisting of body and soule.
