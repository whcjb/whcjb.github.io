<span class="cy-ocr" title="OCR 存疑：两份扫描都判不了">Tobe</span> range <span class="cy-ocr" title="OCR 存疑：两份扫描都判不了">tofriends</span>, specially to godly friends, is an act ibat me ought to be asham'dof.

It is a shamefull thing to be afham'd of friends, especially of godly friends, This shame might justly fall upon many at this day, what strangenesse is there between friends, and godly friends? such as heretofore lay in one anothers bosome, are growne so strange, that they cannot stay with content in on<span class="cy-pb" id="p156" data-p="156" data-leaf="165" title="Page 156 of the printed part begins here (原书第 156 页起)"></span>anothers sight, they will scarce looke upon one another, who not long since ( at least in profession) dearely loved one another. They who met purposely to speake one to another, will scarce speake to one another when they meete accidentally. Yea, they who should be ready to dye for one another, can hardly live by one another. When shall we be ashamed of this strangenes? When will the love of many which hath waxen cold gather heate againe? Tis Englands sin at this day, Strangenesse among the Saints; they who professe themselves to be all acquainted with and friends to Jesus Christ, are unfriendly and unacquainted among themselves. They who are neere to God, behold each other afar off. Were it not monstrous, if one member of the body should withdraw offices of love from another member, or should be as a stranger to it. These unnaturall distances among the members of the same mysticall body, are too open eyther to be hid or denyed. <span class="cy-ocr" title="OCR 存疑：据上下文推定，原读 Isit">Is it</span> not a reproach to <span class="cy-ocr" title="OCR 存疑：两份扫描都判不了">Christianitie</span>, that they who indeed are members of the same Christ, should act as if they were not members of the same world. To how many thousands of Saints may we say, as Job here, Are ye not asham'd te make your selves strange one to another; and consider this further, if strangenesse, if a meer withdrawing of converse be so uncomely that Christians ought to be asham'd of it, what then is that which is the roote ofit, alienation of affections. If Christians have cause to blush when they are not flee and open-hearted one to another, how ought they to be confounded who are guilty of heart-burnings and bitternesse of spirit one against another. If we ought to be ashamed that we are nor kind to one another, that we are not ready to doe one another good how should we be asham'd to be cruel to, or to devise evill one against another? strangenes is not the doing of evil, but a <span class="cy-ocr" title="OCR 存疑：两份扫描都判不了">suspention</span> from doing good, or from shewing wonted respects. Now if it be a shame not to do good, how should we be asham'd of doing evill to our bretheren?

Thirdly, We may consider Job not onely as a friend, and as a godly friend, but also as an afflicted friend, as a man almost overwhelmed with sorrow.

Then observe;

It is a <span class="cy-ocr" title="OCR 存疑：两份扫描都判不了">famefull</span> thing not to owne a good man when he is in a low or in the lowest condition.

<span class="cy-pb" id="p157" data-p="157" data-leaf="166" title="Page 157 of the printed part begins here (原书第 157 页起)"></span>Job knew the time when enow would own him, when enow would visit him, and were ambitious of his friendfhip; but being upon the dunghill, few would come at him, and none cared for his soule. Strangers desire the favour of those who are in prosperitie, and neerest friends will shew themselves strange to those that are in adversitie; but <span class="cy-ocr" title="OCR 存疑：两份扫描都判不了">izilnot</span> their shame to doe so? Paul speakes it to the high commendation of Onesiphorus, (2Tim.1.16.) He est refreshed me, and was not ashamed of my chaine : Many are as much ashamed to look upon a godly friend in chaines, as they are afraid to weare a chaine for godlinesse. No man was ever more proud of his owne golden chaine, then some are ashamed of the iron chaine of others. When Jesus Christ was apprehended and under his sufferings, this temptation went high even upon his owne Disciples and servants, whom he had often forewarned of <span class="cy-ocr" title="OCR 存疑：两份扫描都判不了">sufferingr</span>, and <span class="cy-ocr" title="OCR 存疑：据上下文推定，原读 enstructed">instructed</span> how to suffer, (Matth.26. 56,58.) Then all the Disciples forsooke him and fled. They who before forsooke all and followed him, now forsooke him and fled; Neither was this the act of some one or two, but of all the Disciples. Then all the Disciples forsooke him. And though Peter followed him, yet his following was as bad as a forsaking, for he <span class="cy-ocr" title="OCR 存疑：据上下文推定，原读 follewed">followed</span> him afarre off, (v.58.) And when he was come neere into the house, he did worse then forsake him, he denied him and forswore him. Peter made himselfe so strange to Christ, that he professed strongly, yea with an oath, <span class="cy-ocr" title="OCR 存疑：两份扫描都判不了">Ihnow</span> not the man, as if he had said, if you will not take my word, I will sweare I know him not; O what a distemper of spirit did feare bring him to? how was his spirit <span class="cy-ocr" title="OCR 存疑：据上下文推定，原读 overwrought">over wrought</span> with worldly shame, when he wrought all these sinfull coverings that he might appeare a stranger to Jesus Christ? O Peter mast thou not ashamed to shew thy selfe strange to Jesus Christ when he was afflicted, who was therfore afflicted to make us who were strangers, and a far off from God, neere unto him and his friends? It is no easie thing to be acquainted with the crosse of others, how hard mill it be <span class="cy-ocr" title="OCR 存疑：据上下文推定，原读 tobe">to be</span> acquainted with our own crosse? If men be strange to their friends when they are afflicted, O how strange will they be to their owne afflictions? When there is no sutablenesse in our spirits to suffering and affliction: we cannot suite with those that suffer and are afflicted? The man in the Gospel (Luk.10.30.) travelling from <span class="cy-ocr" title="OCR 存疑：两份扫描都判不了">Tericho</span> to Jerusalem, fell among theeves which <span class="cy-pb" id="p158" data-p="158" data-leaf="167" title="Page 158 of the printed part begins here (原书第 158 页起)"></span>Vers, <span class="cy-ocr" title="OCR 存疑：两份扫描都判不了">Fripped</span> bim of'his rayment, and wounded him, and departed, leaving him halfe dead; then cometh a Levite and a Priest, and what do they? doe they owne the man?no, they goe on the other side, as if the man had been sick of the plague, and they durst not come neere him for feare of infection, they saw him in that lamentable pickle, and passed by, they would not doe the ossice of a neighbour ( that’s the intent of the Parable, to shew who is amans neighbour) if the man had been mounted on his horse riding <span class="cy-ocr" title="OCR 存疑：两份扫描都判不了">gallantly</span> on the way, possibly the Priest and the Levite would have joyn’d with him, and have said, Sir, whether travaile you? they would have fallen into discourse with him, as a man of their owne Tribe, and have gone to the same Inne with him, but seeing him lie wounded, and groveling upon the high way in his bloud, they made themselves strange to him, As men naturally <span class="cy-ocr" title="OCR 存疑：两份扫描都判不了">shunne</span> their owne afflictions, so those that are in affliction; 'Tis against the light and common principles of nature to doe so, yet naturally men doe so. And because ’tis against the light of nature to doe so, therefore’tis a shame to doe so. And if it be against the light of nature, and the principles of <span class="cy-ocr" title="OCR 存疑：两份扫描都判不了">morralitie</span>, how much more is it against the light of grace, and the principles of Religion, when this strangenesse is shewed to those who have grace and are indeed religious ?'Tis a Gospel duty to doe good to all, but especially to the houshold of faith; They breake all the bands and tyes of love, who <span class="cy-ocr" title="OCR 存疑：据上下文推定，原读 resuse">refuse</span> to doe them good that have received and expresse the tokens of divine love, or are strangers in aff-ction to them, who are of the houshold of faith. Every godly man may say to his uncharitable bretheren, as Job did here to his friends; Are yee not ashamed to make your selves strange to me? Thus Job hath reproved his friends undeserved severity, and he reproves them yet further in the next words upon supposition, that he had <span class="cy-ocr" title="OCR 存疑：两份扫描都判不了">deservedit</span>.
