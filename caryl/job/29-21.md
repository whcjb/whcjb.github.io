The word of truth and grace is the most desirable thing in the world; and they that know either the worth of it, or their own want of it, cannot but desire it, as the Earth desireth rain; O what longings, what thirstings are there in poor humbled parched Souls, for the word of God! they esteem it more then the expected rain, more then their appointed food, as was shewed at the <span class="cy-ocr" title="OCR 存疑：据上下文推定，原读 Tuelfth">Twelfth</span> verse of the thirty-second Chapter of this Booke. If a man be hungry, <span class="cy-margin">UMI</span> <span class="cy-pb" id="p617" data-p="617–618" data-leaf="314" title="Columns 617–618 of the 1676–77 folio begin here (对开本第 617–618 栏起)"></span>a little bidding will serve the turn to perswade him to cat; Nature teaches men to cut when they are hungry, to drink when thirsty, and to look for rain when the earth is dry; and doth not grace teach and provoke us to pray and wait for spiritual food and rain, dat our souls may live and fructifie! How forward are we to pray for natural rain, in its season, and the Lord lookes that ve should ; he loves to be entreated for it: And shall we be backward to receive spiritual rain, which (though it be sometimes more, yet) is at all times seasonable! When once we are sensible of our spiritual dryness and barrenness, of our wants and weaknesses, ve shall as earnestly desire and prize the good counsels, the feeding raines of the Word, as ever <span class="cy-ocr" title="OCR 存疑：据上下文推定，原读 wedid">we did</span> rain in the extreamest heat of the dryest Summer. 'Tis much to be lamented, ’tis to be lamented with showers of teares, that those showers of holy instruction are so little valued and desired; how much more is it to be lamented, that many with there might be no more of this rain, that these drops might drop no more, that these Heavens over them might be as brass, and the bottles of these clouds stop for ever. And as many, think we have too much of this rain, so others are afraid of it; and therefore they get to shelter, as men do in a rainy day, lest they should wet their fine cloaths, or take cold by it. And whereas, they are most unwilling to be in the common rain who have their best clothes on, they only are unwilling to come into this rain who have their worst; O how loth are sinners ( who have nothing on them but the filthy garments and rotten rags of old Adam) to come into this rain! And ’tis rare to find or <span class="cy-ocr" title="OCR 存疑：据上下文推定，原读 liear">hear</span> of Job's hearers any where, such, I <span class="cy-ocr" title="OCR 存疑：据上下文推定，原读 meall">me all</span>, as open their mouths wide for holy counsel as for the latter rain. The men of the world are like the land of Egypt, from whence the Israelites came, of which Moses saith (Deut. 11. 10.) Thou watered'st it with thy foot; that is, by digging channels in the earth, thou didst conveigh water to it; But the people of God are like the land of Canaan (to which the Israelites were going) of which Moses saith at the eleventh verse of the same Chapter, It drinketh water of the rain of heaven. Earthly water will serve their turn who are earthly; but they who are heavenly, or are to be made heavenly, must have water from heaven.

I shall conclude with that word, that notable word of the Lord concerning his own word, under this continued Metaphor or Allegory of rain (Isa. 35. 10, 11.) As the rain cometh down <span class="cy-ocr" title="OCR 存疑：据上下文推定，原读 andthe">and the</span> snow from heaven, and returneth not thither, but watereth the earth, <span class="cy-ocr" title="OCR 存疑：据上下文推定，原读 andmaketh">and maketh</span> it bring forth and bud, that it may give seed to the sower, and bread to the eater; so shall my <span class="cy-ocr" title="OCR 存疑：据上下文推定，原读 wordhe">word he</span> that goeth forth out of my mouth: Ie shalt not return unto me void, but it shall accomplish that which please, and it shall prosper in the thing whereto I sent at. What <span class="cy-margin">5e</span> the Prophet discourseth and affirmeth here concerning that special word of God, his word of promise for pardon and reconciliation, is true concerning the whole counsel of God revealed to us in his word, it returneth not (as the rain and snow do not) till it hath watered every soul it is sent to. Some slight the word preacht (as they do an ordinary shower) not considering that God sends it, who never sent, nor did, any thing in vain. And whatsoever the thoughts of vain men are, it will appear at last, that not only no whole shower, but not any one drop of it was either sent or returned in vain. Though men make no present account of it, yet God will have an account of it hereafter; He will enquire whether we have <span class="cy-ocr" title="OCR 存疑：两份扫描都判不了">beenbettered</span>, and made fruitful by it; He will enquire what we have brought forth, and how' much. As the rain and the snow do not return back without accomplishing that which God pleaseth, so neither shall his word; an effect it shall have either to convert' or to condemn, it will be a savour of life or of death to every soul that hears it. And if Sermons be not as showers to refresh, they will make a deluge to overflow. As once the world was overwhelmed with a deluge of water, so it will (in this sence) again; and they who are not made fruitful by showers of Gospel-doctrine, shall be sunk, choaked, and drowned in them.
