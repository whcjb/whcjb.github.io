Hence Observe,

First, The life of man is a <span class="cy-ocr" title="OCR 存疑：两份扫描都判不了">labourious</span> life.

He must accomplish his dayes as a Hireling: He is a Labourer, not a <span class="cy-ocr" title="OCR 存疑：两份扫描都判不了">Loiterer</span>. Sin brought pain into our Labour, but the duty of Labour was before Sin.

Secondly Note,

Me have but a day of Labour.

A Hireling doth not think much of it, it is but a day, and <span class="cy-margin">if</span> some have a longer day then others, yet still’tis but a day: This day will end, and it will be as if it had never begun, we shall forget all our sorrows. A woman in Travel hath great sorrow, <span class="cy-margin">but</span> when the work is over, she forgets all her sorrow, for joy that a man is born into the World: When we come to the wished <span class="cy-margin">acin so</span> <span class="cy-ocr" title="OCR 存疑：两份扫描都判不了">complishment</span> of our Labours, we shall forget that we were labour. As our Labours are little regarded by the World here, we shall as little remember them our selves hereafter, we shall lay them all under our Pillows, when we go to the Grave, and talk no more of them.

Thirdly Note,

This life must be <span class="cy-ocr" title="OCR 存疑：两份扫描都判不了">accoumed</span> for, or, there is a reckoning to come about this life.

Every man accomplisheth as a Hireling his day; a Hireling hath his work viewed before his Wages are paid: Every man must give an account of himself to God. Evil workers shall <span class="cy-margin">be</span> paid with Death: They who do good shall receive the Free gift of Eternal life: Our labour shall not be in vain, either <span class="cy-margin">in</span> the Lord or out of the Lord; all that we do shall be confidered: Wicked men shall have their pay for what they have done, and the Saints shall receive their reward; Their works shall follow them: They shall not receive Wages for their Work, <span class="cy-margin">but</span> they shall receive benefit by their work, worth more then their Work Ten Thousand times told. They shall receive more for the least work, then the best works can deserve. A Cup <span class="cy-margin">of</span> cold Water shall have a reward, Rivers of Oyl have no Merit. It were not worth while to be as an Hireling, or to set <span class="cy-margin">our</span> Hand to the Noblest Works among men, if all the return were to come through the Minds of Men: But as whatsoever <span class="cy-margin">we do</span> <span class="cy-pb" id="p595" data-p="595" data-leaf="601" title="Page 595 of the printed part begins here (原书第 595 页起)"></span>Chap.14.
