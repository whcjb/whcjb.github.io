<p><span class="bn-vr">1–13</span>ANOTHER Psalm "of David," to be sung by all saints, even as It was used by their Head, David's Son. In it we have (ver. 1–3) the picture of the wicked, with a "Selah," that bids us pause over its dark colours. Then we have (ver. 4, 5) a view of the snares spread by the wicked, with another "Selah"-pause. Thereafter, we see a soul in the attitude of faith (ver. 6–8). They are laying their snares, but calm as Elisha beholding the Syrian host assembling (2 Kings 6:15), the stayed soul sings—</p>

<p>"I have said to the Lord, My God art thou;"</p>

<p>And then he prays, putting a "Selah"<sup class="bn-fn"><a href="#fn-1" id="fnref-1">1</a></sup> at the close, that we may again pause and survey the scene.</p>

<p>In ver. 9–11 we have the certainty of the wicked's overthrow. It is spoken of as a thing to be realised as a matter of course. It is a glance at the great day of the Lord, when he destroys the Man of Sin, and all such foes.</p>

<p>"Coals of fire shall be thrown upon them;</p>

<p>He will precipitate them into raging gulfs (<span class="bn-he" lang="he" dir="rtl">מַהֲרֹמוֹת</span>);</p>

<p>They shall rise no more."</p>

<p>And then we have (ver. 12, 13) the calm assurance of blessing to the righteous, introduced by an expression that sends our thoughts away to Job's memorable utterance of his hope that the Redeemer would appear on the earth at the latter day, (19:25). "I know," says the Psalmist—</p>

<p>"I know that Jehovah will execute</p>

<p>The judgment of the poor, the right of the needy."</p>

<p>And then in verse 13 the expression <span class="bn-he" lang="he" dir="rtl">אַךְ</span>, like that in Psa. 73:1, intimates that these are inferences drawn from previous reflection. The issue shall be this, the righteous shall praise his name, and dwell before his face for ever. They shall inherit the promised kingdom, entering in with songs, and continuing their songs for ever. Such is this song of the sweet singer of Israel, under the inspiration of the Holy Ghost, setting forth</p>

<p class="bn-sum">The Righteous One amid snares, confidently expecting the ruin of the ungodly, and his own reward.</p>

<div class="bn-notes">
<p id="fn-1"><a class="bn-back" href="#fnref-1">1</a>We meet with <em>Selah</em> here for the first time since Psa. lxxxix. From Psa. xc. to Psa. cxl. no <em>Selah</em> occurs. Why omitted in these fifty, we cannot tell, any more than why so often occurring in others. However, there are only about forty Psalms in all in which it is used.</p>
</div>
