<p><span class="bn-vr">1–10</span>THE same Asaph takes the harp again, at the bidding of the Holy Ghost, to write an ode that, like many of David's (see Psa. 57), has been marked "Al-taschith," and called, "A Song," lively in theme, and with life in every line.</p>

<p>"We have praised thee, O God! we have praised thee!</p>

<p>And (now at length) thy name is near,</p>

<p>Thy wondrous works are telling it."</p>

<p>This is the delighted cry of Messiah's people, who see Him near at hand, and could join with Isaiah 25:9, "Lo! this is our God!" Their hosannahs are becoming hallelujahs. And Messiah himself responds, as in Isaiah 63:1, 2, explaining his ways. He refers to their words regarding "his name being now near," his long-hid discovery of his person and promised deeds, "I will take a set time" (Heng.), or rather thus:</p>

<p>"(It is so) for I now get the appointed day! (Acts 1:7.)</p>

<p>I (<span class="bn-he" lang="he" dir="rtl">אֲנִי</span>, unlike earth's usurpers), judge uprightly.</p>

<p>Earth and its inhabitants have melted away.</p>

<p>I am he who (<span class="bn-he" lang="he" dir="rtl">אָנֹכִי</span>) have poised its pillars. Selah." (Ver. 2, 3)</p>

<p>He has weighed the pillars and so knows, and has power to order earth. After a pause, Messiah opens his lips to utter the sentences of doom. He addresses the apostate nations, with Antichrist at their head (ver. 4)—</p>

<p>"I say (<span class="bn-he" lang="he" dir="rtl">אָמַרְתִּי</span>, the word has passed my lips) to the boastful, Boast no more," &amp;c.</p>

<p>No help will come to you from east or west, i. e., from land or sea; nay, nor from the wilderness (<span class="bn-he" lang="he" dir="rtl">מִמִּדְבַּר</span>) shall any caravan bring you ought to lift up your head;</p>

<p>"For God is judging now!" (ver. 7). (As Psa. 57:11, <span class="bn-he" lang="he" dir="rtl">כִי אֱלֹהִים שֹׁפֵט</span>).</p>

<p>And the processes of judgment are commenced, the reversal of unrighteous sentences, and the pouring out of that terrible "Cup" (ver. 8). John (Rev. 14:10) saw this cup, full of "wine without mixture," i. e., without one drop of water to alleviate its fierce poison; but here it is "full of mixture," i. e., of all strong ingredients, that make its taste more bitter, and its wrathful poison more sure. This blood-red wine of vengeance is the measured-out portion of all earth's wicked, great or small.</p>

<p>At verse 9, Messiah seems to wind up the proceedings by indicating the general result. As in Psa. 2:7, a full announcement is made of these sentences in their hearing—</p>

<p>"And I (<span class="bn-he" lang="he" dir="rtl">אֲנִי</span>, who am entitled so to do), declare this a tiling eternally fixed;"</p>

<p>and then turning towards his blessed ones, inviting them to join him in praise, he may be said to cry, "Hallelujah!"</p>

<p>"Let me sing (<span class="bn-he" lang="he" dir="rtl">אֲזַמְּרָה</span>) to the God of Jacob!</p>

<p>And I will cut off all the horns of the wicked" (while my song ascends).</p>

<p>As the final issue of the whole, lo!</p>

<p>"The horns of the Righteous One are exalted!"</p>

<p>prophecy is fulfilled; what Hannah sang of in 1 Sam. 2:10, and so many others besides, is now come to pass; for his "name is near," his kingdom is come. And thus, referring all the while to the beseeching prayer of Psalm 74, Asaph has sung</p>

<p>Messiah's response to his people who are expecting His Coming.</p>
