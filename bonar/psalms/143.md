<p><span class="bn-vr">1–12</span>David is still the sweet singer here, his harp sounding in the wilderness of Judah, or, as in Psa. 42, from some still more remote retreat. Here, however, he alludes to no particular external privation —it is his soul's sadness that is the burden of his song. Christ could use these utterances, feeling not his bodily absence from heaven so much as the Father's hidden face. And it suits the believer at times, when he mourns most of all for the absence of any tokens of the Lord's special presence, in himself or the church around.</p>

<p>From verses 1 to 4 we have the strong appeal of one who feels the darkness in which he is enveloped by the absence of special tokens of God's love. Our Lord uses the argument of God's "truth and righteousness," in seeking an answer; and so we, his members, appeal to these same perfections, in our expostulations with our God, warranted by 1 John 1:9, "He is faithful and just to forgive us." And then the deprecation in verse 2,</p>

<p>"And enter not into judgment with thy servant,"</p>

<p>is, in our Lord's lips, equivalent to "If it be possible, let this cup pass from me!" Do not argue the case with me (see Job 9:32, and 22:4, and 34:23), to shew me that there is cause for all this darkness. I know there is a cause; the cause is the sin which I have undertaken to bear, "For before thee shall no living one be justified"—not one of all descended from Adam. (Compare <span class="bn-he" lang="he" dir="rtl">כָּל־חַי</span> here with Gen. 3:20, when Eve's posterity got that name after the Fall.) But my appeal is to thy "truth and righteousness," which are engaged to carry me through; and my need of help is great.</p>

<p>"For the foe is pursuing (<span class="bn-he" lang="he" dir="rtl">רֹדֵף</span>) my soul!" (Ver. 3.)</p>

<p>And then, as if overtaken in the pursuit, he cries, "He has smitten my life to the ground! He has made me stand in deep darkness, like one eternally dead (Hengst); and my spirit is overwhelmed (Psa. 102, title, and 142:4) within me; my heart in the midst of me is desolate."</p>

<p>At verse 5, there is a gleam of light through the "darkness;" he recalls to mind God's love manifested to Israel in former days.</p>

<p>At verse 6, there is a renewed appeal to the Lord's pity, drawn from his state, resembling "a weary land"—(<span class="bn-he" lang="he" dir="rtl">אֶרֶץ עֲיֵפָה</span> like Isa. 32:2)—a land where a traveller's strength is exhausted by the rough roads and crooked paths, and the oppressive heat of the sun's intolerable rays. It was thus our Surety learned by experience to sympathise with us; and thus it was he became the "shadow of a great rock in a weary land." He here calls the Father to notice his self-emptying and humiliation, while bearing wrath for our sins;</p>

<p>"My soul is to thee (see Hebr.) as a weary land."</p>

<p>At verses 7 and 9 he seeks speedy relief; and the clause, "Make me to know the path I should walk in" is similar to the "If it be possible" in his prayer in the garden. In the case of one of his members, the words have of course a different application, and yet one resembling his in the circumstance, that it is a petition for guidance under our personal difficulties.</p>

<p>At verse 10 do we not hear, "Not my will, but thine be done," mingled with the filial accents of confidence, "My God!"</p>

<p>But at verses 11, 12, the prospect opens out on future glory. The Spirit, that same "loving," or "good Spirit" who instructed Israel (Nehem. 9:20), that same "eternal Spirit by whom he offered himself without spot to God," shall lead him forth from those scenes, and place him</p>

<p>"In the land of uprightness."</p>

<p>The land of plainness (<span class="bn-he" lang="he" dir="rtl">מִישׁוֹר</span>), a land where no wickedness of men, and malice of Satan, vex the soul from day to day; a land where no rough paths and crooked turns lengthen out the traveller's weary journey, (see ver. 5); but where all is like the smooth pasture lands of Reuben (Deut. 3:10, Josh. 13:9), a fit place for flocks to lie down. "Thy Spirit" will do this in his love, when "my Spirit" (ver. 7), my overwhelmed human soul, seemed ready to fail. Thou wilt be to me what thou wert to David, in "bringing his soul out of all distress" (1 Kings 1:29), and establishing him on his throne. All foes shall be extirpated, (<span class="bn-he" lang="he" dir="rtl">תַּצְמִית</span>). Is not this the kingdom come, and its King exalted! And is not this a song alike for the Head and the members?</p>

<p>A prayer of the Righteous One, when feeling the heat of the weary land.</p>
