<p><span class="bn-vr">1–12</span>THIS Psalm has three parts, each beginning with <span class="bn-he" lang="he" dir="rtl">אַךְ</span>, "truly;" verses 1, 4, 9. There was a "Rock" spoken of in Psalm 61:2. The God of Israel had long been known under that name, ever since Jacob, and Moses, and Hannah, had appropriated the Rock, with its many properties of shade, shelter, strength, solidity, dignity, to give a people accustomed to level deserts and sands an emblem of the Unchanging One to whom the helpless may resort. This Rock is prominent throughout this Psalm. At the commencement, the soul of the speaker is seen under it as his shelter—he reposes in its shade, and on its strength.</p>

<p>"Only upon God my soul reposeth!" (Horsley.) He is a rock, while enemies are as an "inclining wall and a fence that has had a shove"— on the verge of ruin. Thus he can sing,</p>

<p>"Truly in God</p>

<p>My soul takes rest." (Verses 1 and 6)</p>

<p>Foes and bitter persecutors are around him, and this keeps him very near the Refuge at all times. We have here the soul of the Righteous One—Christ and his members—resorting to Jehovah while iniquity surrounds them, and persecution tries them. We hear them calling on Him, and stirring up one another to do the like (ver. 8), affixing the solemn, "Selah."</p>

<p>"Trust in him at all times, ye people" (<span class="bn-he" lang="he" dir="rtl">עָם</span>, true Israel of God!)</p>

<p>"Our estimate of man (it has been said) depends on our estimate of God;" and here God is felt to be most gloriously great. The sons of men (ver 9) are a mere vapour; their greatness, even when it shall flush up to the splendour of Antichrist's dominion, is a mere mirage. The sentence against it is on the way. Already you may hear God speaking; it is no fancy. Two things have been declared by our God, viz., that he will bring down the proud, and that he has mercy for his own. As out of Sinai, so out of the Rock, we hear a voice telling that Jehovah is God Almighty, and yet merciful too.</p>

<p>"One thing God has spoken,</p>

<p>Two things there are which I have heard—</p>

<p>viz., That might is God's;</p>

<p>And that mercy also is Jehovah's!" (Verses 11, 12.)</p>

<p>In this certainty we look for the Great Day of the Lord—the day when a mismanaged world shall be set in order—a day sure to come, and sure to satisfy us when it has come,</p>

<p>"For thou renderest to every man according to his work."</p>

<p>When the choir of singers, at whose head was Jeduthun, sang this Psalm together, the godly in Israel would feel their souls raised to the very heights of confidence, sympathising with</p>

<p class="bn-sum">The Righteous One, when threatened, looking to the Rock for help.</p>
