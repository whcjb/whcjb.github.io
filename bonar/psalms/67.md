<p><span class="bn-vr">1–7</span>ONCE more the Jewish Church is prominent in this solemn "Psalm," which is sung as a lively "Song," on Negineth. They pray for the outpouring of the full blessing which their High Priest, Jesus, is to bestow by their means on all the earth.</p>

<p>The language of verse 1 refers us to Num. 6:24, 25, and very appropriately; for the time is the Lord's Second Coming, when, as true High Priest, he comes forth from the Holiest to bless the people. The "Selah" at the end of verse 1 and verse 4 is, in both cases, very expressive, indicating, as it does, pauses in the sense and feeling, as well as the music.</p>

<p>"God be merciful to us</p>

<p>And bless us!</p>

<p>And cast the light of his countenance</p>

<p>(So as that it may be) with us." (<span class="bn-he" lang="he" dir="rtl">אִתָּנוּ</span> See Hengst.)</p>

<p>Bless us and guide us in thy way, (thy mode of dealing with thy people), that by us thy way may be known on the earth, as foretold in Gen. 12:3, and since those days, in Amos 8:14; Isa. 60:1, 2; Acts 15:15–17; Rom. 11:15, and many other places.</p>

<p>"The nations (<span class="bn-he" lang="he" dir="rtl">עַמִּים</span>) shall praise thee, O God;</p>

<p>The nations shall praise thee! (Ver. 3)</p>

<p>The peculiar people, <span class="bn-he" lang="he" dir="rtl">הָעָם</span>, here anticipate with joy the time when the <span class="bn-he" lang="he" dir="rtl">עַמִּים</span>, the whole Gentile people, shall praise their God and Saviour, and that through their means.</p>

<p>"Let the tribes (of earth, the <span class="bn-he" lang="he" dir="rtl">לְאֻמִּים</span> who once raged against thee, Psa. 2:1) rejoice and sing,</p>

<p>For thou judgest (i. e., rulest) the (<span class="bn-he" lang="he" dir="rtl">עַמִּים</span>) nations righteously,</p>

<p>And as for the tribes of earth (<span class="bn-he" lang="he" dir="rtl">לְאֻמִּים</span>) thou guidest them" (Isa. 58:11, Hengst.)</p>

<p>And again at the happy prospect they cry, "Hallelujah!" for they repeat their song—</p>

<p>"The nations (<span class="bn-he" lang="he" dir="rtl">עַמִּים</span>) shall praise thee, O God,</p>

<p>The nations shall praise thee! every one of them!" (Ver. 5)</p>

<p>And now Earth, as well as Palestine, giveth its increase, for the curse is away, and the blessing rests on it (Lev. 26:4), Israel rejoices in this communication of their blessing to all men—</p>

<p>"Earth giveth its increase!</p>

<p>God, our God, blesseth us!</p>

<p>God blesseth us!</p>

<p>And they fear Him!</p>

<p>All ends of the earth!"</p>

<p>Horsley says, this is "A hymn for the Feast of Tabernacles, prophetic of a general conversion of the world to the worship of God." Dr Allix entitles this Psalm, "A Prayer of the Synagogue for the Second Coming of the Messias, when her empire is to be extended over all nations, and the temporal blessings which are promised to the Jews in several oracles shall be conferred on them." But it is simpler, and perhaps more correct to describe it thus—</p>

<p>The Prayer of Israel for the blessing which Messiah is to bestow on them, for the sake of earth at large.</p>
