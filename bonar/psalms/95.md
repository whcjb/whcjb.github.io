<p><span class="bn-vr">1–11</span>"HOLY joy in God, not discord nor dejection, appear in the old covenant as the fundamental sentiment of adoration." Thus truly spoke Tholuck in regard to the gladsome calls that begin so many of these Psalms.</p>

<p>The King and Kingdom, the Judge and the Judge's vengeance, are within sight, hastening on, almost at the door.<sup class="bn-fn"><a href="#fn-1" id="fnref-1">1</a></sup> With these solemn prospects influencing them, the flock and the shepherd are now heard inviting men to enter the fold while it is the day of grace. Augustine felt this connexion when he wrote—"Venturus est! præveniamus faciem ejus in confessione."</p>

<p>"O come, let us sing cheerfully to Jehovah (Deut 28:47),</p>

<p>Let us raise the peal of melody to the rock of our salvation!</p>

<p>Let us come early before him (q. d., ere ever he calls), with praise;</p>

<p>We will raise the peal of melody in psalms to him." (Ver. 1, 2.)</p>

<p>He is great, and he is Sovereign over all (ver. 3); the deeps and the heights are his (ver. 4); the sea and the land (ver. 5); he is our Maker —and "MAKER" is equivalent to God who made us all that we ere in grace, as a nation and as individuals. Deut 32:6 illustrates it. So in Psa. 100:2, and many other places. Our God is a shepherd to us who glory in the blessedness of being pastured by him, and defended as well as guided by him (ver. 6, 7)—</p>

<p>"To-day, O that ye would hear his voice!"</p>

<p>This is the force of verse 7, <span class="bn-he" lang="he" dir="rtl">אִם תִשְׁמָעוּ</span>, like Exod. 32:32, "And now, O that thou wouldst forgive their sin!" and like Luke 19:42, "O that thou hadst known!" It is an intensely earnest call on those addressed to hearken to that voice, viz., to the call of God; while verse 8, in the same breath, entreats them not to be as Israel at Meribah and Massah—"like Meribah, like the day of Massah in the wilderness." Only let us not fail to notice, that while it is the flock who speak in verses 1–7, it is the Shepherd who takes up their expostulating words, and urges them home himself at verse 8 to the end, using the argument which by the Holy Ghost is addressed to us also in Hebrews 3.</p>

<p>There is something very powerful in this expostulation, when connected with the circumstances that give rise to it. In themselves, the burst of adoring love, and the full outpouring of affection in verses 1–7 are irresistibly persuasive; but when the voice of the Lord himself is heard (such a voice, using terms of vehement entreaty!) we cannot imagine expostulation carried further. Unbelief alone could resist this voice; blind, malignant unbelief alone could repel</p>

<p class="bn-sum">The flock and the Shepherd together inviting men now to enter the fold.</p>
