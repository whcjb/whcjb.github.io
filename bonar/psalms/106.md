<p><span class="bn-vr">1–48</span>WE are still traversing the same field We are still listening to the gracious acts of the Lord toward one nation of his ransomed— namely, Israel, It is another portion of their history that is reviewed, but there is as much of grace, sovereign grace, in this portion as in the first. And hence it opens, like the former, with praise and adoring wonder—</p>

<p>"Hallelujah! praise ye the Lord!</p>

<p>For he is good! for his mercy is for ever.</p>

<p>Who can tell the mighty deeds of the Lord?</p>

<p>Who can shew forth all his praise?"</p>

<p>The only mode in which the creature can even attempt aright to shew forth his praise is, by continued and ever increasing service —"Blessed are they that keep judgment, and they that do righteousness at all times." So great has the Lord's favour to his Israel been, that the sweet singer, whoever he be—David, as in 1 Chron. 16, or David's Lord, in his day—while remembering that favour and singular love, exclaims (ver. 4, 5),—</p>

<p>"Remember me, Lord, with the favour of (shewn to) thy people!</p>

<p>Visit me with thy salvation! (such as was wrought for them.)</p>

<p>That I may see the good of thy chosen ones,</p>

<p>And may rejoice in the joy of thy nation,<sup class="bn-fn"><a href="#fn-1" id="fnref-1">1</a></sup></p>

<p>And glory with thine inheritance."</p>

<p>If the type afforded such matter for praise, what shall the antitype? If that kingdom of Israel furnished such proofs of Jehovah's love, what shall The Kingdom that is yet to come?</p>

<p>To magnify the Lord's grace, confession is made of Israel's sin— forgetfulness, ingratitude, selfishness (vers. 6–15); and then of rebellion, idolatry, murmuring unbelief, lust, and lewdness; persevering unbelief, neglect of duty, conformity to the heathen world (vers. 16–39). Scene upon scene is brought before us in which Israel is seen defiled, polluted, stained to the core with sin. Yet the Lord saved them, when they returned from their ways (vers. 40–46), fulfilling the promise in Lev. 26:12, and answering the prayer (1 Kings 8:47), left for all ages.</p>

<p>Yes; these scenes of grace toward the basest, most ungrateful, most perverse, are for all ages, to lead them to the cry in verse 47,—</p>

<p>"Save us, O Lord, our God!</p>

<p>And gather us from the Gentiles."</p>

<p>Israel must raise that cry in these latter days. Israel will raise it soon. Ere long, they shall take up the harp of David, and Solomon, and Asaph, and Heman, and the sons of Korah, and use all these songs; and this among the rest—this among the first. On that day, instead of "Amen" to the solemn curse, as in Deut. 27:15–26, the people shall, with one accord, say "Amen" to the song of thanksgiving raised to Jehovah, who has gathered them from the Gentiles.</p>

<p>"Blessed be Jehovah, God of Israel,</p>

<p>From everlasting and unto everlasting!</p>

<p>And let all the people (<span class="bn-he" lang="he" dir="rtl">העם</span>) say, Amen!</p>

<p>Hallelujah!"</p>

<p>Thus setting forth</p>

<p>The Lord glorified in his long-suffering to Israel, and in their final gathering.</p>

<p id="fn-1"><a class="bn-back" href="#fnref-1">1</a>Here the term <span class="bn-he" lang="he" dir="rtl">גּוֹי</span> is applied to Israel. It may also, in such cases, imply that God's people (<span class="bn-he" lang="he" dir="rtl">עַם</span>) are, in themselves, no better than <span class="bn-he" lang="he" dir="rtl">גּוֹיִם</span>. But <span class="bn-he" lang="he" dir="rtl">גּוֹי</span> though they be by nature, they are <em>thine</em>.</p>
