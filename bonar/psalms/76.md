<p><span class="bn-vr">1–12</span>MILTON celebrates "the inviolable saints," the holy hosts that guard the throne of God. But the family of saints on earth may claim that title equally with them, being invincible and inviolable in their King. Asaph sings of these "on Neginoth" (see Psalm 4), and uses the lively "song" for his triumphant strains.</p>

<p>He looks back upon the past, when God made himself renowned as Israel's God, dwelling on Zion, breaking there (<span class="bn-he" lang="he" dir="rtl">שָׁמָּה</span>, thitherward, like Ezekiel's "Jehovah-Shammah," 48:35, q. d., his eye and heart ever toward them) the "flashing arrows of the bow," giving fame to Zion beyond all other hills and kingdoms. All was done by the God of Jacob for his people. The "Selah" (ver. 3) bids us pause to consider this, as it does again verse 9. By him "the stout-hearted were made a prey; they slept deep their sleep;" by him who could give foes the same "rebuke" that he gave to the swelling waters (Psalm 114:7), causing the warrior and his war-chariot to be alike motionless and dead in the silent camp. Ah, it is "Thou" (<span class="bn-he" lang="he" dir="rtl">אַתָּה</span>) who art to be feared, Thou (<span class="bn-he" lang="he" dir="rtl">אַתָּה</span>) alone! And we might have noticed also, there is significance in using the name "Salem," (ver. 2). It reminds us of the reign of Melchizedeck, and hints at the slaughter of the kings, whom God's Abrahams overcome.</p>

<p>And ever shall it be thus. Ever shall it be an unanswerable question, "Who shall stand before thee when thou art angry?" (verse 7), down to that great and terrible day when all earth, at the sight of thy throne, shall ask, "Who shall be able to stand?" (Rev. 6:17). And then shall verses 8, 9, be fulfilled most emphatically—</p>

<p>"Out of heaven thou hast proclaimed judgment (<span class="bn-he" lang="he" dir="rtl">דִין</span>, see Dan. 7:10,<span class="bn-he" lang="he" dir="rtl"></span></p>

<p><span class="bn-he" lang="he" dir="rtl">דִּינָא יְתִב</span>).</p>

<p>Earth fears, and is at rest! (<span class="bn-he" lang="he" dir="rtl">שָׁקָטָה</span>, like Josh. 14:15, and Isa. 14:7)</p>

<p>When God arises to judgment,</p>

<p>Saving all the meek of earth. Selah."</p>

<p>Is not this the day when the Saviour comes to reign?—the day when the results of things shall best be seen—the day when every saint with anointed eye shall see that events all tended to the glory of their God—the day when they shall sing better far than now,</p>

<p>"Surely the wrath of man praiseth thee.</p>

<p>Thou girdest thyself with the remnant of wrath;"</p>

<p>turning it to use, even every particle of it?</p>

<p>Vow, then, and perform the vow, O Israel! a people near to Him (Num. 2:2). Bring a gift to him that is the true object of fear, to him who has cut off, as in a time of vintage (<span class="bn-he" lang="he" dir="rtl">בְצֹר</span>, as Rev. 14:18), the breath, the life, of princes; to him who is terrible to earth's kings. If this Jehovah be for us, who can be against us? Let us even now sing this</p>

<p>Song to the Mighty One, who is the fear of Israel and of Earth.</p>
