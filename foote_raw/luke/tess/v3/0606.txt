THE faculty of speech is very useful, and very ennobling.
The Psalmist calls it “the glory” of man. It is the index
of intelligence. It enables us, as it were, to enter into each
other’s souls; and it contributes, in an incalculable degree,
to the improvement and happiness of the present life. But,
right reason, so far from being confined in its exercise to
perishing things, stretches its contemplations to the great
Creator, and an unseen world; and it must surely be reason-
able to employ the faculty of speech on those subjects on
which reason itself is properly employed. If true wisdom
