in beseeching him to have pity on the poor afflicted mat,
and restore him to the comfort of strength, and to the cape
city for earning his daily bread. But no; suspecting Christ's
gracious intention, the Pharisees, as appears from Matthew,
asked Jesus, “Is it lawful to heal on the Sabbath-days (”
putting the question in the most general way, as including
all the attendance and Jabour connected with the healing
art, that they might have the most plausible ground for
accusing him. No doubt, there are often many things done
in reference to the sick on the Sabbath, which, instead of
being (as they sometimes are, without any good reason) it-
tentionally fixed for the Lord’s-day, had better be deferred
to the day following, or attended to the day before: but
generally speaking, the lawfulness, nay, incumbency, of
healing or endeavouring to heal the sick on the Sabbath, is
unquestionable. Even where much attention and labout
are necessary for this purpose, they ought to be bestowed:
and therefore, it was abundantly evident that there could
be nothing improper, but that there was every thing com-
mendable, in healing on the Sabbath, where hardly any time
or labour was to be required at all—nothing but the speaking
of a word.
