*Compel them to come in.” Not that any bodily, exter-
nal violence is to be used, in order to make men Christians:
the expression does not necessarily imply any such idea,*
and the attempt would be wicked and absurd. The weapons
of our warfare are not carnal. But, the meaning is, that
ministers are perseveringly to employ all scriptural kinds
of representations and pleas, which, under the influence of
the Holy Spirit, are calculated so to inform men’s under-
standings and affect their hearts, as that they may accept
the invitation in the gospel. Thus it is, then, that the
servant wishes to compel, to importune, to press you all to
come in, at present, and would take no denial. Nor need
he go beyond this parable itself for urgent pleas wherewith
to press the invitation.

Be entreated to come in by the consideration of your
naturally miserable and perishing condition. Your state is
much worse than that of the poorest and most destitute
inhabitant of any of our lanes, or beggar on any of our
highways; that is, looking merely:to their temporal poverty
and destitution. Will you not, then, hungry and famishi
as you are, gladly come for spiritual food and refreshment
