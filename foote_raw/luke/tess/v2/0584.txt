There is another kind of death, however, little regarded,
alas! but far more terrible—a death, not of the body, but
of the soul—a death which darkens the light, not of the
bodily eye, but of the understanding—a death which
benumbs, not the hands and the feet, but the moral facul-
ties—a death which separates the soul, not from the body,
but from God. This is often called spiritual death ; it is a
being “dead in trespasses and sins.” This death is most
fearful, as it ends, if grace prevent not, in another kind of
death still—eternal death, endless misery, called, in Scrip-
ture, “ The second death.” This is death indeed—death to
soul and body; not the ceasing to exist, but death for ever
toall that is desirable—death to the possibility of ever bei
