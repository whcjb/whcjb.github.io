to eat, it must have been at least disrespectful to the law to
own, or to herd, This circumstance, then, appears to have
been fixed on by our Lord, as suggesting to the Jewish mind
the idea of the deepest worthlessness and degradation.
Poor and despicable as the employment was in itself, it
was peculiarly wretched in this man’s case, because of the
total inadequacy of the allowance made to him for his
labour. It did not afford him enough of the cheapest food
of man, so that, in the cravings of his hunger, he had a
