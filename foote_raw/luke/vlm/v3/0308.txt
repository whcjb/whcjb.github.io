The historian then goes on to relate all the particulars of a fact, for the truth of which he declares he had innumerable witnesses, namely, that of a woman of eminent rank and wealth, whose name and descent he gives, who, having been stripped and plundered of all her goods and provisions, in hunger, rage, and despair, killed and roasted her babe at her breast, and had eaten part of him before the horrid deed was discovered. The report of this miserable atrocity filled the city with horror and trembling; "those that were distressed by the famine were very desirous to die; and those already dead were esteemed happy, because they had not lived long enough either to hear or to see such miseries."
