About this text. Greenhill delivered this exposition as weekly lectures in London and published it in five volumes between 1645 and 1662. The text here is the keyed transcription of the first editions made by the Text Creation Partnership from the EEBO microfilms (Vol. 1 (ch. 1–5, 1645): A85666; Vol. 2 (ch. 6–13, 1649): A85667; Vol. 3 (ch. 14–19, 1651): A42015; Vol. 4 (ch. 20–29, 1658): A85668; Vol. 5 (ch. 30–48, 1662): A42016), released under CC0. Original spelling and italics are retained; the long s is printed as s. Page numbers of the original are kept as in-page anchors; each volume is paged from 1. The commentary is divided here into passages of a few verses each, following the sections of the original where the transcription marks them; the fourth volume (chapters 20–29), which has no such divisions in the transcription, is divided at the verses it quotes. The marginal heads of the original (Note 1, Observ. 1 …) are shown as small tags. The errata lists, the tables of matters and of Scripture places, and the booksellers’ advertisements are not included.

Illegible places. The transcription is incomplete at 3,286 places: letters or words the keyers could not read from the film images, and the Greek and Hebrew, which they did not transcribe. They have been filled as follows.

(1) Read from the film. Each place was checked against the EEBO film images of the same editions (Internet Archive): 1,824 could be read there and are printed as plain text.

(2) Checked against a second copy. At 199 places the film itself is blotted or show-through covers the letters, and only a conjecture was possible. The film, and the TCP keyers who worked from it, are one and the same copy, so each conjecture was checked against a copy printed from a different original: the Edinburgh reprint of 1863 (J. Nichol, ed. James Sherman; Internet Archive anexpositionofth00greeuoft). 147 places are filled from that check and shown in [brackets]: 137 where the reprint has the same word as the conjecture (the original spelling is kept); 6 where the reprint differs and is followed (chapter and verse numbers restored to the original style); 4 where the reprint drops the passage but its English rendering or verse range confirms the conjecture. At 9 places a second look at the film settled the reading where the reprint had altered it; there the film is followed, including an evident misprint of the original (fifteen thousand miles), which is kept. The reprint modernises spelling, so it confirms the word, not the original spelling.

Greek and Hebrew. The keyers left the Greek and Hebrew blank. In Volumes III–V they were read from the film. Volume I has no film of the 1645 first edition, so 182 places were read from the film of the second edition (1649) at the same place. Volume II has no legible film, and there and wherever the film is unreadable 236 places were supplied from the 1863 reprint, page by page; these too are in brackets. A marginal note that consisted only of an unreadable Greek word is not shown as a footnote.

Volumes I–II, partly legible words. Where the keyers left part of an English or Latin word with one or two letters illegible, and the 1863 reprint has at the same place a word that matches the surviving letters exactly (and that word occurs elsewhere in the book), the missing letters are supplied from the reprint: 361 places, in brackets. The original spelling of the surviving letters is kept.

(3) Still open. The remaining places stay in red. They include 33 where the reprint omits the Latin, Greek or marginal note altogether (every one checked on the reprint’s page images), 1 where the film and the reprint disagree, and 118 Greek or Hebrew words (93 of them in Volume II) that neither the film nor the reprint supplies: Sherman generally dropped the Greek and Hebrew and kept only the English rendering. Only another copy of the first editions of Volumes I–II could settle them.
