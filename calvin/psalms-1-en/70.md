<p class="title-block-h1" style="text-align:center; font-size:22px; font-weight:bold; margin:18px 0 12px;" markdown="1"><span style="color:#0000d4">PSALM 70</span></p>

This psalm is merely a part of the fortieth, and the inscription, *To call to remembrance,* is perhaps designed to indicate this; David having taken these five verses out of that other psalm, and accommodated them for being used on some particular occasion. I shall only here repeat the words of the text; and would refer the reader for the interpretation to the proper place.

<p style="text-align:center; color:#000080; margin:14px 2em;" markdown="1"><span style="color:#000080">*To the chief musician of David, to call to remembrance.*</span></p>

<h2 class="scripture-anchor" id="psalm-70-1-5" data-ref="PSALM 70:1-5" style="display:none">PSALM 70:1-5</h2>

<div class="scripture-box" markdown="1">
<p class="scripture-ref"><span class="ages-code">&lt;197001&gt;</span><span class="book-name">Psalm</span> <span class="verse-range">70:1-5</span></p>

<strong>1.</strong> O God! make haste to deliver me: O Jehovah! hasten to my help. <strong>2.</strong> Let those who seek my life be ashamed and confounded; let those who desire my hurt be turned backward, and put to confusion <strong>3.</strong> Let those who say to me, Aha! aha! perish as a reward of their shame. <strong>4.</strong> Let all those who seek thee rejoice and exult in thee: let those who love thy salvation say, Let God be magnified for evermore! <strong>5.</strong> As for me, I am poor and needy: O God! hasten to me: thou art my help and my deliverer: O Jehovah! make no delay.

</div>
