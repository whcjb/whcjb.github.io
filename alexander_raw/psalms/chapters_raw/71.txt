A sufferer from the spite of wicked enemies prays for deliverance, vs. 1—3. He acknowledges God's goodness to him in early life, vs. 4—8, and prays that it may be continued in old age, vs. 9—13. He confidently anticipates an answer to his prayers, vs. 14—21, and promises a suitable return of praise, vs. 22—24.

The psalm bears a strong resemblance to the others in which the sufferings of the righteous are the great theme, such as the twenty-second, thirty-fifth, thirty-eighth, and fortieth, a portion of which last seems to have been prefixed to it, as a kind of text or theme, or for the purpose of connecting it with the whole class of compositions just referred to. This explains the absence cf a title or inscription in the psalm before us, as in the case of the second, tenth, forty-third, and others.
