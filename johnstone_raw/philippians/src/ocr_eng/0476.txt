The mss. are divided also between παραβουλευσάμενος and
παραβολευσάμενος, but with a great preponderance of authority
in favour of the latter. The verb παραβουλεύεσθαι has the
sense of the Latin male consulere, ‘to make poor provision for,’
‘have little regard for,—rapa here, as in many compounds,
having the force of ‘amiss’—strictly, ‘going aside or beyond,’
missing the mark. The other verb παραβολεύεσθαι does not
occur elsewhere, but is a form which—in the same way as
περπερεύεσθαι (1 Cor. xiii. 4), from πέρπερος, and others—may
be derived from the adjective παράβολος, ‘gambling, reckless.’
Παραβολεύεσθαι, then, will mean ‘to play the gambler,—7r7
ψυχῇ, ‘with his life. ‘Hazard’ excellently represents the
thought, the original meaning of this word (which is probably
derived from the Latin /essera, ‘a die,’ through the Italian
azzardo, a corruption of a-tsar, for tessar,-do) being ‘a game of
chance.’ Obviously, as used by the apostle, παραβολεύεσθαι
has nothing of blame in it, but simply sets forth, with much
liveliness, the utter lack of care for himself which Epaphroditus
had shown in his zeal to serve Christ by ministering to His
servant.
