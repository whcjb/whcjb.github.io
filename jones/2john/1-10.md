HItherto he hath wished her to avoyd their errors; now hee doth advise her to eschew their companie. 1. *Non participando in facto,* not participating with them in deeds. 2. *Non participando in verbo,* not participating with them in words *verse* 11.

*If any come unto you* they be shamelesse fellowes, they will not tarry till they be sent for, they will come of their owne accord, and intrude themselves.

What any without exception? nay, *and bring not this doctrine,* namely of Christ, but the contrary rather.

*Receive him not into your house,* why? this seemes too great incivility, they might receive him, and when they perceived that by him, turne him out againe: I but, *Turpiùs eiicitur, quam non admittitur hospes,* ye shall have more adoe to eject him, than to keepe him out at the first, shut up the doores of your house against him. It seemes, that as *Gajus* was the Churches Host: so this Lady was the Churches Hostesse, her house was open to the Preachers and Professors of the Gospell, but he warneth her not to receive corrupt teachers. 1. It is a thing displeasing to God, to give entertainment to his enemies. *Iehu* the seer reproved King *Iehoshaphat* for joyning with *Achab: Wouldest thou helpe the wicked, and love them that hate the Lord?* <span class="jw-margin" markdown="1">2 Paral. 19.2.</span> God gave us our houses, they must bee for his friends and not for his enemies.

<span class="jw-pb" id="p679" data-p="679" data-facs="345" title="Page 679 of the 1635 edition begins here (原书第 679 页起)"></span>2. The godly will be grieved at it, and shall we grieve them, <span class="jw-margin" markdown="1">1 Thes. 5.22.</span> for whom especially Christ died? 3. It may bring a bad report of our selves, that we like of them, and approve them, whereas we ought *to abstaine from all appearance of evill, and provide things honest before God and men.* 4. It may indanger our owne soules. <span class="jw-margin" markdown="1">2 Cor. 8.21. 2. Tim. 2.17.</span> *For their word fretteth as a canker,* It may over-run us and infect us, ere we be aware. 5. It may encourage them in their wickednesse. 6. It may pull Gods wrath on us and our houses; God blessed the house of *Potiphar* for *Iosephs* sake, <span class="jw-margin" markdown="1">Gen. 39.5. 2 Reg. 4 17.</span> and the house of the *Shunamitish* woman for *Elishaes* sake. His curse will light on those houses where the adversaries of his Gospel are harboured. When Saint *Iohn* heard, that *Cerinthus* the Heretique was in the bathe, saith he, <span class="jw-conj" title="Editorial conjecture: not certain in the original (1635, checked against the 1636 issue); see the table on this book's front page (编者推补；非原书确定文字，依据见本卷首页)">[φύγωμεν, μὴ συμπέσῃ τὸ βαλανεῖον]</span>. Let us flie least the Bathe fall on our heads? Yet what a number be there, <span class="jw-margin" markdown="1">*Euseb Eccles. Hist. l.* 4. *c.* 14.</span> whose houses are receptacles for Seminaries, Priests, and Iesuites, and other Sectaries? *Ahabs* house was for *Baals* Priests: But *Constantines* Pallace was for godly Bishops and Ministers, <span class="jw-margin" markdown="1">*Euseb. de vita Constant. l.* 4.</span> and hee thought their prayers to be the pillars of his house, and indeede they were.

Then for participation in words. Where 1. The prohibition. 2. The reason of it, *verse* 11. *Neither bid him God speed.* Be so farre in shewing him any kindnesse in deeds, as vouchsafe him not a kind word or greeting. The Grecians used two words in their salutations. χαίρειν & εὐπράττειν. Rejoyce and doe well, *celse gaudere, & benè rem gerere, Albinovano,* as the Poet said.

Here is χαίρειν. Wish him no joy, no comfort, vouchsafe him not a good word, or familiar speech, shew no token of familiarity to him.
