# 希伯来书 11:36

<div class="owen-vg-sub">约翰·欧文注释 · Ver. 36</div>

<div class="owen-scr" markdown="0"><p class="owen-scr-en">In the next place we have the example of them who suffered also, but not by tortures, nor unto death, yet in such ways as were a great trial of their faith.</p><p class="owen-scr-grk">Ἓτεροι δὲ ἐμπαιγμῶν καὶ μαστίγων πεῖραν ἔλαβον, ἔτι δὲ δεσμῶν καὶ φυλακῆς.</p><p class="owen-scr-en">Others had trial of [had experience of, or were tried by,] [cruel] mockings and scourgings, yea, moreover of bonds and imprisonment.</p></div>

<p class="owen-d0">The Syriac makes here two distinct sorts, repeating אַחֲרָנֵא, &quot;alii,&quot; &quot;others,&quot; after πεῖραν ἔλαβον: as in the next verse it repeats the same word four times, which is not once in the original. Πεῖραν ἔλαβον it renders by עַלוּ, &quot;they exposed themselves to mocking and stripes.&quot;</p>

<p class="owen-d0">Ἕτεροι. Δέ.</p>

<p class="owen-d0 owen-mk"><b class="owen-num">1.</b> Those spoken of are said to be ἔτεροι, not merely ἄλλοι; not only &quot;others,&quot; but &quot;of another sort,&quot; namely, such as suffered through faith, but not by tortures, nor unto death. And the exceptive particle δέ intimates the introduction of another kind of sufferings.</p>

<p class="owen-d0 owen-mk"><b class="owen-num">2.</b> It is of no use to fix the particulars mentioned unto certain determinate persons, as Jeremiah or others; for seeing the apostle hath left that undetermined, so may we do also. Certain it is, that there were in those days believers who, through faith, patiently and victoriously underwent these things.</p>

<p class="owen-d0">There are four things mentioned distinctly under this head:</p>

<p class="owen-d0 owen-mk"><b class="owen-num">1.</b> &quot;Mockings.&quot;</p>

<p class="owen-d0 owen-mk"><b class="owen-num">2.</b> &quot;Scourgings.&quot;</p>

<p class="owen-d0 owen-mk"><b class="owen-num">3.</b> &quot;Bonds.&quot;</p>

<p class="owen-d0 owen-mk"><b class="owen-num">4.</b> The &quot;prison,&quot; or &quot;imprisonment.&quot; And they contain all the outward ways of the sufferings of the church, when God restrains the rage of the world, so as that it shall not rise to blood and death.</p>

<p class="owen-d0">So it often falls out. It is the utter destruction of the church that Satan and the world do always aim at; but ofttimes there are such bounds set unto their rage, by the division of their own counsels, by their supposed interests, by the more gentle inclinations of some Gamaliels among them, or for want of a pretence to execute the utmost of bloody cruelty, that they take up in mockings, stripes, imprisonments, spoiling of goods, and the like.</p>

<p class="owen-d0">Πεῖραν ἔλαζον. Of these things it is said they &quot;had trial.&quot; &quot;Experti sunt,&quot; they had experience of them, they really underwent them; and so, by consequent, their faith was tried with them.</p>

<p class="owen-d0">Ἐμταγμῶν. And the first thing mentioned is, as we render it, &quot;cruel mockings.&quot; Ἐμταίζομαι is the word constantly used for the mockings that were cast on our Lord Jesus Christ himself, Matt. 20:19, 27:29, 31, 41; Mark 10:34, 15:31; Luke 14:29, 18:32, 22:63, 23:11, 36. Neither is the verb in either voice, active or passive, used in the New Testament, but only as applied to Christ. And it is joined with μαστιγόω, to &quot;scourge,&quot; as it is here with &quot;stripes.&quot; Ἐμπαιγμός, nowhere used but here, is &quot;ludibrium,&quot; a &quot;mocking with reproach and contumely or scorn.&quot; Hence we have rendered it &quot;cruel mockings.&quot; They reproached them with their God, with their religion, with folly, with feigned crimes. Such mockings are recorded in all the stories of the persecutions and sufferings of the church. The world is never more witty, nor doth more please itself, than when it can invent reproachful names, terms, and crimes, to cast upon suffering believers. And whereas the word is derived from παίζω, (as that is from παῖς,) &quot;to play and mock childishly,&quot; it may respect the calumnious reproaches that ofttimes in the streets are cast on suffering professors, by the rude, foolish multitude, like the children that ran after Elisha, mocking and scoffing at him.</p>

<p class="owen-d0">And this is reckoned among severe sufferings, there being nothing more harsh to ingenuous minds, nor any thing almost which they had not as willingly undergo. Nor is there any thing that their adversaries inflict on them with more self-pleasing and exultation of mind. Mockings are persecutors&#x27; triumphs. But these also faith will conflict withal and conquer: it hath done so in all ages. And it is a fruit of faith which we ought to aim at, namely, to keep our spirits composed, unto a contempt of shame under the most severe and scornful mockings.</p>

<p class="owen-d0">Μαστίγων. Unto these sometimes &quot;stripes&quot; are added;—a servile punishment, used towards vagabonds and the vilest of men.</p>

<p class="owen-d0">Of the last two ways of trial, namely, &quot;bonds and imprisonment,&quot; we have had so full an exposition in the days wherein we live, that they need no further explication. And,—</p>

<p class="owen-d0">Obs. There may be sufferings sufficient for the trial of the faith of the church, when the world is restrained from blood and death.—But how long at present it will be so, God only knows.</p>
