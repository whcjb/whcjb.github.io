### <span class="cw-pb" id="p120" data-p="120" data-facs="67" title="Page 120 of the 1613 edition begins here (原书第 120 页起)"></span>ZAIN.

<div class="cw-text" markdown="1">

VER. 49.

Remember the promise (made) to thy seruant; wherein thou hast caused mee to trust.

</div>

<span class="cw-dropcap">T</span>He first verse of this section containes a prayer, that God would perform the promises hee had made vnto him When hee prayes God to remember, <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">How God is said to remember.</span> vvee must not thinke that he chargeth the Lord with any obliuion. *Nulla in Deum cadit aut obliuio,* <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Euthym.</span> *aut reminiscentia.* The godly sanctifie the Lord in their hart, and think more reuerently of his Maiestie then so: but his *Memor esto,* is all one with this; *Perfice et ad finem deducito:* Remember, that is, perfit and accomplish, the promise made vnto me.

*Made to thy seruant.*] As *Dauid* beseecheth the <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Gods promise made to vs, and our promise made to him, would be remembred together.</span> Lord to remember his promise; so hee protests verse 52. that hee remembred the iudgements of GOD, and vvas comforted: and verse 55. that hee remembred the name of the Lord in the night. It is but a moekerie of GOD, to desire him remember his promise made, where vvee make no conscience of the promises wee haue <span class="cw-pb" id="p121" data-p="121" data-facs="67" title="Page 121 of the 1613 edition begins here (原书第 121 页起)"></span> made to him. But alas, how often faile we in this duety, and so in our owne default diminish that comfort we might haue of Gods promises, in the day of our trouble? <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Dauid had a promise of an earthly Kingdome, but his comfort was not in it.</span>

But what promise is this that God made to *Dauid?* There is a promise made him concerning the Kingdome of Israel; and for this he prayed, 2. *Sam.* 7. *Who am I, O Lord God, and what is mine house, that thou hast brought me hitherto, &c.?* <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">2. Sam. 7. 18, 25, 28.</span> *And now Lord, confirme for euer the word that thou hast spoken concerning thy seruant, &c. For thou art God, and thy wordes are true, and thou hast spoken this goodnesse concerning thy seruant.* But his comfort in trouble stood not in this promise, nor performance of it.

The promise whereof he meanes here, is the <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">In the promise of mercy stands the comfort of the godly.</span> promise of mercy and grace, which God in his couenant hath made to his children: and this, as euery one of Gods children accounts their own by a particular application of it, vnto themselues; so *Dauid* not excluding others, and yet including himselfe, speakes of this promise, as properly and particularly belonging to him. And so also did <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">In the generall promise, there is included a particular to euery beleeuer.</span> that woman of Canaan rest in the generall promise of Gods mercie, as if it did containe a particular promise to her selfe. And therefore by no temptation; yea, not by Christs contrary affirmation, made for her tryal, could she be remoued from this ground, That the generall promise of mercy, made in the Couenant, contained a particular <span class="cw-pb" id="p122" data-p="122" data-facs="68" title="Page 122 of the 1613 edition begins here (原书第 122 页起)"></span> promise vnto her; howsoeuer not vnder the name of a childe, yet vnder the name of one (albeit vnworthy) belonging to the family of God.

And this proceedes from the nature of faith, <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">The nature of faith.</span> by which the godly apprehend God as their Father, Christ as their Sauiour; and in him all Gods promises made generally to his people<span class="cw-gap" title="TCP: illegible in source（底本此处读不出）">▪</span> as if they were made particularly and by name vnto themselues, <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">How faith collects out of Gods promises assurance of saluation.</span> collecting hereof the certainty of their owne saluation. The proposition being furnished by the word, the assumption is made by faith, and makes the conclusion most ce<span class="cw-gap" title="TCP: illegible in source（底本此处读不出）">•</span>taine; the ground of all being Gods vnchangeable truth, and the stability of his counsell<span class="cw-gap" title="TCP: illegible in source（底本此处读不出）">▪</span> which he will not change for the changes which are in his weake seruants, but constantly performes for his owne names sake. *Sanctis quodeun que spoponderit implere* <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Ambrose</span> *consueuit, nostrarū immemor iniquitatum, suarum non immemor sponsionum:* the Lord perfits that which hee promiseth to his Saints; vnmindfull of their iniquities, not vnmindful of his owne promises.

*Wherein thou hast caused me to trust.*] This is <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">It stands with the honour of Gods truth to performe his promise.</span> a most forcible argument to moue the Lord to the performance of his promise, When his children declare vnto him, that it stands with the honour of his truth to doe <span class="cw-gap" title="TCP: illegible in source（底本此处读不出）">•</span>o. The like of this argument is vsed by *Ieremie: O Lord, i<span class="cw-gap" title="TCP: illegible in source（底本此处读不出）">•</span> <span class="cw-gap" title="TCP: illegible in source（底本此处读不出）">•</span>e deceiued, thou hast deceiued me;* which is, as he would say a thing impossible that so should be. And so <span class="cw-pb" id="p123" data-p="123" data-facs="68" title="Page 123 of the 1613 edition begins here (原书第 123 页起)"></span> *Dauid* confirmes himselfe here; that he could not be disappointed of his hope, vnless that the Lord did faile in his truth: which cannot be.

Thus ye see, when the godly rest assured of <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Assurance of saluation in the beleeuers, is no arrogancie.</span> their saluation, they are neyther presumptuous nor arrogant, but giue glory to God, and the praise of vndoubted truth to his promises. *Non* <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Ambr.</span> *est arr<span class="cw-gap" title="TCP: illegible in source（底本此处读不出）">•</span>gans vsurpatio; sed fidelis, quia verū Deum non posse fallere confitetur;* It is no arrogant vsurpation, but a faithfull depending: for hee acknowledgeth, that the most true God cannot beguile him. And againe, *Non vsurpatoriè speraui quae vt sperarem ipse fecisti:* I haue not hoped by vsurpation, bu<span class="cw-gap" title="TCP: illegible in source（底本此处读不出）">•</span> it was thou Lord, who caused me to hope. *Seruus sum, expecto alimentum à Domino:* <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">☜</span> *miles<span class="cw-gap" title="TCP: illegible in source（底本此处读不出）">▪</span> sum, exigo ab imperatore stipendium: vocatus sum, postulo ab invitante promissum:* I am thy seruant, and wait for nourishment from thee, who art my Lord: I am thy souldier, and require wages from thee, who art my Captaine: I am called, and seeke from thee who called me, that which thou hast promised.

Let this serue for an answer to the aduersaries <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">An answer to <span class="cw-gap" title="TCP: illegible in source（底本此处读不出）">〈◊〉</span> , who so speake of it.</span> of the truth, who say it is arrogance & presumption, for any man to affirme the certainty of his saluation: *Propter hoo da<span class="cw-gap" title="TCP: illegible in source（底本此处读不出）">•</span>sunt digna quaedam &* <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Ber<span class="cw-gap" title="TCP: illegible in source（底本此处读不出）">•</span> in Septuagesi<span class="cw-gap" title="TCP: illegible in source（底本此处读不出）">•</span>. Ser. 1.</span> *indicia manif<span class="cw-gap" title="TCP: illegible in source（底本此处读不出）">•</span>sta salutis, vt indubitabile sit eum esse de numero electorum in quo ea signa permanserint;* For this cause some signes and manifest tokens of saluation are giuen, to the end it may be <span class="cw-pb" id="p124" data-p="124" data-facs="69" title="Page 124 of the 1613 edition begins here (原书第 124 页起)"></span> out of doubt, that hee in whom they remaine, is of the number of the elect. *Non arrogantia est,* <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Aug. Ser. 28.</span> *sed fides, praedicare quod acceperis:* It is not arrogancy, but faith, to speake of that mercie which thou hast receiued.

To this purpose, let vs remember, that the <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Arguments to confirme vs in the certainty of our saluation.</span> promises made to vs, are of Gods free mercie: that the grace to beleeue, which is the condition of the promse, is also of himselfe; *For faith is the gift of God:* thirdly, that the arguments by which he confirmes our faith in the certainty of our saluation, are drawne from himselfe; not from vs. As first, from the nature of God: *He is faithfull* <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">1. Cor. 1.</span> *who hath called vs to the fellowship of Christ, and will also keepe vs blamelesse to the day of Christ.* And againe, *He who hath begun that good worke* <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Phil. 2.</span> *in you, will also perfite it against the day of Christ.*

Secondly, from the nature of that life which <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Christians are sure of perseuerance; proued by foure reasons.</span> Christ communicates to his members: they liue in him, or rather he liues in them; *Now in that I liue, by the faith of Christ I liue: yet not I any more, but Christ liueth in me.* Now this is the nature <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Gal. 2.</span> of the life of Christ; that *it is no more subiect to* <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Rom. 6.</span> *dying,* as witnesseth the Apostle.

Thirdly, from the nature of that seede, whereof we are begotten: for as the seed is, so is the life which it giueth. Now the seed, <span class="cw-gap" title="TCP: illegible in source（底本此处读不出）">•</span>aith Saint *Peter,* <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">1. Pet. 1.</span> is immortall: and beside, that holy vnction, by which the Lord corroborates and strengthens <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">1. Ioh. 2.</span> his children, is called a permanent and abiding <span class="cw-pb" id="p125" data-p="125" data-facs="69" title="Page 125 of the 1613 edition begins here (原书第 125 页起)"></span> vnction. All these proue a perseuerance of Gods fauour.

VER. 50.

It is my comfort in trouble: for thy promise hath quickned me.

</div>

THis Verse containes a commendation of <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Commendation of Gods word.</span> the word of God, from the excellent effects and fruits thereof; to wit, that the comfort thereof vpholds Gods children in trouble, when all other comforts faile them. The lawfull comforts of nature, such as meat, and drinke, & recreation, becom loath some to men in heauy diseases. And as to the vnlawfull pleasures of sin, they are so farre from helping our comfort, that they encrease our terrour in the day of trouble; only the word of the Lord sustains vs, and assures <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1"><span class="cw-gap" title="TCP: illegible in source（底本此处读不出）">•</span>t will vphold vs when all other comfort will faile.</span> vs, that our light & momentanean afflictions shal cause to vs at the length an infinite weight of glorie: *Facilè superant<span class="cw-gap" title="TCP: illegible in source（底本此处读不出）">•</span> aduersa, si sit spes quae consoletur:* <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Ambrose.</span> *quisquis enim meliora sperat, leuioribus non frangitur.*

*In trouble.*] He telleth not what kind of trouble <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">If we make no conscience of Gods word in prosperity, it shall not comfort vs madue<span class="cw-gap" title="TCP: illegible in source（底本此处读不出）">•</span>sity.</span> he was in; neither was it needfull: for there is no sort of tentation can befal vs<span class="cw-gap" title="TCP: illegible in source（底本此处读不出）">▪</span> for w*ch* the word of God doth not furnish vnto vs sufficient consolation: Prouiding alway we make conscience of it in prosperity; otherwise, it shall be a comfortlesse word to vs in our aduersity.

<span class="cw-pb" id="p126" data-p="126" data-facs="70" title="Page 126 of the 1613 edition begins here (原书第 126 页起)"></span> *For thy promise hath quickned mee.*] As the <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">The Word of God is the life of our soule.</span> life of our soules, at the first commeth by the Word; so is it conserued by the same Word. When tentations oppresse vs, so that we faint, and our life is gone, it is the Word that reuiues vs: *Vt connexio ista animae, & corporis nostri, spiritu vitali animatur, alitur, & tenetur: ita verbo Dei, & spiritali gratia anima nostra viuifi<span class="cw-gap" title="TCP: illegible in source（底本此处读不出）">•</span>atur.* As the connexion of this bodie and soule is made, and conserued by naturall breath; so is our soule quickned by the Word and spirituall grace. As therefore we loue our life, let vs loue the word of the Lord. <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">If such fruit be in his promise, what is in the performance?</span>

And if the promises of God render such ioy to the beleeuing, what ioy may we looke for in the full performance of them?

VER. 51.

The proud haue had me excedingly in derision: yet haue I not declined from thy law.

</div>

HEere is a protestatiō of *Dauid* his honest <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Mockeries and tauntings of euill men, is a part of Christs crosse.</span> and constant affection toward the word of God; That albeit he was sore tempted by the derisions of the proud, yet he declined not from the Lawe of the Lord his God. Let no man who is truely religious, thinke that he can eschue the mockeries and derisions of the wicked: this is a part of the crosse of Christ Iesus; they railed vpon him, they nodded with their head, and fleered with their mouthes, and <span class="cw-pb" id="p127" data-p="127" data-facs="70" title="Page 127 of the 1613 edition begins here (原书第 127 页起)"></span> mocked him: but patiently did he suffer this contradiction of sinners.

*Ismael* was the first, for any thing we reade, <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Ismael first began this kind of persecution.</span> who beganne this kinde of persecution: but euer since, Satan hath continued it in his cursed instruments, like vnto him. *As he that was borne after* <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Gal. 4.</span> *the flesh, persecuted him that was borne after the Spirit; euen so is it now.* Let vs take vp the crosse of our Lord, and follow him, and be content to beare with him the rebuke of the wicked.

Concerning this stile of the wicked, whereby they are called proude, see ver. 69. 78.

VER. 52.

I remembred thy iudgements of olde, O Lord, and haue beene comforted.

</div>

MAny are the impediments of godliness: <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Godlines hath many impediments.</span> for beside the corruption of our owne nature within vs, we haue the corruption of other men fighting against vs. By their profane speeches they corrupt vs; by their euill example they infect vs: and if that way they cannot come to speede, then they speake euill of vs, they lye, they rai<span class="cw-gap" title="TCP: illegible in source（底本此处读不出）">•</span>e, they mocke vs, leauing nothing vndone that may disquiet vs in our selues, and disgrace vs to others.

Against this impediment, *Dauid* here shewes how he was sustained by remembring the iudgements <span class="cw-pb" id="p128" data-p="128" data-facs="71" title="Page 128 of the 1613 edition begins here (原书第 128 页起)"></span> of God, which in former ages he had poured <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">If we remember time begun, forethinke time to come, the tentations of the time present shall not readily ouercome vs.</span> out vpon the like profane men. If wee shall onely looke to the present time, forgetting the by past iudgements of God, & not fore-thinking of the wrath to com vpon the wicked; their present prosperity shall be witch us, and we shall be in danger to be carried away with them, after deceitfull vanity: but let vs go with *Dauid* vnto the Sanctuary of God, and with *Abaeue* to our Watch-towre, where we may looke out, and see what hath bin the miserable ends of the wicked: and we shall say, they buy full deare their short and perishing pleasures; for their prosperity is <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Prou. 1.</span> their ruine.

<div class="cw-text" markdown="1">

VER. 53.

Feare is come vpon me, for the wicked that for sake thy lawe.

</div>

LEast it might seeme out of the former <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Godly men pitie the wicked.</span> words, that *Dauids* comfort stood in the destruction of the wicked by the execution of Gods iust iudgements vpon them; he addes this, that their impiety was the cause of his feare and griefe, and that he was *partim iratus quòd* <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Euthym.</span> *legem Dei contemnerent; partim dolens quòd ipsi perituriessent.* This is the meekenesse and loue which the godly carie, euen toward those who haue offended them; they are touched with a commiseration of them: not so much for any <span class="cw-pb" id="p129" data-p="129" data-facs="71" title="Page 129 of the 1613 edition begins here (原书第 129 页起)"></span> wrong done to themselues, as for the euill they <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Not so much for wrōg done to them, as for the euill that redounds to thē who doe it *Ambr.*</span> see their enemies incurre by wronging them. *Nam qui fortior est, non proprtam dolet contumeliam, sed aliena peccata; et in sua iniuria lapsum alterius ingemiscit.* As a louing father offends at the contumelious words hee receiues from his frantique child, not so much sorrowfull for the wrong done to himselfe, as for the disease of his <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">☜</span> child, which for<span class="cw-gap" title="TCP: illegible in source（底本此处读不出）">•</span>eth him to speake that vvhich he should not: so godly *Dauid; Dolebat, non quia contemnebatur, sed quia lex Dei relinquebatur, & eorum qui hoc faciebant damnū dolebat, quòd Deo perirent.* Hee was afraid and grieued at the sinnes of the wicked, who scorned him; not because he was contemned, but God was offended<span class="cw-gap" title="TCP: illegible in source（底本此处读不出）">▪</span> nor yet for any losse he suffered by their sinnes, but for the harme they did vnto themselues.

The impietie of wicked men, is here described <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">The weight of sin aggrauated by this, that it is a forsaking of Gods law.</span> to be a forsaking of Gods law; thereby letting vs know, what is the weight of sinne. The law is holy & good, contayning in it a most perfect rule of righteousnes; and therefore, the forsaking of it, cannot be but a very high crime. And sure it is, many of this age are guiltie of it, in regard of their deeds; how euer it be, that in regard of their words, they will not hold with it: but when God shall iudge thē, what euer liking of the law they pretend in word, they shall be found indeed forsakers of it.

<div class="cw-text" markdown="1">

<span class="cw-pb" id="p130" data-p="130" data-facs="72" title="Page 130 of the 1613 edition begins here (原书第 130 页起)"></span>VER. 54.

Thy statutes haue beene my songs, in the house of my pilgrimage.

</div>

HE still insists in a commendation of the <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">They who coūt Gods word a wearines, which Dauid accounted a refreshment, are in a hard estate.</span> word of God, frō the comforts which hee found in it in the time of trouble. Naturalists, refresh themselues in their griefes, with profane Ballades and Songs; but these increase guiltinesse, and consequently griefe, but mitigate it not. As to *Dauid,* hee protests hee sought his comfort in the word of God: worldlings count it a melancholique subiect; but he found ioy in it: let men in this take heede vnto themselues.

*Dauid* was a man after Gods owne heart; that is, approued of him: and they who count that to be a wearines, which hee found to be a refreshment, how can they haue this comfort, that they are also approued of God?

Againe, see how the Lord in his wise dispensation <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">The word is conuenient for euery state of life.</span> attempers himselfe to our infirmities. Our life is subiect to many changes: and God by his word hath prouided for vs also many instructions and remedies. Euery crosse hath his owne remedy, and euery state of life his own instruction. Sometime our griefe is so great that we cannot sing; then let vs pray: sometime our deliuerance so ioyfull, that wee must breake out in <span class="cw-pb" id="p131" data-p="131" data-facs="72" title="Page 131 of the 1613 edition begins here (原书第 131 页起)"></span> thanksgiuing; then let vs sing: *If any man among* <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">In it wee haue prayers for crosses, and psalmes for euery deliuerāce</span> *you bee afflicted, let him pray; if hee be merie, let him sing.* Prayers for euery crosse, and Psalmes for euerie deliuerance, hath GOD by his own Spirit penned vnto vs: so that now wee are more then inexcusable if wee faile in this dutie.

*In the house of my pilgrimage.] Vatablus* expounds <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Bodily banishment brings the godly neerer to the Lord, how euer it put them further from men.</span> this, of his banishment among the Philistims; that vvhen hee vvas put from his natiue Countrey and kinred, and all other comforts failed him, that then the word of the Lord furnished matter of ioy to him. And indeed, the banishment of Gods seruaunts, may cast them farre from their kinred and acquaintance: but it chaseth them neerer to the Lord, and the Lord neerer to them. Proofe of this in Iacob; when hee was banished, and lay without, all night in the fields, he found a more familiar presence of God, then he did when he lay in the Tent vvith father and mother.

But wee may rather with *Basil,* referre it to <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Our life vpon earth, is but a banishment.</span> the whole time of his mortall life: *Omnem vi<span class="cw-gap" title="TCP: illegible in source（底本此处读不出）">•</span>am suam peregrinationem vocare arbitror.* So *Iacob* acknowledged to *Pharao,* that his life was a pilgrimage: and *Abraham* and *Isaac* dwelt in the world as strangers.

S. *Peter* therfore teacheth vs, as Pilgrims, to abstaine from the lusts of the flesh; and S. *Paul,* to vse this world, as if we vsed it not: for the fashion <span class="cw-pb" id="p132" data-p="132" data-facs="73" title="Page 132 of the 1613 edition begins here (原书第 132 页起)"></span> thereof goeth away. Many waies are wee taught this lesson: but slowe are wee to learne it. Alas, what folly is this, that a man should desire. to <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">☞</span> dwell in the earth, when God calleth him to be a Citizen of heauen?

Yet great is the comfort wee haue of this, that <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">It were a sore punishment to dwell for euer in our bodies as they are.</span> the houses wherein we lodge vpon earth, are but houses of our pilgrimage. The faithfull Israelites, endured their bondage in Egypt the more patiently, because they knew they were to be deliuered from it. If the houses of our seruitude were eternall mansions, how lamentable were our condition? but God be thanked, they are but wayfaring cottages, and houses of our pilgrimage. Such a house was the womb of our mother: if we had beene enclosed there for euer, what burden had it been to her? what bondage to our selues? Such a house will be the graue: of the which wee <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">☞</span> must all say with *Iob, The graue shall be my house,* <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Iob. 18.</span> *and I shall make my bed in the darke:* if vvee were there to abide for euer, how comfortlesse vvere our estate? But GOD be praised, our mansion house is aboue; and the houses we exchange here on earth, are but the houses of our pilgrimage: & happy is he can so liue in the world, as esteeming himselfe in his owne house, in his owne bed, yea, in his owne body to be but a stranger, in respect of his absence from the Lord.

<div class="cw-text" markdown="1">

<span class="cw-pb" id="p133" data-p="133" data-facs="73" title="Page 133 of the 1613 edition begins here (原书第 133 页起)"></span>VER. 55.

I haue remembred thy name, O Lord, in the night; and haue kept thy law.

</div>

THis verse containes a new protestation of his honest affection toward the word <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Priuate exercises, are surest tryalls of true godliness, and why.</span> of God. Wherin, first let vs mark his sinceritie; he was religious not onely in publick, but in priuat: for priuate exercises are the surest tryalls of true religion. In the publique, oftentimes hypocrisie caries men to simulate that vvhich they are not; it is not so in the priuat: for then, either doth a man, if hee make no conscience of Gods worship, vtterly neglect it, because there is no eye of man to see him; or otherwise, if hee be indeede religious, euen in priuate hee presents his heart to GOD, seeking it to bee approoued by him: *For his prayse is not of men, but of* <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Rom. 2. 29.</span> *God.*

Againe, this argueth his feruencie in religion: <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">This age conuinced of coldnes in religion.</span> for as else-where he protests, that he loued the vvord more then his appoynted food; so heere hee protests, hee vvanted his nights rest, that hee might meditate in the vvord. But now, so farre is zeale decayed in professours, they will not vvant their superfluities, farre lesse their needfull refreshment, for loue of the vvord of GOD. <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Euery mans life declares if he remember God, or no.</span>

*And haue kept thy vvord.] Dauid* vvas not a naked professor of the vvord, but a practiser <span class="cw-pb" id="p134" data-p="134" data-facs="74" title="Page 134 of the 1613 edition begins here (原书第 134 页起)"></span> of it also: his life declared that hee remembred the Lord; *Fides probitas innocentiae memorem* <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Ambrose.</span> *probant.*
