### <span class="cw-pb" id="p90" data-p="90" data-facs="52" title="Page 90 of the 1613 edition begins here (原书第 90 页起)"></span>HE.

<div class="cw-text" markdown="1">

VER. 33.

Teach mee, O Lord, the way of thy statutes: and I will keepe it to the end.

</div>

<span class="cw-dropcap">T</span>HE life of man is compared by the Apostle, <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Our life is a race, & a restlesse battell.</span> to a race; wherein all must run that looke to obtaine the high prize of the calling of God: and so run, that all the way they must fight with sundry aduersaries, that stand vp against them. There must be no declyning, neither to the right hand, nor to the left: but as souldiers direct their way according to the commandement of their Imperator, <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Ambr.</span> *Non ipsi pro suo arbitrio viam carpunt, nec voluntaria* <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">In it we should follow the direction of our Captaine.</span> *captant compendia, ne à signis recedant:* so must it be with Christians; we should stedfastlie looke to IESVS, the authour and finisher of our <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Heb. 12.</span> faith: who for the ioy set before him, despised the Crosse, and endured shame: let vs remember, our life should be a following of him. As Israel, in the vvildernesse, remooued, or remained according as they were directed by the clowde in the day, and piller of fire in the night: so should all the vvaies of our life, be ordered by the direction <span class="cw-pb" id="p91" data-p="91" data-facs="52" title="Page 91 of the 1613 edition begins here (原书第 91 页起)"></span> of God. Otherwise, as the vvarriour, *qui praescripto* <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">The losse of such as walke not, and sight not by direction of their Lord.</span> *non incedit ordine, armatus non ambulat, rectaque via iter non conficit, mansionem paratam non invenit;* who walkes not the way prescribed to him, and goes not into it armed to resist the enemy, comes not to that mansion prepared by the Imperator for his followers, to enioy such furniture, as he hath caused to prouide for them: so shall it be with the straggling Christian, vvhose vvay is a declining from Christ; he cannot come to those mansions, which hee hath prouided for his followers, in his Fathers house; nor be resreshed with his delicates. *Dauid* knew this very well: and therefore is he so inquisitiue to knowe the way of his Commaunder, and earnest to seek grace, that he may walke in it.

*Dauid,* as wee said before, was a Prophet, indued <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">The wisedom of Gods book can neuer bee sufficiently learned.</span> vvith great vnderstanding; yet doth hee earnestlie desire to be further taught of God: for the treasures of manifold wisedome locked vp in the word, are so rich, that no man can attaine to such measure of knowledge, but still hee hath neede to learne more. The Angels, vvho are full of eyes vvithin & vvithout, so figured for the greatnesse of their vnderstanding (for which also <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Nazian.</span> by *Nazianzen,* they are called, *Secundaria lumina*) <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Angels are scholars in the schoole of the Church & shal we think euill of it?</span> are scholars in the schoole of the Church: for those things which are preached to vs in the Gospel by the holy Ghost, sent down frō heauen, *The Angels desire to be hold;* and shal we not delight to <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">1. Peter 1.</span> <span class="cw-pb" id="p92" data-p="92" data-facs="53" title="Page 92 of the 1613 edition begins here (原书第 92 页起)"></span> learne with thē, seeing for our saluation (whereof <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">☞</span> they are surealready) these things are preached?

*And I will keepe it vnto the end.*] Here is a promise <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">If God continue our teacher, we shall continue his seruants.</span> of thankfulness, not in word, but in deede. *<span class="cw-gap" title="TCP: illegible in source（底本此处读不出）">〈◊〉</span> lege<span class="cw-gap" title="TCP: illegible in source（底本此处读不出）">•</span> tu<span class="cw-gap" title="TCP: illegible in source（底本此处读不出）">•</span>m ser<span class="cw-gap" title="TCP: illegible in source（底本此处读不出）">•</span>are studui prosequar haue cursum, neque fatigab<span class="cw-gap" title="TCP: illegible in source（底本此处读不出）">•</span>r in medio itinere* <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Vatab.</span> *vbi tu perrexer is me d<span class="cw-gap" title="TCP: illegible in source（底本此处读不出）">•</span>cere.* If thou continue a teacher of mee, saith *Dauid,* I shall continue a seruaunt to thee. Perseuerance cannot bee, vnlesse continuall light and grace be furnished to vs from the Lord. As the tree which hath not sap <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Perseuerance is of Gods grace.</span> at the roote, may florish, but cannot continue: so a man, whose hart is not watered with the dew of Gods grace continually, may for a time make a faire shew of godliness, but in the end, shall fall away. Wee beare not the roote, but the roote beares vs: let vs tremble and feare. If wee abide not in him, we become withered branches, good for nothing but the fire. Let vs alway pray, that hee would euer abide with vs, to informe vs by his light, and lead vs by his power, in that vvay which may bring vs to him.

VER. 34.

Giue me vnderstanding, and I wil keepe thy law: yea, I wil keep it with mine whole hart.

</div>

HEre followes a prayer agreeable to this <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Wisdom shold be sought from the most wise God.</span> purpose; with a promise of thankfulnes amplified. Whē God appeared <span class="cw-pb" id="p93" data-p="93" data-facs="53" title="Page 93 of the 1613 edition begins here (原书第 93 页起)"></span> to *Salomon* in a vision, and offered to giue vnto him whatsoeuer he would aske; *Salomon* as hee had learned here from *Dauid* his father, seekes a wise and vnderstanding heart: which thing so pleased the Lord, that because *Salomon* did so, and sought not riches nor honour, the Lord promised not onely to giue him wisdome, which he sought, but riches and honour which he sought not. Let vs also be moued, if we be the sonnes of *Dauid,* to seeke best things from the Lord our <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">We are most welcome to God, when we seeke greatest gifts from him.</span> God, specially knowledge & vnderstanding how we may serue him. Certainly so great and gratious is the Lord, so rich in mercy, that then our prayers are most acceptable to him, when we seeke most excellent blessings from him.

*And I will keepe thy Lawe.*] True vnderstanding <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Knowledge which reforms not is dangerous.</span> rests not in speculation, but breakes out in practise. Naked knowledge breeds conuiction; it were better to want it: for greater knowledge makes the iudgement of the wicked greater, because that *when they knew God, they glorified him not.* But true vnderstanding changes the heart; for by it not only do we behold the glory of God, as in the mirrour of his word, but *are changed into* <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">2. Cor. 3. 17.</span> *the same image from glory to glory, by the spirit of the Lord.* And this is that excellent knowledge of Christ, wherein the Apostle reioyces, to *know the vertue of his resurrection, the fellowship* <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Phil 3.</span> *of his afflictions, and bee made conformable to his death.* And indeede then onely is knowledge <span class="cw-pb" id="p94" data-p="94" data-facs="54" title="Page 94 of the 1613 edition begins here (原书第 94 页起)"></span> a blessing, when it workes these happy effects in vs.

*Yea, I will keepe it with mine whole hart.*] As <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">True godliness with perseuerance, hath sinceritie.</span> he promised before perseuerance in Gods seruice; so now he promiseth sinceritie. Some are temporizers in Religion; these perseuer not in seruice. Some are hypocrites; these are not sincere: they draw neere the Lord with their lippes, but are from him in their harts. *Dauid* promiseth both perseuerance, & sinceritie; but so, that stil he craues grace of God to performe thē. When *Dauid* offred materials, as gold & siluer for building of the Temple, he blessed the Lord, & ascribed to him the praise, both of the things which hee had offred (they were Gods own) & of the hart wherby he offred them. *Now therefore, my God, vvee* <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Any good wee doe, is from God.</span> *thank thee, and praise thy glorious Name. But who am I, and what is my people, that we should be able* <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">1. Chron. 29.</span> *to offer vvillingly after this sort? For all things come of thee: and of thy owne hand haue wee giuen thee.* But how much more should we acknovvledge this in spirituall graces? *Haue wee any thing that we haue not receiued?* And when we doe any good by his grace, should not the praise be returned vnto him? Yea indeed, let vs all say with <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">☞</span> *Dauid,* all comes of thee, and of thine owne hand haue we giuen vnto thee. <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Dauid no presumptuous boaster.</span>

And where hee promiseth that hee will keepe Gods law with his whole hart; it is not a presuming <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Phil. <span class="cw-gap" title="TCP: illegible in source（底本此处读不出）">•</span>. See ver. 10.</span> of perfection: *Not as though I were alreadie* <span class="cw-pb" id="p95" data-p="95" data-facs="54" title="Page 95 of the 1613 edition begins here (原书第 95 页起)"></span> *perfect;* but a protesting of his sinceritie, that he had not the false & diuided hart of hypocrites.

<div class="cw-text" markdown="1">

VER. 35.

Direct mee in the path of thy commaundements: for therein is my delight.

</div>

AS before hee craued light to his mind: so <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">It is not enough for vs to haue light; we haue also need that God be our director to walke after it.</span> now he craues grace to direct his heart, that he may follow it. Naturally, man is ignorant of the way to eternall life: and if any light or knowledge he haue of it, yet is he easily miscaried to wander from it. Euen as vvater, if it be not walled in, *ad decliuia fertur;* of it ovvne nature declines to lowest places, if the smallest passage be made vnto it: *Ita & humana natura* <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Bafil.</span> *diabolo viam ad peccatum aperiente, ad id quod peius est proruit:* so the nature of man rushes downeward to sinne, vvhen any way thereto is opened to him by Satan. And therefore *Dauid,* fearing his owne infirmitie, prayes so earnestlie that God would guide him, and not leaue him to himselfe.

Now the way vvhich *Dauid* chooseth to walk <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">The narrow way that leads to heauen.</span> in, is the path of GODS commaundements: not anie nevv vvay; but the old, and pathed vvay vvherein all the seruaunts of GOD haue vvalked before him: and for vvhich, the Graecians (as *Euthymius* noteth) called it <span class="cw-gap" title="TCP: illegible in source（底本此处读不出）">〈Greek〉</span>, <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Euthym.</span> *quasi viam tritam.* But how-so-euer this vvay bee pathed, by the vvalking and treading <span class="cw-pb" id="p96" data-p="96" data-facs="55" title="Page 96 of the 1613 edition begins here (原书第 96 页起)"></span> of many in it, yet he acknowledgeth it is but one, yea, and a narrow and difficil path to keepe; and therefore seekes he to be guided into it. As to <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">The broad way that leads to hell.</span> the broad way there are many rodes and pathed wayes into it, although they meet all in one end: all men goe not to hell by one way; for we see many men abhorring a sinne in others, who yet intertaine a farre more odious sin in themselues: but the narrow way that leades to heauen, hath but one path or road into it, from which if we decline, we fall incontinent into some roade of the broad way that leads to hell.

Let vs therfore vvalke circumspectly, considering our naturall disposition to decline, and the <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Our guide in the way is Iesus, and how we should follow him.</span> dangers and difficulties of the vvay: let vs wait vpon our guide, and follow his foot-steps. *Praecedit Christus vt sequamur nos:* hee hath gone before, that we should follow; he fasted and prayed, to traine vs vp in the like exercises; he suffred, to learne vs patience: *Ascendit Crucem, vt doceret mortem non esse metuendam;* he went vp to the Crosse, that he might teach vs death is not to be feared. By this way of innocence, holinesse, humility, patience, contempt of death, let vs follow our guide, nothing doubting but this path shall lead vs to his Paradise.

*For therein is my delight.* Of this see Verse 47.

<span class="cw-pb" id="p97" data-p="97" data-facs="55" title="Page 97 of the 1613 edition begins here (原书第 97 页起)"></span>VER. 36.

Incline mine heart vnto thy Testimonies; and not to couetousnes.

</div>

THis prayer, among the rest, no doubt <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Man stands between two Suters, that seeke him: happy are wee, if we incline to the best.</span> flowed out of *Dauid* his sense: hee found his hart euill inclined, at least tempted to bow a wrong way. For we stand betweene two parties that seek vs: the one for our weale, the other for our wrack. The Lord on the one hand allures vs; *My sonne, giue mee thine hart:* and offers vs more for it then wee, yea, or tenne thousand worlds can be woorth; *and I shall be thy portion.* <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">☜</span> On the other hand, Satan the deceiuer, by faire, but false allurements, seeks to haue our hart drawne after him. Happy is the man can consider this; that hee may offer his hart to the Lord, and seeke it as a grace, as heere *Dauid* doth.

*Vnto thy testimonies.*] Gods word is called his <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Miserable are they, to whom the good word of God testifies euill tydings.</span> testimonie; because in it hee testifieth his vvill, concerning his worship, and our saluation: and witnesseth also, how he is affected to euery man. For out of it, to some men he speakes as a louing Father: to others, as a feareful Iudge. Men neede not runne vp to Gods secret counsell, to enquire how hee is affected toward them: if they list, they may learne it out of his word.

But, as *Achab* desired not to hear *Michaiah,* because hee prophecied not good thinges vnto him: so many novv, because they doe euill, <span class="cw-pb" id="p98" data-p="98" data-facs="56" title="Page 98 of the 1613 edition begins here (原书第 98 页起)"></span> can not abide the light of the word: they find no ioy in the hearing of it, but are striken vvith feare and trembling; as *Felix* was at the hearing of *Paul.* But in truth the blame is in thy self. *Are not my wordes good to him that walks vprightlie?* <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Mich.</span> *saith the Lord.* No doubt, if thou wert good thy selfe, the word of the Lord should testifie good things vnto thee.

*And not to couetousnes.*] Hee prayes in particular, <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Couetousnes a vile sinne.</span> that his hart may be di<span class="cw-gap" title="TCP: illegible in source（底本此处读不出）">•</span>crted from couetousnes; which is not only an euil, but as saith the Apostle, *The roote of all euill. Dauid* heere oppones it as an aduersarie to all the righteousnesse of Gods testimonies: it inverts the order of Nature, and makes the heauenly soule earthlie. It is a hand-maid of all sinnes; for there is no sinne which a couetous man wil not serue for his gaine. We should beware of all sinnes, but speciallie of <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">☞</span> mother sinnes.

VER. 37.

Turne away mine eyes from regarding vanitie; and quicken me in thy way.

</div>

HAuing prayed for his hart, now he praieth <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Why a Christian should take heed how he lookes with his eyes.</span> for his eyes also: *Omnia à Deo petit, docens illum omnia efficere.* By the eyes oftentimes, as by windowes, death enters into the hart: therefore, to keepe the hart in a good estate, <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Three things requisite to keep the hart.</span> three things are requisite. First, a careful custody <span class="cw-pb" id="p99" data-p="99" data-facs="56" title="Page 99 of the 1613 edition begins here (原书第 99 页起)"></span> of the senses, specially of the eyes: for it is a righteous working of the Lord, *Vt qui exterior<span class="cw-gap" title="TCP: illegible in source（底本此处读不出）">•</span>* <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Gregor. in Iob. lib. 2.</span> *oculo negligenter vtitur, interiori non iniustè caecetur;* that he who negligently vseth the externall eye of his body, shold be punished with blindnes in the internal eye of his mind. And for this cause, *Nazianzen,* deploring the calamities of his soule, <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Nazian.</span> wished that a dore might be set before his eyes & eares, to close them when they opened to anie thing that is not good; *Malis autem sua sponte vtrumque clauderetur.* The second thing is, a subduing of the body by discipline. And the third is, continuance in prayer.

Euery creature of God is good: and the beholding <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Euery creature we see, should waken vs to praise God.</span> thereof, should waken in vs an affection to praise God. *Oculi idcireo dati sunt corpori, vt per eos intueamur creaturam, a<span class="cw-gap" title="TCP: illegible in source（底本此处读不出）">•</span> per huiusmodi mirabilem* <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Athanas. contra Gent.</span> *harmoniam agnoscamus opificem:* for this cause were eyes giuen to the body, that by them we might looke to the creature, and by the maruailous harmony which is among thē, we might learne to know the Creator. But since the fal, the creature also is become subiect to vanitie: in euery <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">☜</span> one of them hath the Serpent laid his deceiuable snare to entrap man, and steale his affection <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1"><span class="cw-gap" title="TCP: illegible in source（底本此处读不出）">•</span>ut corrupt nature makes them snares to intangle vs.</span> from GOD: so that now, *Inter tot passiones huius corporis, inter tantas illecebras huius saeculi, difficile est tutum, & intemeratum seruare ve* <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Ambrose.</span> *stigium:* in the midst of so many passions, of so many allurements of this world, it is a dissicile <span class="cw-pb" id="p100" data-p="100" data-facs="57" title="Page 100 of the 1613 edition begins here (原书第 100 页起)"></span> thing to walke, and not to be snared. Hee that lookes on a woman, saith our Sauiour, and lusts after her, hath committed adultery. Behold in how short a time great guiltinesse is committed. *Malè sic vidit oculus: videat ergo & fungatur suo munere; non lubricae mentis imperio dirigatur ad lapsum, vt vitiū referat pro officio. Dauid* knew this, hee found the euill of it by experience: let vs learn of him to watch ouer our eyes, that we may keepe the hart the better.

*Quicken mee in thy way.*] Man by nature is <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Naturall man quick enough to works of sin, but dead to works of pietie.</span> quick enough to walke in his owne waies; and the works of nature, he can doe them without a teacher: but vnlesse the Lord put life in him, & keepe life in his soule, quickning him at all occasions, hee hath neither knowledge, nor strength, nor pleasure to walke in the waies of God. Of this petition, see ver. 88. 145.

VER. 38.

Stablish thy promise to thy seruaunt, because hee feares thee.

</div>

HEere is a prayer, with a reason. The <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Gods promises are sure in themselues: but it is a great point to make them sure vnto vs.</span> prayer is, that God would make sure his promise to *Dauid.* The meaning is, I knowe ô Lord, that thy promise is sure enough in it selfe, but I pray thee make it sure to me. Sure it is, the promises of God are most cercertaine, the earth may mooue, and mountaines may fall: yea, the earth at length shal be shaken, <span class="cw-pb" id="p101" data-p="101" data-facs="57" title="Page 101 of the 1613 edition begins here (原书第 101 页起)"></span> and the heauens wimpled vp like a garment; but one iot of the word of GOD shall not faile. This is the maine poynt, vvhereat in all our life vvee should aime, To make sure our calling, & election; that the promises of God, most sure in themselues, may be made sure vnto vs.

Many waies hath GOD confirmed his promise <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Many confirmations of Gods promises haue wee.</span> to vs: First, by the blood of his sonne. Next, by his owne oath: he hath not onely spoken that he will giue mercie to the penitent belieuer; but hee hath sworne it, *that by two immutable things,* <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Hebr. 6.</span> *wherin it is impossible that hee should lie, he might shew to the heires of promise, the stabilitie of his counsell.* But for all this, neuer is his promise sure vnto vs, till we receiue the seale of the spirit of adoption. For this let vs pray. <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">To remedy our naturall infidelitie.</span>

For so long as we are in this body of sinne, our infidelitie will euer be sending out feares, and doubtings, and distrusts; against vvhich, vvee haue neede to pray the Lord alwaies to confirme vs. *Omnes enim Dei benignitate indigemus, et qui* <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Bafil.</span> *extremum consequutus est virtut is apicem hac indiget continuò.* No man hath made such progresse in faith, but hee hath neede further to be confirmed: *Lord incrcase our faith: Lord helpe our vnbeliefe.* And therefore, let not our wants and great weakenesse driue vs to despaire; but so much the more vvaken and prouoke vs to feruent prayer.

*To thy seruant.] Dauid* frequently vseth this <span class="cw-pb" id="p102" data-p="102" data-facs="58" title="Page 102 of the 1613 edition begins here (原书第 102 页起)"></span> stile as very honourable: and indeed so it is. The <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">It is an honourable stile to be called Gods seruaunt.</span> seruaunt of God onely is a free man, yea, and a King; but hee who is not the Lords seruaunt, though hee were a Monarch, and ruler of the whole earth, he is but Satans captiue, and a seruaunt of seruaunts. *O quam multos Dominos habet,* <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">A<span class="cw-gap" title="TCP: illegible in source（底本此处读不出）">•</span>lrose.</span> *qui vnum non habet!*

*Because hee feares thee.*] Hee who hath receiued <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">He that feares and loues the Lord, may boldly seeke grace frō him.</span> from the Lord grace to feare him with loue, may be bold to seeke any necessary good thing from him; because the feare of GOD hath annexed the promises of all other blessings vvith it.

Sometime, *Dauid* brings the reason of his petition <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Gods promises are cōditionall<span class="cw-gap" title="TCP: illegible in source（底本此处读不出）">•</span> if wee haue not the condition, we haue no interest in the promise.</span> from the Lord; as from his mercy, or his truth: sometime from himselfe; as from this that hee trusts in the Lord, ver. 42: or that hee hath a great desire toward the Lord, ver. 40: or that he feares God; as heere. All the promises of God are conditionall: if the condition bee no way in vs, how shall the promise be performed vnto vs? It is true, the Lord is gracious & mercifull, readie to forgiue, &c. But what is that to thee, who repentst not, belieuest him not, louest him not, and trustest not in him?

<div class="cw-text" markdown="1">

<span class="cw-pb" id="p103" data-p="103" data-facs="58" title="Page 103 of the 1613 edition begins here (原书第 103 页起)"></span>VER. 39.

Take away my rebuke, that I feare: for thy iudgements are good.

</div>

THere is a rebuke which comes vpon the <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Rebukes of men, how to be borne.</span> godly, from men, without a cause: and this, suppose it grieue the godly, yet they feare it not, but rather reioyce in it, according to that of our Sauiour; *Blessed are yee when men reuile you, and speake all manner euill of you for my* <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Math. 5.</span> *names sake: be glad and reioyce, for great is your reward in heauen.* With this meditation did *Augustine* confirme himselfe, against the detractions of his Aduersarie, who sought to empair the credit of his name: *Quisquis volens detrahit* <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">August.</span> *famaemeae, nolens addit mercedi meae:* Hee that willingly would empaire my name, against his will increaseth my reward. And *Dauid* glorieth in this, that the rebukes of them, who rebuked the Lord, had fallen vpon him. <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">The rebukes of God, how they are to be eschewed.</span>

There is againe a rebuke and shame comming from God, as the fruite and iust punishment of sin; & for this the godly are afraid: if they looke to their begun sinnes, they know they haue deserued it; they feare it, and therefore pray vvith *Dauid,* Psalmo 6<span class="cw-gap" title="TCP: illegible in source（底本此处读不出）">▪</span> *Lord rebuke mee not in thine anger.* If againe they looke to the time to come, they feare, least their infirmities bring them to <span class="cw-pb" id="p104" data-p="104" data-facs="59" title="Page 104 of the 1613 edition begins here (原书第 104 页起)"></span> shame, euery one of them according to their seuerall tentations. And this also they preuent by prayer; as heere *Dauid* doth.

For it is to be marked, that a godlie man is <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">A godly man is more afraid for sinne that he may do, thē the wicked is for sinne hee hath done.</span> more afraid for sinnes vvhich hee may doe, then a vvicked man is for sinnes that he hath done: as hee liues in a continuall sorrow for begun sinnes, so also in a continuall feare of sinnes wherein he may fall. *Semper est in dolore et tremore:* the <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">☞</span> one for repenting of sinne; the other for preuenting of sinne.

*For thy iudgements are good<span class="cw-gap" title="TCP: illegible in source（底本此处读不出）">▪</span>* ] This reason <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">*Dauid* afraid of Gods rebuke, not of mans.</span> heere subioyned, declares that the rebuke which he feared, was Gods rebuke. The meaning is; As to man his iudgement, it is so peruerse, that I passe not for it at all: I knowe hee condemnes vvhere thou vvilt absolue. It is high many a time in mans eye, vvhich is abhomination to thee: but as to thy rebuke, I know Lord it comes neuer vndeserued; for thy iudgement is good. And therefore prayes he, that the Lord vvould keepe him from those sinnes which may bring shame and reproach vpon him.

<span class="cw-pb" id="p105" data-p="105" data-facs="59" title="Page 105 of the 1613 edition begins here (原书第 105 页起)"></span>VER. 40.

Behold, I desire thy commandements: quicken me in thy righteousnesse.

</div>

SOmetime he confesseth his naturall inclination <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Motions of sin in the godly are not without motions of grace.</span> to euill, as yee may see out of his prayers, in the verses preceding; sometime his new disposition by grace, vnto that which is good. The children of God finde in themselues motions of sinne, but not without motions of grace to restrain them; the one fights against the other: and in this battell are we militant here vpon earth; let vs mourne for the one, and giue thankes for the other with the Apostle, *Rom.* 7.

He offers his desires to the Lord to be looked <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">It is a good heart which dares present it selfe to the Lord.</span> vpon. It is an argument of a well set, and disposed heart, when a man dare present it vnto the Lord, that hee may looke vpon the desires and intention thereof. An euill conscience dare not doe this: for as an eye, when it is hurt, craues some couering to hide it from the light; so the guiltie conscience flies from the Lord, and hath no desire to come before him: let vs refuse to liue in that course of life, wherein we dare not be bold to looke to the Lord, nor content that he should looke vnto vs.

*I desire.*] Our perfection in this life is rather <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Our perfection in this life is rather in desires then in deeds.</span> in godly desires, then in a full obtayning of things desired, *Phil.* 3. yet haue the godly this comfort; that where desires goe before, satisfaction <span class="cw-pb" id="p106" data-p="106" data-facs="60" title="Page 106 of the 1613 edition begins here (原书第 106 页起)"></span> shall follow: for the Lord hath promised to fulfill the desires of them that feare him; *Blessed are they who hunger and thirst for righteousnesse,* <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Matth. 5.</span> *for they shall be satisfied:* yea, sure it is, how great soeuer our desires be, we cannot desire so much as the Lord hath to giue vs; for he is rich in mercy, able to doe abundantly, *aboue all that* <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Satisfaction shall follow desires, and God shall giue vs more then we desired.</span> *we can aske or thinke.* Example hereof we haue in *Zaccheus;* he desired but to see Christ, and hee got not onely a sight of him, but familiar speech of him: yea, Christ went to his house and dined with him, and made him by grace the childe of *Abraham.* So shall the Lord do to all his children; he shall giue them more then eyther they desired or looked for at his hands: *For the heart* <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">1. Cor. 2.</span> *cannot vnderstand those things, which God hath prepared for them that loue him.*

*Thy commandements.*] He saith not I haue done <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">God accepts from his children a will for a deed.</span> thy commandements; but, I desire to do them. Let this serue as a Commentarie to other places, wherein he makes protestation of his begun obedience: so gratious is the Lord, that in his children he accepts their will for a deed.

There is no man so wicked but he desires mercie: <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">As the godly desire mercie, so they vse the<span class="cw-gap" title="TCP: illegible in source（底本此处读不出）">▪</span> meanes.</span> but the godly doe so desire mercy, that first they desire the way to mercy. They know, that *without sanctification no man can see the Lord;* therefore all their care is to *purge themselues, euen* <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">1. Ioh. 3.</span> *as God is pure:* as here ye see for this end *Dauid* prayeth for the grace of obedience. It was a preposterous <span class="cw-pb" id="p107" data-p="107" data-facs="60" title="Page 107 of the 1613 edition begins here (原书第 107 页起)"></span> desire of *Balaam,* who wished the <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Not so to the wicked.</span> death of the righteous, and would not liue the life of the righteous: no better are the desires of wicked men.

*Quicken me.*] He acknowledgeth that his desires <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Desires without grace are like a false conception.</span> were nothing, vnlesse the Lord gaue him life. As a false conception, which evanisheth and comes not to the birth; so is the desire of man, not quickned nor continued by the grace of God. More of this see ver. 149. 159.
