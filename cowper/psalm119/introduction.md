### <span class="cw-pb" id="p1" data-p="1" data-facs="7" title="Page 1 of the 1613 edition begins here (原书第 1 页起)"></span>A HOLY ALPHAbet for Sions Scholars.

<div class="cw-text" markdown="1">

My helpe is in the Name of the Lord.

</div>

<span class="cw-dropcap">T</span>Here is no way, by which a <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">So many waie<span class="cw-gap" title="TCP: illegible in source（底本此处读不出）">•</span> doth God te<span class="cw-gap" title="TCP: illegible in source（底本此处读不出）">•</span>ch man, that man is inexcusable if he learn not.</span> man may learne, but by the same God doth teach him: by precepts, he instructs; by requests, he exhorts; by promises, hee allures; by threatnings, he terrifies: and <span class="cw-gap" title="TCP: illegible in source（底本此处读不出）">•</span>herfore hath he sent his seruants, of sundry qua<span class="cw-gap" title="TCP: illegible in source（底本此处读不出）">•</span>ities, according to their seuerall gifts, vnto vs; <span class="cw-gap" title="TCP: illegible in source（底本此处读不出）">•</span>ome like *Moses,* to teach vs; some like *Esay,* to <span class="cw-gap" title="TCP: illegible in source（底本此处读不出）">•</span>omfort vs; some like *Ieremie,* mourning to vs; <span class="cw-gap" title="TCP: illegible in source（底本此处读不出）">•</span>om like *Dauid,* singing to vs. If when they teach, <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Matth. 11. 17.</span> we learne not; if when they mourne, we lament not; if when they pipe, we dance not: yea, if the sweetest songs of the sweet Singer of I<span class="cw-gap" title="TCP: illegible in source（底本此处读不出）">•</span>rael (*Qut* <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Euthym.</span> *noster Orpheus est*) delight vs not, to ra<span class="cw-gap" title="TCP: illegible in source（底本此处读不出）">•</span>e vp our affections to the Lord; are we not inexcusable?

<span class="cw-pb" id="p2" data-p="2" data-facs="8" title="Page 2 of the 1613 edition begins here (原书第 2 页起)"></span>By the deceit of vaine pleasure, Sathan<span class="cw-gap" title="TCP: illegible in source（底本此处读不出）">▪</span> steales <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Gods courle in dealing with man, contrary to Sathans.</span> the heart of man from God: and the Lord most artificially by the sweetnesse of true pleasure, al<span class="cw-gap" title="TCP: illegible in source（底本此处读不出）">•</span>lures him home againe; *What could the Lord haue* <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Esa. 5. 4.</span> *done any more vnto vs, that he hath not done?* He propones most profitable things for vs in his word: and to make them the more welcome, he seasons them with pleasures. *Nam quod gratum est, iucundius recipitur, & constantius ret<span class="cw-gap" title="TCP: illegible in source（底本此处读不出）">•</span>netur.*

Vnto this vse serues the whole booke of the <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">A commendation of the booke of the Psalmes. *A<span class="cw-gap" title="TCP: illegible in source（底本此处读不出）">•</span>ib.*</span> Psalmes, which is *Commune quoddam medicinae promptuarium;* but in speciall this Psalme, which, in the iudgement of *Ambrose,* exceeds the rest, as farre as the light of the Sunne excels the light of the Moone. It was written by one of the Saints of God, but in so maruellous a manner, that the wordes therof convene and agree vnto all; *Quod huius operis proprium est.* So that it is, *Veluti penu doctrinae publicum, vnicuique apta & conuenientia distribuens;* and therefore should be in no lesse account with those, who loue the spirituall lise, then is the vse of the Sunne, the ayre and the fire, for the entertainment of this naturall life.

The Author of this Psalme, was most properly <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Dauid both the heart, and the tongue, and the pen of the great King.</span> called by *Euthymius, Primi regis & cor, & lingua, & calamus:* for he receiued this testimony from the heauenly Oracle, That hee was a man after Gods heart. Now because no greater ioy can come to the children of God, then to <span class="cw-pb" id="p3" data-p="3" data-facs="8" title="Page 3 of the 1613 edition begins here (原书第 3 页起)"></span> know that God esteemes of them, as he esteemed of *Dauid,* that they are also men after the heart of God; let them here learne a ready way whereby they may know it. It was promised, that vnder <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">If we be partakers of Dauids disposition, we shall also be partakers of his approbation.</span> the Kingdome of Christ, the meanest or least should be like vnto *Dauid.* If therefore by grace thou be partaker of *Dauids* disposition, recorded to thee in this Psalme, thou maist be out of doubt <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">☜</span> thou art also partaker of his approbation: for the Lord is no accepter of persons; what he approues in one, he will also approue in another.

This is it which *Pontius* the Deacon records <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">A notable sentence of Cyprian to this purpole.</span> of *Cyprian,* that when euer he heard or read any person commended in the Booke of God, hee warned vs, to make diligent inquisition of their actions, for which they were commended: as namely, when we heare, said he, that *Iob* receiued <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Iob 1. 1. 8.</span> this praise from God, *that he was an vpright and <span class="cw-gap" title="TCP: illegible in source（底本此处读不出）">•</span>ust man, one that seared God, and eschued euill, and how none was like him on the earth,* let vs turne ouer to the 31. chapter; where we haue a <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Pontius.</span> short compend of his life: and let vs endeuour to order our lise as he did his<span class="cw-gap" title="TCP: illegible in source（底本此处读不出）">▪</span> *Vt dum & nos paria facimus, simile in nos Dei testimonium prouoce<span class="cw-gap" title="TCP: illegible in source（底本此处读不出）">•</span>us.* We need not then goe vp to the counsell <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">We should marke these ver<span class="cw-gap" title="TCP: illegible in source（底本此处读不出）">•</span>es for which God hath commended his seruants.</span> of God, to enquire there, whether or not we be beloued of him; let vs come downe to our selues, *Make sure our calling and election by wel doing:* let vs trie our selues by this disposition, which here is in *Dauid;* where we finde a conformity: <span class="cw-pb" id="p4" data-p="4" data-facs="9" title="Page 4 of the 1613 edition begins here (原书第 4 页起)"></span> let vs giue thankes for the beginnings of Gods grace in vs; where not, let vs prease to amend: being assured, vnlesse wee follow *Dauid* in the practise of piety, we cannot be partakers of his praise, which hee receiued from God.

Now among many points of godlinesse manifest <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">In euery verse of this Psalme, except one, Dauid makes mention of Gods word.</span> in him, there is one here throughout the whole Psalme, more euident then the rest; and that is his feruent affection toward the word of God: for in euery verse, except one onely, to wit, <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Vatablus.</span> the 122. he makes mention of the word of God, vnder some of these names, *Lawe, Statutes, Precepts, Testimonies, Commandements, Word, Promises, Wayes, Iudgements, Name, Righteousnesse, Truth:* the reason hereof is, because in euery state of life he found light, life, & comfort in the word of God; and therefore was his affection inflamed toward it. Euen as children, new borne by the instinct of nature, haue an appetite to milk for conseruation of their life: so the children of God, once regenerate by the instinct of grace, haue a spirituall appetite to the word of God: according to that of Saint *Peter; As new borne babes* <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Such as are regenerate by the word, liue by it, as by the food of their soules.</span> *desire the sincere milke of the word, that yee may growe thereby.* Neyther is it possible that any man can esteeme of that worde as a foode for their nourishment, but they to whom first it hath beene a seede of their newe birth: and the onely cause, why the cold professors of <span class="cw-pb" id="p5" data-p="5" data-facs="9" title="Page 5 of the 1613 edition begins here (原书第 5 页起)"></span> this age esteemes it not as a food, for which they <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Others cannot account of it.</span> hunger and thirst, but rather be weary of it more then Israel was of Manna; is, that it was neuer vnto them a liuely seed of their new birth: and so <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">☜</span> sith they haue neyther gotten life nor light by it, what maruell is it, they haue no delight in it? <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">The order and diuision of this Psalme.</span>

As to the order of this Psalme, it is diuided into two and twenty Sections; euery Section hath in it eight Verses, and euery Verse beginnes in the Hebrew, with that letter, wherewith the Section is intituled: as, all the Verses of the first Section begin with *Aleph;* the Verses of the second with *Beth,* and so forward, according to the Hebrew *Alphabet:* for which wee may call this Psalme an *A. B. C.* of godlinesse. Another method of this Psalme can we not lay, saue that euery Verse containes in it, eyther a praise of Gods word, from some excellent quality of it; or a protestation of *Dauid* his vnfayned affection toward it; or else a prayer for grace, to conforme himselfe vnto it: for vnto one of these three, *Prayses, Prayers,* or *Protestations,* may all the Verses of this Psalme be reduced. And so we enter into it.
