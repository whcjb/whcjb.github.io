### <span class="cw-pb" id="p47" data-p="47" data-facs="30" title="Page 47 of the 1613 edition begins here (原书第 47 页起)"></span>GIMEL.

<div class="cw-text" markdown="1">

VER. 17.

Be beneficiall vnto thy seruant, that I may liue and keepe thy word.

</div>

<span class="cw-dropcap">I</span>N the end of the former Section *Dauid* <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Our best estate vpon earth, is, that we haue not that which we should, and yet want not altogether.</span> had protestations: now in the beginning of this he hath prayers. In his protestations he acknowledgeth the beginnings which by grace he had made in godlinesse, to the praise and glory of God: in the prayers he acknowledgeth his wants, that he was farre from that which he should be; and therfore still beseecheth the Lord to bring forward to perfection, that which he had begun in him.

If ye marke the course of this Psalme, ye <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">The strength of a Christian is in his prayer.</span> shall see, *Dauid* cannot long abide in any purpose without prayer: he learned by experience, that prayer is the life of the soule. No more can the Soule liue without prayer, then the Body can without breathing: and as *Samsons* strength was in his haire; so a Christians strength is in the grace of prayer. If this be taken from vs, we soone become a prey to the spirituall Philistims.

<span class="cw-pb" id="p48" data-p="48" data-facs="31" title="Page 48 of the 1613 edition begins here (原书第 48 页起)"></span>Now the first thing he craues as a speciall benefite, <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">The greatest benefit men receiue from God, is grace to obey him.</span> is, that hee may liue and keep the word of God. Many benefits had the Lord bestowed vppon him: of a Shepheard he made him a King to them all: he seeks this benefit, grace so to liue, that he might keepe the word of the Lord: he knew that without this, all other benefits are nothing; and so doe all Gods children, illuminate with the light of God, if they were preferred to be Monarches of the world. They count more of <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Constantine the great his notable saying, It is more to be a Christian then a Monarch of the world.</span> this, To be Christians, wanting earthly Kingdoms, then To be Kings, wanting Christianity. As, good *Constantine,* albeit he knew he was obliged to the Lord for making him an Emperor; yet much more bound to his mercie, for making him a Christian. And indeede this life without the other grace, though it were seconded with the highest honour and wealth that euer befell to any man in his life, what is it? Not an Introduction onely to eternall death; but a procuring, and iust deseruing of it.

*Thy seruant.*] That hee stiles himselfe so <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">So Dauid reioyceth more in this, that he was Gods seruant, then king of Israel.</span> frequently the seruant of GOD, notes in him tvvo things: first, a reuerent estimation hee had of his GOD; in that hee accounts it more honourable to bee called the seruant of God, who was aboue him, then the King of a mightie, auncient, and most famous people, that vvere vnder him. And indeede, <span class="cw-pb" id="p49" data-p="49" data-facs="31" title="Page 49 of the 1613 edition begins here (原书第 49 页起)"></span> sith the Angels are styled his ministers, shall man <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Sith Angels serue him, shal we think shame to serue him?</span> thinke it a shame to serue him with them? and especially, sith he of his goodnes hath made them our seruants, *Ministring spirits to vs?* Should we not ioyfully serue him, who hath made all his creatures to serue vs; and exempted vs from the seruice of all other, and hath onely bound vs to serue himselfe?

*That I may liue.] Dauid* ioynes heere two together, <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Naturall life makes a reprobate man in a worse case, then if he had neuer been.</span> which whosoeuer disioynes, cannot be blessed. He desires to liue; but so to liue, that he may keep Gods word. To a reprobate man, who liues a rebel to his Maker, it had bin good (as our Sauiour said of *Iudas*) that he had neuer been borne; that the knees had not preuented him; or, that incontinent after his birth, he had been buried. The shorter his life is, the fewer are his sinnes, and the smaller his iudgements.

But to an elect man, life is a great benefit: for <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">But to an elect man, euen naturall life is a great benefit.</span> by it he goes from election to glorification, by the way of sanctification. The longer he liues, the more good he doth, to the glory of God, the edification of others, and confirmation of his owne saluation; making it sure to himselfe, by wrestling and victory in tentations, and perseuerance in well dooing.

Without grace, this life is but a death: so the <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">This life without grace, is but a death.</span> Spirit of God accounts of it; and therefore giues to most liuely naturall men, the name of dead men. *Omnia hic plena mortis sunt.* By the law, if <span class="cw-pb" id="p50" data-p="50" data-facs="32" title="Page 50 of the 1613 edition begins here (原书第 50 页起)"></span> a liuing man had touched the dead, hee was defiled: <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Num. 19.</span> and wee, beside that wee carry death in our selues, where can we go and not touch the dead? Great need therfore haue wee, with *Dauid,* earnestly to seek this grace, that we may liue keeping the word of God: aunswering that commandement of our lord, *Relin que mortuos;* and of his Apostle, <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Math. 8.</span> *Awake thou that sleepest, and arise from* <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Eph. 5.</span> *the dead.*

For, as to thē who loue to liue in the body, for <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Miserable are they, who desire to liue for loue of the pleasures of sin.</span> no other end, but that they may enioy carnall pleasures in the body, no tongue can expresse their miserie. To a godly man, sin makes his life bitter: so the Apostle protested, *O miserable man! who shal deliuer me from this body of death?* And if they loue to liue, it is that they may mourn for sin, & break off the course of their sins by amēdement of life. This straited godly *Nazianzen,* that <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">A worthy meditation of *Nazianzen.*</span> he knew not whether to make choise of death, or life: when he considered that death makes an end of sin, he desired to die; but when he remembred that after death there was no time to mourne for sin, he desired to liue.

VER. 18.

Open mine eyes, that I may see the wonders of thy law.

</div>

MAn by nature is blind in the matters of <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Satan by experience is found a false deceiuer.</span> saluation: *He vnderstands not the things of God, neither can he, because they are* <span class="cw-pb" id="p51" data-p="51" data-facs="32" title="Page 51 of the 1613 edition begins here (原书第 51 页起)"></span> *spiritually discerned.* Satan promised man great knowledge, both of good & euill: but experience may declare what a false deceiuer he is. By nature we are all borne blind, ignorant of things vvhich concerne the kingdom of God: and by reason of our darkned mind, so wicked is the hart of man, that by looking to the best works of God, miserable <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">How blind mā is by nature.</span> man contracts euill. So euill doth hee see, with the eyes which are left him, that by looking <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">☜</span> to that which is good, the hart of him is wakened vnto euill. So traiterously hath this aduersarie dealt with mankind, that where he promised thē more knowledge, he spoiled them of that which they had; and hath brought now vpon them all, a greater shame then that vvhich *Naash* the *Ammonite* vvould haue done to *Israel.* For hee hath put out their right eye, that no light remaines in them vvherby they can see that which is good to saluation, till the Lord restore it againe vnto them.

And heere if it be asked, seeing *Dauid* was a <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Our regeneratiō is wrought by degrees.</span> regenerate man, and so illuminated already, How is it that hee prayes for the opening of his eyes? the aunswere is easie; That our regeneration is wrought by degrees. The beginnings of light in his mind, made him long for more: for no man can account of sense, but he who hath it. The light vvhich he had, let him see his owne darknes: and therefore feeling his wants, hee seekes to haue them supplied by the Lord. *Neque enim medicū* <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Ambrose.</span> <span class="cw-pb" id="p52" data-p="52" data-facs="33" title="Page 52 of the 1613 edition begins here (原书第 52 页起)"></span> *rogat, nisi qui remedium agritudini suae poscit.*

But the word which here *Dauid* vseth, imports <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">If we be ignorant of the word, the blame is in our darke mind, not in it.</span> the taking of a vaile from his eyes; *detrahe velamen oculis meis.* So that the blame of his ignorance he layes not vpon the word, which is cleer enough in it selfe; as he confesseth, ver. 130. *The* <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Vatab.</span> *entrance of thy vvord, giues light to the simple:* but he blames himselfe, and the vaile which couered the eyes of his mind. Till this be remooued, no reading, no hearing, no teaching, can make a man vnderstand the wonders of the lavv of God. This is it, saith the Apostle, which hinders the Iews, that in reading the old Testament, <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">2. Cor. 3. 14.</span> they cannot see Christ, because of the vaile that couers their harts. And this same is it, that this day hinders many wise and learned men, that <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Why many learned men attaine not to the knowledge of the truth.</span> albeit they read the most cleare prophecies of Antichrist, whereas S. *Paul* and S. *Iohn* poynts him out, as it were with the finger: yet can they not perceiue him. The doctrine of Christ, is the *mysterie of godliness:* the doctrine of Antichrist, <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">☞</span> is the *mysterie of iniquitie.* Who can learne it, till God remoue the vaile that couers the mind? *Non omnes, qui diuina eloquia legunt, quae in illis* <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Basil.</span> *admiranda sunt considerant, nisi illi qui coelesti splendore potiuntur.*

But whose worke is this to remoue the vaile? Who is able to doe it? Certainly, neither Angel, <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Illumination of the mind, is Gods worke.</span> nor Power, nor Dominion. It is onely the vvorke of GOD, who giues sight to the blind. <span class="cw-pb" id="p53" data-p="53" data-facs="33" title="Page 53 of the 1613 edition begins here (原书第 53 页起)"></span> *Hee sends forth his spirit, and renewes the face of* <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Psal. 104.</span> <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Luk 24.</span> *the earth.* He opened the disciples eyes, & made them to know him. He made scales to fall from the eyes of the Apostle *Paul* when he conuerted him. These two benefits hee giues together to his owne; the opening of the eyes, and conversion of the hart. So long as a man abides vnder the seruitude of sin, and earthly affections, he can neuer haue eyes to see the secrets of Gods word. Therefore, saith the Apostle (speaking of the blinded Iewes) *when their harts shall be turned to* <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">2. Cor. 3. 16.</span> *the Lord, then the vaile shall be taken away.*

*The wonders of thy law:*] The wonders of the <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Euery Article of our faith, <span class="cw-gap" title="TCP: illegible in source（底本此处读不出）">•</span>s a wonderful mysterie.</span> works of God are many and great: the wonders of his word are greater. Euery article of our faith, is a mysterie to be wondred at: that a Virgine conceiues a child; that GOD is manifested in the flesh. If wee will be fruitfull scholars in the learning of these mysteries, let vs pray for the opening of our eyes, and for grace to belieue.

VER. 19.

I am a stranger on earth: hide not thy commaundements from me.

</div>

THis petition in effect is one with the former; <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Man on earth is a stranger, & knowes not the way hee should walk, till God shewes it vnto him.</span> hauing no more but an annexed reason, vvhich is this: I am a stranger on earth, and knowe not the way may lead me to <span class="cw-pb" id="p54" data-p="54" data-facs="34" title="Page 54 of the 1613 edition begins here (原书第 54 页起)"></span> heauen, vnlesse thou teach me; therefore hide not thy cōmandements from me. The like of this he hath, Psa. 43. *Send thy light, & thy truth; let them lead me, & bring me to thy holy Mountaine.*

This is not the voice of euery man, I am a stranger on earth; *Sed eius qui terrenis renuntiauit* <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Ambrose.</span> *voluptatibus, & mundanae cupiditatis exuerit* <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Worldling<span class="cw-gap" title="TCP: illegible in source（底本此处读不出）">•</span> shal not continue on earth; yet cannot say they are strangers in it.</span> *affect us.* It is the voice of him who hath renounced pleasures of the earth; is weary of this life; & desires to be dissolued: not fearing when his dissolution approches; but rather reioycing, because he knows he shall be with Christ, *vtitur hac vita vt transitoria, ad aliam nimirum vitam festinans.* He vseth this life as a transitory life, because he makes hast to a better.

It is true, in regard of time and continuance, <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">And that because in affection they be content with this, & desire not a better.</span> the wicked are also strangers on earth; but in affection they are not so: for neither know they of a better Cittie, neither desire they a better. But the Christian accounts himselfe a stranger, not so much for his short continuance vpon earth, as for that his <span class="cw-gap" title="TCP: illegible in source（底本此处读不出）">〈Greek〉</span> is aboue: that Citie wherof he <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Phil.</span> is Burges and freeman; that countrey frō which he came; that fellowship wherein he reioiceth, is aboue; & therfore longs he to be where they are.

But as to the wicked, they are called by Gods Spirit, inhabitants, or indwellers of the earth: <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Reuel. 8.</span> *Men of this world, who haue their portion in this* <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Psal. 17.</span> *life; they haue receiued their cōsolation here;* their generation is of the earth; they speak of the earth; <span class="cw-pb" id="p55" data-p="55" data-facs="34" title="Page 55 of the 1613 edition begins here (原书第 55 页起)"></span> their whole disposition is earthly. Higher then <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Worldlings <span class="cw-gap" title="TCP: illegible in source（底本此处读不出）">•</span>e inhabitants of the earth; Christians are <span class="cw-gap" title="TCP: illegible in source（底本此处读不出）">•</span>ut strangers in it.</span> the earth, in thoughts and desires they cannot go: *Praesentibus bonis quasi diuturnis inflati insolescunt.* The godly are not so: they are (as I said) pilgrims on earth, not indwellers. Suppose they <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Basil.</span> walke on earth, their conuersation is in heauen: they are risen with Christ, and set their affections on those things, which are at the right hand o<span class="cw-gap" title="TCP: illegible in source（底本此处读不出）">•</span> God. They vse this world, as if they vsed it not: knowing, that <span class="cw-gap" title="TCP: illegible in source（底本此处读不出）">〈Greek〉</span>, the shape or figure thereof, passeth away; they soiourne in it, but vvill not dwell in it. But like vnto the Eagles (as they are <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Luke.</span> figuratiuely called in the parable) they see, and sent things which are aboue, and mount vp toward their prey Christ Iesus; counting the most excellent things on earth, to be but doung, in respect of him.

*On earth.*] He makes no exception heere: the <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">The whole earth is but a place of banishment.</span> whole earth hee acknowledged a place of his pilgrimage. Not only whē he was banished among the *Moabits,* & *Philistims,* was he a stranger: but euen when he liued peaceably at home, in *Canaan,* stil he thinks himselfe a stranger. This consideration mooued godly *Basile,* to despise the threatning of *Modestus,* the deputy of *Valens* the Emperor, when he braued him with banishment: *Ab exilij metu liber sum, vnam hominum cognoscens* <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Nazian<span class="cw-gap" title="TCP: illegible in source（底本此处读不出）">▪</span> in vita Basil.</span> *esse patriam, paradisum, omnem autem terram commune Naturae exilium.* And it shall moue vs to keepe spirituall sobrietie in the midst of <span class="cw-pb" id="p56" data-p="56" data-facs="35" title="Page 56 of the 1613 edition begins here (原书第 56 页起)"></span> pleasures, if we remember that in our houses, at <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">A man euen in his owne house should esteeme himselfe a strāger.</span> our owne fire end, and in our owne beds, wee are but strangers, from which we must shortly remoue, and giue place to others.

*Hide not.*] The maner of *Dauid* his reasoning, <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">This world can neither wil nor teach men a way to go out of her selfe: we must seeke a guide frō heauen.</span> we touched before to be this: I am heere a stranger, and knowe not the way; therefore Lord direct me. The similitude is taken from passengers, who comming to an vncouth countrey, vvhere they are ignorant of the way, seeke the benefit of a guide; but the dissimilitude is heere. In any <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">☞</span> Countrey, the country people can guide a stranger to the place where he would be; but the indwellers of the earth, cannot shew the way to heauen: and therefore *Dauid* seekes no guide among them, but prayes the Lord to direct him.

*Thy commandements.*] We are not to thinke <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">The right knowledge of the ten Commandements.</span> that *Dauid* was ignorant of the ten commaundements; no doubt hee knew them, and could distinctly repeat them: but hee craues that GOD would further teach him the vse, the necessity, the vtility, the obedience of them. And this for our carnall professors, who if they can repeat the beliefe, and the commandements, think they are religious enough, albeit they neither belieue nor obey, but remaine ignorant of the vse & practice of thē both: without which, ignorance is better then knowledge; for the seruant that knowes his Masters will, and doth it not, is worthy of double stripes.

<div class="cw-text" markdown="1">

<span class="cw-pb" id="p57" data-p="57" data-facs="35" title="Page 57 of the 1613 edition begins here (原书第 57 页起)"></span>VER. 20.

Mine hart breaks for the desire of thy iudgements alway.

</div>

HEre is a protestation of that earnest desire <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Two things required in true obedience.</span> he had to the obedience of the word of God; he amplifies it 2. waies: first, it was no light motion, but such as beeing deeply rooted, made his hart to breake when hee saw that he could not do in the obedience therof as he would. Next, it was no vanishing motion, like the morning dew: but it was permanent, *Omni tempore,* he had it alway. <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Both the word and the plagues of God, are called his iudgments; & how.</span>

*Thy iudgements.*] Gods iudgements are of two sorts: first, his Commands; so called, because by them right is iudged, & discerned from vvrong. Next, his plagues executed vpon transgressors according to his word. *Dauid* here meanes of the first. Let men who haue not the like of *Dauids* desire, remember, that they whose hart cannot <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Such as are not moued by the first, shall be confounded by the second.</span> break for transgressing Gods word because they loue it, shall find the plagues of GOD to bruse their body, and breake their hart also. Let vs delight in the first sort of these iudgements, and the second shall neuer come vpon vs.

Now, that *Dauid* this way presents his desire, <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">A hart full of spirituall desires, is an argument of great grace.</span> is an effectuall manner of supplication; for desire of grace, is a great grace: *Non est potestatis nostrae desiderium sanctum, sed gratiae;* and the Lord <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Ambr.</span> hath bound himselfe by his promise to fulfill it. *Blessed are they vvho hunger and thirst for righteousnes,* <span class="cw-pb" id="p58" data-p="58" data-facs="36" title="Page 58 of the 1613 edition begins here (原书第 58 页起)"></span> *they shall be satisfied.* And againe; *The Lord will fulfill the desires of them that feare him. Cum oblectarinos viderit Deus cupiditate iudiciorum* <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Ambrose.</span> *suorum, sobrium auget affectum.* And therefore *Dauid* here presents his hart full of desires to the Lord, that he may fill it.

And here, because the profane world makes a <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Comfort against cōtempt of men, wherby they scorne the godly for sighing and teares.</span> scorne of the contrite disposition of the godlie, laughing at their teares, taunting them in their sighes and sobs; let vs looke heere to *Dauid.* If it cannot alway be an argument to mooue men to godlinesse, yet is it forcible to moue God to mercie. The broken hart, for the griefe of sin, and loue of righteousnes, how euer the world despise it, the Lord wil not despise it: yea, it is the sacrifice of God, acceptable to him in Christ Iesus.

<div class="cw-text" markdown="1">

VER. 21.

Thou hast destroyed the proud. Cursed are they that do erre from thy commandements.

</div>

HEre *Dauid* confirmes himselfe in godlines, <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">The begunne wrath of God on them, shold confirme vs against their contempt.</span> by meditation of the miserable estate of the wicked, who lead a life cōtrary to Gods word: wherof the sum is; The begun iudgements of God executed on the wicked, may let vs see the curse of God on them, who follow their footsteps. Walking in the trade of their sinnes, how-euer they seem to prosper & flourish for the present, there is a curse of God vpō them, which will consume and destroy them.

<span class="cw-pb" id="p59" data-p="59" data-facs="36" title="Page 59 of the 1613 edition begins here (原书第 59 页起)"></span>Begun, or present iudgements of God vpō the <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">But many wax worse with Lamech, when they see euill men spared.</span> wicked, should moue vs to abhor their sins. But in this great securitie we haue many *Lamechs,* few *Dauids. Lamech* saw the impunity of *Caine:* because he was not punished for his cruell murthering of his brother; therefore his hart was prouoked to the like impietie, and he reioyced in it. *I would slay a man in my wound, and a young man* <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Gene. 4. 24. Few become better with Dauid, when they see them punished.</span> *in mine hart: if Caine shall be avenged seauen fold, truly Lamech seauentie times seauen fold.* Thus in the pride and profaneness of his hart, did he mock, and abuse the patience of God. And so fareth it with many, of whom *Salomon* speakes; *Because iudgement is not speedily executed on the* <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Eccles.</span> *wicked, therefore the hart of the children of men is set in them to do euil.* But few are like *Dauid,* who when he saw *Vzzah* striken to death, for vnreuerent touching of the Arke, was mooued in his hart to a greater reuerence of God.

*The proud.*] This is a stile commonly giuen <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Wicked men cōmonly styled proud men, and why.</span> to the wicked: because as it is our oldest euill, so is it the strongest and first that striues in our corrupt nature, to cary men to a transgression of the borders appointed by the Lord. From the time that pride entred into *Adams* hart, that he would be higher then God had made him, he spared not to eate of the forbidden tree. And what else is the cause of all transgression, but that man in his ignorant pride, vvil haue his wil preferred to the will of God?

<span class="cw-pb" id="p60" data-p="60" data-facs="37" title="Page 60 of the 1613 edition begins here (原书第 60 页起)"></span>Satan in his presumption, vsurped to be equal <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Proud Satan hath made disciples prouder then himselfe.</span> with GOD; *Similis ero altissimo,* like vnto the most high: *Cum sit ne quissimus peiores tamen discipulos erudiuit.* He himselfe is a Prince of spirituall <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Esay 14.</span> <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Ambr.</span> wickednesse, yet hath he trained vp disciples more vvicked then himselfe; such as *That man of sinne:* who, as if it were little to be equall with GOD, extolls himselfe aboue GOD. And like him, are many blinded captiues of Satan, vvho in the pride of their heart, doe all they can, to subiect the Lord, his throne, his will, to their wicked and corrupt will.

These are fooles, and of all fooles the greatest, <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">The miserable condition of a proud man. Iam.</span> they set themselues as parties against the Lord, *for he resists the proud;* not considering that he is stronger then they, & they cannot stand before him. The Lord is the most high GOD: but it is <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">☞</span> not height makes a man stand before him. A notable example heereof, we haue in that Pharisee, who was not so farre before the Publican in his ovvne estimation, as he was behind him in the account of Christ, who iudgeth of things according as they are: *O quantum crimen superbiae, vt ei etiam* <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Ambrose.</span> <span class="cw-pb" id="p61" data-p="61" data-facs="37" title="Page 61 of the 1613 edition begins here (原书第 61 页起)"></span> *adulteria praeferantur.*

*Cursed are they which doe erre from thy commaundements.*] <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Euery error is dangerous, but proud error accursed.</span> Heere first we haue to see hovv these words must be vnderstood. The answer is easily made, if we conioyne his words together; The proud which erre, are cursed.

Heere then we must put a difference, between <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Sinnes of pride and of infirmitie, should be distinguished.</span> sinnes of pride, and of infirmitie: he that of rebellion and pride departs from Gods comman dements; not so, he that sinnes of weaknesse: in whom euery sin committed, increaseth a griefe for sinne, a hatred of sinne, and a care to vvithstand it; for these, there is no condemnation. These are not vnder the law, but vnder grace. Mercie alwaies waits on them, as a refreshing medicine to restore them, when of infirmitie they fall.

But as for the wicked, who sinne & are proud <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Curse of God on the wicked<span class="cw-gap" title="TCP: illegible in source（底本此处读不出）">▪</span> is like a secret consumption.</span> and impenitent in their sinnes, the curse of God is vpon them, though it be not seene at the first. Like a Moth, or secret cōsumption, it eates them vp; it shall deuoure their substance, & shal quickly turne their glory and prosperitie, into shame and confusion.

*DAuid,* beeing a young man, liued godlie <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">*Dauid* his appellation to God, from the wrongful iudgments of men.</span> in the Court of *Saul:* and for godlinesse was mocked and disdained of others. But from time they saw that *Saul* the King was displeased with *Dauid,* then did all his flatterers speake against *Dauid,* doing what they could to sley his honest name with calumnies and slaunders: And hee, borne downe with the iniquitie of time, commits his cause to the Lord; beseeching him, who knew his conscience, to cleere his innocencie. VER. 24.

Thy testimonies are my delight, & my Counsellers.

</div>

THe other protestation *Dauid* makes, is <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">The word, renders vs both counsell for gouernment, & pleasure for delectation.</span> of the great profit, and fruite hee had found in the vvord: that it was both a delight to him, and a counseller of him. *Saul* had his Nobles and Counsellers, by whom he ruled his affaires. *Dauid* protests, that he had no other for the men of his counsell, but the testimonies of God. These are two great benefits, which commonly men craue: Pleasure to refresh thē; Con̄sell to gouerne them. *Dauid* protests hee found them both in the word: and sends all other, who would haue them, to seeke them there where he found them.

As for ioy and recreation of mind, commonly <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Comfort gotten by other recreations, continues not.</span> men seeke it in other Cisterns; but with no good successe: for as a man in a hote Feuer, is <span class="cw-pb" id="p67" data-p="67" data-facs="40" title="Page 67 of the 1613 edition begins here (原书第 67 页起)"></span> eased no longer by drinking strong drinke, then he is in drinking of it; for then it seemes to coole him, but incontinent it increaseth his heat: so is it with the troubled and heauie hart, which seeks comfort in externall things; how-euer for a time they seeme to mitigate the heauinesse, they doe <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">☜</span> but increase it. Onely solid and permanent comfort must be drawne out of the fountaines of the word of GOD.

The other is wisedom: which vvithout Gods <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">No wisedome without the word.</span> word can neuer be obtained. As *Ieremie* spake of the wicked in his time, *They haue reiected the word of God, and what wisedome then is in them?* So is it true of all the wicked: The wisedome of this world is but foolishnes. *Achitophel* his end, vvith innumerable moe, may teach all men, that hee shall neuer be found wise, who is not godlie. The beginning of wisedome, is the feare of the Lord.
