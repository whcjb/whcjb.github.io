### <span class="cw-pb" id="p198" data-p="198" data-facs="106" title="Page 198 of the 1613 edition begins here (原书第 198 页起)"></span>CAPH.

<div class="cw-text" markdown="1">

VER. 81.

My soule fainteth for thy saluation: yet I wait for thy word.

</div>

<span class="cw-dropcap">D</span>*Auid* beeing sore troubled by wicked <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Desertions spirituall, doe sore cast downe the godly.</span> men, & finding that God delaied to comfort and deliuer him, was sore deiected & cast downe in his owne mind: so that his soule fainted, his eyes failed, and his body became like a bottle in the smoake. All hatred, and inimitie of man, is easily comported, where God shewes his fauorable face: but when the godly are sore troubled by men, and find not their soules comforted of God, their strength failes them. Euen as the body, wanting naturall helps to refresh it, becomes <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">As a body wanting meat fain<span class="cw-gap" title="TCP: illegible in source（底本此处读不出）">•</span>th: so &c.</span> faint, and falls in a swoune: so the soule, destitute of heauenly comfort, languisheth. For the godly liue by mercy; and can no more endure to want the sense of mercy, then the body can consist without those naturall means, which increase and conserue the life thereof.

Yet doth hee neuer so faint, but that some life <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">The godly neuer so faint, that they lose all life.</span> remaines in him; for he subioynes immediatlie, *I wait for thy word.* Waiting for comfort, is an <span class="cw-pb" id="p199" data-p="199" data-facs="106" title="Page 199 of the 1613 edition begins here (原书第 199 页起)"></span> action of faith, & an effect of life. As in the midst of Winter, there is a substance in the Oake and Elme, euen when it seemes to bee dead: so is it with the Christian; in his greatest extremities, <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">☜</span> some spark of life remaines in him. For the life o<span class="cw-gap" title="TCP: illegible in source（底本此处读不出）">•</span> Christ Iesus whereby hee liues, is of that nature that it cannot die: it may be weakened, but can not be extinguished. <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Our petitions should all be warranted by the word.</span>

*For thy word.*] In the first part of the verse, hee protested that he waited for Gods saluation; and now he saith, he waited for Gods word. Teaching vs first, not to seek any thing frō God, which his word warrants vs not to craue. And next, how we should giue such credit to the word of God, as to belieue it; euen then, when in our sense there is no likelihood of the performance of it.

<div class="cw-text" markdown="1">

VER. 82.

Mine eyes faile for thy promise: vvhen wilt thou comfort me?

</div>

IT is a customable manner of Gods working <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Answer of our prayers delaied many times, and why.</span> with his children, to delay the aunswere of their prayers, & suspend the performance of his promises: not because he is vnwilling to giue; but because he will haue them better prepared to receiue, *Tardiùs dando quod petimus instantiā nobis* <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Chrys. in Math.</span> *orationis indicit:* he is slow to giue that which we seek, that we should not seeke slowly, but may be wakened to instancie and feruencie in prayer, which he knows to be the seruice most acceptable <span class="cw-pb" id="p200" data-p="200" data-facs="107" title="Page 200 of the 1613 edition begins here (原书第 200 页起)"></span> vnto him, and most profitable vnto our selues. And for this cause, continuance in prayer is commended vnto vs by the Apostle.

<div class="cw-text" markdown="1">

VER. 83.

For I am like a bottle in the smoake: yet doe I not forget thy statutes.

</div>

HEe still insists in his former complaint, <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">The troubles of the mind distemper the body.</span> declaring how the greatnes of his inward anguish, had extenuated & worne the natural strength of his body, so that hee was becom like a bottle dryed in the smoak; his skin contracted, withered & wrinkled with the greatnes of his griefe. The like he hath Psa. 32. that the moisture of his body, was turned into the drouth of Sommer. The troubles of the mind affect the body, & distemper it: and the best way, in such cases, to mitigate bodily diseases, is to pacifie the mind.

But again, when we see the great anguish of *Dauids* <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Gods children exercised with sharp afflictions.</span> mind, and how his beautifull body was now become but a withered skin; let vs consider how how hardly the Lord deales with his children, whom he loues most dearly. He iudgeth vs in this world, that wee should not be condemned in the world to come. By the fire of affliction, he burns vp the superfluities of our nature, which in prosperity increase vpon vs, to the great hinderance of the work of our saluation. And againe, that he <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Reasons hereof giuen.</span> may make vs capable of heauenly cōfort, he takes <span class="cw-pb" id="p201" data-p="201" data-facs="107" title="Page 201 of the 1613 edition begins here (原书第 201 页起)"></span> carnal comforts away from vs: for so long as we are delighted with the one, we can neuer feele the consolation of the other. This should learn vs, not to be discouraged, when in the same maner God deales hardly with vs. His rods may be sharp: but his way is mercy. He may doe to his children, as *Ioseph* did to his brethren; speak roughly to them, and make himselfe strange toward them: but his louing affection cannot euer be hid from them.

<div class="cw-text" markdown="1">

VER. 84.

How many are the daies of thy seruaunt? when wilt thou execute iudgement on them that persecute me?

</div>

THis verse containes a supplication; wherin <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">The reason of Dauids supplication.</span> *Dauid* craues, that God would iudge between him & his enemies. The reason hee vseth, lurks in the Interrogation; *How manie are my daies?* My time is not long, my dayes are but few: then let them not passe comfortlesse; but let me see that thou art a God of iudgement (who wilt do according to thy word) and men: *There* <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Psalm. <span class="cw-gap" title="TCP: illegible in source（底本此处读不出）">•</span>8. 11.</span> *is fruit for the righteous: Verely, there is a God that iudgeth righteously on the earth.*

To shew the breuitie of mans life, he reckneth <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Mans life measured by dayes, not yeeres.</span> it not by yeers, but by daies. And this consideration of the shortnes of our life, *Dauid* vseth it as an argument, somtime to moue the Lord to compassion, and sometime to stirre vp himselfe to <span class="cw-pb" id="p202" data-p="202" data-facs="108" title="Page 202 of the 1613 edition begins here (原书第 202 页起)"></span> further piety & godliness: as we may see Psa. 39. And truly, it were good for vs, oftner then we doe, to thinke vpon this question of *Dauids, How* <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">A question alway to be remembred.</span> *many are my daies?* For we are deceiued vvith the shadow of this life, cōceiting it to be longer, then we shall find it to be: whereof it comes to passe, <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">☞</span> that men are prodigall of their daies, as if they would neuer be done. Their rents, their money, their garments, or any other thing they haue, they spend sparingly, & with moderation; onely they are wasters of their daies, as if they had *Methusalems* yeeres in a treasure. Let vs pray vvith *Moses,* for grace to number our dayes, that wee may apply our harts vnto wisedome. When wee look to the by-gone time, and see how wee haue <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">☜</span> misspent it; when we looke to the time to come, and see how vncertaine we are of it, let vs redeem the time we haue, to vse it well.

*When wilt thou execute.] Dauid* was far from <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Prayer against our enemies, how it should be framed.</span> hatred, crueltie, or priuate affection: for in all these his petitions he was the pen-man of the holy Ghost, and spake nothing of any priuate motion. Where, that wee may know how farre forth wee are to follow him in these, let vs remember, that God executes two sorts of iudgements vpon <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Such iudgements as are preparatiues to mercy, may be prayed for.</span> men: some are preparatiues to mercy; such as were inflicted vpon *Saul,* when hee went to persecute the Saints at *Damascus:* he was cast from his horse, and strooken with blindnesse: and for these we may pray, that God will execute them <span class="cw-pb" id="p203" data-p="203" data-facs="108" title="Page 203 of the 1613 edition begins here (原书第 203 页起)"></span> vpon them, who are sleeping in their sinnes; that rather they may be wakened by Gods iudgmēts, and moued to repent, then perish in their impietie. <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">But not those iudgements that are forerunners of wrath.</span> Others again of his iudgments, are but forerunners of that great and last iudgement, which he wil execute on all the wicked; such as were the punishments of *Caine* and *Iudas.* The first, works conuersion; the second, confusion; and for these wee can not pray against our enemies: because, howsoeuer their works be euill for the present, <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">☜</span> yet wee know not what the Lord may doe with them heereafter.

*Who persecute mee.] Dauid* was a godly man<span class="cw-gap" title="TCP: illegible in source（底本此处读不出）">▪</span> <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Godly men, euil recompenced of the world.</span> approued of God, and a profitable instrument to his King and Country. When *Saul* was vexed, he did mitigate his trouble with the Harpe: hee slew *Goliah,* and ouerthrew the Philistims: yet ye see how he is recompenced. Such commonlie is the reward which godly men gette in this world, at the hands of them, to whom they haue been instruments of greatest good.

And this persecution, many waies are the wicked <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Sundry sorts of persecution.</span> guiltie of. The railing of *Shimei* against *Dauid;* the mocking of *Isaac* by *Ismael:* these the world esteemes no sinnes, or very small; but the Lord ranks them in among bloudie sinnes, and calls them persecution: learning all men not to speake euill, or scorne the godly, least they fal into greater sinnes then they are aware of.

<div class="cw-text" markdown="1">

<span class="cw-pb" id="p204" data-p="204" data-facs="109" title="Page 204 of the 1613 edition begins here (原书第 204 页起)"></span>VER. 85.

The proud haue digged pits for mee; which is not after thy law.

</div>

HE insists stil in his complaint against his <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Enemies of Dauid described.</span> enemies; & describes them first frō the quality of their persons: They were proud. Next, from their labor & subtilty; They digged pits for him. Thirdly, from their manifest iniquitie; Their courses against him, were not according to Godslaw.

*The proud.* See ver. 69. 21. 51. 78. 85.

*Haue digged pits.*] Their labour and subtiltie <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Pridecan humble it selfe for a greater vantage.</span> whereby they oppugned *Dauid,* is heere noted. Whereby this seemes strange, that a proud man should be a digger of pits: but so it is, that pride for a time can submit it selfe, for a greater vantage ouer him whom it would tread vnder foot. *The wicked is so proud, that he seeks not God: yet hee* <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Psal. 10. 4. 10.</span> *croucheth and boweth, to cause heapes of the poore fall by his might.* So proud *Absalom* abased himselfe, to do all men reuerence; subiecting himselfe, to meanest subiects: that so hee might prepare a way to vsurpation ouer his king and father.

But mark; he saith not that he had fallen in the <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">The wicked are taken in their owne s<span class="cw-gap" title="TCP: illegible in source（底本此处读不出）">•</span>are.</span> pits which his enemies had digged. No, no: in Gods righteous iudgmēts, the wicked are snared in the work of their own hands, while the godly escape free. *He hath made a pit and digged it, and* <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Psalm. 7. 15.</span> *is fallen in the pit that he made: his mischiefe shall return vpon his own head, & his cruelty vpon his* <span class="cw-pb" id="p205" data-p="205" data-facs="109" title="Page 205 of the 1613 edition begins here (原书第 205 页起)"></span> *owne pate.* Thus *Haman* hanselled the gallowes <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Example hereof in Saul and Haman.</span> which he raised for *Mordecai:* and *Saul,* when he thought by subtilty to slay *Dauid* with the Philistims sword (when he sent him out to seeke two hundreth of their fore-skinnes in a dowrie) was disappointed of his purpose: but he himselfe at length was slaine by their sword.

*Which is not after thy law.*] As *Dauid* by this <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Comfort when we see wicked men are our enemies.</span> aggrauates the grosse impiety of his aduersaries; so doth he also greatly comfort himselfe. For why shall we be discouraged, to haue these men our enemies, of whom we clearely see, that neyther their persons nor actions are according to Gods lawe?

<div class="cw-text" markdown="1">

VER. 86.

All thy commandements are true: they persecute me falsly; helpe me.

</div>

HEere he reasons from the equity of his <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">The Word giues comfort against all trouble.</span> cause, & the iniquity of his enemies: & vpon these sends he vp his prayer, Lord *helpe me.* As for my enemies, they persecute me falsly and without a cause; as for me, I know thy commandements are all true, and that they cannot be deceiued who depend vpon them, nor yet prosper that are against them. He shal neuer want comfort in trouble, who cleaues vnto the word of God: who euer be against vs, keepe it on our side, and it shall be well with vs.

<span class="cw-pb" id="p206" data-p="206" data-facs="110" title="Page 206 of the 1613 edition begins here (原书第 206 页起)"></span>Againe it is to be marked, that he declines no <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Profitable for vs to be exercised with afflictions.</span> persecution, but seeks Gods help in it. He knew, that all they who will line godly in Christ, must suffer persecution; *Deuotionem fidei, praelia persequuntur, & Cito fides inexercitata languescit;* Faith not exercised with tentation easily languishes: yea, sith the Lord chastises euery one whom he loues, hee may iustly suspect himselfe, who liues without a crosse. *Si desint certamina, vereor ne deesse videatur, quicertare desideret;* If <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Amb. in Ps. 119</span> there be a man who hath no battell, it is to be feared, least he be such a one as desires not to fight: <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">☞</span> and so what can he be but a captiue of Satan?

But in al our persecutions let vs euer take heed <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">We should take heed to the cause for which we are troubled. *Cyprian.*</span> that our cause be righteous, and we may say, *They persecute me falsly: potest enim quis persecutiones pati & non iniustè:* and it is euer *Causa non poena facit Martyrem.* But let none of you suffer as a murtherer, or a thiefe, or an euill doer, or a busie body: if any man suffer as a Christian, let him not <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">1. Pet. 4.</span> be ashamed, but glorifie God in this behalfe.

*Helpe mee.*] Profane mockers thinke that a man is weake when he is driuen to Gods helpe; but indeed then he is strongest when he is weakest in him selfe, and seeks help in his God. So long as we depend on the arme of flesh, whether it be our own or others, the Lord will do the lesse for vs: but when distrusting our selues we relie vppon him, then is he strongest to worke for vs.

<div class="cw-text" markdown="1">

<span class="cw-pb" id="p207" data-p="207" data-facs="110" title="Page 207 of the 1613 edition begins here (原书第 207 页起)"></span>VER. 87.

They had almost consumed me vpon earth; but I for sooke not thy precepts.

</div>

IN this verse are two things; a complaint of the malice of his enemies, & a protestation of <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Malice of the wicked is insatiable against the godly.</span> his constant affection toward the word of God. In the first he complains, they had not only troubled him, but almost consumed him: the malicious cruelty of the wicked is insatiable, it cannot be quenched: nay, not by death; for euen after death they rent both the name and the bodies of godly men. *Dauid* was not thus farre deliuered <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">They rent both their names and their bodies after they are dead.</span> into his enemies hands; but he complaines, that their malice almost had vndone him. We mark it for this end, that we may know it is Gods great prouidence, that at any time we liue among the wicked, and are not consumed by them.

Secondly, we marke here the great weakenesse and infirmity of Gods children, who oftentimes <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">It is mans infirmity to be cast down with tentations cōming from men more then those that come from God.</span> are more discouraged w*th* those tentations which come immediatly from men, then with those others which com immediatly from God; in the one they stand strong; in the other they faint and are ouercome. Surely because wee are in a great parte carnall, therefore is it, that the want of mans comfort and countenance commoues vs. If we were as we should be, or as *Nazianzen* <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Nazian.</span> compares vs, like Rockes in the Sea; what <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">☜</span> would we regard the wind of mens mouthes, or raging waues of their turbulent affections? As an <span class="cw-pb" id="p208" data-p="208" data-facs="111" title="Page 208 of the 1613 edition begins here (原书第 208 页起)"></span> arrow shot at a stone, returnes to him who sent it, or otherwise fals downe to the ground: so an iniurie done to a spirituall man, who is built like a liuely stone vpon the Rocke Christ Iesus, returns to the doer, but troubles not the receiuer. If it be otherwise, that we consume and faint for the iniuries of men, all this weakenesse proceeds from our vnregenerate nature.

Thirdly, we marke here the Lords dispensation, <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Why God will haue his children brought very low by affliction.</span> in his manner of working with his children; who not onely suffers them to be troubled, but brought so lowe by trouble, that in their owne sense they are almost consumed. Both in outward & inward troubles doth the Lord humble them so farre, that they are brought euen to the doore of death, that so their faith and his truth may be the more manifested. Thus the Apostle protests, that he receiued in himselfe the sentence of <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">2. Cor. <span class="cw-gap" title="TCP: illegible in source（底本此处读不出）">•</span>.</span> death; all for this end, that hee might learne not to trust in himselfe, but in God, who raiseth the dead. For so long as in danger there is any hope of remedy, our infidelity casts her eye a wrong <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Infidelity readie to goe <span class="cw-gap" title="TCP: illegible in source（底本此处读不出）">•</span> whooring frō God.</span> way: but when we are brought to such extremity, that with *Peter* we begin to sinke in affliction, then we looke to the Lord; we c<span class="cw-gap" title="TCP: illegible in source（底本此处读不出）">•</span>aue the helpe of his hand, and hee releeueth vs; and wee cannot but acknowledge that our d<span class="cw-gap" title="TCP: illegible in source（底本此处读不出）">•</span>iuerance is come from him onely: and so by the greatnesse of our trouble, our faith is <span class="cw-gap" title="TCP: illegible in source（底本此处读不出）">•</span>roborate, and the praise of Gods truth more <span class="cw-gap" title="TCP: illegible in source（底本此处读不出）">〈◊〉</span> manifested.

<div class="cw-text" markdown="1">

<span class="cw-pb" id="p209" data-p="209" data-facs="111" title="Page 209 of the 1613 edition begins here (原书第 209 页起)"></span>VER. 88.

Quicken mee, according to thy louing kindnesse: so shall I keepe the testimonies of thy mouth.

</div>

MAny a time in this Psalme doth *Dauid* <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Desertion is like death to the godly.</span> make this petition: & it seems strange, that so often hee should acknowledge himselfe a dead man, and desire God to quicken him. But so it is vnto the child of God; euerie desertion, and decay of strength, is a death: so desirous are they to liue vnto GOD, that vvhen they faile in it, and find any inabilitie in their soules to serue GOD<span class="cw-gap" title="TCP: illegible in source（底本此处读不出）">▪</span> as they would, they account themselues but dead, and pray the Lord to quicken them.

*According to thy louing kindnesse.*] Hee oppones <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Gods kindness is our defence against mans malice.</span> Gods kindnesse to his enemies malice; and it is very comfortable: I am troubled with their malice for thy sake; and therefore I craue to be refreshed with thy kindnesse: acknowledging, that in Gods kindnesse, there is comfort enough against all the malice of wicked men whatsoeuer; according to that opposition vvhich hee makes in another place: *Why boastest thou thy selfe* <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Psalme 52. 1.</span> *in thy malice? the louing kindnesse of the Lord indureth daily.*
