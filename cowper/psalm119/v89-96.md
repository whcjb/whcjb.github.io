### <span class="cw-pb" id="p212" data-p="212" data-facs="113" title="Page 212 of the 1613 edition begins here (原书第 212 页起)"></span>LAMED.

<div class="cw-text" markdown="1">

VER. 89.

O Lord, thy vvord endureth for euer in heauen.

</div>

<span class="cw-dropcap">I</span>N the last Section, *Dauid* complained <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">A comfortable meditation of the eternitie of Gods word.</span> to God of the malicious doings of his enemies; he comes now to shew what sustained him in his troubles: to weet, the certaintie and eternitie of the word of God; by the which he considers, that both heauen and earth were sustained: and thereof concludes, that so long as he trusted to Gods word, he could not faile. So that he giues this glory to the Lord, that his word onely kept him, that he perished not in his affliction.

*Thy word.] Dauid* here makes a secret opposition <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">The word of God, opposed to the word of man.</span> between the words of his enemies, and Gods word. Sore was he troubled with the long toūgs of the wicked. *Hoc enim est opus diaboli, vt seruos Dei mendacio laceret, & falsis opinionibus gloriosum* <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Cypr. lib. 4. Epist. 2.</span> *nomen infamet; vt qui conscientiae suaeluce clarescunt, alienis rumoribus sor didentur.* It is true which *Philo* saith, *Veritas opinione melior est, non tamen est negligenda fama:* truth is better then opinion; <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Philo.</span> <span class="cw-pb" id="p213" data-p="213" data-facs="113" title="Page 213 of the 1613 edition begins here (原书第 213 页起)"></span> and it makes not much, what wrong opi <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">The good name of a christian should be regarded: and why.</span> nion be conceiued of vs: yet the same or good name of a man is not to be neglected (specially if he be in a publick office) that by his name he may do good vnto others. But because this cannot be obtained, and Innocencie itselfe cannot hold off the scourge of the tongue, wee must oppone the word of God, to the words of wicked men; resting <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Innocence defends vs not frō the calumnies of m<span class="cw-gap" title="TCP: illegible in source（底本此处读不出）">•</span>n.</span> with *Io<span class="cw-gap" title="TCP: illegible in source（底本此处读不出）">•</span>* in this, *That our witnesse is in heauen:* and with S. *Paul,* in the testimonie of a good conscience. Then shall we not be discouraged for the words of man, hauing both Gods word, and our owne conscience, to warrant vs.

And for our farther comfort, let vs remember <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Words of men most effectless, when they are stoutest.</span> how the words of men, when they are greatest, are most vaine and effectlesse; and on the contrarie, that GOD is as good, as his word; yea, and better, doing more then can be expressed by word.

It was a proud word of *Iezabel, God doe so to me, and more also, if I haue not the head of Elijah ere tomorrow at this time:* but the dogs got her head, and shee got not the head of *Elijah.* Such also were the words of *Sennacherib,* boasting by *Rabsache,* that he would make the inhabitants of Ierusalem to drink their own piss: but these were vaine words, and vanished with the wind. Onely the counsell of the Lord stands: what hee determines, shal be done; and as he speakes, it shall assuredly come to passe.

<span class="cw-pb" id="p212-2" data-p="212" data-facs="114" title="Page 212 of the 1613 edition begins here (原书第 212 页起)"></span> <span class="cw-pb" id="p213-2" data-p="213" data-facs="114" title="Page 213 of the 1613 edition begins here (原书第 213 页起)"></span> <span class="cw-pb" id="p214" data-p="214" data-facs="115" title="Page 214 of the 1613 edition begins here (原书第 214 页起)"></span> *In heauen.*] By this, *Basile* vnderstands *Angelorum* <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">What is meant here by heauē.</span> *coetus coelum inhabitantes, qui diuin<span class="cw-gap" title="TCP: illegible in source（底本此处读不出）">•</span>m legem custodiunt:* the company of innumerable Angels, who obserue the law of God. But the words following, declare hee meanes the frame of the same visible works; wherein the constancie of Gods truth doth appeare most manifestly.

For two causes then doth *Dauid* so speak. First, <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">The changes of this world, cumber our faith.</span> to draw vs from looking to the instabilitie of things heere vpon earth (wherein are restlesse changes and vicissitudes) to a consideration of the stabilitie of Gods decree in heauen. *Vtcunque versentur res humanae, & omnia sint varijs inclinationibus* <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Vatab.</span> *obnoxia, tamen verbum Dei non est subiectum ordini naturali:* In earth, the word of God is not onely controuerted, but contradicted <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Then it is good to looke vp vnto heauē.</span> and resisted; and the state of things so mutable, that oftentimes the wicked prosper and are exalted, the godly cast downe, and sore oppressed. To vphold vs against this tentation, let vs looke vp, & cast the anchor of our soules within the vaile: <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Heb. 6. 19.</span> there we shal see an vnchangeable decree of God, which shall change all these things againe, and bring to the end spoken of in his word.

The other cause why he so speaks, is, that this <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">The visible frame of heauen, a witnesse of Gods eternall truth.</span> visible frame of the heauen, stands as an eternall witnes of the veritie of Gods word, that hee will not retreat nor call back that which hee speaks: for he once commanded the heauens to be, and incontinent they were, and euer since continue. <span class="cw-pb" id="p215" data-p="215" data-facs="115" title="Page 215 of the 1613 edition begins here (原书第 215 页起)"></span> Thus wee see how the godly profit by looking into the works of God; euer drawing some spirituall consideration out of them: but of this hereafter.

<div class="cw-text" markdown="1">

VER. 90.

Thy truth is from generation to generation: thou hast laid the foundation of the earth, and it abideth.

</div>

AS hee collected the certaintie of Gods word, by the indurance of heauē; so now <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">The creatures cannot teach the way of saluation, but cōfirme vs in it.</span> he confirmes it, by considering the foundation of the earth. Sith the foundation of the earth, made by the word of God, abides sure, shal we not think, that the foundation of our saluation, <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">☜</span> laid in Christ Iesus, is much more sure? Though the creatures cannot teach vs the way of saluation (for, that we must learn by the word) yet doe they confirme that which the word saith. *Thus saith the Lord, vvho giues the Sunne for a* <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Iere. 31. 35.</span> *light to the day, and the courses of the Moone and Starres for a light to the night; If these ordinances depart out of my sight, then shall the seede of Israel cease from beeing a Nation before mee for euer.* As there *Ieremie* gathers the stabilitie <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">The certaintie of our saluatiō</span> of the Church, from the stabilitie of the creatures: so here *Dauid* cōfirmes the certainty of our saluation, by the most certaine & vnchangeable course of the creatures: and both of them are <span class="cw-pb" id="p216" data-p="216" data-facs="116" title="Page 216 of the 1613 edition begins here (原书第 216 页起)"></span> amplified by Christ Iesus; *Heauen & earth may passe, but one iot of Gods word shal not fall to the ground.* Let vs therefore be strengthened in the faith, and giue glory to God.

*Tho<span class="cw-gap" title="TCP: illegible in source（底本此处读不出）">•</span> hast laid.*] This worke of God is often mentioned in holy Scripture, to commend the <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">The glory of God shineth in the worke of creation. Iob. 38.</span> glory of Gods wisdom and power. Yea, the Lord himselfe glories in it: as ye see in his speech to *Iob; Where was<span class="cw-gap" title="TCP: illegible in source（底本此处读不出）">•</span> thou when I laid the foundation of the earth? declare, if thou can, who laid the measures thereof? who first stretched the line ouer it? whervpon are the foundations of it set? or who layd the corner stone therof?* He hath founded it without <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Earth founded without a foūdation.</span> a foundation: for the pillars of the earth, mentioned by *Anna,* are no other but his word and decree. But alas! how great is our stupiditie? vvee walk vpon earth, & it beareth vs vp: but we neuer consider, how God sustaineth it by his mightie power, that we may giue glory to him.

*And it abides.*] As the Lord *by his vvord made the vvorlds; so beares hee vp all things by* <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Creation as a mother; Prouidence a nurse conseruing things created.</span> *his mightie word.* Creation is as the mother, and Prouidence the nurse & conseruer of al the works of God. God is not like man; for hee, when hee hath made a worke, cannot maintaine it: hee <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">☞</span> buildeth a shippe, and cannot saue it from shipwrack; hee edifies a house, but cannot keepe it from decay: it is otherwise with God; we daily see his conseruing power, vpholding his creatures: vvhich should confirme vs, that hee will <span class="cw-pb" id="p217" data-p="217" data-facs="116" title="Page 217 of the 1613 edition begins here (原书第 217 页起)"></span> not cast vs off, nor suffer vs to perish (sith we are the works of his hands) if wee doe depend vpon him, and giue him the glory of our Creator, Conseruer, and Redeemer.

<div class="cw-text" markdown="1">

VER. 91.

They continue to this day by thine ordinance: for all are thy seruaunts.

</div>

THe same argument by which *Dauid* here <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Out of the wholsom word profane men (like wasps) gather poyson.</span> proues the truth of Gods word, is vsed by profane mockers to improue it. *Where is* (say they) *the promise of his comming? for since the fathers died, all things continue alike from the* <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">2. Pet. 3. 4.</span> *beginning of the Creation.* There is so great difference between a godly, and a godlesse hart, that where the one of euery thing learnes to be more godly; the other learnes to be more profane: like poysonable Waspes, who out of most wholsome hearbs, gather nothing but poyson.

*For all are thy seruants.*] From the Angel to <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">All creatures, frō the Angel to the worme, serue the Lord of Hosts.</span> the worme, all serue him: he is the Lord of Hosts; let vs also serue him, and then shall they serue vs. But it is both sinne and shame, that where the raging water, and furious fire serue God at his commaund, onely man, made to his own image, disobeyes him. And therefore againe, feareful is the recompence; that where it is said, The Lord <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Psalm.</span> reioyceth in all his works, of man onely the Lord said, *It repents mee that I made man.* <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Gene. 6.</span>

<span class="cw-pb" id="p218" data-p="218" data-facs="117" title="Page 218 of the 1613 edition begins here (原书第 218 页起)"></span>The Lord grant it may repent vs, that euer we offended so good a God: and so the Lord may be pacified toward vs in Christ Iesus.

<div class="cw-text" markdown="1">

VER. 92.

Except thy law had been my delight, I should now haue perished in mine affliction.

</div>

HE now declares vnto vs what profit hee <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Sith other creatures are vpholdē by his word, much more the Christian.</span> made of his former meditatiō: to weet, that the certaintie of Gods word kept him, that he perished not in his affliction. Sith he saw it was a sure and powerful word, whereby other creatures were sustained, he collects it could neuer faile him: & so leaning vpon it, he foūd him selfe vpholden by it in his greatest afflictions.

Let vs learne of *Dauid,* to seeke our comfort in <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Comfort which is not from Gods word, brings two great euills.</span> time of trouble, out of the word of GOD: for all other comfort, which is sought with neglect of this comfort, brings two great euils. First, it encreaseth griefe, how-euer for a time it seeme to mitigate it. And next, it leaues a man destitute, when he hath most need of comfort. Onelie the comfort of Gods word bides with vs, when all other comfort for sakes vs.

*My delight.*] The word signifieth delights, in <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">The word hath comfort for euery estate of life.</span> the plurall number. Many were the sorowes of *Dauids* life: but against them all, he found as many comforts & delectations in Gods word. With such varietie of holy wisedom, hath God penned <span class="cw-pb" id="p219" data-p="219" data-facs="117" title="Page 219 of the 1613 edition begins here (原书第 219 页起)"></span> his word, that it hath conuenient comfort for euery state of life: & therfore the children of God account nothing so deere as it; they preferre it to their appointed foode. Yea, as the body cannot liue out of the owne element of aire; so cannot the soule of a godly man, without comfort of the <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">An answer to worldlings, who say they find no delight in Gods word.</span> word. Where, if it be obiected to vs by worldlings, Where are those manifold delights which ye say are in the word? we hear nothing but one & the selfe same thing repeated vnto vs: The best answer to them is, *Come and see. Taste* and *consider* <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">☜</span> how gracious the Lord is: and yee shal find, that euery repetitiō of it brings new delight. And this they may learne by their naturall affection toward bodily foode: which is so great, that the same kind of foode, where-vpon they feede this day, they long for it againe to morrow; and with a new appetite, long to haue renewed euery day. And if they were as well acquainted with the spirituall life, their affection would be, in the same manner, renewed euery day toward the word of God. For the truth is, that no Monarch in the world can furnish his table with such varietie of delicates, as are offred to vs in the treasures of Gods word.

*Perished in mine affliction.] Dauid* was a godly <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Men fruitful in godlinesse, by affliction made more fruitfull Iohn 15.</span> man, and deerely beloued of the Lord his GOD; yet is hee sore humbled by affliction: for euen those branches which are fruitfull, God will purge, to make them more fruitfull.

<span class="cw-pb" id="p220" data-p="220" data-facs="118" title="Page 220 of the 1613 edition begins here (原书第 220 页起)"></span>Our nature is so rebellious, that without sore afflictions, it cannot be tamed & subdued. Stones cannot be squared for palace-worke, without strokes of the hammer; gold can not be purged and prepared for work, without fire; neither is corne separated from the chaffe, without wind. We are Gods corne: let vs abide the wind of tentation, to clense vs from the chaffe of our corruption. Wee must suffer the fire of affliction, that <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">☞</span> we may be fined, and made vessels of honour for the house of God. And we must be content, that the hammer of God strike vpon vs, to beat away our proud lumps, that so we may be squared, and made ready, like liuely stones, to be laid in heauenly Ierusalem. More of this, see ver. 50. 52.

<div class="cw-text" markdown="1">

VER. 93.

I will neuer forget thy precepts: for by them thou hast quickned me.

</div>

THis verse hath a protestatiō of his thankfulnes; <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">None cōtemne Gods word, but such as haue gotten no benefit by it. *Basile* in Psal. 119.</span> he had found life in Gods word, and he resolues neuer to forget it: *Experientia edoct us iustificationes tu as producere vitā, inextinguibilem illarū memoriā seruabo.* It is no maruel, that they who neuer got life by the word of God, haue no delight in it: the excuses of men here auaile not. Certain it is, the cause of all contempt of Gods word, is from this, that men neuer found life, nor comfort by it. Whereof see Sect. 1.

<span class="cw-pb" id="p221" data-p="221" data-facs="118" title="Page 221 of the 1613 edition begins here (原书第 221 页起)"></span>But it is worthy to be marked, that he saith <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">It is not the Word that quickens, but God by his Word.</span> not, the Word quickned him; but God by the Word quickned him. The Word was preached at one time, by one Preacher S. *Paul,* to many people assembled together at *Philippi:* but many heard it who got not life by it; onely *Lydia* by that Sermon had her heart opened. *Paul* may plant, *Apollo* may water; but God must giue the increase. Let vs thanke God for the word he hath giuen vs; and let vs pray to God that grace may be ioyned with it: so with the Gospell, hauing the blessing of the Gospell, it shall become the power of God to our saluation.

<div class="cw-text" markdown="1">

VER. 94.

I am thine, saue me: for I haue sought thy precepts.

</div>

MAny reasons hath *Dauid* vsed hitherto, <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Sith man is carefull to keep that which is his owne, shall we thinke that God loues not those who are his owne?</span> to mooue the Lord to bee mercifull to him: but none more forcible then this; *I am thine.* A strong reason indeed, where man in a good conscience can vse it: for if we who are euill and weake, haue a care of euery thing which is ours, that we will not (if we may) suffer it to perish; euen our garments, and our houses, and our cattell, we haue a care to conserue them, because they are our owne: how great may we thinke is the Lords care toward his owne, whom he tenders with greater compassion thē any earthly father <span class="cw-pb" id="p222" data-p="222" data-facs="119" title="Page 222 of the 1613 edition begins here (原书第 222 页起)"></span> can doe to his children?

But howsoeuer this seeme *facilis vox & communis,* <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Euery man c<span class="cw-gap" title="TCP: illegible in source（底本此处读不出）">•</span>n not say to the Lord, I am <span class="cw-gap" title="TCP: illegible in source（底本此处读不出）">•</span>hine.</span> *Tuus sum:* as easie a voyce as it is common, to say, *I am thine;* yet few are they, who in a good conscience may say it. *Mentitur enim* <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Basil. in Ps. 119</span> *peccator, si se seruum Dei nominet:* A man in his sinnes, lies, if hee say that hee is the seruant of God; for to what-euer thing a man giues obedience, vnto that same is hee a seruant. O how <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Rom. 6. 16.</span> many people, and nations, and wealthy men are there in the world, *Qui paupertatem putant Domino seruire,* who thinke it a pouertie <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Ambrose.</span> to serue GOD! Hee who is aboue all seemes nothing to them: and they seeke other things with such insatiable affection, as if the Lorde Iesus, in whom are all things, were not sufficient for them. Can these men say to the Lord, *I am thine?*

But these men are not the worst: for yee shall <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Yea, rather men now are so profane, that euery kinde of si<span class="cw-gap" title="TCP: illegible in source（底本此处读不出）">•</span>ne may say vnto them, Thou art mine.</span> finde now a bastard Christian, so profane, that when all vices are gathered, euery one may say to him, *Thou art mine: Quomodo ergo qui hui<span class="cw-gap" title="TCP: illegible in source（底本此处读不出）">•</span>smodi est, potest dicere Christo, Tuus sum?* How then may such a man as he say vnto Christ, *I am* <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">☞</span> *thine?* Concupiscence, Auarice, Ambition, and the rest vpon iust reasons of seruice, done by him to them, challenge him to be theirs. Of such a man it may be spoken that was said of *Constantinus Copronymus; Eum nee Christianum esse,* <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Cent.</span> *nee Iudaeum, nee Paganum, sed colluuiem quandam* <span class="cw-pb" id="p223" data-p="223" data-facs="119" title="Page 223 of the 1613 edition begins here (原书第 223 页起)"></span> *impietatis,* That he was neyther a Christian, nor a Iew, nor a Pagan; but a confused masse of all impietie. And how then can such a one say to the Lord, *I am thine?*

But if so they will say, let them receiue their <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">How the Lord acknowledges not profane men to be his.</span> answere; *Away ye workers of iniquitie:* hee is not alway mine, that saith Lorde, Lorde. Wicked men may so say, *Sed vo<span class="cw-gap" title="TCP: illegible in source（底本此处读不出）">•</span>e non corde;* but it is with their tongue, not with their heart. As *Iudas* euen then pretended that he was one of Christs, when Satan had entred into him: but the aduersary iustly might challenge him by the like of these speeches, which by *Ambrose* properly are ascribed to him; *Non est* <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">How Satan challenged Iudas as his ow<span class="cw-gap" title="TCP: illegible in source（底本此处读不出）">•</span>.</span> *Tuus, Iesu, sed mens est: quae mea sunt, cogitat, qua mea sunt in pectore volutat, tecum epulatur, & me<span class="cw-gap" title="TCP: illegible in source（底本此处读不出）">•</span>um pascitur: à te panem, à me pecuniam accepit, tecum bibit, & mihi sanguinem tuum vendit:* He is not thine, said Satan to <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">☜</span> the Lord Iesus, he is mine; his thoughts and cogitations are mine; he eats with thee, but is fedde by me; he takes bread from thee, but mony from me; he drinkes with thee, and sel<span class="cw-gap" title="TCP: illegible in source（底本此处读不出）">•</span>es thy bloud to me. A fearfull conuiction of men, who giue their word to Christ, professing his name; but their workes to Satan, doing what hee commaunds them. These men cannot say with *Dauid, I am thine, O Lord.*

*For I haue sought.*] See here how *Dauid* qualifies his protestation: from his earnest affection to <span class="cw-pb" id="p224" data-p="224" data-facs="120" title="Page 224 of the 1613 edition begins here (原书第 224 页起)"></span> the word of God, hee proues that hee was Gods <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">How Dauid proues that he was Gods man.</span> man and his owne seruant. It is not wordes, but affections and actions, which must proue vs to be the Lords. *Tuus sum, quia id solum quod tuum est quasiui:* I am thine, because I sought nothing but that which is thine, and how I might please thee. *Mihi in tuis iustificationibus est omne patrimonium:* in the obseruance of thy precepts is all my patrimony.

<div class="cw-text" markdown="1">

VER. 95.

The wicked haue waited for me, to destroy me: but I will consider thy Testimonies.

</div>

TWo things againe he notes in his enemies: <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">A great miracle that the sheepe of Christ are preserued in the midst of rauening Wolues.</span> diligence, in waiting all occasions whereby to doe him euill, and cruelty vvithout mercy; for their purpose was to destroy him: wherin, still we see how restlesse and insatiable is the malice of the wicked against the godly. *Daniel* his preseruation in the Lions den was a great miracle: but it is no lesse a maruellous vvorke of God, that the godly who are the flock of Christ, are daily preserued, in the midst of the vvicked; who are but rauening Wolues, & thirst for the bloud of the Saints of God, hauing a cruell purpose in their heart if they might performe it, vtterly to destroy them. When we see them disappointed, let vs giue thankes with the Iewes; *If the Lord had not beene on our side (may Israel* <span class="cw-pb" id="p225" data-p="225" data-facs="120" title="Page 225 of the 1613 edition begins here (原书第 225 页起)"></span> *now say) If the Lord had not beene on our side,* <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Psal. 124.</span> *when men rose vp against vs, then had they swallowed vs quick, when their wrath was kindled against vs: but praised be the Lord, who hath not giuen vs a pray to their teeth.*

*But I will consider.*] The cōsideration of Gods word, is a strong stay against all temptations. See verse 9. 15. 59. 95. 159.

<div class="cw-text" markdown="1">

VER. 96.

I haue seene an end of all perfection: but thy commaundements are exceeding large.

</div>

IN the conclusion of this Section, hee compares <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">The vanitie & instabilitie of all earthly pleasures.</span> Gods word, with all other most perfect and excellent things that are in the vvorld; and hee sheweth how they shall faile and vanish, but the word of the Lord endureth. *Nihil tam perfectum, vel absolutum est omniex parte, quod finem suum non habeat.* Experience lets vs see this to be a truth: for there is no day so pleasant, but <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">☜</span> a night puts end to it; no Sommer so fruitful, but a barren Winter ouertakes it; no body so pleasant and liuely, but death destroyes it; frequent eating and drinking in the dayes of *N<span class="cw-gap" title="TCP: illegible in source（底本此处读不出）">•</span>h,* but the flood came and tooke them all away; great mirth among the Philistims vpon their Patrons holy-day, but their banquetting house became <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Iudg. 16.</span> their buriall.

<span class="cw-pb" id="p226" data-p="226" data-facs="121" title="Page 226 of the 1613 edition begins here (原书第 226 页起)"></span>The Monarch of *Babel,* that golden head, had <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Esay 14. 1<span class="cw-gap" title="TCP: illegible in source（底本此处读不出）">•</span>.</span> feet of clay, and in the end, wormes spred vnder him, and wormes couered him. All the pomp of the world is like the gourd of *Ionas,* flourishing one day, and fading another. The wind shall cary <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Psalm. 1.</span> away the vngodly like chaffe: all their most perfect pleasures, are like a light thing bound vp in the wings of the wind, which is easily caried away.

When we come to be men, we laugh at those <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">A cleere declaration of the vanitie of this life.</span> things wee did when we were children: for they, as wee see, take great delight to build houses to themselues in the streets, of shells and stones, and delight to behold them; which albeit they were <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Chrys. in Math. hom. 24.</span> able to stand, yet could they not profit them. We <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">☞</span> are not yet come to the perfect age of Christian men: but when we shall be, then shall wee vnderstand how foolish now men are, whose care is to increase their rents, and inlarge their sumptuous buildings, *Quae ciuem coeli capere non possunt:* which cannot lodge a Citizen of heauen. Let vs therefore so vse this world, as if wee vsed it not; possessing it so this day, as ready to part with it to morrow.

*But thy commandements.*] There's the other <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">The word of God is called large, because the comfort therof indures. *Euthy.* in Psalm. 119.</span> member of the opposition. Hee calls the commaundements or word of God exceeding large; *Quia cos omnes recreat, & corda corum dilatat, qui in angus<span class="cw-gap" title="TCP: illegible in source（底本此处读不出）">•</span>ijs, & afflictionibus versantur:* because it refresheth them all, and inlargeth their <span class="cw-pb" id="p227" data-p="227" data-facs="121" title="Page 227 of the 1613 edition begins here (原书第 227 页起)"></span> harts who are in distresse and affliction. And againe, it is so large, that it hath none end, *Propter amplitudinem suam fideles nunquam deseret:* and the amplitude thereof is so great, that it neuer <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Whē all world ly comforts fai'e.</span> failes the faithfull. Worldly riches serue worldly men so long as they liue, and make some seruice also in conueying their bodies with a pompous funerall to the graue: but there it leaues them, and goes no further with them. Onely the word of God sustaines the godly against all tentations, so long as they liue; it conuoies them vnto death; and the comfort therof abides with them for euer when they are dissolued.
