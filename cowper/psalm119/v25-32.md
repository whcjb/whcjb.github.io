### <span class="cw-pb" id="p68" data-p="68" data-facs="41" title="Page 68 of the 1613 edition begins here (原书第 68 页起)"></span>DALETH.

<div class="cw-text" markdown="1">

VER. 25.

My soule cleaueth to the dust: quicken me according to thy word.

</div>

<span class="cw-dropcap">M</span>Any states of life did *Dauid* change: <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">The word of God is conuenient for euery state of life.</span> but his hart neuer changed from the Lord, & the loue of his word. In his doubts, the word was his counseller; in his griefs, the word was his comforter: therefore was hee not cast downe, or overcome vvith griefe. In his greatest prosperitie, the word was his greater ioy: therefore vvas hee not puft vp with pleasure; teaching vs, that in euery state of life we shall find comfort, if we will rule our life by the word.

In this verse, *Dauid* hath a complaint; *My soule cleaueth to the dust:* And a prayer; *Quicken* <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">*Dauid* sore troubled with a spirituall oppression.</span> *mee according to thy vvord.* The prayer, beeing vvell considered, shall teach vs the meaning of the complaint: that it was not as some thinke, any hard bodily estate vvhich grieued him, but a very sore spirituall oppression (as I may call it) bearing downe his soule; that where he should haue moūted vp toward heauē, he was <span class="cw-pb" id="p69" data-p="69" data-facs="41" title="Page 69 of the 1613 edition begins here (原书第 69 页起)"></span> pressed downe to the earth, and was so clogged with earthly cogitations, or affections, or perturbations, that hee could not mount vp. His <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Tentations of the godly, somtimes cannot be told: sometimes it is not expedient they should be told.</span> particular tentation hee expresseth not: for the children of God many times are in that estate, that they cannot tell their own griefs; and somtime so troubled, that it is not expedient, albeit they might, to expresse them to others.

And heereof wee learne, how that vvhich the <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">In a worldling the very heauenly part is becom earthly.</span> worldling counts wisedome, to the Christian is folly: what is ioy to the one, is griefe to the other. The ioy of a vvorldling, is to cleaue vnto the earth; when he gripes it surest, hee thinks himselfe happiest; for it is his portion: to take heed to his worldly affaires, and haue his minde vpon them (in his estimation) is onely wisedom. For the Serpents curse is vpon him, he creepes on the earth, and licks the dust all the daies of his life. This is the miserable condition of the vvicked, that euen their heauenly soule is become earthly. <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Ambrose.</span> *Qui secundum corporis appetentiam vi<span class="cw-gap" title="TCP: illegible in source（底本此处读不出）">•</span>it ca<span class="cw-gap" title="TCP: illegible in source（底本此处读不出）">•</span> est, etiam anima eorum car<span class="cw-gap" title="TCP: illegible in source（底本此处读不出）">•</span> est;* as the Lord spake of those who perished in the Deluge, that they were but flesh<span class="cw-gap" title="TCP: illegible in source（底本此处读不出）">▪</span> no spirit in them: that is, no spi<span class="cw-gap" title="TCP: illegible in source（底本此处读不出）">•</span>uall or heauenly motion.

But the Christian, considering that his soule is <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">The contrary disposition of Christians.</span> from aboue, sets his affection also on those things which are aboue: he delights to haue his cōuersation in heauen: and it is a griefe to him, when he finds his motions and affections drawne downe, <span class="cw-pb" id="p70" data-p="70" data-facs="42" title="Page 70 of the 1613 edition begins here (原书第 70 页起)"></span> and entangled with the earth. His life is to cleaue to the Lord: but it is death to him, when the neck of his soule is bowed downe to the yoke of the vvorld. *Erecta ad Deum ceruix, & <span class="cw-gap" title="TCP: illegible in source（底本此处读不出）">•</span>ugo Christi* <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Ambr.</span> *habilis, quae nulla in terrarum illecebras, inflexione curuetur, regalis Christi turris est.* The necke of the Spouse of the Lord IESVS, is raysed vptoward <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Cant. 4. 4.</span> <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">The royall towre of christ, is a soule mo<span class="cw-gap" title="TCP: illegible in source（底本此处读不出）">••</span>ting vp to heauen.</span> God, meet to receiue the yoke of Christ<span class="cw-gap" title="TCP: illegible in source（底本此处读不出）">▪</span> but wil no way bow for any earthly allurements, to beare the yoke of another; such a soule is the royall towre of Christ Iesus. By this disposition, let men try themselues, and see of what Spirit they are. Againe, the vicissitude and change of estates, where-vnto the children of God are <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Change of estates, wherevnto the godly are subiect.</span> subiect, comes heere to be marked: sometime<span class="cw-gap" title="TCP: illegible in source（底本此处读不出）">•</span> they are borne downe by carnall affections to the earth: sometimes raised vp by spirituall desires to the heauen. This comes to passe of the battell that is between two irrecōcileable parties, the old man & the new; Nature & Grace. For as in them who wrestle with any equall strength, sometime the one is aboue, and sometime the other, till at length one be ouercome: so is it in this combat, In the Christian, sometime the power of Nature, othertimes the power of Grace preuailing: but this comfort wee haue, that at length Grace shall <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">☞</span> preuaile; & the power of corrupt Nature, shal be captiued vnder the obedience of Christ.

*Quicken me.] Dauid* being sore oppressed, as I said, and dead in himselfe, doth now beseech <span class="cw-pb" id="p71" data-p="71" data-facs="42" title="Page 71 of the 1613 edition begins here (原书第 71 页起)"></span> the Lord to quicken him, & raise him vp againe: <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Godly men, by <span class="cw-gap" title="TCP: illegible in source（底本此处读不出）">〈◊〉</span> wāts, falls, & infirmities, become more godly.</span> for he knew, that not onely the beginnings, but the continuance of our life, must come from the Lord. Thus we see how the children of God are wakened by their wants, stirred vp by their falls and decaies, to a greater feruencie in prayer. And truely, these changes of our life, which this manner of waie wakens vs, and prouokes vs to prayer, we are to account them as the mercifull hand of God, stretched out toward vs, to drawe vs home to himselfe. <span class="cw-gap" title="TCP: illegible in source（底本此处读不出）">•</span>et no man thinke euill of that trouble that chaseth him to the Lord. Yea, I may say, his falls, which make him more humble and feruent in prayer, are of Gods dispensation: who when he will, in most maruailous manner, can bring light out of darknes.

But seeing hee was aliue, how prayes hee that <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">The life of a Christiā, wherin stands it.</span> God would quicken him? I answere, The godly esteeme of life, not according to that they haue in the body, but in their soule. If the soule want the sense of mercie, and a heauenly disposition to spirituall thinges, they lament ouer it, as a dead soule: for sure it is, temporall desertions, are more heauie to the godlie then temporall death.

*According thy word.*] This is a great faith; that <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">It is great faith to belieu<span class="cw-gap" title="TCP: illegible in source（底本此处读不出）">•</span> when there is no feeling of mercie.</span> vvhere in respect of his present feeling, he found himself d<span class="cw-gap" title="TCP: illegible in source（底本此处读不出）">•</span>ad, yet he hopes for life frō God, according to his promise. Such was the faith of *Abraham,* who vnder hope, belieued aboue hope. And <span class="cw-pb" id="p72" data-p="72" data-facs="43" title="Page 72 of the 1613 edition begins here (原书第 72 页起)"></span> truly, many times are Gods children brought to this estate, that they haue nothing to vphold them but the word of God: no sense of mercie, no spirituall disposition; but on the contrarie, great darknes, horrible feares and terrors. Onely they are sustained by looking to the promise of God, and kept in some hope, that he will restore them to life againe; because it is his praise to finish the worke which he beginnes.

VER. 26.

I haue declared my waies, and thou heardest me: teach me thy statutes.

</div>

THis verse containes a prayer, vvith a <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Dauids argument to moue the Lord vnto mercie.</span> reason after this forme. O Lord, I haue oft before declared vnto thee, the whole state and course of my life, my wandrings, my wants, my doubts, my griefes; I hid nothing srō thee, & thou, according to my necessity didst alway hear me: therfore, now Lord, I pray thee to teach me, by thy light illuminate me that I may know thy st<span class="cw-gap" title="TCP: illegible in source（底本此处读不出）">•</span>tutes, & receiue grace to walk in thē.

This is a good argument in dealing vvith the <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Where God begins to shew mercy, he cea<span class="cw-gap" title="TCP: illegible in source（底本此处读不出）">•</span> not t<span class="cw-gap" title="TCP: illegible in source（底本此处读不出）">•</span>ll hee crowne with <span class="cw-gap" title="TCP: illegible in source（底本此处读不出）">•</span>cie.</span> Lord; I haue gotten many mercies, and fauourable answers from thee: therefore Lord, I pray thee to giue memo; sor whom he loues, he loues to the end; & where he begins to shew mercy, he ceaseth not til he crown his children with mercy<span class="cw-gap" title="TCP: illegible in source（底本此处读不出）">▪</span> And <span class="cw-gap" title="TCP: illegible in source（底本此处读不出）">•</span>o gratious is the Lord, that he esteemes he<span class="cw-gap" title="TCP: illegible in source（底本此处读不出）">•</span> <span class="cw-pb" id="p73" data-p="73" data-facs="43" title="Page 73 of the 1613 edition begins here (原书第 73 页起)"></span> is honoured as oft as we giue him the praise, that we haue found comfort in him, and therefore come to seeke more. It is farre otherwise with men: if they doe vnto vs any small good, they thinke it vncourteous importunity, to require them to do any more. But as the Strand is easily emptied where the Ocean can neuer be exhau<span class="cw-gap" title="TCP: illegible in source（底本此处读不出）">•</span>ted: <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Mans liberality is but like a Strand, Gods like the great Ocean.</span> so mans liberality is soon dried vp; but the goodnes of the Lord remains for euer: who hath not the less because he hath giuen, but stil delights <span class="cw-gap" title="TCP: illegible in source（底本此处读不出）">•</span>o giue more to such as are thankefull vnto him.

Next, is to be marked how he saith, I haue declared <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Happy is the soule wherein mercy & truth meet together.</span> my wayes, and thou heardst me: these two go well together, Mercy & Truth; Truth in the near<span class="cw-gap" title="TCP: illegible in source（底本此处读不出）">•</span> of man confessing; Mercy in God, hearing & forgiuing: happy is the soule wherin these two meete together. Many there are who want this comfort: they cannot say, God hath heard me; <span class="cw-gap" title="TCP: illegible in source（底本此处读不出）">•</span>nd all because they deale not plainely and truely with the Lord, in declaring their waies vnto him. <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">An euill conscience hides it self from God.</span>

For the wicked neyther will nor dare manifest heir wayes vnto the Lord. As the eye which is <span class="cw-gap" title="TCP: illegible in source（底本此处读不出）">•</span>urt, dare not look to the light; so the conscience of him that doth euill, dare not look to the Lord: <span class="cw-gap" title="TCP: illegible in source（底本此处读不出）">•</span>ea, they doe what they can to hide their wayes <span class="cw-gap" title="TCP: illegible in source（底本此处读不出）">•</span>rom him, and *to conceale the iniquity of their bosome:* against such the Prophet denounces a wo; *<span class="cw-gap" title="TCP: illegible in source（底本此处读不出）">•</span>o to them that seeke in the deepe to hide their* <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Esa.</span> *counsell from the Lord, their workes are in secret, and they who sees it.* Their turning of deuises, and <span class="cw-pb" id="p74" data-p="74" data-facs="44" title="Page 74 of the 1613 edition begins here (原书第 74 页起)"></span> flying to refuges of vanity, in the infidelity of their hearts, will no more auaile them then the figge leaues did *Adam* to hide his nakednes; *For the Lord will bring euery worke to iudgement.*

Better were it for vs to iudge our selues in time, <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">What a great benefit it is to manifest our wayes to God in time.</span> and with *Dauid* to examin our harts in our secret chamber, & to declare truely our wayes vnto the Lord (that if they be good, he would by his grace confirm vs in them; if they be euill, he would diuert vs from them) then that otherwise we should sleep securely in our sins, & tarry till our aduersary accuse vs, and the Lord enter into iudgement with vs: nay, rather let vs tell our sins our selues, that the Lord who is gratious may forgiue, and Sathan who is a most shameless and malitious accuser, may be confounded. *Qui se accusauerit, vocem parati accusatoris excludit, qui solet acer<span class="cw-gap" title="TCP: illegible in source（底本此处读不出）">•</span>are* <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Ambrose.</span> *peccata & vniuscuius que exaggerare <span class="cw-gap" title="TCP: illegible in source（底本此处读不出）">•</span>agitia:* <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Sathan confounded when we confesse our sinnes.</span> *obstruit <span class="cw-gap" title="TCP: illegible in source（底本此处读不出）">•</span>s eius qui de seipso fuerit ante confessus;* This is the great vantage we haue by declaring our own sins to the Lord, *Qui sua delicta non ta<span class="cw-gap" title="TCP: illegible in source（底本此处读不出）">•</span>uit,* <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">☞</span> *videtur in se doluisse quod fecit, in diabolo prodidisse quod suasit;* that in so doing, wee shewe our selues sorrowfull, and penitent for the euill we haue done, and therwithall also becom accusers of Sathan, by whose subtile instigation we did the euill.

Last of all, we learne here that then our life is well gouerned, when we declare the course of it <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">A profitable rule to make vs liue godly.</span> to the Lord, & lay open our harts nakedly before <span class="cw-pb" id="p75" data-p="75" data-facs="44" title="Page 75 of the 1613 edition begins here (原书第 75 页起)"></span> him. This *Dauid* protests of himselfe, *Acta mea* <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Basil.</span> *tibi Domine manifestani omnia:* and in another place, that he set the Lord alway before him. And this is it which is praised in *Henoch,* that he walked with God. Happy is the man, whose wayes are so ordered, that before his actions he consults with the Lord; in his actions, he remembers the Lord; & when he hath done, presents them to the Lord: if they be good, seeking his approbation; if they be euil, seeking pardon & forgiuene <span class="cw-gap" title="TCP: illegible in source（底本此处读不出）">•</span>of thē.

*Teach me thy Statutes.*] Now follows his prayer, <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">After confession he ioynes prayer for amendment.</span> wherein he seekes light from God to direct him in his waies *Non satis est vt confiteamur errorem; verumetiam si corrigi volumus, à Domino* <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Ambros.</span> *postulemus, vt doceat nos iustificationes suas ne postea* <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">See ver. 12.</span> *errare possimus:* It is not enough to confesse and declare our former errours to the Lord, but <span class="cw-gap" title="TCP: illegible in source（底本此处读不出）">•</span>e should seeke to be coriected by his grace, in <span class="cw-gap" title="TCP: illegible in source（底本此处读不出）">•</span>ime to come. These two benefits the Lord giues <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Remission and renouation are two inseparable benefits.</span> them together; <span class="cw-gap" title="TCP: illegible in source（底本此处读不出）">•</span>emission of sinnes; and renoua<span class="cw-gap" title="TCP: illegible in source（底本此处读不出）">•</span>ion of their sinfull nature: and his Saints seeke them together, Mercy and Grace; Mercy for remission <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">☜</span> of former sinnes, and Grace for renoua<span class="cw-gap" title="TCP: illegible in source（底本此处读不出）">•</span>ion of their hearts in time to come. And this is to be marked for carelesse and carnall professors, who seek pardon of former sinnes, but haue no care of amendment for the time to com: these <span class="cw-gap" title="TCP: illegible in source（底本此处读不出）">•</span>buse the mercy of God, and turne his grace into wantonnesse; seeking, as it were, a libertie to sin, and not a deliuerance from sinne. <span class="cw-pb" id="p76" data-p="76" data-facs="45" title="Page 76 of the 1613 edition begins here (原书第 76 页起)"></span> More concerning this petition, see out of the twelfth Verse in the second Section *Beth.*

VER. 27.

Make me to vnderstand the way of thy precepts, and I will meditate in thy wondrous workes.

</div>

DAVID yet continues in his former supplication, <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">We haue great need to pray for further light.</span> for further illumination of his vnderstanding. *If the light that is in thee be darknes, how great is the darknes?* Where the mind is darkned, the heart can neuer be rightly ordered; therefore prayes he the more instantly for light vnto his minde.

The wayes of sinne, we know them, and can <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">We can walke the wayes of sinne without a teacher, not so the wayes of God.</span> walke in them without a teacher: yea, man out of his owne corruption hath been the first inuenter of many sinfull waies, which before him haue not beene knowne; but as for the wayes of God, we can neyther know them nor walke in them, vnlesse we be taught of God.

Againe in this continual opposition, which he <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Miserable is man so long as his way and Gods way are different.</span> makes betweene his wayes, and Gods wayes; let vs remember, how by nature man hath a way of his own, different from the way of God. This diuision began by the first transgression, wherein man preferred the way of Sathan to the way o<span class="cw-gap" title="TCP: illegible in source（底本此处读不出）">•</span> God: & yet the diuision continues, because man naturally is enclined to walke in a way contrary <span class="cw-pb" id="p77" data-p="77" data-facs="45" title="Page 77 of the 1613 edition begins here (原书第 77 页起)"></span> to that which God commaunds him; which if in time it be not remedied, will not faile to bring man to a miserable and vnhappy end: for he who walkes not in the way of God, can neuer come where God is; but must be diuided frō the light & ioyes of God, and haue his portion in vtter darkneesse, in that lake that burneth with fire & brimstone.

*And I will meditate.*] If we seeke good things <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Good things should be sought from God for good ends.</span> from God, we should secke them for good ends, specially that we may be enabled by them to serue God; so will the Lord be intreated to giue them, when he sees we seek them that we may return them to himselfe. Ye aske, saith S. *Iames,* and <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Iam 4.</span> receiue not, because ye aske amisse, that ye may consume them on your own lusts: therfore *Dauid* seeking knowledge from God, promiseth to vse it well; not as many doe, who by knowledge waxe proud, seeking knowledge to get glory to themselues. *Dauid* seeks it that he may be the more able to meditate on the workes of God, and in them to shew forth his glory.

He stiles the works of God, maruellous works: <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">The works of God are all maruellous.</span> and so indeed they are; both his works of creation, whereby he made the things which are; and of his administration or prouidence, whereby he conserues them: but most of all, maruellous are his works of mans redemption; that is the *Great mysterie of godlines.* But som of them we admire not, because we know them not; others of them custom drawes in contempt.

<span class="cw-pb" id="p78" data-p="78" data-facs="46" title="Page 78 of the 1613 edition begins here (原书第 78 页起)"></span>VER. 28.

My soule melteth for heauinesse: raise me vp according to thy Word.

</div>

THe meaning is, that he was sore weakned <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">The godly sore humbled by affliction.</span> by the greatnes of his griefe; & therfore prayeth the Lord to reuiue, strengthen, & confirm him. Euen as a thing w*ch* melteth, consumes away by piece-meale, till nothing be left; *Vires eius paulatim difflu<span class="cw-gap" title="TCP: illegible in source（底本此处读不出）">•</span>hant sicut aquae:* so did he finde the life & strength of his soule docaying, through the extremity of his griefe.

In the first part of the verse, his eye is vpon <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">A Christian is eyther looking to his owne necessities, or to God his mercies.</span> him selfe: in the second it is vp to the Lord. Happy is the man, whose whole care is about one of these two; that eyther he is looking to himselfe, considering his necessities; or looking to the Lord, attending his mercies.

*Heauiness.*] There is nothing may comfort a <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Naturall comforts cannot sustaine a man i<span class="cw-gap" title="TCP: illegible in source（底本此处读不出）">•</span> spirituall troubles.</span> naturall man, but *Dauid* had it: yet cannot all these keepe him from that heauinesse, wherunto, as witnesseth S*t*. *Peter,* the children of God are subiect in this life, through their manifold tentations. <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">1. Pet. 1.</span> The men of the world are so farre from this disposition, that if they haue health and wealth, they maruell what it is should make a man heauie: they are not acquainted with the exercises of a feeling conscience; they know not the defects of the spirituall life, & are not grieued at them: being dead in sinne, they feele not that <span class="cw-pb" id="p79" data-p="79" data-facs="46" title="Page 79 of the 1613 edition begins here (原书第 79 页起)"></span> they want life: all their care is to eate and drink, and make merry. But miserable are they; for in their best estate, they are as Oxen fedde for the slaughter. *Wo be to them who laugh now,* they shal <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Luk. 6.</span> mourne: but blessed are they who mourne now, for they shall be comforted.

The particular causes of the heauinesse of a <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Christians should not thinke that their tentations are singular.</span> Christian<span class="cw-gap" title="TCP: illegible in source（底本此处读不出）">▪</span> are according to their particular tentations; which no man is able to rehearse: where because it is a common disease to them all, that euery one of them thinkes his own tentations singular; let them remember that warning of the Apostle, *No tentation hath ouertaken you, but that* <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">1. Cor. 10.</span> *which appertaines to men.*

But seeing our Sauiour saith, that *His yoake is* <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Christs crosse is such a burden, is easeth them on whō it <span class="cw-gap" title="TCP: illegible in source（底本此处读不出）">•</span>eth<span class="cw-gap" title="TCP: illegible in source（底本此处读不出）">▪</span></span> *easie, and his burden light;* how is it that his children finde such heauinesse in bearing of it, and following him? It is true indeede, the burden of Christ is light: *Quidenim leuius eo o<span class="cw-gap" title="TCP: illegible in source（底本此处读不出）">•</span>ere, quod* <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1"><span class="cw-gap" title="TCP: illegible in source（底本此处读不出）">•</span>ern Epist. 72.</span> *non solum non oneraet, sed portat omnom cui portandum* <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">☜</span> *imponitur?* for it is such a burden as is not burdensome, but rather bears vp euery man who bears it: not vnlike the wings of a fowle, w*ch* are so borne by the body, that they beare it, and make it more agile or light.

If then we finde any heauinesse, it is because <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">If we finde it heau<span class="cw-gap" title="TCP: illegible in source（底本此处读不出）">•</span>, blame <span class="cw-gap" title="TCP: illegible in source（底本此处读不出）">•</span>ur corruption.</span> our corrupt nature will not conforme her selfe to the similitude of Christ, and refuseth to submit her neck vnto his yoke. This is it that breeds in vs heauinesse and griefe, that wee finde a <span class="cw-pb" id="p80" data-p="80" data-facs="47" title="Page 80 of the 1613 edition begins here (原书第 80 页起)"></span> power of corruption in vs, which will not subiect it selfe to him whom our soule loueth.

*Raise me vp according to thy word.*]

They haue not all learned to pray, who vse <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">They haue not all learned to pray, who vse to pray.</span> prayer: many in this age send vp vnto God confused prayers, rising from the sense of their miseries, whereof they desire to be relieued. Pagans doe as much; when they are in trouble, they lift vp their eyes to God, and desire to be deliuered from it: But we must remember first to <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Rules for prayer.</span> offer vp our prayers in the name of CHRIST; whom because Pagans know not, the Father cannot bee well pleased with them. Next, to frame our petitions according to Gods promises, as heere *Dauid* doth: otherwise, to offer prayers for that which GOD hath not promised, or are not according to the word, it is to offer vncouth fire to the Lorde, as *Chore, Dathan* and *Abiram* did, to their owne destruction.

<div class="cw-text" markdown="1">

VER. 29.

Take from me the way of lying, and gratiously grant me thy Lawe.

</div>

IN the remanent foure Verses of this Section, <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Our life should be ordered by the word.</span> there is a continued praier; wherin *Dauid* craueth grace of God to order his life: not according to the counsell of Nature, which is a lying way; but according to the word, which he onely <span class="cw-pb" id="p81" data-p="81" data-facs="47" title="Page 81 of the 1613 edition begins here (原书第 81 页起)"></span> acknowledgeth to be the way of truth, adding these protestations: that he had chosen it; his hart cleaned to it; and he would continue in it.

In this Verse, we haue first to see what is this way of lying, that we may vnderstand the meaning of his petition. By the way of lying is to be <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">All the wayes of sinne are long wayes.</span> vnderstood all that is in man his nature, not agreeable to the word, whether it be counsels, or conclusions of the heart, or externall actions: and it is called a lying way, because nature promises a good to be gotten by sinne, which man shall not finde in it.

And vnto this deceit of sinne *Dauid* acknowledgeth <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">The deceitfull way of sinne is within euery man by nature.</span> himselfe subiect with the rest; for why? he would not pray the Lord to take that away from him, which were not in him. *In nobis est, & interius manet via iniquitatis, ideo que studiose agendum* <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Amb.</span> *est, vt eam à nobis separemus. Dauid* was a regenerate man; but there are none so well renewed in this life, who may not finde something in themselues that need further reformation: *Sed quia haereditarium iniquitatis glutinum mentibus inhaesit humanis, opus est liberantis auxilio:* but this separation of a man from the way of iniquitie, is a worke of great difficulty, to be done only <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">It is a worke of Gods power to separate vs from it.</span> by the power of God, both because the way of sinne is within vs, and as an heritable euil is glewed to our minds. For as it is *Maiorum nostr<span class="cw-gap" title="TCP: illegible in source（底本此处读不出）">•</span>rum attrita vestigijs,* pathed and trod with the footesteps of our fathers; so are wee naturally enclined <span class="cw-pb" id="p82" data-p="82" data-facs="48" title="Page 82 of the 1613 edition begins here (原书第 82 页起)"></span> without a teacher or counseller, to follow them in it. *Ideoque quia vulnus grande ac vetus est, & diu serpens, perfectioris medicinae remedia deposcit.* And therefore, because it is a great wound & an old, it requires the remedie of more perfect medicine, then the wit or power of man is able to affoord: which *Dauid* vvell knowing, beseecheth God of his mercie to take away this euill from him.

And further we see heere, that what-euer vvas <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Euery sight a godly man gets of himselfe, is a spurre to prayer.</span> *Dauid* his disposition, hee gathers alwaies out of it, an argument to mooue him to prayer. It is wisedom for a man<span class="cw-gap" title="TCP: illegible in source（底本此处读不出）">▪</span> aboue all things to take heed to himselfe. But sure, hee hath neuer learned this aright, who when hee hath looked to himselfe, lookes not incontinent vp to God; moued vvith the sight of his manifold necessities, to seek mercie and grace to supply them.

*And grant mee graciously thy law.*] Hee oppones <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">The law of God, the way of truth in three respects.</span> the law of God, to the way of lying. First, because it is the onely rule of all truth, both in religion and manners: that which is not agreeable <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">1.</span> to it, is but a lye, vvhich shall deceiue men. Secondly, it destroyes, and shall at length vtterlie <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">2.</span> destroy all contrary errors. As the rodde of *Aaron,* deuoured the rods of the Enchaunters: so the word, which is the rodde of the mouth of <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">☞</span> God, shall in the end, eate vp and consume all vntruthes whatsoeuer. Thirdly, according to the <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">3.</span> sentence of this vvord, so shall it be vnto euerie <span class="cw-pb" id="p83" data-p="83" data-facs="48" title="Page 83 of the 1613 edition begins here (原书第 83 页起)"></span> man; it deceiues none. Men shall find by experience it is true: he who walkes in a way condemned by the word, shall come to a miserable end. And on the contrary, it cannot be but well vvith them who liue according to this rule; *Non est* <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Ambr.</span> *via veritatis honor saeculi, sollicitudo mundi: vanitas temporalium est, veritas aeternorum.*

But had not *Dauid* the law already? No doubt <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Then haue we learned Gods law, when it is written in the table of our hart.</span> he wanted not the booke of the law. Hee heard it, he read it, he professed it: yea, some-way hee vnderstood it. What then is it hee craues? Surely, that the law might be so imprinted in his hart, that it might abolish that naturall vanitie, and deceit of sinne, which carried him to the offence of God. A necessary petition for these dayes: vvherin the knowledge of the word is exceeding <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">A warning to the professors of this age.</span> great; but the zeale, spirituall life, and feeling of the harts of men is not answerable vnto it. They thinke all is vvell, in that publiquely they professe it. They hear it with their eares; they speak of it with their mouthes; they read it in their bookes finely bound; though in that dutie many faile also. But certainly, vvhen they thinke they haue it, they want it; so long as it is not printed in the table of their hart, to frame their motions, affections and actions conformable to it. And this is it which *Dauid* heere craues.

<div class="cw-text" markdown="1">

<span class="cw-pb" id="p84" data-p="84" data-facs="49" title="Page 84 of the 1613 edition begins here (原书第 84 页起)"></span>VER. 30.

I haue chosen the way of truth: and thy iudgements haue I laid before me.

</div>

IN these last three verses we haue the reason of <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">If we haue not good purpose<span class="cw-gap" title="TCP: illegible in source（底本此处读不出）">▪</span> and desires, let vs not looke that God will blesse vs.</span> his former petition, from his honest affection toward the word of God: wherein he declares that he had chosen it, he did cleaue vnto it, and was determined in al time to<span class="cw-gap" title="TCP: illegible in source（底本此处读不出）">▪</span>com more and more to cleaue vnto it; therefore praieth he God more and more to confirme him in this purpose. *Non enim diuina gratia datur bonū propositū non habentibus,* the grace of God, is not giuen to such as in their heart purpose not to do good: and yet <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">And yet good purposes haue we not till God by grace work them in vs.</span> this purpose and desire to doe well is not in the heart of man by nature, vnlesse God worke it by grace; for we are not of our selues sufficient to think a good thought: *It is God who works in vs, both the will and the deed. Nos volumus, sed Deus* <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Aug. de don<span class="cw-gap" title="TCP: illegible in source（底本此处读不出）">•</span> perseuer. ca. 13.</span> *operatur in nobis, & velle: nos operamur, sed Deus in nobis operatur, & operari; hoc nobis expedit & credere, & dicere: hoc pium, hoc verum; vt sit humilis & submissa confessio, & detur totum Deo.* We will indeede; but it is God, who workes in vs that will also: we worke; but it is <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">☞</span> God who workes in vs that working of good also; it is expedient for vs both to beleeue and to say so: this is godly, this is true; that there may be in vs a submisse and humble confession, & the whole prais of wel-doing may be ascribd to God.

<span class="cw-pb" id="p85" data-p="85" data-facs="49" title="Page 85 of the 1613 edition begins here (原书第 85 页起)"></span>And because man is ingenious to defraud God <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Another notable testimony of *Augustines,* to proue that our good desires are of grace, and not of the power of nature.</span> of the praise of grace, and to magnifie the arme of Nature, let vs marke another notable testimonie this same Father giues to this truth, whereby the idle distinctions of the Aduersaries are dissolued; *Deus qui lux est interior is hominis plus illi praestat, quam lux oculo: nam lux oculum ad se* <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Augu. contra Pelag. lib. 2. cap. 5.</span> *conuersum illuminat, auersumà se, & clausum deserit; Deus vero non solum mentem ad se conuersum* <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">☜</span> *illuminat, sed etiam mentem ad se conuertit, quod lux oculo non praestat:* GOD who is the light of the inward man, doth more to him, then the light doth to the externall eye: for the light illuminates the eye that lookes vnto it, but forsakes the eye which is closed and turned from it. But the Lord doth not onely illuminate the mind conuerted to him, but he also converts the mind vnto him. Which thing the light cannot doe to the eye. *Diligenter itaque notandae sunt hae loquutiones* <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Conuersion to God, is not of any free will in nature.</span> *in scripturis, Conuertimini ad me: & cum his comparandae, Conuerte nos Deus sanitatum nostrarum; ne putemus libero arbitrio nos ad Deum conuerti.* Wee haue diligently therefore to obserue these manner of speeches in the Scripture, wherein God commaunds vs to conuert to him: and to compare them with others, wherein we are taught to pray, that the God of our saluation would conuert vs, and then shall wee be conuerted; least otherwise, wee thinke that wee conuert to God of our owne free will.

<span class="cw-pb" id="p86" data-p="86" data-facs="50" title="Page 86 of the 1613 edition begins here (原书第 86 页起)"></span>This choise which *Dauid* makes here of Gods <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Our election of Gods word, proceeds from his election of vs.</span> truth, proceeds from that choise and election, vvhereby the Lord before all time made choise of *Dauid,* in Christ, to be one of his Elect. For as it is true of loue; *Heerein is loue, not that wee loued God first, but that he loued vs:* vvec could neuer haue loued him, if first he had not loued vs: so is it true of election; if he before time had not chosen vs to be his people, wee could neuer in time haue chosen him to be our God. And this I mark <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">What great comfort they haue, who sincerely loue the Lord.</span> in them vvho loue the word of God, & delight in it, who can say out of a good heart that the Lord is their portion, and the ioy of their hart: this is a sure seale of their election, imprinted by the finger of God in their heart; assuring them, that what they are in loue and affection toward him, that hath he been first toward them: and so much the more aboundant in his loue toward vs, <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">How far Gods loue is aboue ours.</span> as he himselfe is greater then we. What is the eye to the sunne? vvhat is the Well to the Ocean? <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">☞</span> what is the earth to the heauen? By infinite degrees is man lesse then his Maker; and as farre our loue and affection to him, inferiour to his loue & affection toward vs. *As far as the heauens* <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Esay.</span> *are aboue the earth: so far are my thoughts aboue yours, saith the Lord.* This doth some-vvhat shadow it: but how far his thoughts are aboue ours, no similitude can expresse it.

*And thy iudgements.*] Gods word is called his <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Gods word called his iudgement, and why.</span> iudgement, because it discernes good from euill, <span class="cw-pb" id="p87" data-p="87" data-facs="50" title="Page 87 of the 1613 edition begins here (原书第 87 页起)"></span> and is not a naked sentence: but as it points out euill, so it pronounceth plagues against it; vvhich shall be executed according to the sentence therof. The remembrance of this, scrued as an awband to keepe *Dauid* from sinne; and shall keepe vs also, if as saith he, Psal. 16. *wee set the Lord still in our sight:* and if, as heere he doth, we lay his iudgements before vs.

VER. 31.

I haue cleaued to thy testimonies: O Lord, confound me not.

</div>

THe protestation of his former affection, <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Constancie in godlinesse, an effect of grace.</span> is amplified by this; that as he had once chosen the testimonies of God: so by a constant affection hee cleaued vnto them. *Dauid* was not a Temporizer; to make choise of the word this day, and reiect it to morrow: as were those Iewes, who for a time reioyced in the light of the Gospel, brought to them by the Baptist, and after reiected his testimonie.

True godlinesse, wants neuer vpon her head <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">True godlines, wants neuer the crowne of perseuerance.</span> the garland of perseuerance: where the hypocrisie of temporizers, who make a shew of beginning in the spirit, and end in the flesh, is expressed by our Sauior, by co<span class="cw-gap" title="TCP: illegible in source（底本此处读不出）">•</span>n which springs, & shooteth to the blade, but grows not to perfection, because it was neuer vvell rooted. And *Basil* compares <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Basil in princip. Preuerb.</span> these, *qui bene coeperunt, & nō perseuerarūt,* who <span class="cw-pb" id="p88" data-p="88" data-facs="51" title="Page 88 of the 1613 edition begins here (原书第 88 页起)"></span> begins well, but continues not, to vnhappy passengers, *qui naufragium prope portum patiuntur:* vvho suffer shipwracke, not farre from the harbour.

*Confound me not.*] Forasmuch as *Dauid,* in a <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Confusion, which godly men feare, is either desertion of God.</span> good conscience, endeuoured to serue God, hee craues that the Lord would not confound him. This is two waies done: either when the Lord forsakes his children, so that in their trouble, they feele not his promised comforts; and then great confusion of mind & perturbation is vpon them: <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Or contempt and oppression of men.</span> or otherwise, when hee leaues them as a prey to their enemies, who scorne them for their godly and sincere life, and insult ouer them in time of their trouble; when they see, that all their prayer, and other exercises of religion, cannot keepe them out of their enemies hands. *Hee trusted in God, let him deliuer him.* From this shame and contempt, he desires the Lord would keep him; and that he should neuer be like vnto them, who being disappointed of that wherein they trusted, are ashamed.

VER. 32.

I will run the way of thy cōmandements, when thou shalt enlarge mine heart.

</div>

HIs affection toward the word of God, is <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">No constrained, but cheerfull seruice pleaseth the Lord.</span> yet further amplified by this; that as in time past hee had cleaued to it: so hee <span class="cw-pb" id="p89" data-p="89" data-facs="51" title="Page 89 of the 1613 edition begins here (原书第 89 页起)"></span> promiseth with ioy & alacritie to continue in it for the time to come; which he expresseth by the word of running. But hee adds this protestation, or rather condition; that the Lord would enlarge his hart: without which grace, hee grants he can make no progresse in the way of godliness.

Heereof wee learne, how wee should doe the will of God, not by constraint, but cheerfullie, & willingly: as one that runneth a race, intends the whole force of his body to advaunce himselfe forward, toward the end thereof.

But heere we may stand and lament; Alas, we <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Better not to begin then to breake off the course of godlinesse.</span> runne not with *Dauid;* oh that we could halt to *Canaan* with *Iacob:* or at least, creep forward like children, to our fathers house. But many, in steed of running, lie downe; and which is worse, goe backeagaine, like dogges to their vomits; or carnall Israelites, to their flesh-pots of Egypt: for whom it had beene better, not to haue knowne the way of rightcousnes.
