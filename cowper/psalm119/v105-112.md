### <span class="cw-pb" id="p240" data-p="240" data-facs="128" title="Page 240 of the 1613 edition begins here (原书第 240 页起)"></span>NVN.

<div class="cw-text" markdown="1">

VER. 105.

Thy word is a Lanterne vnto my feet, and a light to my pathes.

</div>

<span class="cw-dropcap">T</span>He vse of a Lanterne, is in the darke <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">The word of God compared to a Lanterne.</span> night; for no man will carrie it when the Sunne shineth clearly. *Dauid* here protests, what a Lanterne is to men in darknesse, the same the word of God was to him in his pilgrimage. *In nocte huius praesentis vitae,* <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Euthym. in Psal. 119.</span> in the night of this present life, it shewed him the <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">1 Because it sheweth light in darkenesse.</span> right way, to leade him forward to his desired and determinate end. And as men when they come to their lodging, require no more the help of the Lanterne; so shall we, when we shall finish <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">2 We shall set it by when we come home.</span> our pilgrimage, and shall come to our heauenly Ierusalem, not thē any more stand in need of this ministeriall light: there shall bee no Sunne nor Candle there; *The Lord is the light of that Citie.* <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Reuel. 22. 5.</span>

*Basil* makes heere a comparison betweene the <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">The light of the Gospell is clearer then the light of the Lawe.</span> Lawe and the Gospell; and thinkes that the Law is called a Lanterne, because it did but illuminate the house of Israel: but Christ Iesus, the *Sonne of righteousnesse,* by his light of the Gospell, hath illuminate <span class="cw-pb" id="p241" data-p="241" data-facs="128" title="Page 241 of the 1613 edition begins here (原书第 241 页起)"></span> all the houses and families of the earth. It is true indeed, the light of the Gospel is both cleerer and larger then the light of the law: but that which heere *Dauid* speakes, doth properlie belong to the whole word of God; as S. *Peter* <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">2. Pet. 1. 19.</span> saith, *It is as a light shining in darknes, to the which we shall doe well to take heed.*

Alway we see the necessitie of the vvord of <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">As the Sun is necessary for the day, so the light of the word to direct our way.</span> God; and how impossible it is for men to direct their waies aright without it. As the Sun is necessary for the day, & the Moone for the night; and as in a darke house, nothing can be seene without a Lanterne or candle: so cannot the right way, be discerned from the wrong, in this darknes of our life, without the light of the word. *Multae foueae,* <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">*Ambr.* in Psal. 119.</span> *multi scopuli in istius seculi caligine non videntur; praefer tibilucernam, quam Prophet a monstrauit:* there are many pits in this mistie world, many rocks in this turbulent sea, which are not scene; carie before thee this Lanterne. *Nullicre<span class="cw-gap" title="TCP: illegible in source（底本此处读不出）">▪</span>* <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Ambrose.</span> *das tuum, nisi praeeunte lucernae istius luce, processum:* Credit not thy steppes to any, vnlesse the light of this Lanterne goe before to direct thee.

*Dauid* was a man of very good wit, and naturall <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Our waies are in darknesse without the word.</span> vnderstanding: but he giues to God this glorie, that his best light was but darknesse, when he was not lightned and ruled by the word of God. Oh that wee could consider this, that in all our waies wherein the word of God shines not vnto <span class="cw-pb" id="p242" data-p="242" data-facs="129" title="Page 242 of the 1613 edition begins here (原书第 242 页起)"></span> vs, to direct vs, wee doe but walke in darknesse: and our waies without it, can lead vs to none other end, but vtter darknes. If we hearken not to the word of GOD, if wee walke not by the rule thereof, how is it possible wee can come to the face of God?

*Israel* in the wildernesse were gouerned by a <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">As Israels course in the wildernes was directed by the Lord, so should ours be.</span> clowde in the day, and by a piller of fire in the night: their campe was raised and settled, as God gaue them the signe, till at length they came to *Canaan.* Happy shall we be, if so our life be gouerned in all the course thereof, that our resting and remoouing, our going in, and going out, and all that we do, may be ordered by the Lords direction: for so shall he bring vs at length to his promised *Canaan.*

But that this may be done, let vs remember, <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">As a mā lights one light at another, so should we light our minds at the word.</span> that as a man lighteth one light at another: so we must seeke to haue the light of our mind illuminated by the light of the word. *Ex hac lucerna accende et tu lucernam, vt luceat interior oculus tuus,* <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">☞</span> *qui lucerna est tui corporis:* At this Lanterne of the word, light thou the Lantern of thy mind; that the interior eye, which is the light of the whole body, may shine cleerly.

<span class="cw-pb" id="p243" data-p="243" data-facs="129" title="Page 243 of the 1613 edition begins here (原书第 243 页起)"></span>VER. 106.

I haue sworne, and will performe it; that I will keepe thy righteous iudgements.

</div>

THis verse containes a protestation of *Dauids* <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Dauids resolution confirmed by an oath.</span> resolution, to walke in Gods obedience; and not a naked protestation, but confirmed by an oath: which in effect is no other thing, but a ratification of his promise which he made in circumcision; to weet, that hee should serue God, as one of his people. And this kind of <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">A godly oath a necessary helpe of our great weaknes. *Ambr.* in Psal. 119.</span> oath whereby we confirme our harts to doe that which wee haue promised, according to Gods words, as it is *et iudicium scientiae, & testimoniū conscientiae,* both a testimony of our science, and a testimonie of our conscience, that we know we should doe so, and therefore resolue we will doe it: so is it a necessary help of our great weaknes, who are ready either to forget and not remember; or if we remember, to faile and not perform. For remedie whereof, it is good that by an holy oath we should strengthen our selues to the performance of that dutie, which once by the light of God we haue resolued.

O! but heere the weake conscience wil obiect <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">The obiection of a weak conscience concerning an oath.</span> and say, If I swear and afterward faile, doe I not increase my guiltinesse? But to such it is answered, Shal we neuer resolue to doe good, yea, and to confirme ourselues by an oath to do it, because we are infirm, & faile in many things, and can not <span class="cw-pb" id="p244" data-p="244" data-facs="130" title="Page 244 of the 1613 edition begins here (原书第 244 页起)"></span> bring our good purposes to perfection? Or shall we think, that euery faile in our particular duties, against our resolution, our promise, yea, & against <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Why we should not cease to cōfirme our good purposes by an oath, albeit we be weake in performing.</span> our oath, is alway a falling away frō the couenant, which by our great oath in Baptisme wee once ratified. No, no: for the certaintie and continuance of this couenant, is not grounded on vs, but vpon GOD himselfe. It falls not by our falling: <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">☞</span> but wee rise againe, and stand by the stabilitie thereof; *Because I am not changed, yee are not consumed.* Thirdly, albeit of infirmitie, there be many failes in performance of our promise and oath, yet is there any renouncing of it? God forbid: but these same sinnes where-into wee fall, make vs the more ashamed of our selues; the more afraid of our weakenes; the more earnest to renue our former resolution; and the more carefull to call vpon God, for grace to performe it.

*Thy righteous iudgements.*] So *Dauid* stiles the <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Gods word, why called his iudgement.</span> word of God, because it iudgeth most righteously between right and wrong, truth and falshood. And secondly, because according to the iudgement giuen therein, God will doe. Let vs take heede vnto it: for the word containes a catalogue of such as shall not inherite the kingdom of God; and another of such as shall dwell in Gods tabernacle: let vs read and see, in which of the two catalogues our selues are; for according to that word will the iudgement goe.

<span class="cw-pb" id="p245" data-p="245" data-facs="130" title="Page 245 of the 1613 edition begins here (原书第 245 页起)"></span>VER. 107.

I am very sore afflicted, O Lord: quicken me according to thy word.

</div>

THe godly, in al their troubles make their <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">How familiar the godly are, in declaring their griefes to the Lord.</span> recourse to God; in most homely manner pouring out their griefes in his bosome: so that I may well say, there are none in earth, nay, not the most deare vnto them, to whō they can speake so familiarly, as vnto the Lord their God. Their wants, their weakenes, their griefe, their sinnes, whereof they think shame to speake to others, they think not euill homelie to communicate to him. But as *Moses* in all his troubles, ranne euer to the dore of the tabernacle; so they to the throne of grace: and happy are they, who close not this doore vpon themselues by their sinnes.

Againe, we are still remembred, that afflictions <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Afflictions no arguments of hatred.</span> are no arguments of Gods hatred, but rather of his loue. A father that spares the rodde, hates his <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Amb. in Psalm. 119.</span> child; *Nunquid non frequentius corripitur filius quam seruus?* Is not the son oftner chastised then the seruant? It is the voice of our heauenly father, *Quos diligo, castigo: If yee be without correction, ye are bastards, and not sonnes.* Where the Physitian <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Heb. 12.</span> <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">By the contrary, impunitie & libertie to sin, is an argumēt of Gods anger. Hosea 2.</span> refuseth to giue medicine, it is a token there is no hope of life: & where the Lord giues a man loose reanes & liberty, to goe where hee pleaseth, not hedging in his waies with thornes, as he promiseth <span class="cw-pb" id="p246" data-p="246" data-facs="131" title="Page 246 of the 1613 edition begins here (原书第 246 页起)"></span> to his *Israel,* it is an argument of his great displeasure. But seeing hee protested before, it <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">The state of the godly in trouble, & after trouble, to be distinguished.</span> was good for him, that God afflicted him; how doth hee now complaine? The answere is, that there is a great difference between the sense of the godly in trouble, and after it: *For no chastisement* <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Hebr. 12.</span> *is sweet for the present; but afterward, it brings the quiet fruit of righteousnes, to them who are thereby exercised.*

*Sore afflicted.*] Two things should restraine <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Two great motiues to patience in trouble.</span> vs from cōplayning of the greatnes of our trouble. First, if wee consider it is measured by the Lord; who is faithfull, and wil lay no more vpon vs, then we are able to beare. And next, that our stripes are not according to our sinnes: for if hee should beat vs with as many roddes, as wee haue grieued him with sinnes, he should adde yet tenne times more to all our greatest afflictions.

*Quicken me.] Defatigatas vires me as restitue,* <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Vatab.</span> *sicut pollicitus es.* Restore my decayed strength, as thou hast promised. Hee confesseth, he was almost dead by the greatnes of his trouble. But of this, see ver. 17. 77. 116.

VER. 108.

O Lord, I beseech thee, accept the free offrings of my mouth, & teach me thy iudgmēts.

</div>

IT is a great grace, that the Lord should accept <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">It is a great sauour that the Lord accepts any thing from vs, and that in three respects.</span> any thing from vs, if we consider these three things: First, who the Lord is. Next, <span class="cw-pb" id="p247" data-p="247" data-facs="131" title="Page 247 of the 1613 edition begins here (原书第 247 页起)"></span> what we are. Thirdly, what it is we haue to giue <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">1. If we consider who the Lord is.</span> vnto him. As for the Lord, he is All sufficient, & stands in need of nothing wee can giue him. *Our* <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Psalme 16.</span> *goodnes extends not to the Lord.* As for vs, we are <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">2. If we consider who our selues are.</span> poore creatures, liuing by his liberalitie: yea, begging from all the rest of his creatures; from the sunne and moone; from the ayre, the water, and the earth; from fowles and fishes; yea, from the worms: some giue vs light; some meat; some cloth; and are such beggers as we, meet to giue to a King.

And thirdly, if wee well consider, What is <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">3. If wee consider what our oblation <span class="cw-gap" title="TCP: illegible in source（底本此处读不出）">〈◊〉</span> .</span> it that we giue? Haue we any thing to giue, but that which wee haue receiued from him? and whereof wee may say with *Dauid; O Lord, all* <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">1. Chron. 29.</span> *things are of thee: and of thine owne hand haue vvee giuen thee againe.* Let this humble vs, and restraine vs from that vaine conceit of meriting at Gods hand.

*Of my mouth.] Dauid* at this time, in his great <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">No gift so smal, if it come from a good hart, but God accepts it.</span> necessitie, hauing no other sacrifice to offer vnto the Lord, offers him the calues of his lippes; but no doubt, when he might, he offred more.

There is nothing so small, but if it come from a good heart, God will accept it: the widowes myte; a cup full of cold water; yea & the praise of our lippes, although it haue no other externall oblation ioyned with it: but, where men may doe more and will not, it is an argument their heart is not sincerelie affected toward him, <span class="cw-pb" id="p248" data-p="248" data-facs="132" title="Page 248 of the 1613 edition begins here (原书第 248 页起)"></span> and their praises are not welcome to him.

And such as haue libertie to come to the holie <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">A reproofe of them, vvho praise not God with their lips.</span> assemblies, and offer not there the praises of their mouth to God, but sit as if they were dumbe; as also they that offer naked words, without the willing heart, are here much more conuinced. They were great beasts vnder the law, that thought to <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">And of thē also, who offer seruice of their words, not of their harts vnto him.</span> please God, by offring a beast vnto him, keeping back themselues: and they are greater beasts vnder the Gospel, that will giue their hart to another, and thinke to please God, by giuing some other gifts vnto him.

*Will the Lord be pleased vvith thousands of Rammes, or with tenne thousand riuers of Oyle? Shall I giue him my first borne for my transgression?* <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Mich. 6. 7.</span> *or the fruit of my wombe, for the sinne of my soule?* Is it such sacrifices will please the Lord? No, if thou wilt not offer thy selfe vnto him, hee will haue nothing that is thine.

VER. 109.

My soule is continually in my hand: yet doe I not forget thy law.

</div>

THis verse hath a renouation of his former <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">The godly mā hath his life alway ready in his hand, to offer to the Lord.</span> protestation; with an amplification, that he would keep it, albeit he was daily in peril of his life: and was also resolued & readie to lay it downe. For so this phrase imports, when he saith he had his soule in his hand, ready to giue <span class="cw-pb" id="p249" data-p="249" data-facs="132" title="Page 249 of the 1613 edition begins here (原书第 249 页起)"></span> when euer God should take it. And this is to be obserued, that there is no trouble so ready to take away the life of Gods children, as they are readie to giue it. As *Elijah* came out to the mouth <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">☜</span> of his caue to meet with the Lord; and *Abraham* stood in the doore of his tabernacle to speake to the Angel: so the soule of the godly, stands readie in the doore of the tabernacle of this body, to remooue when the Lord shall commaund it: whereas the soule of the wicked lies back, hiding <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">It is far otherwise with the wicked.</span> itselfe, as *Adam* among the bushes; and is taken out of the body perforce: as was the soul of that worldling, *This night they vvill take thy soule from thee:* but they neuer sacrifice their soules willingly to the Lord.

<div class="cw-text" markdown="1">

VER. 110.

The wicked haue laid a snare for mee: but I swarued not from thy precepts.

</div>

*DAuid* continueth his complaint to God <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Three things concurre in wickednes.</span> against the malice of his enemies; but changeth the stiles hee gaue them: and now hee calls them wicked men; which importeth three things. First, they worke wickednes. Secondly, they loue it. Thirdly, they perseuere in it.

The godly want not their owne sinnes; but this wickednes is far from them: for when they sin, they are rather like men oppressed, & forced <span class="cw-pb" id="p250" data-p="250" data-facs="133" title="Page 250 of the 1613 edition begins here (原书第 250 页起)"></span> against their will, to doe what they will not, then <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Which are not in the godly.</span> otherwise, workers with their will. And if at any time they doe euill, they continue not in it, but rise againe by repentance: so that their sins, are rather sinnes of weakenes, then of wickednes.

*Yet haue I not swarued.*] The constancie of <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Constancie of Christians.</span> the godlie, is tryed by trouble; when neither the euill example of the wicked can infect, nor their cruell persecutions can enforce them, to follow them in euill: but rather, the more they are troubled, the faster they cleaue to the Lord; and the more they see the impietie of euill men, the more they are confirmed to be good.

VER. 111.

Thy testimonies haue I taken as an heritage for euer: for they are the ioy of mine hart.

</div>

THere is nothing in the earth so excellent, <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Excellencie of Gods word aboue all other things.</span> but if it be compared with Christ Iesus, it is, as the Apostle saith, *but dung.* Such is the estimation of all the godly: when they cōpare <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Phil. 3. 8.</span> the word of God, with other most precious things, they account them of no price in comparison of it. The hony, and hony-combe, hath no sweetnes, when it is compared with the comfortable word of God: all treasures of gold and siluer are nothing to it; yea, the kingdome of *Canaan* made him not so ioyfull as did the vvord of GOD; which hee esteemed his greatest inheritance. <span class="cw-pb" id="p251" data-p="251" data-facs="133" title="Page 251 of the 1613 edition begins here (原书第 251 页起)"></span> For beside the present comfort it renders vnto vs, it is the Charter sealed and confirmed by <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">It is the sure Charter of our heauenly inheritance.</span> GOD; by Sacraments sealed; by the oath of God ratified; with the bloud of Christ subscribed: whereby we are made sure of our heauenlie inheritance.

But alas! by most part of men it is not regarded; <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">The contempt of Gods word reproued.</span> they preferre their trifles before it. Take from them a foote breadth of their earthly inheritance, they shew such a carnall zeale, as stirres <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">☜</span> them vp to hazard their liues in defence of it: take from them the comfort of Gods vvord, they regard not; neither are they any more touched there-with, then if it did not concerne them. And all the cause heereof proceeds from this; that they neuer found the ioy of this word in their harts, as *Dauid* heere saith: for according to euery man his feeling of the comfort which is in it, so doth hee esteeme of it.

<div class="cw-text" markdown="1">

VER. 112.

I haue applyed mine hart to fulfill thy statutes alway, vnto the end.

</div>

HE concludes this Section, with a protestation <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Seeing mans hart is not in his owne hand, how saith Dauid, he had applied his hart?</span> of his constant & sincere affection toward the word of God. Where first, it comes to be considered, seeing the hart of man is in the hand of GOD, and that man is not able to change a haire of his head, farre lesse <span class="cw-pb" id="p252" data-p="252" data-facs="134" title="Page 252 of the 1613 edition begins here (原书第 252 页起)"></span> the affections of his hart; how saith he, that hee had applied his hart to Gods statutes?

The answere ariseth from distinguishing these two-fold estates. A man before his conuersion <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">How a man after grace receiued, works his own saluation.</span> is dead in sinne and trespasses, and can doe no good, till GOD strengthen him: but after conuersion, hee becomes, by grace, a worker vvith GOD, to stirre and incline the heart toward God.

*Animae consilium lanci est simile:* the counsel <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Basil. in Psalm 119.</span> of the soule is like to a balance: *et mens, imperium nacta, lancem adea quae meliora sunt inclinat:* and the mind, which hath gotten the commaunding power ouer the affections, inclines the balance to that which is best.

*To fulfill.*] Hee affirmes not any time that in <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">The godly fail in performāce, not in purpose.</span> practice hee did fulfill Gods law, but in purpose. In resolution hee euer willed and desired: and albeit hee failed many times in performance, yet <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">☞</span> neuer did he change his purpose.

*To the end.] Dauids* godly motions vvere <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Beginnings of good are nothing without perseuerance.</span> not taken by starts: hee was no Temporizer, whose goodnesse is like the morning dewe. The seede of Gods vvord was deeper rooted in his heart: as hee had a care to beginne well; so also to continue well. *Frustra velociter currit,* <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Greg. moral.</span> *qui priusquam ad metam veniat, defecit:* Our life on earth is a race; in vaine beginnes hee to runne swiftly, that fainteth, and giues ouer before hee come to the end. <span class="cw-pb" id="p253" data-p="253" data-facs="134" title="Page 253 of the 1613 edition begins here (原书第 253 页起)"></span> And this was signified (saith *Gregory*) when in the Lawe the tayle of the beast was sacrificed with the rest; Perseuerance crowneth all. It is good we haue begun to do well; let vs also striue to perseuere to the end.
