### <span class="cw-pb" id="p6" data-p="6" data-facs="10" title="Page 6 of the 1613 edition begins here (原书第 6 页起)"></span>ALEPH.

<div class="cw-text" markdown="1">

VERS. 1.

Blessed are these that are vpright in their way, and walke in the Lawe of the Lord.

</div>

<span class="cw-dropcap">T</span>His verse beginnes the Psalme, with a <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">The felicity of man stands in a conformity with God.</span> summary proposition of the blessed estate of all Gods children. Properly onely God is blessed: *God ouer all* <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Basil. prae<span class="cw-gap" title="TCP: illegible in source（底本此处读不出）">•</span>in Psal.</span> *blessed for euer.* For this word <span class="cw-gap" title="TCP: illegible in source（底本此处读不出）">〈Greek〉</span>, signifies <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Rom. 9. 5.</span> *Immortalē; vt sit <span class="cw-gap" title="TCP: illegible in source（底本此处读不出）">〈Greek〉</span> idē quod <span class="cw-gap" title="TCP: illegible in source（底本此处读不出）">〈Greek〉</span>, id est immortalis,* the letter <span class="cw-gap" title="TCP: illegible in source（底本此处读不出）">〈Greek〉</span> in both the syllables being turn'd into <span class="cw-gap" title="TCP: illegible in source（底本此处读不出）">〈Greek〉</span> for the pleasantnes of the sound: yet this name is also attributed to Gods children, who of his mercy are deliuered from death, and allotted vnto eternall life.

The blessednesse of man is foure manner of <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Foure wayes is mans felicity described in holy Scripture.</span> wayes described in holy Scripture: First, from the originall thereof, and fountaine from which it flowes; and that is our election: so the Apostle, when he blesses the Lord, who hath blessed vs with all spirituall blessings in Christ, he begins <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Ephes. 1. 3.</span> at this; *As he hath chosen vs.* Secondly, from the beginning thereof in vs; and that is our calling and iustification: *Blessed is the man whom* <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Psalme.</span> <span class="cw-pb" id="p7" data-p="7" data-facs="10" title="Page 7 of the 1613 edition begins here (原书第 7 页起)"></span> *thou chusest and causest to come vnto thee.* Againe, *Blessed is he whose wickednesse is forgiuen,* <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Psal. 3<span class="cw-gap" title="TCP: illegible in source（底本此处读不出）">•</span>. 1.</span> *and whose sinne is couered.* Thirdly, from the progresse thereof, which is made by sanctification; as, *Blessed are they that hunger and thirst for* <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Matth. 5.</span> *righteousnesse:* and so here, *Blessed are they that are vpright in way.* Lastly, from the perfection therof; which shall be done in our finall glorification: vnto one of these may be referred euery sentence of holy Scripture, which makes mention of the happinesse and felicity of man.

*Vpright in their way.* We haue here first to see what is this Way; next, who are vpright in it. By the Way, vnderstand *Transitoriam hanc vitam;* <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Euthym.</span> and so indeed properly mans life on earth is <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Euery mans life is a Way, wherein without intermission, he walkes to the graue.</span> called a Way: for in it man walkes from the wombe to the graue, by so continuall a motion, that there is no resting nor returning for him into it. But as a Ship, which goes right before the winde, makes haste (without staying) toward her hauening place, what-euer they doe that are in her; whether they eate, or they walke, or they rest, still the Barke that carries them goes on to her proposed end: so is it with man in the body, so soone as he comes out of the wombe, he is carried by a continuall course to the graue, whether he sleepe or walke; whether he haue minde of it or not, he is carried on in his course to his end, without intermission. And in this sense the life of euery man, whether good or euill, is a <span class="cw-pb" id="p8" data-p="8" data-facs="11" title="Page 8 of the 1613 edition begins here (原书第 8 页起)"></span> Way, *Via vita est, hanc siquidem ab vtero ad sepulchrum* <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Basil.</span> *ambulamus omnes.*

But in this Way there is a great difference; for some are vpright in it: these are exponed to be such as walke in the way of the Lord; others againe *turne as<span class="cw-gap" title="TCP: illegible in source（底本此处读不出）">•</span>e after their crooked wayes.* So <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Psal. 125.</span> that in the life of euery man there are two motions <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">The different courses of good and euill men in it.</span> & courses: the one seene, w*ch* is common to good and euill; the other more secret: the seene course, is the way of a man from the wombe to the graue; this is common, and as *Ioshua* calles it, *The way of all flesh:* this is so sensible, that all men perceiue it; the growing of a man from infancie to child hood, from child-hood to young age, from young age to olde age: the other I said is more secret, wherein some by the crooked wayes <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">For there is one common to them both, which is seene: one other proper to each of them, which is secret.</span> of sinne, walke on vnto perdition; others by the way of holinesse to glorification. And this way I call more secret, because the course of wicked men is oft-times couered with hypocrisie: the course againe of godly men obscured by calumnie; both of them being esteemed among men, the one for good, the other for euill, that which indeed they are not.

*Who walke in the Law of the Lord.*] What before <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">A godly man directs his course by the word.</span> he spake obscurely, hee now deliuers more plainly; namely, that they who walke in the Law of the Lord, are vpright in way. The Lawe of God is the rule of our life; that narrow rodde, within the limits whereof, we should alway containe <span class="cw-pb" id="p9" data-p="9" data-facs="11" title="Page 9 of the 1613 edition begins here (原书第 9 页起)"></span> our selues, neyther declining to the right hand nor to the left; but euer proceeding, by this one rule, we should forget that which is behind, <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1"><span class="cw-gap" title="TCP: illegible in source（底本此处读不出）">〈◊〉</span> . 3. 1<span class="cw-gap" title="TCP: illegible in source（底本此处读不出）">•</span>.</span> and endeuour our selues to that which is before: and truely, seeing by the lawe onely our actions will be iudged, wisedome requires that by the lawe onely our actions should be squared.

<div class="cw-text" markdown="1">

VER. 2.

Blessed are they who keepe his testimomonies, and seeke him with their whole heart.

</div>

EVery man desires to be blessed; euen they <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Mans felicity stands in God his approbation.</span> who had but the light of nature sought after it, but could not find it: here the Lord, who in the last day will pronounce some to bee blessed, and some to be cursed, dooth now tell vs who they are. What can comfort them, to whom the Lord shall say, Depart from me ye cursed? Where away shall they goe, whom the Lord shall command to depart from him? And what greater ioy can com to a man, then to heare the Iudge of all saying vnto him, Come to me ye blessed? Oh that we were wise in time, to thinke of this, that so we might endeuour to becom such men, as to whom God, in his word, hath promised the blessing.

The doubling of the sentence, *Blessed blessed,* in the first verse and second, is to let vs see the certainty of the blessing belonging to the godly. <span class="cw-pb" id="p10" data-p="10" data-facs="12" title="Page 10 of the 1613 edition begins here (原书第 10 页起)"></span> The word of God is as true in it selfe, when it is <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">No malice of Sathan, no wrath of man, can curse where God hath blessed.</span> once spoken, as when it is many times repeated: the repetition of it, is for confirmation of our weake faith. That which *Isaac* spake of *Iacob, I haue blessed him, and he shall be blessed,* is the most sure decree of God vpon all his childen. Sathan would faine curse Israel, by the mouth of such as *Balaam* was: but he shall not be able to curse, because God hath blessed.

*Keepe his Testimonie.* The word of God is called <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Why Gods word is called the Testimony of God.</span> his Testimony, not onely because it testifies his will, concerning his seruice; but also his fauour and good wil, concerning his own in Christ Iesus. If the word of God were no more but a Law, yet were we bound to obey it, because we are his creatures: but sith it is also a Testimony of his loue, wherein as a father he witnesseth his fauour toward his children; we are twise inexcusable, if most ioyfully we doe not embrace it.

And againe, sith the word is Gods Testimony, <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Euery man should take heed what Gods word testifies to him.</span> let vs take heed what it testifies vnto vs. *Achab* complained to *Iosaphat* on *Michaiah,* that he neuer prophecied good things vnto him; but the blame was in *Achab* himselfe. *Are not my words,* <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Mich. 2. 7.</span> *saith the Lord, good vnto him that walkes vprightly?* If the word of the Lord witnesse no good vnto <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">☞</span> vs, it is because we are not good our selues<span class="cw-gap" title="TCP: illegible in source（底本此处读不出）">▪</span> *Vtilis enim aduersarius est,* it is enemy to none, but <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Aug.</span> such as are enemies to themselues. Let vs therefore take heed vnto it; for if the word condemne <span class="cw-pb" id="p11" data-p="11" data-facs="12" title="Page 11 of the 1613 edition begins here (原书第 11 页起)"></span> vs, then our conscience, and the Lorde who is greater then them both will much more condemne vs: but if we repent of our sinnes, and continue in mourning, seeking mercy, & amend our liues, then shall the word bring vnto vs the glad tidings of peace; yea, not onely shall the word without, but the spirit also testifie to our conscience within, that our sinnes are forgiuen vs.

Now, as concerning the keeping of Gods Testimonies, <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">How the children of God keepe his word in mind, affection, and action.</span> the children of God do it in minde, in affection, in action. In mind, as *Mary* laid vp the words of the Angell in her heart, and *Dauid* laid vp the promises of God in his hart, that he should not sin against the Lord: in affection, they haue euer a godly desire, and a purpose to practise it; ioyfull when they doe the commandements of God; sorrowfull when they transgresse them: in action, they haue a begun obedience, which God at length shall perfite in them; for *The righteousnesse* <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Rom. 8.</span> *of the Law shall be fulfilled in vs also.*

*And seeke him with their whole heart.* The <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">How necessary it is for vs to seeke the Lord.</span> seeking of God so often recommended in holy Scripture, imports, that naturally men are strangers from God, diuided from him by sinne: for what needeth a man to seeke that which he hath not lost? This is man his most miserable condition by nature; he hath lost the familiar presence of his God: *<span class="cw-gap" title="TCP: illegible in source（底本此处读不出）">•</span>or lo they that withdraw themselues from thee, shall perish; thou destr<span class="cw-gap" title="TCP: illegible in source（底本此处读不出）">•</span>yest all them* <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Psal. 73 27.</span> <span class="cw-pb" id="p12" data-p="12" data-facs="13" title="Page 12 of the 1613 edition begins here (原书第 12 页起)"></span> *that goe a whooring from thee.* What shall we do to remedie it? but practise that which there *Dauid* protests hee had concluded with himselfe; <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Ver. 28.</span> *As for me, it is good for me to draw neere vnto God:* we lost him by sinne, let vs seeke him by grace: we went a whooring from him by our iniquity, let vs returne vnto him by repentance: other-way, as he that will hide himselfe from the <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Miserable are they who seeke him not.</span> Sunne, empaires not the light thereof; hee may well shut himselfe in miserable darkenesse: so he that departs from the Lord, shall derogate nothing to his all-sufficient Maiesty; hee may well plunge himselfe in the bottomlesse pit of remedilesse misery.

But this euill, the losse of Gods familiar presence, <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">With what affection God should be sought.</span> is not felt by carnall men, so long as they enioy the comfort of his creatures: and therefore is it, that where they can seeke any other thing they want, with solicitude and care, eyther else they seeke him not at all; or then seeke him not as they should. As he that seekes a jewell which he hath lost, lookes still to see it, with so earnest an attention, that what <span class="cw-gap" title="TCP: illegible in source（底本此处读不出）">•</span>uer other thing is in his way, he sees it not; casting all other things out of his minde, *Vnum illud intendit,* <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Chrysost. in mat. hom. 24.</span> *quod solum requirit:* so is it with him that seekes the Lorde, *Mundana omnia pertransie<span class="cw-gap" title="TCP: illegible in source（底本此处读不出）">•</span>s illum solum inquirit;* passing by all other things <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Euthym.</span> in the world, he makes inquisition for God only. <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">☞</span> And truely, if it be the Lord whom we seeke, <span class="cw-pb" id="p13" data-p="13" data-facs="13" title="Page 13 of the 1613 edition begins here (原书第 13 页起)"></span> it will appeare by this; no other thing shall content vs, but still we will goe on, seeking himselfe, till we finde himselfe.

But we must remember sixe conditions, required <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Six conditions to be obserued in the seeking of God.</span> in them, who would seeke the Lord rightly: First, we must seeke him in Christ the Mediatour. No man can come to the Father but by the Son; <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">1 Seeke him in Christ.</span> and *He is able also perfectly to saue all that come vnto God by him.* And this excludes Papists (who <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Heb. 7. 25.</span> content them not with the Mediatour) from the right seeking of God. <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">2 Seeke him in truth.</span>

Secondly, we must seeke him in truth: for God is a God of truth; he will be worshipped in <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Ier. 10 10.</span> spirit and truth: for God is a Spirit, and loueth <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Ioh. 4. 2<span class="cw-gap" title="TCP: illegible in source（底本此处读不出）">•</span>.</span> truth in the inward affections. And this is the <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">P<span class="cw-gap" title="TCP: illegible in source（底本此处读不出）">•</span>l. 51.</span> condition which here is required; Blessed are they who seeke him with their whole heart. And this excludes hypocrits. <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">3 Seeke him in holinesse.</span>

Thirdly, we must seek him in holiness: *Let euery one that calles on the name of the Lord depart from iniquity:* and again, saith the Apostle, *Follow* <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Heb 12.</span> *peace and sanctification, without which no man can* <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">1 Ioh. 3.</span> *see the Lord.* And, *Whosoeuer hath this hope in himselfe* (namely, that he shall see God as he is) *purgeth himselfe, euen as he is pure.* And this condition excludes from seeking of God all Atheists, vncleane, vnpeni<span class="cw-gap" title="TCP: illegible in source（底本此处读不出）">•</span>ent persons. <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">4 S<span class="cw-gap" title="TCP: illegible in source（底本此处读不出）">•</span>eke him aboue all things, and for himselfe.</span>

Fourthly, we must seeke him aboue all things, and for himselfe: not as the carnall Iewes did, with whom the Lord was angry; *They howle* <span class="cw-pb" id="p14" data-p="14" data-facs="14" title="Page 14 of the 1613 edition begins here (原书第 14 页起)"></span> *vpon me for wine and oyle.* It is a great dishonouring <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Hos.</span> of God, when any thing is sought from him more then himself, or not for himself: *Quisquis à deo, praeter deum quaerit, non castè deum quaerit;* <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Aug.</span> as if his creatures serued not to this end, principally <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">☞</span> to lead vs vnto himselfe, or among them all there were any thing more precious then himselfe: and this excludes mercenaries and worldlings.

Fiftly, we must seek him by the light of his own <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">5 Seeke him by his owne light.</span> word: the Gentiles sought to finde him by the light of nature, but they *became vaine in their imaginations,* <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Rom. 1. 21.</span> *and their foolish heart being full of darknes,* could neuer find him. As the Sun without the light, of it selfe, cannot be knowne; so God, without his owne light, which shines in his word, cannot be known: *No man hath seene God at any time: the Sonne who hath come from the bosome of the Father, he hath declared him;* and the direction of the Sonne is, *Search the Scriptures.* And this excludes all those children of darkenesse, who disdayning the light of the word, depend vpon phantasies, or presumptions of their owne.

Last of all, we must seeke him diligently, and <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">6 Seek him without wearying.</span> with perseuerance; neuer resting till we finde him, with the Spouse in the Canticles. We must not seeke him by startings, casting off all ca<span class="cw-gap" title="TCP: illegible in source（底本此处读不出）">•</span>e when we finde him not at the first; but without wearying, wee must wait vpon him: *They that* <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Psal. 34. 5.</span> <span class="cw-pb" id="p15" data-p="15" data-facs="14" title="Page 15 of the 1613 edition begins here (原书第 15 页起)"></span> *looke vnto him* (that is, constantly attend vpon him) *their faces shall not bee ashamed.* And this excludes carelesse Christians, and temporizers, who seeke him by starts, but continue not.

VER. 3.

Surely they worke none iniquity, that walke in his wayes.

</div>

THis Verse containes a commendation of <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">If we keepe Gods word, it keepes vs.</span> the word of God, from this notable fruit and effect thereof: that they who keepe it, are kept by it from iniquity; and so made partakers by it, of the third degree of man his felicity, which is sanctification.

If it be demaunded here, How is it that they <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">How it is that the Saints of God worke no iniquity.</span> who walke in Gods wayes, worke none iniquitie? Is there any man who liues, and sinnes not? And if they be not without sinne, How then are they to be blessed? The answere is, as the Apostle sayes of our knowledge; *Wee knowe but in a part:* so is it true of our felicity on earth, wee are blessed but in a part. It is the happinesse of Angels, that they neuer sinned: it is the happinesse of triumphant Saints, that albeit they haue beene sinners, yet now they sinne no more; but the happinesse of Saints militant, is, that our sinnes are forgiuen vs; and that albeit sinne remaine in vs, yet it raignes not ouer vs: <span class="cw-pb" id="p16" data-p="16" data-facs="15" title="Page 16 of the 1613 edition begins here (原书第 16 页起)"></span> it is done in vs, but not by our allowance: *I doe the euill which I would not; not I, but sinne that* <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Rom. 7.</span> *dwels in me.*

Secondly, to the working of iniquity, these <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Three things concurre to the working of iniquity.</span> three things must concurre; first, a purpose to do it; next, a delight in doing it; thirdly a continuance in it: which three, in Gods children neuer <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">☞</span> concurre; for in sinnes done in them by the old man, the new man makes his exceptions and protestations against them: It is not I, sayes hee; and so farre is hee from delighting in them, that rather his soule is grieued with them: euen as *Lot,* dwelling among the Sodomites, was vexed by hearing and seeing their vnrighteous deeds. In a word, the children of GOD are rather sufferers of sinne against their willes, then actors of it with their willes; like men spiritually oppressed by the power of their enemie: for which they sigh and crie vnto God, *Miserable man that I am, who will deliuer mee from this bodie of Death?* And in this sense it is, that the Apostle saith, *Hee who is borne of GOD sinneth* <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">1. Ioh. 3.</span> *not.*

*In his wayes.*] The course of mans life, ordered <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Why the life of a godly man is called Gods way.</span> according to the worde of the Lorde, is called the way of GOD; first, because it pleaseth him; and next, because it leades vs vnto him. There are many desirous to be where the Lord is, that shall neuer com there; & all because they delight not in the way, that leads vnto him: <span class="cw-pb" id="p17" data-p="17" data-facs="15" title="Page 17 of the 1613 edition begins here (原书第 17 页起)"></span> blessed are they who walke in this vvay. Confirme mine heart, ô GOD, that I may keep it vnto the end.

VER. 4.

Thou hast commanded to keepe thy precepts diligently.

</div>

AL the sinnes vvhich in this age men commit <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">The sinnes of this age, are either of obliuion or rebellion: excuse of ignorance is taken from them.</span> against the Lord, proceed eyther from rebellion, or at least, from obliuion of his commaundements: for now in this great light, all excuse of ignorance is taken away. None of the former two are good; but of the two, rebellion is the vvorst. Gods children abhorre it as a diuelish euill, to rebell against God: how-euer by obliuion many times they fall, both in sinnes of omission and commission; so long as they remember what God hath commanded to be done, and what reasons they haue to obey him, they are not easily snared: but beeing once <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">By obliuion men are easily caried to rebellion.</span> preuented by obliuion, they are easily caried vnto transgression. It is good therefore to keep in our hearts with *Dauid,* this short remedy against obliuion. *Thou hast commaunded to keepe thy precepts diligently.* As the eyes of seruants looke vnto the hand of their Masters: so should our <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Psal. 123. 2.</span> eyes vvait vpon the Lord our GOD, euer looking to that way, vvhere-vnto his countenaunce <span class="cw-gap" title="TCP: illegible in source（底本此处读不出）">•</span>oth direct vs.

<span class="cw-pb" id="p18" data-p="18" data-facs="16" title="Page 18 of the 1613 edition begins here (原书第 18 页起)"></span> *Diligently.*] In worldly affaires no weighty <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Three reasons to moue vs to a carefull diligence to obey the Lord.</span> thing can be done without diligence: farre lesse in spirituall. *Cursed be he that doth the worke of the Lord negligently.* For three causes should we keepe the commandements of the Lord with diligence: first, because our aduersary, that seeks to snare vs by the transgression of them, is diligent in tempting; *For he goes about, night and day, seeking to deuoure vs:* next, because we our selues are weake and infirme; by the greater diligence haue we need to take heed to our selues: thirdly because of the great losse we sustaine by euery vantage Sathan gets ouer vs. For we finde by experience, that as a wound is sooner made then <span class="cw-gap" title="TCP: illegible in source（底本此处读不出）">•</span> is healed: so guiltines of conscience is easily contracted, but not so easily done away.

<div class="cw-text" markdown="1">

VER. 5.

O that my wayes were directed to keepe thy Statutes!

</div>

IN the former verse, *Dauid* hath meditated <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">The godly answer Gods pre<span class="cw-gap" title="TCP: illegible in source（底本此处读不出）">•</span>epts with a prayer.</span> vpon the commandement of God. Now the fruit of his meditation is, ye see, a prayer vnto God; wherein he wishes, that all his actions were answerable to the commandements of God<span class="cw-gap" title="TCP: illegible in source（底本此处读不出）">▪</span> this is customable vnto the godly, to answere all the precepts of God by prayers; what he commaunds them to doe, they seeke from himselfe grace to doe it.

<span class="cw-pb" id="p19" data-p="19" data-facs="16" title="Page 19 of the 1613 edition begins here (原书第 19 页起)"></span> *My wayes.*] In this Psalme, sometime ye see <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">It cannot be well with man, when his way is contrary to Gods way.</span> mention is made of Gods wayes, as Vers. 3. and sometime of man his wayes, as Vers. 5. It is well with man, when his wayes and Gods wayes are all one: for if man haue another way then the way of God, of necessity his end must be miserable. God is the fountaine of life; he that walks not with God, abides in death: God is the father of light; he that will goe from him, shall goe to blacknes of darknes, yea, to vtter darknes, where there is weeping and gnashing of teeth: *Loe, all they that withdraw themselues from thee shall perish;* therefore will we resolue with *Dauid, It is good for me to draw neere vnto the Lord.*

*Dauid* knowing that naturally man is diuided <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">It is a worke of Gods mercy and power, to draw a man from his owne way to Gods way.</span> from God, and hath a way of his own, which will not faile to lead him vnto a miserable end: he wisheth from his heart, to be vnited with the Lord; that Gods will were his will, and Gods way were his way. This is a worke aboue the power of nature: and therfore he humbly prayes, that God <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Euthym.</span> would worke it, *Dignoscens quod ex seipso nihil possit efficere, nisi Dei ope, & gratia adiutus fuerit;* humbly acknowledging, that in the framing of his waies to Gods will, of himselfe he was able to do nothing, without the help of grace: *We are not of our selues able to thinke a good thought; our sufficiency is of God:* neither is it in him that wils, nor in him that runnes, but in God that shewes mercie.

<span class="cw-pb" id="p20" data-p="20" data-facs="17" title="Page 20 of the 1613 edition begins here (原书第 20 页起)"></span>VER. 6.

Then should I not be confounded, when I haue respect vnto all thy commandements.

SInne hath many euill fruites: it offends <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">The miserable effects of sin.</span> God, and grieues his Spirit; it hurts thy neighbour: for by it, thou temptst some, and infectst others; but the sosest wound it giues to thy selfe: for it brings vpon thee shame & confusion, and in the end eternall death; *For sinne* <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Iam. 1.</span> *when it is finished, brings out death. Thy owne* <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">It offends both God and man; but hurts him most that did <span class="cw-gap" title="TCP: illegible in source（底本此处读不出）">•</span>ommit it. Ierem.</span> *wickednes shall reproue thee: and thou shalt knowe that it is an euill thing & a bitter, that thou hast for saken the Lord thy God.* How were *Ad<span class="cw-gap" title="TCP: illegible in source（底本此处读不出）">•</span>m* and *Euah* consounded after their fall? how ran they away from the Lord, in whom they delighted before? How lurked they among the b<span class="cw-gap" title="TCP: illegible in source（底本此处读不出）">•</span>shes? thinking shame of thēselues, they sought to couer their nakednes. *Praeuaricationis enim fructus* <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1"><span class="cw-gap" title="TCP: illegible in source（底本此处读不出）">•</span>asil.</span> *est cōfusio.* If we think to pluck better fruit from the tree of sinne, we doe but deceiue our selues. It standeth true in all, which the Apostle spake of the Romans: *When ye were the seruants of sin, yee were freed frō righteousnes: what fruite had yee then of those things wherof yee are now ashamed<span class="cw-gap" title="TCP: illegible in source（底本此处读不出）">▪</span>* The end of these things is death. If therefore vvee thinke euill to be confounded with shame, let vs bevvare we be not perverted by sinne.

*Respect to all thy Commandements.*] Wee must not make a diuision at our owne hand of the <span class="cw-pb" id="p21" data-p="21" data-facs="17" title="Page 21 of the 1613 edition begins here (原书第 21 页起)"></span> commaundements of GOD; making a shew to <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Wee should make conscience of all God his commaundements.</span> keepe some, and taking libertie to transgresse others: as *Naaman* did, who resolued that hee vvould offer no sacrifice vnto any other God, saue onely to the Lord; onely this he reserued, that he would bow with his Master the King of Assur to the Idole *Rimmon.* <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">The halfe obe<span class="cw-gap" title="TCP: illegible in source（底本此处读不出）">•</span>ience of the wicked, is like the voice of an Eccho.</span>

Like him are many now; vvho professe they will serue the Lord, but still reserue an Idole of their owne, to the which their heart enclines: so answer the Lord with halfe obedience; like the Eccho, which makes not a perfit respondence of the voice of men, but of some part thereof. But the commaundements of GOD are so vnited among themselues, that *hee who failes in one point* <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Iam. 2. 10.</span> *of the law, is guiltie of all:* and therfore, to euery one of them should we giue obedience; wherin, if wee cannot doe what wee should, yet at least should we haue a respect, a purpose, and a care so to do, hauing a begun obedience to them all, not exempting our selues frō any. And this for them who thinke all is well if they be not adulterers, when in the meane time they are idolaters; and so forth of the rest.

<span class="cw-pb" id="p22" data-p="22" data-facs="18" title="Page 22 of the 1613 edition begins here (原书第 22 页起)"></span>VER. 7.

I will praise thee with an vpright heart, vvhen I shall learne the iudgements of thy righteousnes.

</div>

IN this verse wee haue a prayer for further <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Thankfulnes to God commended for foure causes.</span> knowledge; together with a protestation of *Dauid* his thankfull affection for it. Thankfulnes is a dutie, wherein we are all obliged to the Lord. *It is a good thing for vpright men to praise* <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Psalme.</span> *thee Lord.* It is good, first, in regard of the equitie <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">First, for the equitie of it; sith wee take good things from God, why should we not giue him glory?</span> of it. Sith the Lord giues vs good things, shal not we giue him praises againe? especially seeing the Lord is content so to part all his works between his Maiestie, and vs, that the good of them be ours, the glorie of them be his owne. When we haue taken good things from him, why shall we defraude him of his part, that is, glory by thanksgiuing? Surely, the earth, vvhich renders increase to them that labour it, the oxe that knowes his owner, and euery beast in their kind, that bowes to the hand of him who giues it meat, shal condemne vnthankfull man, who receiues dailie from the Lord, but neuer returnes praises vnvnto him.

Secondly, it is good to praise the Lord in regard <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Secondly, for the obiect thereof; which is God, the treasure of all good.</span> of himselfe, who is the obiect of our prayses. Sith he is the treasure of all good, the author of all blessings, it cannot be but a good and blessed thing to blesse him.

<span class="cw-pb" id="p23" data-p="23" data-facs="18" title="Page 23 of the 1613 edition begins here (原书第 23 页起)"></span>Thirdly, it is good in respect of our associates <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Thirdly, for the associates wee haue in this exercise; namely, Angels, & our elder brethren.</span> & companions in this exercise: the Angels, Cherubins, and Seraphins, delight continually in his praises. Our elder brethren, that glorious congregation of the first borne, are described vnto vs, falling downe on their faces, casting their crownes at the feet of the Lord, to giue him the glory of their redemption. Now, seeing wee pray that the will of God may be done in earth, as it is in heauen, why doe we not delight in these exercises of praising God, by which we haue fellowship with them who are glorified in heauen? <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Lastly, for the great good we get by it.</span>

Lastly, it is good in respect of the great benefits vvee receiue by it: nothing thereby accrues to the Lord, all the vantage is our owne; *Gratiarum actio, est ad plus dandum in vitatio.*

*With an vpright heart.*] Many musicall instruments <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">The best musicall instrument for Gods prayses, is an vprigh: <span class="cw-gap" title="TCP: illegible in source（底本此处读不出）">•</span>are.</span> had they vnder the law, where-with they praysed God, as ye may see in the last Psalm: but such as were spirituall among them, knew that all these availed nothing, vnlesse the hart had been well tuned, and prepared. This is to make them ashamed, who now in a greater light, come to praise GOD, but forget their hart behind them; or else bring it very euill tuned, full of so many discordant desires, that they cannot (as they are commaunded) make melody to the Lord in their harts. <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Both the matter, & the grace of thankfulnes is from God.</span>

*When I shall learne.*] But when, saies *Dauid,* he will be thankfull? euen when GOD shall learne <span class="cw-pb" id="p24" data-p="24" data-facs="19" title="Page 24 of the 1613 edition begins here (原书第 24 页起)"></span> him: both the matter and the grace of thankfulnesse is from God. As he did with *Abraham,* he commaunded him to worship, and gaue him the sacrifice: so doth he with all his children; for he giues not onely good things for vvhich they should thanke him, but in like manner grace, by which they are able to thanke him.

VER. 8.

I will keepe thy Statutes, forsake me not ouer-long.

</div>

THis verse containes a protestation, that <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Purposes wold be seconded with prayers.</span> he was resolued to keepe the Lords Statutes: which because he knewe of himselfe he was not able to accomplish; with this protestation of his purpose, he ioynes a prayer, wherin he craues to be assisted, and not forsaken of the Lord.

It is a great helpe to godlinesse, to resolue that <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">A resolution to be godly, is a great beginning of a godly life.</span> we will liue godly; for that which is not concluded, how shall it be performed? or what hope is there we should attaine to the end, that is, to the perfection of pietie, when we are carelesse of the beginnings thereof? which are purposes, intentions, and resolutions that wee will be godly? Where, when of weakenesse we faile in following foorth our resolution, it shall be well done againe to renew it: for by often renewing of our resolution to doe any good, wee become the <span class="cw-pb" id="p25" data-p="25" data-facs="19" title="Page 25 of the 1613 edition begins here (原书第 25 页起)"></span> stronger to accomplish it. <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">A fearefull plague to be forsaken of God.</span>

*Forsake me not ouer-long.*] It is a feareful thing to be vtterly and finally forsaken of the Lord, as *Iudas* was, who therefore *Parata inimico praeda factus est.* For man left vtterly to himselfe, becomes <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1"><span class="cw-gap" title="TCP: illegible in source（底本此处读不出）">•</span>asil.</span> a prey to the enemy, and can no more stand by himselfe, then a staffe not sustained by the hand of man: and therefore doth *Dauid* pray that the Lord would not forsake him.

And yet because he knew, that the Lord exercises <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Gods children many times exercised with temporall desertions.</span> his deerest children with temporall desertions, forsaking them for a time, and withdrawing his helping hand from them, *ex eo commodū ipsorum procurans* (as is euident in *Peter,* vvho was left for a time to himselfe, that the proofe of his owne weaknes might make him more humble<span class="cw-gap" title="TCP: illegible in source（底本此处读不出）">▪</span> and lesse confident in himselfe) therefore he craues not simply to be freed of desertion; but that, if the Lord will desert him, to try him, or to humble him, he would not desert him ouerlong.

Such as know what help and comfort the presence <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">To a godly man, a <span class="cw-gap" title="TCP: illegible in source（底本此处读不出）">•</span>hort time of Gods absence is long</span> of the Lord in mercy brings to his childrē, they think a short time of his absence a very long time. *Absalom* spake out of his policie, that it was better for him to die, then liue and not see his Fathers face: but in truth, it is more bitter then death to the godly, to liue in the body, and not be refreshed with the fauourable beames of the countenance of God: and therfore they preuent <span class="cw-pb" id="p26" data-p="26" data-facs="20" title="Page 26 of the 1613 edition begins here (原书第 26 页起)"></span> the desertion with prayer, as heere *Dauid* doth, *Forsake me not ouer-long.* And when they are exercised with it, al other comforts are loathsome to them: they sigh and cry continually, *How long, ô Lord, how long? Will the Lord absent himselfe for euer, &c.* They run with the Spouse in the *Canticles,* to & fro, seeking him; and with the mourning *Maries,* they shed teares vncessantly, till they find him againe, and hee shew his fauorable face vnto them.
