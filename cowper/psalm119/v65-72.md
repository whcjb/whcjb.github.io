### <span class="cw-pb" id="p165" data-p="165" data-facs="89" title="Page 165 of the 1613 edition begins here (原书第 165 页起)"></span>TETH.

<div class="cw-text" markdown="1">

VER. 65.

O Lord, thou hast dealt graciously with thy seruaunt; according to thy word.

</div>

<span class="cw-dropcap">I</span>N the verse following, *Dauid* seekes <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Gods mercy shewed to a man, is an vndoubted argument, that hee will shew more mercie.</span> mercy; in this, hee giues thankes for mercy receiued. Mercies receiued, as they should be returned with praise to him who gaue thē; so should they confirme our harts in an expectation of greater to bee receiued. It is not with God, as it is with man; a man, the more he giueth, the lesse he hath: it is not so with the Lord. Among men, this is a reason as they alledge, why they should not giue, I haue giuen you already, why then doe yee trouble mee any more? but it is not so with God; the treasures of his grace can neuer be emptied: whom he loues, he loues vnto the end; and to him that hath, hee giueth more. Yea, all that now wee get, hee willeth <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">His present gifts, are but pledges of greater.</span> vs to receiue, as an earnest, or a pledge of greater good hee hath to giue vs.

So *Dauid* confirmes himselfe, Psalme 23. that because the Lord had beene a Shepheard to him <span class="cw-pb" id="p166" data-p="166" data-facs="90" title="Page 166 of the 1613 edition begins here (原书第 166 页起)"></span> in time by-gone, hee gathers this conclusion, *Doubtlesse, kindnesse and mercy shall follow me all* <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Psalm. 23.</span> *the daies of my life.*

But how saith *Dauid,* did God deale graciously with him, seeing hee did humble him from his <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">How God is gracious, euen whē he afflicts.</span> youth with many sore afflictions? These agree very well together: for the Lord is most louing when he chastiseth. *Etiā in seueritate est bonitas* <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Ambrose.</span> *Dei; vt recurrat vnusquisque castigatus, & pedem referat à peccatis, & ad tramitem, bonamque conuersationem reuertatur:* euen in seueritie, Gods gracious goodnesse appeares; in that hee vvho is chastised, returnes from his sinnes, to the right trade of a godlie conuersation.

Will any man accuse a Physition of cruelty, <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">The same declared by sundry similitudes.</span> because hee cutteth away the rotten member vvith yron, or burnes with fire a fretting canker? Or shall the Master bee blamed, for correcting his negligent Disciple, that hee may make him more diligent, and attentiue to learning? *Sic castigare, amantis est, non execrantis; & ideo bonitatis est, noncrudelitatis<span class="cw-gap" title="TCP: illegible in source（底本此处读不出）">▪</span>* so to chastise, is the vvorke of one that loueth, not that hateth; there is no crueltie in that, but gratious goodnesse.

And albeit these chastisements seeme not sweete for the present, yet afterward (as the Apostle saith) they bring the quiet fruite of righteousnesse to them vvho are thereby exercised. And therefore, *Dauid* still craueth to bee <span class="cw-pb" id="p167" data-p="167" data-facs="90" title="Page 167 of the 1613 edition begins here (原书第 167 页起)"></span> taught of GOD the same manner of way; *Sciebat namque Propheta disciplinae vtilitatem:* because <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Basil.</span> hee had learned by experience, vvhat great good Gods discipline doth to the soule of a man.

Yee may perceiue by this, how *Dauid* kept remembrances <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Memorials of mercy should be kept.</span> of the seuerall proofes of Gods fauour shewed vnto him; in performing to him the promised kingdome; and in deliuering him from many outward, and inward temptations. Thus the children of GOD, keepe with themselues memorials of mercie receiued: for albeit the time of the full performance of Gods promises bee not yet come; yet doth he performe so much of them, as binds vs in all conscience to remember his praises, who is a most true and mercifull God vnto vs.

*Thy seruaunt.] Dauid* frequently delights in <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">The seruant of God, an honorable and comfortable stile.</span> this stile. Hee found that his greatest comfort stood in the seruing of GOD in a good conscience: and if we do not so, with what boldnes can we looke for comfort from him in the day of our trouble, or houre of death, who made no conscience of his seruice? Yea rather, iustly may the Lord giue vs that fearefull answer, which he gaue to the rebellious Iewes, whē they sought his help in the day of their distresse; *Goe vnto the gods whō* <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Iudg. 16.</span> *yee haue serued, and let them deliuer you.*

*According to thy vvord.*] Naturall men will not belieue, that GOD will doe according to <span class="cw-pb" id="p168" data-p="168" data-facs="91" title="Page 168 of the 1613 edition begins here (原书第 168 页起)"></span> his vvord: they haue it in derision. *The vision* <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">The wicked dishonor God, when they call in doubt the truth of his word.</span> say they, *is but wind.* In so dooing, they highly offend the maiestie of GOD, imputing this note of dishonestie vnto him, That hee is not so good as his word: but in experience they shall find the contrary.

The godly shall find the truth of his word in mercie; as *Ezechias* did, so shall they praise him: <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Esay 37.</span> *The Lord hath said it, and the Lord hath done it;* but the wicked shal find it in iudgement, *Your* <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Zach. 1. But they shall find it a true word.</span> *Fathers, vvhere are they? and doe the Prophets liue for euer? But did not my word, and my Statutes, vvhich I commaunded by my seruaunts the Prophets, take hold on your Fathers? And they returned, and said; As the Lord of hosts hath determined to doe vnto vs, according to our ovvne waies and works, so assuredly hath he dealt with vs.* So shall all the wicked find at the length, that as God hath a mouth to speak: so hath he a hand to execute it; *Then shall they discerne betweene* <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Malac. 3. 18.</span> *the righteous and the wicked, between him that serueth God, and him that serueth him not.*

<div class="cw-text" markdown="1">

VER. 66.

Teach mee good iudgement & knowledge: for I haue beleeued thy cōmaundements.

</div>

THis verse containes a prayer with a reason. In the prayer, he beseecheth God to teach him good iudgement. The word <span class="cw-pb" id="p169" data-p="169" data-facs="91" title="Page 169 of the 1613 edition begins here (原书第 169 页起)"></span> *Tob Tagnam,* signifieth the goodnesse of taste: so <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">The Vnderstanding is the taster of the soule, that discerns between truth & falsehood.</span> *Vatablus, Bonitatem sensus doce me.* The naturall sense of tasting, is heere by a Metaphortranslated to signifie iudgement and vnderstanding: and that because as taste discernes meat, & makes choise of vvhat is to bee sent into the stomacke; <span class="cw-gap" title="TCP: illegible in source（底本此处读不出）">•</span>o the vnderstanding discernes betweene truth to be embraced, and falshood to be reiected. This is the grace for which the Apostle prayeth, to the <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Phil. 1. 9. Many want this Sense.</span> *Philippians. I pray that yee may abound more and more in knowledge, and in all iudgement, that yee may discerne things which are excellent.*

This is a rare grace. For many by the iudgement of light, knowes what is good, and what <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">☜</span> is euill, who knowe it not by the iudgement of taste: for if by sense they felt the bitternesse of sinne, and sweetnesse of righteousnesse, they wold not so loue the one, & set light by the other as they doe.

And againe, this puts a difference between that knowledge which wee haue in this life, and that <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Our knowledge in this <span class="cw-gap" title="TCP: illegible in source（底本此处读不出）">•</span>ife, <span class="cw-gap" title="TCP: illegible in source（底本此处读不出）">•</span>ar infe<span class="cw-gap" title="TCP: illegible in source（底本此处读不出）">•</span> to that we sh<span class="cw-gap" title="TCP: illegible in source（底本此处读不出）">•</span> haue in the <span class="cw-gap" title="TCP: illegible in source（底本此处读不出）">〈◊〉</span> <span class="cw-gap" title="TCP: illegible in source（底本此处读不出）">•</span>o come.</span> which we shall haue in the life to come. Now we haue but a taste, then shall we be filled, and satisfied with his image; now wee knowe but in part, heereafter wee shall knowe fully; now we walke by faith, then wee shall walke by sight; now we <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">2. Cor. 5. 7.</span> haue but the earnest of the Spirit, but in heauen wee shall be filled with his graces, and receiue the principall summe.

Now that *Dauid* <span class="cw-gap" title="TCP: illegible in source（底本此处读不出）">•</span>o frequentlie and feruentlie <span class="cw-pb" id="p170" data-p="170" data-facs="92" title="Page 170 of the 1613 edition begins here (原书第 170 页起)"></span> desires the Lord to teach him, see ver. 34.

*For I belieue.*] Sometime, *Dauid* brings the <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Gods promises cannot cōfort vs, if we be not of their number to whom they belong.</span> reason of his petition, from Gods mercy, goodnesse, righteousnesse, power, and so forth: sometime againe from himselfe; as from his loue, his feare, his faith in God, and some such like. It is not sufficient to seeke from God, because of that which he is; wee must also consider what vvee are: otherwise, as *Iehu* said vnto one demanding, *Is it peace; What hast thou to doe vvith peace?* So may it bee answered to vs; Though the Lord bee good, and gracious, and mercifull, yet vvhat is that vnto vs, if vvee bee such as beleeue not in him, loue him not, and feare him not?

<div class="cw-text" markdown="1">

VER. 67.

Before I was afflicted, I went astray: but now I keepe thy word.

</div>

IN this verse, *Dauid* protests that the chastisements <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Affliction in the godly, worketh humiliation.</span> of God, had made him more godlie. *Euthymius* thinks, *Dauids* meaning is, that his sinnes had made him more humble. This is true in the godly, that their sinnes humble them, and make them more godly, as is euident in *S. Paule:* but the former sense is most agreeable to these words: as ye may perceiue, ver. 71.

As the *Philistims* could not vnderstand *Samsons* riddle, How sweet came out of the sowre, <span class="cw-pb" id="p171" data-p="171" data-facs="92" title="Page 171 of the 1613 edition begins here (原书第 171 页起)"></span> and meat out of the eater: so can not worldlings <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Worldlings can not vnderstand how good can come by the Crosse.</span> vnderstand, what comfort is in the Crosse, or what profit comes by afflictions. But certaine it is, there is none truly godlie, but they will testifie this, that either they first learned Religion, or at least, vvere confirmed in it, by some notable crosse.

But this is to be vnderstood of sanctified crosses, <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Onely sanctified Crosses worke good.</span> vvith the vvhich GOD ioynes grace: such a Crosse changeth the nature of it, and is not *Poena, sed remedium delinquentis;* a punishment <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Ambrose.</span> of sinne, but a remedie. Otherwise, as of their owne nature, afflictions are euill, being the fruits of sinne: so they bring out euill fruites in the wicked, as impatience, murmuring, blasphemie; <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">☜</span> for, if euen good things become euill vnto them, what maruaile that by those which are euil, they become worse?

*But now I keepe thy word.*] To be afflicted <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">To be afflicted & not purged, chasti<span class="cw-gap" title="TCP: illegible in source（底本此处读不出）">•</span>ed, & not corrected, is an euill token.</span> and not purged by affliction, to be striken vvith the roddes of God, and no correction to follow, is an argument of fearefull induration. As siluer metall put in the fire, if nothing come of it but <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Ierem. 6.</span> dross, is found to be reprobat siluer: so these men who are not fined in the fornace of affliction, if they so continue, shall be found reprobate men; the lead is consumed, the bellowes are burnt, but their wickednesse is not taken from them.

Alas! how many are there such among vs, of whō the Lord may complaine, *Wherefore should* <span class="cw-pb" id="p172" data-p="172" data-facs="93" title="Page 172 of the 1613 edition begins here (原书第 172 页起)"></span> *yee be smitten any more? for yee fall away more* <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Esay 1.</span> *and more.* God hath strooken them with Pestilence, <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Many now become worse after deliuerance from trouble.</span> beside many other roddes; he hath marked their bodies with the Byle: but after their deliuerance, they haue become more profane then euer they were before; as if now they had escaped all wrath of God, and no plague after this could ouer-take them, sith the Pestilence had not deuoured them.

But as *Esau* spake of his fathers blessings, *Hast thou but one blessing, my father?* so doe wee wish <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">These are reserued to sharper scourges.</span> them consider of Gods plagues: Hath the Lord but one plague? The *Sodomits* escaped the sword of *Chedarlaomer,* and the perill of the Lime-pits: but they perished with fire from heauen. And the Israelites, who drowned not in the Red sea; because they repented not, many of them were stinged to death by fiery Serpēts. So the wicked, escaping some heauie iudgement, and still continuing in their sins, are but reserued to a worse: <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Gods last stroke is alway heauiest.</span> as if one flying a Beare, should be deuoured of a Lyon; for the last plague of God, is euer heauiest <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Amos 5. 19.</span> to the impenitent.

<div class="cw-text" markdown="1">

VER. 68.

Thou art good and gracious: teach mee thy statutes.

</div>

HE repeates his former petition; desiring <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">A godly man is couetous of heauenly instruction.</span> againe that God would teach him. And no maruaile he frequently makes this <span class="cw-pb" id="p173" data-p="173" data-facs="93" title="Page 173 of the 1613 edition begins here (原书第 173 页起)"></span> prayer, *Diuinae doctrinae auidus,* couetous of heauenly instruction: for he had not onely himselfe to gouerne in his priuat conuersation; but being called to be a gouernour of others, he prayeth so much the more earnestly, that God would teach and gouerne him.

The reason is taken from Gods goodnesse in <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">How due vnto God is the praise of goodnesse.</span> himselfe, & his beneficence toward his creature: *Bonus es & beneficus.* When we haue to do with men, we labour by praysing them to conciliate their fauour; and oftentimes we praise them for that which is not in them, but we would wish to be in them: or else in so praysing them, admonishing them what they should be: *Quanto magis Deo laus sua est deferenda, cuius proprium est, vt* <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Ambros. in Psa. 119.</span> *bonus sit? qui nisi bonus esset super terram stare quis possit?* How much more should Gods owne praise be giuen to himselfe, whose propertie it is to be good, yea, goodnesse it selfe? And if he were not good, none vpon earth could stand before him.

And it is this goodnesse of God, that moues <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">It is his owne goodnesse that makes him good to his creature.</span> him to be beneficiall, & gratious to his creature: *Omne enim bonum est Communicatiuum sui;* for euery good thing communicates the selfe vnto another. And as it excels in goodnesse, so excels it in this vertue, of communicating the selfe vnto others: as ye see the Sunne, which is a most excellent light, doth aboue all <span class="cw-gap" title="TCP: illegible in source（底本此处读不出）">〈◊〉</span> lights communicate his light to other <span class="cw-gap" title="TCP: illegible in source（底本此处读不出）">〈◊〉</span> . And if so it <span class="cw-pb" id="p174" data-p="174" data-facs="94" title="Page 174 of the 1613 edition begins here (原书第 174 页起)"></span> be with the creatures which are good, how much more with the Creator, who is goodnes it selfe? And this is the reason whereby our Sauiour confirmes vs; *If ye who are euill, can giue good thinges* <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Luke 11.</span> *to your children, how much more shall your heauenly Father, who is good, giue good things to them that seeke from him?* And surely, if wee knew with *Dauid,* how good the Lord is in himselfe, <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">A great blessing to be in fellowship with God.</span> how gracious, and beneficent to them who wait vpon him, we would account it a greater blessing then we doe, to be in fellowship with him; and would testifie it, by a more carefull seeking of his fauour by prayer.

<div class="cw-text" markdown="1">

VER. 69.

The proud haue imagined a lie against me: but I wil keep thy precepts with my whol hart.

</div>

HE professeth here again his constancy in <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Constancie in Religion.</span> religion, amplifying it by this circumstance; that albeit hee was hated & persecuted by wicked men, yet still he followed after godliness. Here we haue three things: First, who were *Dauids* enemies; Proud men. Secondly, how they did warre against him; by lies. Thirdly, how they trimmed their lies. <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Wicked men are restlesse enemies to the godly.</span>

To the first, it is manifest by *Dauids* example, how the godly are exercised with the continuall inimitie of the wicked. *Quanto magis quis Deo* <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Ambr. in Psal. 119.</span> *seruice <span class="cw-gap" title="TCP: illegible in source（底本此处读不出）">•</span>esiderat, tanto magis in se excitat aduersarios:* <span class="cw-pb" id="p175" data-p="175" data-facs="94" title="Page 175 of the 1613 edition begins here (原书第 175 页起)"></span> yea, the more a man sets himselfe to serue God, the more stirs hee vp Sathan and his instruments, to be aduersaries vnto him. No band of <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">No band of nature can conioyne them, whom grace hath not conioyned.</span> nature can conioyne them, whom grace hath not conioyned. *Iacob* & *Esau,* both gotten of one father *Isaac,* both borne of one mother *Rebecca,* lying both together in one wombe, yet euen there doe they fight together. Let vs neuer looke for peace, where God hath proclaimed warre; the world wil hate vs bicause we are not of the world. Let vs neuer be dismaied when we are crossed by them, but rather so much the more comforted; being assured, that while as wicked men are enemies to vs for a good cause, we are vpon that side whereof Christ is Captaine, his Saints are souldiers, and victory is most certaine. <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Why wicked mē are termed proud men.</span>

Neither is it without cause, that wicked men are so commonly called proud men; for pride is the mother of all rebellion against God and man. By pride, Satan and his confederat Apostats vsurped to be like vnto God: and by the same sin, he drew man into the similitude of his condemnation; so that now, euery man by nature is a proud man: which makes him shake off the yoke of God, <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">P<span class="cw-gap" title="TCP: illegible in source（底本此处读不出）">•</span>ide an horrible euill.</span> and without regard, transgress the limits of obedience appointed to him by God. As *Phara<span class="cw-gap" title="TCP: illegible in source（底本此处读不出）">•</span>* <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">☜</span> would not let Israel go til the Lord slew his first borne: so our nature now corrupted, shall neuer render obedience to God, nor loue to man, til the first borne sin, that is, pride, be subdued by grace.

<span class="cw-pb" id="p176" data-p="176" data-facs="95" title="Page 176 of the 1613 edition begins here (原书第 176 页起)"></span>Forgrace, on the contrary, euer works humility: <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Grace workes humility in the godly.</span> so soone as the eyes of Gods children are opened to see their sinnes, they abhorre themselues; the combe of their naturall pride is pulled downe, and they abase themselues before God and man. It was the humble speech of *Abraham* the great father of the faithfull; *I am but dust and ashes:* it was the voyce of *Iacob; I am not* <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Gen. 18. 27.</span> *worthy of the least of Gods mercies: Dauid* hath the like; *Who am I Lord? Gedeons* voyce; *My fathers* <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Iudg. 6. 15. Examples prouing that they who are greatest in Gods estimation, are least in their owne eyes.</span> *house is the least in all Israel:* and the Baptist (who receiued this praise, That a greater Prophet was not among the children of women) acknowledged in humility, That he was not worthy to loose the latchet of Christs shooe: the Centurion confessed, hee was not worthy that Christ should come vnder his roofe: Saint *Peters* voyce was; *Depart from me, for I am a sinfull man: S. Pauls; I am not worthy to be called an Apostle:* yea, he confessed plainely, he was the least of all the Apostles, and the chiefe of all sinners. Thus all the children of God giue glory to God, by downe-casting themselues. And if ye will go thorough all the examples of the booke of God, ye shall finde, that they who haue beene greatest in Gods estimation, haue beene alway smallest in their owne eyes: the heart which hath receiued most from God, <span class="cw-gap" title="TCP: illegible in source（底本此处读不出）">〈◊〉</span> thinking least of it selfe.

*Alye.*] The <span class="cw-gap" title="TCP: illegible in source（底本此处读不出）">〈◊〉</span> <span class="cw-gap" title="TCP: illegible in source（底本此处读不出）">•</span>cumstance is h<span class="cw-gap" title="TCP: illegible in source（底本此处读不出）">•</span>re, shewing with what <span class="cw-gap" title="TCP: illegible in source（底本此处读不出）">〈◊〉</span> proude did fight against <span class="cw-pb" id="p177" data-p="177" data-facs="95" title="Page 177 of the 1613 edition begins here (原书第 177 页起)"></span> him, namely, with lyes. Satans two armes <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Satans two armes wherby he wrestles against the godly, are violence and lies.</span> by which hee wrestles against the godlie, are violence and lyes: vvhere hee cannot, or dare not vse violence, there be sure he will not faile to fight with lyes. And herein doth the Lord greatly shew his carefull prouidence, in fencing his children against Satans malice, and the proud bragges of his instruments, in such sort, that their <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">☜</span> proudest harts are forced to forge lyes; their malice beeing so great, that they must doe euill: and yet their power so bridled, that they cannot doe what they would.

The third circumstance, is in the words, *they* <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Lies trimmed vp with the garment of truth.</span> *haue imagined. Vatablus* translates it, *Concinnarunt mendacia.* So *Tremel. They haue trimmed vp lyes.* As Satan can transforme himselfe to an Angel of light: so can hee trimme vp his lies vnder couerings of truth, to make them the more plausible vnto men. And indeed, this is no small temptation, when lyes made against the godlie, are trimmed vp with the shadowes of truth, and wicked men couer their vnrighteous dealings, with appearances of righteousnes. Thus, not only are the godly vniustly persecuted, but simple ones are made to beleeue, that they haue most iustly deserued it. In this case, the godly are to sustaine themselues by the testimonie of a good conscience.

*But I will keepe thy precepts.] Dauids* enemies fought against him by lies. Hee takes him to the <span class="cw-pb" id="p178" data-p="178" data-facs="96" title="Page 178 of the 1613 edition begins here (原书第 178 页起)"></span> obedience of Gods word. Wee should not fight <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">We should not fight against Satan and his instruments with their armour.</span> against the wicked with their owne armour, rendring one wrong for another, a lie for a lie, rebuke for rebuke: no more then *Dauid* could fight against *Goliah* with *Saul* his armor, which was like vnto the armor of *Goliah.* If we encounter Satan with his owne armour, he shall soone ouerthrowe vs; for by striking, lying, euil doing, we are deadly wounded: But to the weapons of flesh, we must oppose the weapons of the spirit; ouerocmming, after the manner of our Lord, the furie of men <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">☞</span> with our patience; their persecutions vvith our prayers; their euill with our good: so shall we either winne them vnto vs, or else heape coales of <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Rom. 12. 20.</span> fire on their heads.

*With my whole hart.*] See ver. 2. 10. 34. 58. 69.

<div class="cw-text" markdown="1">

VER. 70.

Their hart is fat as grease: but my delight is in thy law.

</div>

*DAuid* makes here an oppositiō, between <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Dauids disposition, and his enemies, contrary.</span> his disposition, and the disposition of his enemies; shewing how their hart vvas become fat and senselesse through their worldlie wealth: but he, being humbled by the rods of the Lord, had his delight in the law of God, & counted more of it, then thousands of gold and siluer.

When the godly looke into their owne harts, or vp vnto GOD, they see in themselues, such <span class="cw-pb" id="p179" data-p="179" data-facs="96" title="Page 179 of the 1613 edition begins here (原书第 179 页起)"></span> a power of corruption as humbles them, and <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">How different effects are wrought in godly men, by their different sights.</span> makes them account themselues the cheefe of all sinners: but vvhen they looke to the effects of Gods grace in them, working a renouation which is not in the wicked, then ariseth to them matter of reioycing.

That which the Pharisee, in the pride of his heart spake of the Publican, the penitent Publican, in humilitie, and a good conscience, may turne ouer to the Pharisee; I thanke God, I am <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Luke 18.</span> not like this Pharisee. For the Christian, by the light of God, seeing the miserable estate of the <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Acts 26. 29.</span> wicked, they could wish as S*t*. *Paul* did to *Agrippa,* that the wicked were like vnto them; but would not change their state of grace, with the most honourable estate that worldlings can haue on earth, without grace.

In this that hee saith, their heart was fatte as <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">A soft and melting heart a great blessing.</span> *grease;* hee noteth two things. First, that they abounded in worldly wealth. Next, that their hart was become fat, senselesse, and voyd of feeling. *Quaedam veluti crassities occupat corū corda, vt stupidi sint in sua obstinatione, neque Deum curent, audeantque simul contra seruos eius insurgere:* a certaine grossenes possesseth their harts, which makes them senseless in obstinacy. It is the principal blessing of the new couenant, to haue a soft, feeling, & melting hart, like the hart of good <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">A hard & stony hart, a fearfull cu<span class="cw-gap" title="TCP: illegible in source（底本此处读不出）">•</span>se.</span> *Iosiah:* but a hard hart, called a stony hart, an adamantine and stubborne hart, is a great signe, & a <span class="cw-pb" id="p180" data-p="180" data-facs="97" title="Page 180 of the 1613 edition begins here (原书第 180 页起)"></span> sure fore-runner of a further and more fearefull iudgement to come, if it be not remedied. It is an enemy to grace; it is the mother of presumptuous sins; it makes smallest sins irremissible; it makes a man Gods aduersary: for the Lord resisteth the proud. As two hard things when they encounter together, the weakest of thē is broken in peeces: <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">☞</span> so is it with man, when with his hard hart he contends with the invincible God. Let vs pray wee may be deliuered from this plague of a hard hart.

*But my delight.*] see ver. 14. 16. 35. 76. 77. 174.

<div class="cw-text" markdown="1">

VER. 71.

It is good for mee, that I haue beene afflicted; that I may learne thy statutes.

</div>

HEe renewes his former protestation <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">How a sanctied crosse may be discerned from a cursed.</span> which he madever. 67. declaring how his afflictions made him more godlie then he was before. Heereby may we discerne a sanctified crosse, from a cursed: the crosse which makes thee better then thou wast, how sharpe so euer it be, if it worke a correction of thee, thou maist be sure it is a blessing. It is commonly in the mouthes of many, when they are in trouble, We must thanke God for his correction. But alas! how can that be called a correction, which <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Namely, if the crosse be a correction.</span> corrects thee not? If thou be not corrected with <span class="cw-pb" id="p181" data-p="181" data-facs="97" title="Page 181 of the 1613 edition begins here (原书第 181 页起)"></span> *Dauid,* thou hast matter of griefe in thy trouble but none of ioy; and so canst not say with *Dauid,* It is good for me, that I haue been afflicted.

Affliction, as we said, ver. 67. in it owne nature <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Nature of affliction changed to the godly.</span> is euill, being a punishment of sinne: but the Lord, who changed the bitter waters of *Marah,* and made them sweet vnto Israel, hath also changed to his children, the nature of the crosse; that not onely they find comfort in it, but most happy effects are wrought in them by it.

In trouble wee see three things better then in <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Three things wee see in trouble, better then in prosperitie.</span> prosperitie: First, how prouident, mercifull, and true the Lord is. Secondly, how weake and miserable our selues are. Thirdly, how false & changable the world is. Beside all these, it is an effectuall meane, whereby the Lord beates downe the pride of Nature, and humbles vs for our sinnes. *Hagar* was proud and blind in *Abrahams* house: but in the wildernesse she is humbled, and begins to looke to the Lord. Israel learned not to mourn vntill they were sent into Babel. *Ionas* sleepes in the shippe; but wakes, and prayes in the Whales belly. *Blessed is the man whom the Lord corrects,* <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Iob. 5.</span> *therefore refuse not the chastisement of the Almighty.* For tribulation bringeth forth patience; <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Rom. 5.</span> patience, experience; experience, hope; & hope maketh vs not ashamed.

*That I may learne.*] Hee now condiscends in <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">The crosse makes a man learned by experience.</span> particular, and shewes what good hee got by afflictions: he learned Gods statutes by them. He <span class="cw-pb" id="p182" data-p="182" data-facs="98" title="Page 182 of the 1613 edition begins here (原书第 182 页起)"></span> speakes not of that learning which is gotten by hearing or reading of Gods word; but of the learning which he had gotten by experience: that he had felt the truth & comfort of Gods word more effectual and liuely in trouble, then he could doe without trouble; which also made him more godly, wise and religious when the trouble was gone. Wicked men are somwhat good whē they <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">The goodnes of wicked men, ends whē their trouble ends: it is otherwise with the godly.</span> are in affliction: but so soon as they are deliuered, they return to their old sinnes, as ye see in *Pharao.* These are like Iron, which is soft and will bow at the workmans wil when it is in the fire; but soon after it is drawn out, it returns to the old hardnes. These men vse repentance, as worldlings doe an old garment: which they put about thē in time of a showre, but cast it away so soon as the weather becomes faire. It is otherwise with the godly; the fire of affliction purgeth their drosse, and makes them finer all the daies of their life: so that with good *Ezechia,* being once rightly humbled, they <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Esay 38.</span> recount their by-gone sinnes, in the bitterness of their hart, all their dayes.

<div class="cw-text" markdown="1">

VER. 72.

The law of thy mouth, is better vnto me, then thousands of gold and siluer.

</div>

*DAuid,* at this time, beeing spoyled of his <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">The less worldly comfort wee haue, the strōger is the cōfort of Gods word.</span> kingdome by *Absalom* (as *Basile* thinks) reioyceth that hee found more comfort <span class="cw-pb" id="p183" data-p="183" data-facs="98" title="Page 183 of the 1613 edition begins here (原书第 183 页起)"></span> in Gods word, then all the gold, or siluer, or treasures of *Canaan* could afford. And indeede, the lesse worldly comfort wee haue, the greater find we the comfort of the word of God.

But heere his speech is to be marked; for hee <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">The authoritie of Gods word, should be distinguished frō the ministrie thereof.</span> calls the word, the law of Gods mouth. Which leades vs to consider two things concerning it: the authoritie of the word; and so it is from Gods mouth: the ministery of the word; & so it comes from mans mouth.

Sometime men looke to the ministery of the word, and not to the authoritie of it; and then it is no maruell they disesteeme of it: euen as *Samuel* <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">1. Sam. 3.</span> ranne to *Eli,* when he should haue runne to God; because hee thought the voice that called him, had been but the voice of *Eli.* So, many regard not the word, because they take it to be the voice of man, and not of God.

*Then thousands.*] Worldly riches are gotten <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Vanity of worldly riches.</span> with labour, kept with care, lost with griefe. They are false friends, farthest from vs, when we haue most need of comfort: as all worldlings shall trie to be true in the hour of death. For then, as *Ionas* gourd was taken from him in a morning, when he had most need of it against the Sun: so is it with the comfort of worldlings. It is farre otherwise with the word of God: for if we wil lay it vp in our harts, as *Mary* did, the comfort thereof shall sustaine vs, when all other comfort shal faile vs.

<span class="cw-pb" id="p184" data-p="184" data-facs="99" title="Page 184 of the 1613 edition begins here (原书第 184 页起)"></span>This is it that makes vs rich vnto God, when <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">It is not pouertie to want gold, but to want grace.</span> our soules are Storehouses, filled with the treasures of his word. Shall we thinke it pouertie to bee scant of gold and siluer? *An ideo Angelus pauper est, quia non habet iumenta, &c.* Shall we <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Chr<span class="cw-gap" title="TCP: illegible in source（底本此处读不出）">•</span>s. in Mat. hom. 9.</span> esteeme the Angels poore, because they haue not <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Otherwise, Angels might be called poore.</span> flocks of Cattell? or that *S. Peter* was poore, because he had not gold nor siluer to giue vnto the Criple? No, he had store of grace, by infinite degress more excellent then it.

Let the riches of gold be left vnto worldlings: wee haue to remember, these are not currant in *Canaan;* not accounted of in our heauenly countrey. If we would be in any estimation there, let vs inrich our soules with spirituall graces, which we haue aboundant in the Mines and treasures of the word of God.
