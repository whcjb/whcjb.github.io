### <span class="cw-pb" id="p273" data-p="273" data-facs="144" title="Page 273 of the 1613 edition begins here (原书第 273 页起)"></span>AIN.

<div class="cw-text" markdown="1">

VER. 121.

I haue executed iudgement and iustice: leaue me not to mine oppressors.

</div>

<span class="cw-dropcap">I</span>N this Section *Dauid* continueth his <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">A good conscience makes boldnes in prayer.</span> prayer, for protection against his enemies; as also for grace to knowe his way vpon earth, and follow it. He begins with a Petition; Leaue me not to mine oppressors: and he giues the reason; I haue executed iudgement and iustice. Where we are not to thinke that he is iustifying himselfe before God; but onely declaring how iniustly he was oppressed <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Amb. in Ps. 119</span> by men: *Desensio est, non arrogantia.* Our lesson is; If we would haue our prayers forcible, let vs intertaine the testimony of a good conscience.

*Iudgement and iustice.*] These two are distinguished <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Iudgement and iustice, how distinguished.</span> by *Ambrose,* in such sort, that he makes the one the effect of the other: *Iudicij finis, iustitia est: in altero veritatis custodia est, in altero fructus aequitatis;* iudgement is the keeper of verity, iustice the fruit of equity: the one of these perteyning to the minde, makes it giue light for discerning <span class="cw-pb" id="p274" data-p="274" data-facs="145" title="Page 274 of the 1613 edition begins here (原书第 274 页起)"></span> betweene right and wrong; the other rectifying the affections and actions. Happy is the man in whom these two concurre together: *Quis autem hodie tanta animi praeditus est puritate,* <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Basil<span class="cw-gap" title="TCP: illegible in source（底本此处读不出）">▪</span> in Psalm. 119.</span> *vt cum fiducia verbis ist is vti possit?* But who this day is indued with such purity of minde, that with boldnesse he may vse these words; *My reioycing is the testimony of a good conscience?*

*Leaue me not.*] We haue often said that *Dauid* <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">We cannot in <span class="cw-gap" title="TCP: illegible in source（底本此处读不出）">〈◊〉</span> life be <span class="cw-gap" title="TCP: illegible in source（底本此处读不出）">〈◊〉</span> o<span class="cw-gap" title="TCP: illegible in source（底本此处读不出）">•</span> trouble by wicked men.</span> was a man after Gods owne heart, who had also the testimony of a good conscience to sustaine him; yet could he not liue free from the oppression of wicked men. So long as this battell lasts, and Satan wants not instruments, let vs euer look to be troubled by them; and not to be discouraged thereat, but rather comforted, considering the inimity proclaimed in Paradise is without reconciliation: and sith the Prince of our saluation sustained such contradiction of sinners; why shall it grieue vs to beare his crosse?

And sith *Dauid* so earnestly prayed, not to be <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">How should we pray against our soules oppressors?</span> left into the hands of his bodily oppressors; what shall we doe against our soules oppressors? Satan seekes continually to spoile vs of that sparke of spirituall life which God hath put into vs. It is a searfull iudgement, where God leaues men to his tyrannie: let vs pray for mercy against it.

<div class="cw-text" markdown="1">

<span class="cw-pb" id="p275" data-p="275" data-facs="145" title="Page 275 of the 1613 edition begins here (原书第 275 页起)"></span>VER. 122.

Answer for thy seruant in that thing which is good: and let not the proud oppresse me.

</div>

AMong many crosses wherewith *Dauid* <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Calumnies of men heauy crosses.</span> was exercised, the strise of tongues is not one of the least: they did persecute <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Psal. 35. 16.</span> him with lies and calumnies: scoffers and bellygods made a by-word of him in their meetings; and flattering Courtiers belied him vnto *Saul.* This was not a small crosse; *Molesta enimres calumnia* <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Basil.</span> *etiamsi magnum afferat praemium:* against it *Dauid* makes his refuge to God; *Answer for thy seruant.* His meaning is, Lord thou kno west how iniustly I am calumniate and euill spoken of in many parts: where I am not, nor may not answere for my selfe, Lorde answere thou for me.

And his petition imports not onely a crauing of helpe from God; but that God would take his part, and ioyne himselfe with his righteous cause. <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">God is a partaker with his owne in their innocent sufferings.</span> And so it is indeede: the Lord is partaker with his children in their innocent sufferings, and their cause is his cause; *Blessed are ye when ye are* <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">1. Pet. 4.</span> *railed vpon for righteousnesse sake, &c. For the spirit of grace and glorie rests in you, which on their part is euill spoken of, but on your part is glorified.* This might serue for a warning to wicked men: if any wisedom were in them, they should be loath to meddle with the children of God: for if so <span class="cw-pb" id="p276" data-p="276" data-facs="146" title="Page 276 of the 1613 edition begins here (原书第 276 页起)"></span> they will, they meddle with a stronger partie then they are aware of. <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Dangerous to commend a wrong cause to Gods protection.</span>

Againe, it is to be considered, that hee craues protection onely in his good and honest cause. It is not for vs to call for the Lords assistance to all our willes and workes: *Hee is the righteous Iudge of the world, and neyther will nor can* <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Gen. 19.</span> *do vnrighteously.* To commenda wrong cause to his protection, is to prouoke him to hasten our punishment.

And on the other hand, albeit our cause were <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Or yet were our cause neuer so good, we must not think to beare it out by our wisedome.</span> neuer so righteous, yet we must not think to bear it out with our owne strength and wisedome. It falles out oft-times, that men fall downe and faint through feeblenesse, euen in a good cause; because they giue not to God his glory. In their lawfull affairs they prosper not, because they take counsel, but not from me (saith the Lord). They commit not their way to God, as *Dauid* exhorts; <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Psal. 37. 34.</span> neyther cast they their burden vpon the Lord, as <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">1. Pet. 5. 7.</span> *Peter* commaunds them: but sacrifice to their own net, & think to cōpasse their affaires by their own wi<span class="cw-gap" title="TCP: illegible in source（底本此处读不出）">•</span>. Sure it is, the Lord is then most carefull of vs, when we cast our greatest care vpon him.

*For thy seruant.*] Customably King *Dauid* delights in this stile, to call himselfe Gods seruant; <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Mans greatest honor is to be Gods seruant.</span> learning vs also to count this our greatest honor, to be the seruants of the great and euer-liuing God: for we are not our owne (as saith the Apostle) *We are bought with a price.* But here is our sin; <span class="cw-pb" id="p277" data-p="277" data-facs="146" title="Page 277 of the 1613 edition begins here (原书第 277 页起)"></span> such as are vndervs, whether they be bought, or hired seruants, wee thinke they should serue vs, and are offended if they doe not; but are not so carefull to doe seruice to him, who is aboue vs: who hath not onely bought vs with a deer price, and promised to vs excellent things heereafter; but daily rewards vs with wages, that farre surmounts our seruice. <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">The seruice is not base but honourable wherein God imployes vs.</span>

And this dutie, should the more willinglie be discharged by vs, because the seruice is honourable where-vnto the Lord imployes vs: for it is not to doe any base or seruile thing, as to worke in brick and clay; which way the Israelites serued *Pharao,* and common seruants serue their Lord and Master. No: *Pretiosa h<span class="cw-gap" title="TCP: illegible in source（底本此处读不出）">•</span>c seruitus virtutum* <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Ambrose.</span> *constat expensis:* this pretious and honourable seruice, stands in the practice of vertue, in prayer, and prayses of the Lord thy God. These are the principall poynts of this seruice; in this wee haue the Angels to bee our companions: for in these same things they serue GOD with vs, and serue vs for Gods sake. And shame will be vnto vs, if wee bee ashamed to serue GOD vvith them; and one of vs to serue another for Gods sake.

<div class="cw-text" markdown="1">

<span class="cw-pb" id="p278" data-p="278" data-facs="147" title="Page 278 of the 1613 edition begins here (原书第 278 页起)"></span>VER. 123.

Mine eyes haue failed in waiting for thy saluation, and for thy iust promise.

</div>

*DAuid* here renues the protestation of his <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Dauids seruent desire of Gods saluation.</span> earnest desire he had of Gods saluation: *Et vt desider ij vehemētiam significet,* and <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Basil.</span> to expresse the vehemencie of his desire, he protests that his eyes failed in waiting: meaning *Oculos mentis,* as *Euthymius* thinkes, of the eyes of his mind. So also *Ambrose* expounds it; *Oculi animae tota fidei intentione in Deum defixi erant,* that the eyes of his soule, and whole intention of his faith, were fixed vpon God.

But what is this saluation for which he waited <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Saluation is of two sortes: Dauid seekes the best.</span> so earnestly? Not that temporall deliuerance from his enemies; for which also many times he prayeth to the Lord his God: but especially here he prayeth for that saluation, mentioned *Ps.* 106. *Visit me with the saluation of thy people, that I may reioyce with the ioy of thine inheritance:* and for which also he prayeth; *Many say, Who will shew vs any good; but Lord lift thou vp the light of* <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Psal. 4.</span> *thy countenance vpon me.* And this is it, wherevpon the desires of our soule should alway be intended.

If a man shall separate himselfe in his cogitation, <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">The restlesse vanity of worldlings.</span> and take a view of mens actions in the world, he shall see them running with insatiable desires; som after one thing, & som after another: they <span class="cw-pb" id="p279" data-p="279" data-facs="147" title="Page 279 of the 1613 edition begins here (原书第 279 页起)"></span> are all in a restlesse businesse, like rauening birds and beasts of the earth pursuing their prey. But is not their laboura wandring after vanitie? Will the fruite of their actions remaine with them? Are they not fooles, to be busie about <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">☜</span> many things with *Martha;* carelesse about one thing, which is <span class="cw-gap" title="TCP: illegible in source（底本此处读不出）">•</span>eedfull, with *Mary?* Let vs be wise in time. Happy is he whose heart is well set, to couet the best things: if, with *Dauid,* we can waite, and hunger and thirst for Gods saluation, God will not faile in his owne time to satisfie vs.

*And for thy iust promise.*] He sheweth here <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Gods promise is the warrant of our hope.</span> vpon what warrant he did wait for Gods saluation; namely, his promise. There is no people, who know there is a God, but they wait for som good things from him: neuerthelesse, all these walke in vncertainties; only we haue Gods warrant for our hope: which is his sure promise hee hath made and sealed to vs in Christ Iesus.

But in this point also the vnthankfulnesse of <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Small present gifts more esteemed by worldlings then promises of greater things to com.</span> this age is manifest: for who now delight in the promises of God? No, no; as if they were but vaine wordes, men now account more of one of his temporall gifts presently giuen them, then they doe of the most excellent promises of better things to come, which he hath made <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">☜</span> to <span class="cw-gap" title="TCP: illegible in source（底本此处读不出）">▪</span>them: and therefore is it they waite not for those which are to come, but let loose their hearts, to delight in these which are present. <span class="cw-pb" id="p280" data-p="280" data-facs="148" title="Page 280 of the 1613 edition begins here (原书第 280 页起)"></span> An vnthankful, vnfruitfull, & back-slyding generation is this, wherein we are fallen. God be mercifull vnto it.

<div class="cw-text" markdown="1">

VER. 124.

Deale with thy seruaunt according to thy mercy; and teach me thy statutes.

</div>

HE protests now, as oft before, that hee was Gods seruant; that he did not liue <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">We cannot serue God as we should.</span> according to his owne will, but depended on the will of God. Euen as the eyes of a seruant looke to the Master, and the eyes of a handmaid to her Mistresse: so did hee looke vnto the Lord his God. But because he was priuie to the conscience of his owne sinnes, and that his seruice was not such as it should haue been, therefore he appeales to mercy; praying GOD not to deale with him after his seruice, but after Gods mercie. *Non vno facto seruus Dei absoluitur:* It is not one dutie which completes the seruice wee owe vnto God. Yea, when we haue done all that we can, we are but vnprofitable seruants: for who can serue so great a Master as the Lord is, according as he should be serued?<span class="cw-gap" title="TCP: illegible in source（底本此处读不出）">▪</span>

Yea, to let be himselfe; who can serue him, <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Yea, our best seruice is far<span class="cw-gap" title="TCP: illegible in source（底本此处读不出）">•</span>e inferior to his wages.</span> worthy of that wage we haue receiued from him? *Quis tanta naturae munera, vitae salutisque seruatae diuina beneficia, digno possit aequare seruitio?* <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Amb. in Psalm. 119.</span> *Quis potest soluere quod accepit? ideoque misericordiam* <span class="cw-pb" id="p281" data-p="281" data-facs="148" title="Page 281 of the 1613 edition begins here (原书第 281 页起)"></span> *petit Dauid:* VVho can giue equall seruice for so great benefits of nature, life, and saluation, which we haue receiued from God? who can recompence him that which hee hath giuen? or pay him that which he oweth vnto him? And therefore *Dauid* knowing all these, he cryeth for mercy.

And yet shall we not be defrauded of the comfortable <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Two things proue a man to be Gods seruaunt.</span> stile of Gods seruaunts, if wee find these two things: First, that wee are glad to doe any thing in his seruice, that wee knowe is according to his will. Next, that wee are sorrowfull for the euill which wee doe; and for the good which of weakenesse we leaue vndone.

*Teach mee.] Dauid* had *Nathan* and *Gad* the <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Outward and ordinary teachers, are nothing if God teach not.</span> Prophets; and beside them, the ordinary Leuites to teach him. He read the word of God diligently, and did meditate in the law night & day; but hee acknowledgeth all this was nothing, vnlesse God did teach him. Other teachers speake to the eare; but God speakes to the hart: so *Paul* preached to *Lydia,* but God opened her hart. Let vs pray for this grace.

<div class="cw-text" markdown="1">

VER. 125.

I am thy seruant: grant me therefore vnderstanding, that I may know thy testimonies.

</div>

IN this verse, his former petition is renewed, with a reason. The thing he seeks, is vnderstanding. <span class="cw-pb" id="p282" data-p="282" data-facs="149" title="Page 282 of the 1613 edition begins here (原书第 282 页起)"></span> *Intellectus spiritale munus est: & ideo quod* <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">They may lawfully seeke all good from God, who can truely say they are his owne.</span> *Dei est, à Domino postulatur;* vnderstanding is a spirituall gift. *Dauid* seekes it from God, because it is at his Donation. The reason he vseth; *I am thy seruant: Non quasi extraneus poscit.* <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Amb. in Ps. 119</span> I am not a stranger to thee, but thine own domestique seruant; let me want no grace may enable mee to serue thee. *Omnes homines natura sunt serui Dei; adoptione verò illi qui dominationem* <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Basil. in Ps. 119.</span> *Dei cupidè sunt amplexi:* All men by nature are Gods seruants; by adoption onely, they who are glad to come vnder his Dominion: these serue him, not by constraint, nor for feare onely, but willingly and with ioy; as being such seruants as are also his sonnes.

He acknowledgeth here his owne wants; that <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">The godly can not learne so much, but they would still vnderstand more.</span> as a naturall man he had no vnderstanding of the wayes of God: his Word is full of mysteries; which we cannot know, till God reueale them. And as a Regenerate man, hee had not so much knowledge as he should haue. *Habebat intellectum:* <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Ambrose.</span> *sed vt redundaret, vberiorem inquirit;* he had vnderstanding, but he craues he might more & more abound in all knowledge & iudgement. We will not with *Euah* aspire to that knowledge which God hath forbidden: but that which God hath reuealed vnto vs, wee will neuer thinke wee can learne it so well, but that still we haue need to learne it better.

*To knowe thy Testimonies.*] Hee prayed before <span class="cw-pb" id="p283" data-p="283" data-facs="149" title="Page 283 of the 1613 edition begins here (原书第 283 页起)"></span> for knowledge of Gods Statutes: now he <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Statutes and Testimonies of God would be learned together.</span> prayeth for the knowledge of Gods Testimonies. The Statutes (more strictly taken) are that parte of Gods Word declaring his Will, which wee should obey: the Testimonies properly are that part of Gods Word, declaring his Promises, which we should beleeue. To know the first without the second, will not make vs godly: for it is the sense of Gods loue, and faith in his Testimonies, that workes kindly obedience; and therefore *Dauid* prayeth for it. See vers. 2. 14. 22. 24. 36. 46. 59. 79. 88. 95. 99. 111. &c.

<div class="cw-text" markdown="1">

VER. 126.

It is time for thee to worke: for they haue destroyed thy Lawe.

</div>

HEe hath complained before of the oppression <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Dauids complaint against his enemies.</span> of his aduersaries; & besought the Lord to arme him against them, with knowledge & vnderstanding: now he complaines their iniquity was come to such a height, that they did giue battell euen to God himselfe; and were not only enemies to *Dauid,* but such as had done what they could to destroy the law of God. So the verse containes a prayer, that God would work; and a reason, taken from the ripenesse of the wickednesse of his enemies.

According to our sense, there is a time wherin <span class="cw-pb" id="p284" data-p="284" data-facs="150" title="Page 284 of the 1613 edition begins here (原书第 284 页起)"></span> God worketh not: whereof proceedes in the <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">God is a continuall working vertue, euen when we think he is resting.</span> godlie the like of these complaints; *Arise Lord, why sleepest thou? How long wilt thou forget?* It is true, in himselfe hee is a continuall working vertue; but wee, beeing ignorant of the waies whereby he walkes to his owne end, think sometime that hee is not working, because hee is not executing: although euen then, when wee so thinke, hee be most busily working a way, to effect his owne determinate conclusion.

But how is this, that hee prescribeth a time to <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">How Dauid prescribes a time to the Lord.</span> the Lord? Is not this to fall into that fault, which is reprooued, Psalme 78? *They limited the Holie one of Israel.*

For answere of this; GOD hath made some promises with a time, declaring to his children when hee would performe them. So hee promised <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Some promises made with a time when they shall be performed.</span> to free *Abrahams* seede, from the Egyptian persecution, after foure hundred yeeres; as hee did: for it was no lesse between the beginning of that persecution in *Ismael* mocking *Isaac,* & the deliuerance from it, in *Pharao* oppressing *Israel* with burdens.

Again, he promised to bring *Israel* out of *Babel* after seauenty yeers: and therfore the godly, who thought very long for expiring of that date, praied earnestly, that God would haue mercy on *Sion,* because the appointed time was come. So also, he promised to send *Shiloh,* the true deliuerer of his people, at that time when the Scepter shold <span class="cw-pb" id="p285" data-p="285" data-facs="150" title="Page 285 of the 1613 edition begins here (原书第 285 页起)"></span> depart from *Israel:* and therefore at that time, *Simeon* waited for the consolation, and his eyes saw the saluation, of God.

Other promises againe hee makes without a <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Some without a time.</span> time; that is, not telling when he will performe them. He put *Noah* in the Ark; but told him not when hee would bring him out: and hee taried a yeere and a day, waiting Gods time with patience. He sent *Ioseph* and *Mary* into Egypt, commaunding them to tarie there, till he told them: they inquired not how long; neither did hee tell them: Learning vs, when God layes a crosse on vs, not to capitulate with him, concerning the time of our deliuerance; but patiently to beare it, <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">In these wee should haue patience waiting till Gods time come.</span> till his time come. It was *Sauls* ouer-hastiness; he taried for *Samuel* seauen daies, but would tarie no longer. And it was the blasphemous speech of *Iehoram,* Why should I attend any longer?

Let vs not dishonour the Lord, by prescribing a time to him. If he should alway tell vs the time of our deliuerance, the praise of our patience should be the lesse, and our prayer the colder: but when hee conceales the time, and wee with patience <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">☜</span> wait vpon it; we giue good proofe of our faith and patience, and finde our deliuerance the sweeter when it comes.

Yet in publick troubles of the Church, when <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">When the godly may vse this reason in their prayer, The time is come.</span> the pride of the enemie is become great, and the cup of the Amo<span class="cw-gap" title="TCP: illegible in source（底本此处读不出）">•</span>ites seemes to be full; when the children of God, are brought low, & their soules <span class="cw-pb" id="p286" data-p="286" data-facs="151" title="Page 286 of the 1613 edition begins here (原书第 286 页起)"></span> humbled to the dust; it is no limitation of the Lord, when with *Dauid* they pray, that GOD would haue mercy on them, because the time is come.

*For they haue destroyed thy law.*] It is a great proofe of true godlinesse, when we are more displeased <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Offences done against God, should grieue vs more then our owne iniuries.</span> with offences done against the Lord our GOD, then with such as are done against our selues. But it is now far otherwise with most part of Professors: so that they bee not preiudiced in their names, and commodities, they care not what be done against the glory of God. An euident argument, that they neuer loued him.

In the second verse of this Section, hee complained, <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Godly mē haue no enemies, but such as are enemies to God.</span> that the proud would oppresse him: now he complaineth, that they destroyed the law of God. Who then are *Dauids* enemies, and seek to oppresse him? Onely such as are enemies to God, and seeke to destroy his law. A great comfort haue we in this, that if we loue the Lord, and studie in a good conscience to serue him, wee can haue no enemies, except such as are enemies to God. And so long as God wants his due at their hands, that is, loue and seruice; may not wee be content to want their affection toward vs? Truly, it should greatly increase our patience, to remember, that if they were not enemies to God, they would neuer be ou<span class="cw-gap" title="TCP: illegible in source（底本此处读不出）">•</span> enemies.

But, how is this h<span class="cw-gap" title="TCP: illegible in source（底本此处读不出）">•</span> <span class="cw-gap" title="TCP: illegible in source（底本此处读不出）">〈◊〉</span> They haue destroyed <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Seeing Gods law can not be destroyed, how are the wicked charged with this crime?</span> thy law? Is this pos<span class="cw-gap" title="TCP: illegible in source（底本此处读不出）">•</span>ble, that Gods law can be <span class="cw-pb" id="p287" data-p="287" data-facs="151" title="Page 287 of the 1613 edition begins here (原书第 287 页起)"></span> destroyed? No indeed: yet because their malice would, if their power might, they shall be charged with it.

There is a law of God written in holy Scripture, <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">The law written in the Bible they cannot destroy.</span> which the wicked in all ages haue sought to destroy; but GOD hath marueilously preserued it. There is a law written in the booke of euery <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">The law written in the conscience, they can neuer destroy.</span> mans conscience, which the most profane in the world, doe what he can, is not abloto scrape out; but still it iudgeth him, conuinceth him, and rebukes him when he doth wrong. And, as for the execution of the law, all the wicked in the world are not able to stay it, when Gods time commeth: yet because (as I said) the wicked would <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Yet because their malice would doe it if they might, they shall be charged with it.</span> do it if they might, they are charged with it, that they haue done it. For as the godly shall haue imputed to thē, that good, which faine they would haue done, albeit they did not performe it: so shall the wicked be charged as verely with that euill, which they would haue done, albeit they neuer did it. O what a heape of finnes shall be gathered <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">☜</span> against them, whē with the sinnes of their actions and words, the sinnes of their affections shall be conioyned also!

And here we see, how-euer God for a time tolerate <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">There is a time appointed for the punishmēt of euil men.</span> them, yet he hath his owne appointed time to punish them, and will not passe it. *Omnia in statera gubernat Deus;* God ruleth all things in a balance. *Diu quidem fert <span class="cw-gap" title="TCP: illegible in source（底本此处读不出）">〈◊〉</span> peccata: vbi* <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Basil.</span> *verò patientiae eius intuit<span class="cw-gap" title="TCP: illegible in source（底本此处读不出）">•</span> a<span class="cw-gap" title="TCP: illegible in source（底本此处读不出）">•</span>geri malitiam videt,* <span class="cw-pb" id="p288" data-p="288" data-facs="152" title="Page 288 of the 1613 edition begins here (原书第 288 页起)"></span> *tum p<span class="cw-gap" title="TCP: illegible in source（底本此处读不出）">•</span>nas sumit:* Long doth the Lord suffer the sinnes of mortall men; but when they abuse his patience to increase their wickednes, then hee doth punish them, he doth nothing out of time. *Totum oportunum est quod fecerit;* but whatsoeuer <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Ambrose.</span> hee doth, hee doth it most seasonably: and therefore whether it bee deliuerance to vs, or iudgement vpon our enemies, that God delaies; let vs knowe it is because his houre is not yet come.

<div class="cw-text" markdown="1">

VER. 127.

Therefore loue I thy Commandements aboue gold: yea, aboue most fine gold.

</div>

WE may see here that *Dauid* was not <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">True religion cannot be tryed without trouble.</span> a temporizer in religion; whose affection towards Gods word depends vpon the state of times, and persons of men: but euen when his enemies did disdaine it, yea because they sought to destroy it, therefore he loued it. This is a tryall of true religion, euen then to cleaue to the word of God and professe it constantly, when honourable, and great men of the world are against it. This was *Iosua* his resolution, *Albeit all the world should for sake God, yet I and my fathers house will worship him.* And *Peters* in like manner, when many of Christs disciples did forsake him, and it was asked at them, *Will yee go also from me?* he answered, *where away* <span class="cw-pb" id="p289" data-p="289" data-facs="152" title="Page 289 of the 1613 edition begins here (原书第 289 页起)"></span> *shall we goe? thou hast the words of eternall life.* To professe religion, when it is warranted by law; when both rulers and people professe it, is no great matter: but when the powers of the world forsake it; then to stand to it, is an argument of true religion.

*Loue I thy commandements.*] Hee professeth <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Loue of Gods obedience, an argument of true godlinesse.</span> not that hee fulfilled them; but that hee loued them: and truely it is a great progresse in godlines, if we be come thus far, as from our heart to loue them. The natural man hates the commandements of God; they are so contrary to his corruption: but the regenerate man, as he hates his owne corruption, so he loues the word; because according to it he desires to bee reformed. And here is our comfort, That albeit we cannot doe what is commanded; yet if we loue to doe it, it is an argument of grace receiued.

*Aboue gold.*] It is not vnlawful to loue those <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">With what cōditions Gods creatures may be loued.</span> creatures, which God hath appointed for our vse; with these two conditions: the one is, that the first seat in our affection of loue, be reserued to God; and any other thing wee loue, that we loue it<span class="cw-gap" title="TCP: illegible in source（底本此处读不出）">▪</span> in him and for him, and giue it onely the second roome. Thus *Dauid,* being a naturall man, loued his naturall foode: but he protesteth hee loued the law of the Lord, more then his appointed foode; and here he loues the commandements of God, aboue all gold.

<div class="cw-text" markdown="1">

<span class="cw-pb" id="p290" data-p="290" data-facs="153" title="Page 290 of the 1613 edition begins here (原书第 290 页起)"></span>VER. 128.

Therefore I esteeme all thy precepts most iust; and I hate all false wayes.

</div>

IN this verse are two protestatiōs. In the first, <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">As the minde esteemes of any thing, the affections go after it</span> he declares how he esteemed of Gods word in his minde: in the second, how in his affection he was disposed toward it. As the minde of a man esteemes of any thing, so are his affections moued to flie or follow it. It is shame for many professors, now to say they esteeme of Gods word, when they shew their affections more set vpon any thing in the world, then vpon it.

*Most iust.*] In two respects is the word of God <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">The word of God called iust in two respects.</span> iust: first, because it commands nothing, but that which is most reasonable; and next, because it shal not faile one iot, but the euent of things shall bee according to the predictions of this word. What cause haue we then to reioice, That how euer our tribulations be many, yet wee heare, out of this most iust word, It cannot bee but well with them that feare the Lord? And again<span class="cw-gap" title="TCP: illegible in source（底本此处读不出）">▪</span> what cause of feare is there to the wicked? when this same word saith, *There is no peace to the wicked, saith my God. And a sinner of an hundred yeeres old shall dy accursed.* If wee behold the wicked in their most flourishing estate, and looke to them in the glasse of the word, we shall see their miseraend, long before it come. <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1"><span class="cw-gap" title="TCP: illegible in source（底本此处读不出）">〈◊〉</span> of euill <span class="cw-gap" title="TCP: illegible in source（底本此处读不出）">•</span>s a <span class="cw-gap" title="TCP: illegible in source（底本此处读不出）">•</span>val of our <span class="cw-gap" title="TCP: illegible in source（底本此处读不出）">〈◊〉</span> to God.</span>

*And I hate all false wayes.*] The best tryall of <span class="cw-pb" id="p291" data-p="291" data-facs="153" title="Page 291 of the 1613 edition begins here (原书第 291 页起)"></span> our loue to God and his word, is the contrary; hatred of sin and impietie: *Yee that loue the Lord hate that which is euill.* He that loues a tree, hates the worme that consumes it: he that loues a garment, hates the moth that eates it: he that loueth life, abhorreth death<span class="cw-gap" title="TCP: illegible in source（底本此处读不出）">▪</span> and he that loues the Lord, hates euery thing that offends him. Let men take heede to this, who are in loue of their sinnes: how can the loue of God be in them?

*All false wayes.*] Religion binds vs not onely <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">The perfection of the godly is in parts, not in degrees.</span> to hate one way of falsehood, but all the wayes of it. As there is nothing good, but in some measure a godly man loues it: so is there nothing euil, but in som measure he hates it. And this is the perfection of the children of God: a perfection, not of degrees; for we neither loue good, nor hate euill as wee should: but a perfection of parts; because euery good we loue, and wee hate euery euill in some measure.

The worst man in the world loues some good, and hates some euill. *Plerumque enim peccata huiusmodi* <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Ambrose.</span> *sunt, vt si alterum declines, incurras alterū;* <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">It is sufficient to mans damnation, if he be captiued by any one sinne.</span> ofttimes he that declines one sin, fals into another: It may be thou ha<span class="cw-gap" title="TCP: illegible in source（底本此处读不出）">•</span>est couetousness, & yet art snared with lechery; there is one very temperate of his mouth, but of a proud & hauty heart; there is another not ambitious of honour, but a seruant to gluttony: *Diuerso vsu in eundem indeuotionis errorem vterque concurrit.* And this is very dangerous, when as men because some good <span class="cw-pb" id="p292" data-p="292" data-facs="154" title="Page 292 of the 1613 edition begins here (原书第 292 页起)"></span> thing is in them, take the greater liberty to cōmit some euill: for, if Sathan get a gripe of thee by any one sin, is it not enough to carry thee to damnation? As the butcher carries the beast to the slaughter, sometime bound by all the foure feete, and sometime by one onely: so is it with Satan. Though thou be not a slaue to all sin; if thou be a slaue to one, the gripe he hath of thee, by that one sinfull affection, is sufficient to captiue thee.
