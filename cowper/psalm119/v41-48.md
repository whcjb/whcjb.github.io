### <span class="cw-pb" id="p108" data-p="108" data-facs="61" title="Page 108 of the 1613 edition begins here (原书第 108 页起)"></span>VAV.

<div class="cw-text" markdown="1">

VER. 41.

And let thy louing kindnesse come vnto me, O Lord; and thy saluation, according to thy promise.

</div>

<span class="cw-dropcap">O</span>Vt of this Psalme we see how feruent, <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">The feruent prayer of Dauid may make vs ashamed.</span> and continuall *Dauid* was in prayer. Euery third Verse hee turnes him to prayer. No maruell he was inriched with a great measure of spirituall grace: for hee sought it diligently. Not like the colde professors of our time, to whom a colde morning prayer is sufficient for the whole day; and of that also oftentimes they are weary before they haue done it. A lamentable euill, that hauing all good for the seeking, yet we should not seeke.

If we would consider how many sinnes we <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Foure notable motiues to make vs more earnest and diligent in prayer.</span> haue contracted, which we must craue to be forgiuen, & would offer vp but one supplication for euery one of them: and againe, that albeit all our sinnes vvere presently forgiuen, yet hovv <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">☞</span> readily should we fall into new sinnes, if we get no grace daily to reforme vs: and thirdly, how difficil a thing it is, against so many strong enemies, <span class="cw-pb" id="p109" data-p="109" data-facs="61" title="Page 109 of the 1613 edition begins here (原书第 109 页起)"></span> as Principalities, Powers, and spiritual wickednes, which with restlesse tentations pursue vs vpon the right hand, & the left, to enter through them all into the kingdome of God: and lastly, how our time is short; and when it is done, no more place of supplication will be left vnto vs: If all these, with many moe, wee did consider, they would waken in vs a more earnest care, to call vpon our God, while we haue time.

This whole section consists of petitions and <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">It is not enough to seeke from God, wee must also promise to giue him.</span> promises. Petitions are two, ver. 41. 43. Promises are six. This, among many, is a difference betweene godly men and others: all men seeke from GOD: but the wicked so seeke that they giue him nothing backe againe; nor yet wil promise in sinceritie to giue. Their prayers must be vnprofitable, because they proceed from loue of themselues, and not of the Lord. If so be they obtaine that which is for their necessitie, they haue no regard to giue to the Lord, that which is for his glory: but the godly, as they seek, so they <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">A triall of men truly religious, from counterfet.</span> giue praise to God when they haue gotten; and returne the vse of things receiued, to the glorie of God who gaue them. They loue not themselues, <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">☜</span> for themselues, but for the Lord: vvhat they seeke from him, they seeke it for this end, that they may be the more able to serue him. Let vs take heed vnto it; because this is a cleere token, whereby such as are truly religious, are distinguished from counterfet dissemblers.

<span class="cw-pb" id="p110" data-p="110" data-facs="62" title="Page 110 of the 1613 edition begins here (原书第 110 页起)"></span> *Let thy louing kindnes, &c.*] In his first prayer <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">The kindnes of God, euer brings saluatiō.</span> he ioynes these two, Gods louing kindnes, and his saluation: and so it is, they goe together inseparably. As for the kindnes of man, thou maist haue it, and not be the better for it: the friend may loue his friend, and not be able to help him; <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">But man may be kind where he is not able to helpe.</span> yea, the Father may looke with compassion vpon his child in danger, and not be able to relieue him; but the kindnes of the Lord euer works saluation: he can doe what he will, and none is able to resist him. All creatures in heauen & earth are vnder his commaund; health & sicknes; life & death; heauen and hell: for he hath the keyes of both. Let vs seek his fauour, that we may be saued: let vs feele his loue, and none euill present, nor to come, temporall, nor eternall, shall make vs afraid.

*According to thy promise.*] As *Dauid* seekes <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">As comfort shold be sought from God, so for Gods sake onely.</span> his comfort in GOD onely, so seekes hee it for Gods sake: alleaging nothing in himselfe, for which hee should obtaine it; but that the Lord, as of his mercie hee had promised, so of his truth hee vvould performe.

And this is first for Papists; who either doe <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Papists reproued.</span> seeke from others beside the Lord; or if they seek from him, they seeke for others sake then for his: founding their prayers vpō the merits of others, not vpon the mercie of God, and merits of Christ onelie.

And next, it giues this vvarning vnto all men, <span class="cw-pb" id="p111" data-p="111" data-facs="62" title="Page 111 of the 1613 edition begins here (原书第 111 页起)"></span> that seeing the chiefest argument vvee can vse in <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Such as would haue God to remember his promise shold remember their promise.</span> prayer, to mooue the Lord to pittie, is his own promise; vvee should remember alwaie to make conscience of our promise of seruice and obedience, vvhich vve haue made to the Lord; otherwise, vvee can not vvith boldnesse seeke comfort in that promise which God hath made vnto vs.

<div class="cw-text" markdown="1">

VER. 42.

So shall I make aunswer to my blasphemers: for I trust in thy word.

</div>

SEe what great effects the sense of Gods louing <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">The kindnes of God, is a sufficient defence against all euil will of man.</span> kindnes works in the harts of his children: hee feares no malice, nor power of man, vvho finds the Lord kind and mercifull to him. This works gloriation against euill men; a bold confession before all men, as after followes; and toward God an holy conuersation, in all his obedience to the law.

*For I trust.*] Confidence in the promises of <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Faith in Gods promises, is the Anchor of the soule.</span> Gods word, is the anchor of the soule; vvhich holdeth it fast, that it abide stable, not driuen to and fro with the wind of tentations: nay, not with the feares & terrors of death. *I know whom I haue belieued.*

<div class="cw-text" markdown="1">

<span class="cw-pb" id="p112" data-p="112" data-facs="63" title="Page 112 of the 1613 edition begins here (原书第 112 页起)"></span>VER. 43.

And take not the word of truth vtterly out of my mouth: for I wait for thy iudgements.

</div>

BY the word of truth, *Euthymius* vnderstands <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">*Dauid* prayes for continuance of the grace of prophecie.</span> the grace of prophecie: by which *Dauid* comforted his owne hart, and was able to speake to the edification of others, and to stop the mouthes of his Aduersaries. This grace the Prophet craues, that the Lord would neuer vtterly take it from him. If at any time, in thy wise dispensation thou relent it, yet I beseech thee, neuer vtterly to remoue it.

This iudgement which *Dauid* heere craues to <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">A great iudgement, that men cannot vse Gods vvord which they haue learned, to the edification of others.</span> be averted from him, lyes vpon many: who doe not marke nor perceiue it, that they are so much the more miserable, that they knowe not their misery; in this, that hauing, and hearing the word of GOD so plentifully, they can neither vse it to their owne consolation, nor to the edification of others. And this proceeds from the euill conscience which is in them, that neither can they heare it with comfort, no more then *Achab* could heare *Michaiah,* because hee neuer prophecied good vnto him; nor yet can they speake it to the comfort of others: *Vnto the vvicked, God saith,* <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Psalm. 50.</span> *What hast thou to doe to take mine ordinances in thy mouth, seeing thou hatest to be reformed? Obmutescit* <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Ambrose.</span> *facundia, si aegra sit conscientia:* cloquence becomes dumbe, when the conscience <span class="cw-pb" id="p113" data-p="113" data-facs="63" title="Page 113 of the 1613 edition begins here (原书第 113 页起)"></span> waxes diseased.

*For I wait.*] Gods iudgements are some-times <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">God at length wil declare that his word is a iudgement, and that he will execute it.</span> taken for his plagues, executed according to his vvord vpon the wicked; with the which is euer<span class="cw-gap" title="TCP: illegible in source（底本此处读不出）">▪</span> conioyned a performance of his promised deliuerance to the godlie: vvhereby hee makes knowne, *That there is fruite for the righteous,* <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Psalm. Malach.</span> how euer for a time hee suffer them to bee euill intreated; and that there is a difference between them vvho feare him, and them that feare him not.

And this *Dauid* heere praies for; that the Lord would so deale with his enemies, that the truth of his word being declared vpon them, the godly might haue no cause to be ashamed for taking that word of truth in their mouthes.

<div class="cw-text" markdown="1">

VER. 44.

So shall I alway keepe thy law for euer and euer.

</div>

HEre is a protestation of thankfulnes to <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Because the Lord changeth not, we are not consumed.</span> his God; which he promiseth to declare in obedience. Whereof first wee obserue, hovv our standing, and perseuering in the obedience of Gods law, proceedes from Gods louing kindnesse toward vs, keeping his word of truth in our harts and mouthes. Hee is the stocke, vvee are the branches; vvee beare not him, he beares vs: let vs abide in him, and <span class="cw-pb" id="p114" data-p="114" data-facs="64" title="Page 114 of the 1613 edition begins here (原书第 114 页起)"></span> giue him the praise of all our well dooing, and perseuerance in it.

Now, the doubling of the word, notes his <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">The resolution of the godly, is not limited within termes, but extended to eternitie.</span> promptitude to Gods obedience, and willing resolution to pers<span class="cw-gap" title="TCP: illegible in source（底本此处读不出）">•</span>uere in it; not for a day, or a yeere, but alway, yea for euer and euer: not in this life onely, but in that also which is to come, as *Ambrose* expounds it; *Pollicetur se & hic &* <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Ambrose.</span> *ibi legem Dei custoditurum: hic in exemplari, in speculo, in aenigmate; illic in ipsa facie veritatis.*

And the same also hath *Euthymius. Custodiam legem tuam non tantum in hoc saeculo, sed & in* <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Euthym.</span> *futuro; quod saeculum saculi appellatur, tanquam praesenti saeculo honoratius.*

<div class="cw-text" markdown="1">

VER. 45.

And I will walke at libertie: for I seeke thy precepts.

</div>

THere is a libertie of the flesh, taken by <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Carnall liberty is but thraldome.</span> men, not giuen by God; falsely so called: for it is not libertie indeede, but thraldome; where men casting off the yoke of GOD, giue ouer themselues to follow their owne affections: vvhich can neuer but breed them doubts, feares, and horrible terrors, wherwith they are straited, so that they know no outgate; *Improbus in seipso coarctatur malitiae suae* <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Ambrose.</span> *laqueis strangulatus.*

Take to you for an example, a couetous <span class="cw-pb" id="p115" data-p="115" data-facs="64" title="Page 115 of the 1613 edition begins here (原书第 115 页起)"></span> wretch, euery day extending the borders of <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">This is euident in men giuen to couetousnes.</span> his possession, excluding his neighbours: doth the enlarging of his bounds, enlarge his heart? doth hee therfore walke at libertie, because hee hath more ample roomes? No, no: *Quantumcunque domus suae spatium porrexerit, clauditur angustis opinionis suaefinibus, eui quod habet non est satis.* But how-soeuer large his possessions be, hee is still inclosed in the straits of his owne narrow heart, vvhich thinks hee hath nothing at all. And so is it in all other sinnes; the giuing <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">☜</span> of libertie to the will of man, drawes on a thraldome: whereby he is not onely captiued of his owne affections, but straited with the sense of wrath in his conscience, which alway followes euill dooing. <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Esay 28.</span>

This is that strait bedde, wherein God threatens <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">In men giuē to concupiscence.</span> to cast the wicked, in a punishment of their carnall libertie. *Herod* tooke this liberty to defile his brothers wife: he beheaded *Iohn* for reproouing of it; and so thought the more freelie hee might enioy his sinfull pleasure: but did hee by this meanes walke at libertie? No: on the contrarie, hee cast himselfe into the straits of a terrifying and accusing conscience; hee could neuer bee quit of *Iohn,* vvhom hee had beheaded: for hee conceited, that *Iesus* was *Iohn* risen againe from the dead. What euer mirth he pretended in his countenaunce, hee found himselfe sore straited vvith anguish in his conscience.

<span class="cw-pb" id="p116" data-p="116" data-facs="65" title="Page 116 of the 1613 edition begins here (原书第 116 页起)"></span>True libertie is that which GOD giues, and <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">True libertie, wherein it consisteth.</span> whereof our Sauiour speaketh, saying; *Then are you free, if the Sonne make you free:* hee looseth vs from all other bondage, that hee may bind vs to himselfe onelie. And this libertie consisteth, first in our deliuerance from the tyranny of our owne lusts, by which all those are thralled, who shake off the yoke of God: and next, in the freedome of a peaceable conscience, which is a Christians Paradise on earth.

<div class="cw-text" markdown="1">

VER. 46.

I vvill speake also of thy testimonies before Kings, and will not be ashamed.

</div>

IN these three last verses, *Dauid* promiseth a <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">A three-fold dutie of thankfulnes, promised by Dauid.</span> three-fold dutie of thankfulnes. First, the seruice of his tongue. Next, the seruice of his affections. Thirdly, the seruice of his actions. *Bona conscientia maximum semper praestat solatium:* <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Euthym.</span> a good conscience, renders alwaies great <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Basil.</span> consolation: *& vitalegitima fiduciam procreat,* <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">A good conscience makes great boldnes of speech.</span> and an honest life, makes great boldnes, to speak without feare or shame: as yee see in *Dauid* toward *Saul;* in *Elias* to *Achab;* in *Paule* to *Agrippa,* to *Festus* and to *Felix.*

The first dutie that heere hee promiseth, is to speake of the testimonies of God before Kings. This is not a small thing, if vvee doe consider, <span class="cw-pb" id="p117" data-p="117" data-facs="65" title="Page 117 of the 1613 edition begins here (原书第 117 页起)"></span> that albeit no sort of men bee more obliged to <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">No sort of men more obliged to God then Kings: yet cōmonly, none more vnthankfull.</span> God, then Kings are, yet vnto none is Gods word more vnwelcom then to thē: for the most part, they are so accustomed to commaund, and to giue out words for lawes vnto others, that they thinke strange to heare of a word which should stand in steed of any law to them. It is a rare thing to find a *Iosiah,* whose heart melted at the hearing <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">2. Kings. 13. 25</span> of the law: like vnto him was no King before him, who turned vnto the Lord with all his hart. Where such are giuen to a people, they are the exceeding great blessings of God.

If *Dauid* was not ashamed to speake of Gods <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">The subiect of out talk, should be Gods word.</span> word before Kings; farre lesse may wee thinke was he ashamed to speake of it before his familiars and companions: as hee protests hee vvould doe in another place; *Come vnto mee, all ye that feare God: and I will tell you vvhat God hath done to my soule.*

This may convince the vnthankfulnesse of this age, who hath tongues for euery purpose, but none to speake of the testimonies of God: yea, they are ashamed to speake of that subiect, who vvithout shame or measure speake of all other things.

<div class="cw-text" markdown="1">

<span class="cw-pb" id="p118" data-p="118" data-facs="66" title="Page 118 of the 1613 edition begins here (原书第 118 页起)"></span>VER. 47.

And my delight shall bee in thy commaundements, which I haue loued.

</div>

THe next thing hee promiseth, is the seruice <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Delight in Gods word, is a proofe of true godlinesse.</span> of his affections. It is not a small progresse in godlinesse, to delight in the commaundements of God. Our corrupt nature counts them burdenable: but the grace of Christ makes vs find his yoke easie, & his burden light. And indeede, so doe his children esteeme of it; who haue found by experience, there is more solid ioy in the obedience of Gods commaundements, then in all the perishing pleasures of sin. And would to GOD, they who of a long time <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">None can discern between worldly & spiritual pleasures, but they who haue prooued them both.</span> haue proued the pleasures of transgression, wold turne them, and proue by experience on the other hand, what comfort there is in mourning for sin; what is the ioy of a good conscience, and the sweet inward pleasures of a godly conuersation: they should then easily perceiue, that the delights <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">☞</span> of the one, doe by infinite degrees surmount the pleasures of the other.

Naturall men, do some externall workes of <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Actions of Gods worship, are nothing without a delight in them.</span> Gods worship; but not with an inward delight: this is no acceptable seruice to GOD. They assemble themselues on the Sabboth with the godly, to heare the word: but what the one doth of delight, the other doth of custome, or compulsion. The Lord lookes to the affection, moreth<span class="cw-gap" title="TCP: illegible in source（底本此处读不出）">•</span>n <span class="cw-pb" id="p119" data-p="119" data-facs="66" title="Page 119 of the 1613 edition begins here (原书第 119 页起)"></span> to the action: and we should not onely consider what we doe, but how we doe it; <span class="cw-gap" title="TCP: illegible in source（底本此处读不出）">•</span>o to come to the Temple, that we come with *Simean,* by motion of the Spirit; so to heare the word, that it be with spirituall ioy and delight, as *Dauid* did. And this also condemnes those of our age, to whom the word of the Lord is a reproach & weariness.

VER. 48.

Mine hands also will I lift vp to thy commandements, which <span class="cw-gap" title="TCP: illegible in source（底本此处读不出）">•</span>haue loued: and I wil meditate in thy Statute.

</div>

THe thi<span class="cw-gap" title="TCP: illegible in source（底本此处读不出）">•</span>d <span class="cw-gap" title="TCP: illegible in source（底本此处读不出）">〈◊〉</span> pron<span class="cw-gap" title="TCP: illegible in source（底本此处读不出）">•</span>h, is the seruice <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">The world is now full of <span class="cw-gap" title="TCP: illegible in source（底本此处读不出）">•</span>outilate Christians.</span> of hi<span class="cw-gap" title="TCP: illegible in source（底本此处读不出）">•</span> actions; that hee will lift vp his hands to <span class="cw-gap" title="TCP: illegible in source（底本此处读不出）">〈◊〉</span> practise of Gods commandements: *Dabo ope<span class="cw-gap" title="TCP: illegible in source（底本此处读不出）">•</span> in vt sactis exprima<span class="cw-gap" title="TCP: illegible in source（底本此处读不出）">•</span>* <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Vatab.</span> *mandatatua.* The kingdome of God, is not in word, but in power: we are the d<span class="cw-gap" title="TCP: illegible in source（底本此处读不出）">•</span>ciples of that <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Act. 1. 1.</span> Master, who first beganne to doe<span class="cw-gap" title="TCP: illegible in source（底本此处读不出）">▪</span> then to teach. *Qui fecit & docuit hi<span class="cw-gap" title="TCP: illegible in source（底本此处读不出）">•</span> magnus v<span class="cw-gap" title="TCP: illegible in source（底本此处读不出）">•</span>cabitur:* but <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">Basil.</span> now the wo<span class="cw-gap" title="TCP: illegible in source（底本此处读不出）">•</span>ld is full of mutilate Christians; eyther else they want an eare, and cannot heare <span class="cw-margin" markdown="1">☜</span> Gods word; or if they hear, they want a tongue, and cannot speak of it; or if they haue both, they want hands, and cannot practise it.

Concerning his protestation of the loue of Gods commandements, and his meditations of them, see ver. 15. 103. 111.
