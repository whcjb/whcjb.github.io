<div class="dk-text" markdown="1">

*Ver. 5.* But all their works they do for to be seen of men; they make broad their phylacteries, and enlarge the borders of their garments.

</div>

<div class="commentary-anchor" id="matthew-23-5"></div>

Phylacteries were some ribans, or fringes, wherupon were drawn the words of the Law, to put them in mind to keep the ten Commands, as is prescribed among other ceremonies, *Exodus* 13. *Numbers* 15.38. *Deut.* 6.8. and here our Lord reckoneth up their faults more specially: the first is, their vain oftentation of holinesse, and ambitious seeking of vain <span class="dk-pb" id="p262" data-p="262" data-facs="137" title="Page 262 of the 1651 edition begins here (原书第 262 页起)"></span> applause of men, to which end they did write the words of the law on the borders of their garments, as if they had been all made up of the love of the law. *Doct.* 1. The nature of hypocrites is to study more to seem religious, than to be religious) to please men with appearances, rather than to please God in truth; for, *They do,* saith he, *All to be seen of men.* 2. Hypocrites are most in affecting of ceremoniall observations, and outward parts of commanded duties, neglecting the substance; for, *They make broad,* saith he, *their phylacteries.*
