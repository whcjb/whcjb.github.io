<div class="dk-text" markdown="1">

*Verse 22.* Then Peter tooke him, and began to rebuke him, saying, Be it far from thee, Lord; this shall not be unto thee.

</div>

<div class="commentary-anchor" id="matthew-16-22"></div>

Peter out of his carnall wisdom rashly forbiddeth Christ to suffer himself to be so used. H<span class="dk-gap" title="TCP: illegible in source">•</span>nce learn, 1. That if the crosse be looked unto, without looking to the Appointer of it, the use, end, and outgate of it, men will never consent, that either they, or such as they love should bear it; therefore saith Peter; *Far it be from thee, Lord.* 2. Naturall wit is very confident and presumptuous to judge of Gods matters; for Peter leaning unto it, dare take upon him to rebuke Christ. <span class="dk-pb" id="p196" data-p="196" data-facs="104" title="Page 196 of the 1651 edition begins here (原书第 196 页起)"></span> 3. Yea, if a man lean unto, and follow his own naturall wit and affection, he may be led to oppose God, and the worke of his own Salvation; for, This *shall not be unto thee, Lord,* saith Peter.
