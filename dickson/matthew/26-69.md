<div class="commentary-anchor" id="matthew-26-70"></div>

How Peter denied Christ thrice, and then repented, is set down in the end of the chapter: wherin we learn, 1. That it is dangerous to be in the place with, and company of Christ's enemies, wi<span class="dk-gap" title="TCP: illegible in source">•</span> hour a speciall calling; for the occasion of *Peters* being tempted, *was his sitting in the high priests hall.* 2. A smal blast of temptation is sufficient to overturn a man who is puffed up with confidence of his own strength; as here, *The voyce of a Damsel <span class="dk-gap" title="TCP: illegible in source">•</span>ver <span class="dk-gap" title="TCP: illegible in source">••</span>rneth Peter.* 3. The shifting of a confession for Christ when it is requisite, is in effect a denyal of him; for *Peters* saying, *He knew not* what the damsel said, is called here, *A denyall.*
