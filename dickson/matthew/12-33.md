<div class="dk-text" markdown="1">

*Ver. 33.* Either make the tree good, and his fruit good; or else make the tree corrupt, and his fruit corrupt: for the tree is known by his fruit.

</div>

<div class="commentary-anchor" id="matthew-12-33"></div>

There were two things which might weaken the power of his threatning: 1. The conceit of the holinesse of these Pharisees. 2. That their blasphemy was but a matter of words; Therefore for the peoples edification, Christ breaketh down these sconces, shewing these men to be most wicked to ver. 36. and Gods judgement to be strict, even in the matter of words, to verse 38. As for the first he proveth these men not, to be holy, but wicked, by four reasons: The first is, because their *Fruits were evill,* set down in the comparison of trees which being good, do bring forth goods fruits: and being evil, do bring forth evill fruits; Hence learn, 1. As a tree is known to be good, or evil, by the fruits; so is a man known by the course of his deeds; for this is the intent of the similitude. 2. As a man carryeth himself for Christ, or against him, so is he good or evil; and though he were as blameless otherwise, as these Pharisees, yet he is a wicked man if he be against Christ; for *The tree is known by his fruit.*
